# Closest home RF device to Thermage: what I would actually buy

*Research and U.S. listing snapshot: October 2, 2026. This guide answers the home-device question. [The professional alternatives](index.html#doc17) remain a separate reference.*

**My practical first choice is CurrentBody Skin RF ST030. My stronger direct clinical evidence choice is the original wired NEWA, if an authentic, supported unit matching that evidence is available.** Neither is proven to produce Thermage-equivalent results. I would choose their documented delivery controls over a product selected for a Thermage-like MHz number, advertised depth, cooling badge or higher wattage. This is an evidence-informed buying judgment; no reviewed home-to-Thermage trial establishes an efficacy winner. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)[[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf)

## 2. What actually needs to resemble Thermage?

Thermage FLX uses noninvasive **6.78 MHz monopolar RF**, a treatment tip with a return electrode, cooling and delivery feedback. Its clinic protocol, tip geometry and operator-controlled energy delivery matter together. A home bipolar/multipolar device can pursue heat-mediated wrinkle improvement while producing a different current path and thermal distribution. Matching frequency alone does not reproduce the procedure. The FLX return pad is separately identified in AccessGUDID; that identity record does not prove clinical efficacy. [[16]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170758.pdf)[[19]](https://accessgudid.nlm.nih.gov/devices/00816995021285)

## 3. Why the NEWA result matters—and its limits

NEWA's pivotal home study enrolled 69 women, with 62 completers. FDA Table 2 reports **59/62** meeting a ≥1-point wrinkle improvement threshold by agreement of at least two of three independent raters at the end of the four-week course. This is a responder proportion, not 95% wrinkle removal or collagen growth. Follow-up extended to three months; there was no sham or untreated control and no Thermage comparator. The separate 12-week instrumental study adds a supportive signal, with manufacturer affiliation and no control. Neither establishes biopsy-confirmed human collagen gain or a clinic-equivalent lift. [[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27351303/)

For CurrentBody, I give substantial weight to a clear exact-model regulatory record and redundant controls. I do **not** upgrade its retail outcome percentages into independently audited clinical results. The reviewed public trials page documents LED studies; it did not supply an attributable full RF trial report in this pass. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)[[18]](https://www.currentbody.us/pages/currentbody-skin-user-and-clinical-trials)

## 4. AMIRO: stronger evidence, unresolved current-model transfer

Ai et al. studied **AMIRO ABF202 with 19 electrodes** in 22 women, Fitzpatrick III–IV, for eight weeks. The open-label single-arm course combined grid-mask deep mode with gel daily mode, three times weekly. Blinded wrinkle scores and ultrasound thickness improved; several image measures did not. Thickness is not a direct collagen assay. One participant had temporary erythema; no serious event was reported. Accompany Tech supplied devices and supported the journal service fee. The paper also contains a table sample label inconsistent with the stated enrollment. [[8]](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.16076)

The current S2/Seal2-Max manual also labels the unit ABF202, but describes **48 electrodes** and 1/1.5/2/2.6 MHz RF plus microcurrent and LEDs. Its **15 W adapter specification is a power-supply rating**, not proven RF output. A temperature-control fault indication is documented, but no numeric skin cutoff was located. This noninvasive grid-stamping design is not evidence of penetrating RF microneedling. Until hardware and outcome transfer are demonstrated, the older study earns B as an evidence reference; current S2 remains C. [[9]](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/0053/8866/4950/files/S2_Seal-Max.pdf?v=1695808061)

## 5. My final buying rule for this goal

1. **For a practical home purchase:** start with CurrentBody ST030 and verify its exact local IFU, eligibility and treatment areas. Expect a course aimed at modest wrinkle/skin-quality improvement, rather than a promised Thermage result.
2. **For stronger direct device evidence:** prioritize authentic original wired NEWA if available with support. The official store's stock status makes this conditional.
3. **For a stamping or cooling feature:** inspect AMIRO and Lumo+ separately. AMIRO has a real older-device human study; Lumo+ has a cooling claim. Neither feature alone wins the collagen recommendation.
4. **For a bigger lift:** I would not escalate to a poorly characterized hotter home machine. The literature does not establish a safe home setting or a number of sessions that converts these products into FLX.

Use the purchased model's instructions for gel, motion/stamping, cadence, exclusions and permitted areas. Do not raise temperature or power, improvise a return electrode, or bypass controls to imitate a professional treatment. RF microneedling belongs to a separate professional category. This guide ranks purchases; it does not prescribe a personalized treatment dose.

## 6. Research trail and remaining uncertainties

This focused pass reviewed regulatory records, exact-model manuals, the primary AMIRO paper, current official/brand listings and clinical-index pages. It is a targeted evidence review, not an exhaustive systematic review. No home-to-Thermage human comparison was located. Missing study or clearance identifiers describe what was verified here, not proof that a device has no effect or no authorization in any country.
