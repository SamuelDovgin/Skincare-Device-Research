# Home RF device: evidence-led specification blueprint

*Decision brief and engineering research plan, updated 2026-10-02. This is a product-specification proposal, not a treatment protocol or a claim that any device is clinically superior.*

## Recommendation first

If choosing a home RF device today, prioritize an exact, region-supported model with disclosed temperature feedback, an automatic output stop, a clearly bounded home-use indication, and a verifiable customer-support/warranty route. On the public records reviewed here, **CurrentBody Skin RF ST030 is the clearest currently purchasable U.S. specification benchmark**; its 1 MHz carrier, 5 ±1 W bench specification, two redundant thermistors and maximum skin-control temperature of 40.5 ±0.5 °C are specified in its FDA 510(k) summary. That establishes documented engineering controls and substantial equivalence for its labeled wrinkle indication, not superiority or a new clinical proof of benefit. [S01](source_docs/FDA_K232424_CurrentBody.pdf) [S86](https://www.currentbody.us/products/currentbody-skin-radio-frequency-device)

**NEWA is the home-device evidence benchmark, subject to verifying that the exact original model or an identified successor is currently available and supported.** Its FDA De Novo record includes direct home-use clinical performance testing and detailed RF/temperature bench tests. The study was not a sham-controlled head-to-head trial, and its results do not transfer automatically to NEWA Plus or other devices. [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) [S70](https://mynewa.com/products/newa)

For a new home-device design, start with a **well-controlled approximately 1 MHz bipolar/multipolar baseline** and use **0.5–2.5 MHz as the most interesting controlled engineering comparison band**. This is an evidence and testability choice, not a finding that those carriers cause the best remodeling. Do not make 6 MHz, 6.25 MHz, 40.68 MHz, a high wattage number, or a high claimed dermal temperature the target until exact-device thermal measurements and comparative human outcomes support it. The current evidence still does not identify a universal optimal MHz or remodeling temperature. [S15](https://www.ya-man.com/en/products/bloom-6.php) [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/) [S82](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SR90.html)

## The proposed home-device brief

| Requirement | Proposed specification | Why it earns priority | Boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Intended use and user** | Non-invasive home RF for a narrow, clearly labeled cosmetic endpoint such as mild-to-moderate facial wrinkles in adults; publish exact population, facial zones, exclusions, cadence, and jurisdiction. | It keeps the product and evidence aimed at a defined use, rather than implying it is a home Thermage or clinical resurfacing replacement. FDA's OTC RF framework expects tested performance, understandable labeling, usability, and clinical performance for each indicated use. [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) | Do not generalize an indication to deeper laxity, body contouring, darker skin types, the eyelid, or post-procedure use without supporting evidence and labeling. |
| **Energy class** | Use non-invasive RF as the distinct, measurable core mode; disclose whether bipolar, multipolar, capacitive/displacement-current, or another coupling design. If adding EMS or light, make each output independently switchable and test outcomes separately. | Electrode geometry, spacing, contact, coupling layer, impedance and motion all shape the electric and heat fields. A device with one carrier can heat differently from another. Combination-device benefits cannot be assigned to RF without a suitable comparison. [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/) | Home RF microneedling is outside this brief; FDA says RF microneedling is a medical procedure and should not be used at home. See the [FDA safety communication](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication). |
| **Carrier frequency** | Use approximately **1 MHz** as the reference design; if variable frequency is a real product differentiator, bench-test **0.5, 0.8, 1.0, 1.7 and 2.45 MHz** in otherwise matched conditions. | The well-documented home-device lineage is around 1 MHz. YA-MAN's current Bloom 6 claims 0.5–2.5 MHz; professional Reaction experiments include 0.8/1.7/2.45 MHz. These provide useful comparison points. [S01–S03](source_docs/FDA_K232424_CurrentBody.pdf) [S15](https://www.ya-man.com/en/products/bloom-6.php) [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/) | No human comparison isolates carrier frequency as the cause of better results. Match power, electrode geometry, coupling, temperature-time profile and protocol before attributing differences to MHz. |
| **Temperature sensing and output control** | Closed-loop contact-surface sensing with at least two independent sensors, disclosed sensor locations and tolerances, continuous control, and a separate hard stop if a sensor/contact check fails. Publish the stop/restart behavior. | CurrentBody and Sensilift Pro FDA summaries document two redundant thermistors; NEWA's De Novo filing documents RF shutoff above a specified temperature. This makes controls inspectable. [S01](source_docs/FDA_K232424_CurrentBody.pdf) [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) [S03](source_docs/FDA_K250341_Sensilift_Pro.pdf) | A light, beep, impedance estimate or “smart temperature” label is not a temperature measurement. Sensor values at the head are not a depth-resolved dermal temperature map. |
| **Temperature target** | Define the measured location first. As an initial engineering reference, investigate **surface/contact regulation within roughly 40–42 °C**, then choose and validate a specific cap through risk analysis and model-specific clinical work. Record temperature versus time, not peak alone. | FDA files for CurrentBody and Sensilift Pro list maxima near 40–40.5 °C; NEWA's file tests a 42 °C surface cutoff. These are actual device-control examples, not a universal efficacy range. [S01](source_docs/FDA_K232424_CurrentBody.pdf) [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) [S03](source_docs/FDA_K250341_Sensilift_Pro.pdf) | **Do not state that 40, 42, 45, or 50 °C is the proven “collagen remodeling optimum.”** Surface cutoff, sensor reading, claimed dermal temperature and measured experimental tissue temperature are different quantities. There is no validated universal home-RF temperature-time target in this evidence set. |
| **Power and waveform** | Disclose RF carrier tolerance, waveform, pulse cycle/width, duty cycle, delivered RF output with measurement method and tolerance, and performance across stated resistive/tissue-simulating loads. Provide the electrode map, treatment area and coupling requirements. | NEWA's file, for example, reports 1 MHz, a 750 ms pulse cycle, 300 ms pulse duration and 10 W validation on a 360 Ω load; CurrentBody reports 5 ±1 W and 1 MHz. These show why units and test conditions matter. [S01](source_docs/FDA_K232424_CurrentBody.pdf) [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) | Do not compare a power-supply rating or an unqualified “18 W” claim with RF output from a cleared device. Watts alone do not define field strength, depth or tissue dose. |
| **Contact and motion safeguards** | Detect loss of contact/coupling and pause RF immediately; manage dwell and movement with a validated timer/guide; stop if movement stalls, a sensor fails, or the head exceeds its limit. | YA-MAN describes contact detection and resistance-factor feedback on Bloom 6; CurrentBody lists rapid stop/start on contact loss. These are valuable design features when their operation is validated. [S15](https://www.ya-man.com/en/products/bloom-6.php) [S86](https://www.currentbody.us/products/currentbody-skin-radio-frequency-device) | Contact sensing and impedance adjustment complement temperature sensing; neither substitutes for the other. |
| **Clinical package** | Test the exact marketed model under its labeled routine with a registered, adequately powered human study, a credible comparator, blinded outcome grading, standardized photography, adverse-event reporting, relevant skin phototypes, and follow-up after treatment. Publish protocol, withdrawals, sponsor and full results. | Current home-RF outcome evidence is small, mixed-modality, uncontrolled, or model-mapping limited; NEWA remains one direct pivotal record rather than a current-device comparative win. YA-MAN/Jmoon results concern a multimodal regional device study and do not establish an isolated-frequency effect. [S02](source_docs/FDA_DEN150005_NEWA.pdf) [S74](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.70096) [S75](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13555-022-00697-y) | A predicate clearance, generic RF paper, internal consumer survey, patent, or cross-model family claim is not an exact-model efficacy trial. |
| **Usability and value** | Make the labeled routine short and repeatable; give clear gel/interface instructions, cleaning, treatment-area sequence, use logging and recovery guidance. State device price, recurring interface costs, return terms, warranty, local service and software/data handling. | A technically sophisticated tool that a customer will not use consistently may be poor value. Current prices and stock differ by country; treatment regimens and outcomes are too heterogeneous for an honest “cost per collagen” figure. | Compare total ownership cost and service in the customer's region. Do not turn price or feature count into an efficacy score. |

### Which frequency is most promising?

| Lane | Research priority | What would change the recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **~1 MHz** | **Baseline to beat.** Strongest device-specific home documentation in this archive: CurrentBody, NEWA and Sensilift Pro filings. | A matched study showing another carrier improves a predefined clinical endpoint at equal geometry and controlled thermal exposure. |
| **0.5–2.5 MHz** | **Most promising engineering experiment.** Includes YA-MAN Bloom 6's claimed variable band and 0.8/1.7/2.45 MHz professional research settings. | Exact-home-model tests that report calibrated output and spatial/temporal heat distribution, followed by an exact-model clinical comparison. |
| **3–4 MHz** | Feature-led Panasonic lane; compare SR85 (3 MHz) and SR86 (4 MHz) as combinations. | A same-family randomized comparison that isolates RF carrier while controlling the other functions. |
| **1–6 MHz Panasonic EH-SR90 / 6.25 MHz MimiSilk** | **Watch and validate, not buy on MHz.** Panasonic now officially lists EH-SR90 for purchase-period eligibility, with a 1–6 MHz RF range and eight electrodes. MimiSilk markets 6.25 MHz. [S82](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SR90.html) [S83](https://panasonic.jp/beauty/campaign/26autumn-face-cashback.html) [S85](https://www.mimisilk.com/products/mimisilk-vera-rf-sculpt-6-25mhz-gel-free-radio-frequency-skin-lifting-device) | Independent thermal maps, exact-model human outcomes and a meaningful matched comparison. A nearby number to Thermage's 6.78 MHz does not make the coupling design, power, treatment or effect equivalent. |
| **40.68 MHz** | Professional research and product-marketing lane; do not make it a home-device target on frequency alone. | Exact consumer model characterization and direct comparison with lower-frequency devices under matched dose/geometry. |

## A practical validation plan for a prototype

1. **Freeze one consumer indication, one target user and one geography.** Write labeling and exclusions before selecting the temperature or power values.
2. **Characterize the hardware.** Record carrier frequency and tolerance, waveform, pulse timing, true RF output under defined loads, electrode area/spacing, coupling impedance, and output with poor contact, stalled movement and sensor faults.
3. **Map temperature over time and space.** Use calibrated, independent contact sensors and a skin-relevant phantom to measure hot spots and repeatability at multiple locations/depths. Keep bench values distinct from living-human temperatures. Show sensor uncertainty, time to steady state, overshoot and cooling after shutoff.
4. **Verify protection and usability.** Test loss of contact, coupling changes, sensor disagreement/failure, dwell, low gel, motion pauses, skin variation, electrical safety, cleaning and label comprehension under the applicable regulatory plan.
5. **Only then run human outcomes.** Register the study, size it for a meaningful endpoint, blind evaluators, report all withdrawals/adverse events, use the marketed device and regimen, and preserve exact logs and follow-up. Do not adjust the treatment to chase a hotter reading.
6. **Compare frequency scientifically.** Keep electrode geometry and material, contact layer, output setting, temperature-time curve, treatment time and protocol matched; randomize the carrier assignment. Otherwise the frequency comparison is confounded by device design and delivered heat.

This plan supports product research and controlled development. It does not define a home treatment protocol.

## Decision and research gaps

**Current best-supported buying lane:** a named non-invasive home model with transparent thermal controls and exact-model regulatory scope, currently represented by CurrentBody ST030. **Most interesting frequency experiment:** controlled 0.5–2.5 MHz, anchored to 1 MHz. **Most promising product-design features:** redundant temperature sensing plus contact/impedance feedback, repeatable delivery, a fault-safe cutoff and a short usable routine. **Largest evidence gap:** no robust trial shows a consumer 6 MHz/6.25 MHz design outperforms the documented approximately 1 MHz devices when thermal dose and geometry are controlled.

Before a premium purchase, verify the exact SKU and IFU, price in your market, total gel/consumables cost, return window, local warranty/service, labeled treatment areas and contraindications. For health history, implants, active skin disease, recent procedures or medication-related sensitivity, follow the model's instructions and ask an appropriate clinician; this comparison does not determine individual suitability.
