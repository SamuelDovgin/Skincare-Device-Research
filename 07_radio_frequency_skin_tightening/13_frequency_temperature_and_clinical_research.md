# Which RF technology, MHz and temperature deserve attention?

*Research update 2026-10-01; Panasonic status and device-spec recheck 2026-10-02. Device selection analysis, not a treatment setting prescription. Surface targets, sensor cutoffs, claimed dermal targets and directly measured experimental temperatures are different quantities.*

## October 2 collagen and model-revision update

The [collagen synthesis](index.html#doc16) and [actual product tiers](index.html#doc10) now distinguish direct human outcomes, indirect regulatory bridges and combination-device evidence. STOP Vx2’s retail-linked manual lists 0.85/1.0/1.15 MHz ±10%, while the 2025 mouse experiment reports 0.9/1.0/1.25 MHz: verify the exact revision rather than treating either as a universal Vx2 specification. New 2026 YOUMAGIC versus Thermage CPT and water-cooled monopolar studies inform professional delivery, not consumer MHz superiority. The [visualizer](rf_spec_fit_visualizer.html) now centers collagen evidence and fixed recommendation tiers.

## The answer to “what MHz is best?”

**No universal best MHz for facial collagen remodeling has been established.** For buying now, focus first on **controlled bipolar/multipolar home RF around the well-documented1 MHz lineage**, then evaluate promising designs in the **0.5–3 MHz range** by exact-model evidence and feedback. **6–6.78 MHz** is a legitimate professional architecture range and an emerging consumer marketing lane, not a requirement for effective home treatment. **40.68 MHz** can also be used professionally; its large number is not an efficacy score. [CurrentBody S01](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf), [NEWA S02](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf), [Reaction study S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/), [POLARGEN S49](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28557650/).

This priority order is an **inference from documentation and evidence**, not a measured treatment-effect ranking. A consumer 6.25 MHz multipolar device, professional 6.78 MHz monopolar Thermage, and 40.68 MHz microcurrent combination can heat different volumes and produce different temperature histories.

## Why the number cannot determine depth or results

RF heating depends on electric field, conductivity/impedance, electrode geometry and spacing, contact/coupling, drive voltage/current, power control and time. A local resistive-heating approximation is **q = σ|E|²**, with q inW/m³, conductivity σ inS/m and field E inV/m (RMS convention). Tissue temperature also depends on thermal conduction, perfusion and cooling. This equation explains a dependency; it is not calibrated to a consumer device and cannot predict its thermal dose.

For closely spaced bipolar electrodes, current tends to remain more local; monopolar active-to-return-pad delivery can involve a broader volume. Capacitive coupling can alter the interface. None of this supports a universal **MHz → millimeters** conversion or a constant half-electrode-spacing treatment depth across faces. The Reaction experiments found small ex-vivo frequency-dependent differences that did not clearly persist in vivo. [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/). Thermage's return pad, controlled pulse delivery and cooling distinguish it from a home multipolar wand even when carrier frequencies nearly match. [CPT technical manual S54](https://ca.thermage.com/siteassets/hcp/pdf/thermage-cpt-technical-users-manual-model-tg-2b-p-np009240-06-2021.pdf).

**Audit corrections:** charging power is not RF output;6 in Bloom6 is not6 MHz;1 MHz ultrasound in older Panasonic specs is not automatically RF;6.25≈6.78 does not establish Thermage equivalence. Carrier frequency, EMS repetition rate and ultrasound frequency must have separate columns. [Bloom S15](https://www.ya-man.com/en/products/bloom-6.php), [Panasonic S11](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SR73/spec.html), [MimiSilk S39](https://www.mimisilk.com/blogs/news/professional-grade-rf-frequency-at-home-how-mimisilk-vera-closes-the-gap-safely).

## “What temperature remodels collagen?” has more than one endpoint

Distinguish **temporary contraction, stress signaling, matrix synthesis, tissue coagulation and visible durable tightening**. They are not interchangeable.

| Exposure concept | Research interpretation | Translation boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Repeated gentle heating | Heat-shock pathways can support collagen-processing biology. One 31-woman steam study used surface 40–42°C for ten minutes/day over two months | Steam/cell evidence does not establish which RF product works best [S51](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1574075706000076) |
| Controlled RF thermal stimulation | Professional Reaction study supports fine-line improvement with parameter-controlled heating and preclinical collagen change | Does not establish consumer targets or one safe universal threshold [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/) |
| Higher-temperature contraction/coagulation | Temperature must be paired with exposure duration and location; increasing peak heat can increase injury | No license to defeat a home cutoff or hold a sliding device stationary |
| Surface thermistor value | Useful feedback at/near electrode contact | Does not measure every dermal voxel or exclude focal hot spots/fat injury |

A common explanation that 45°C is the exact point at which collagen contracts and regeneration starts is too categorical. Different assays, thermal histories and tissues yield different responses. A burn boundary is also not one temperature independent of time. No dose calculator here converts these bands into a “safe” or guaranteed-remodeling verdict.

## Clinical and mechanistic studies: what is transferable?

### Home-device evidence already in the archive

The [study-level home evidence map](index.html#doc11) retains NEWA, original TriPollar STOP, Silk'n HST, split-face RF versus cosmetic, Chinese baseline-controlled RF and Korean multi-energy trials. Original NEWA's pivotal file enrolled69 women,62 completed and59/62 met its blinded responder criterion; no sham comparison. It supports a real wrinkle signal, not a95% sham-adjusted probability of benefit. The indication and exclusions are model/population-specific. [S02](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf).

CurrentBody and **Sensilift Pro** supply substantial-equivalence/bench evidence rather than new subject-device clinical outcomes. That is a reason to trust their disclosed technical envelope, not to declare them more effective than all other models. [S01](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf), [S03](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K250341.pdf).

### YA-MAN/Jmoon controlled bundle trial, 2025

Bu et al. (PMID40099405) randomized90 volunteers;80 completed a four-week comparison of YA-MAN ACE fifth-generation, Jmoon and a power-free control. Study-table RF ranges were 0.5–2.5 and0.8–2 MHz,  respectively. Bundled energies, gel and a meridian-guided technique were used five times/week. Imaging/photo endpoints improved, but the short duration, multiple endpoints, observer disagreements and imperfectly sensory-matched control limit confidence. Mild/moderate redness resolved within 30 minutes. No specific grant and no conflicts were declared. There was no direct human collagen biopsy endpoint, isolated-frequency comparison or verified mapping to today's Bloom SKU. [S74](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jocd.70096).

### Re-extracted 2022 split-face hardware and endpoints

Shu et al.:33 women/32 completed,12 weeks,1 MHz bipolar ring RF plus630±10nm red light,37–42°C skin regulation and motion sensing. The comparison was device+gel versus a different cosmetic. Wrinkle/thickness/radiance changes favored the device bundle; **hydration and elasticity did not differ significantly between sides**. No adverse reactions reported. Ecolite supported the publication fee. Retail SKU remains unnamed in primary methods; a review attributes it to YA-MAN. Cadence differs across text; design section gives five/week for two months, then three/week. [S75](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13555-022-00697-y).

A YA-MAN-funded registry targeted16 people and lists completion without posted outcome data in the reviewed fields; it cannot upgrade a buying verdict. [S76](https://center6.umin.ac.jp/cgi-open-bin/ctr_e/ctr_view.cgi?recptno=R000061175).

### New focused extraction: Reaction CORE,2.45 MHz

Bai/Ni et al., PMID39327749 / DOI10.1111/jocd.16600, compared0.8,1.7 and 2.45 MHz experimentally, then used **professional Reaction/Sinclair 2.45 MHz** in an uncontrolled facial cohort. **46 enrolled; six did not complete; three sessions three weeks apart; three-month assessment.** Fine lines improved; the experimental work favored 2.45 MHz for more stable heating on that platform. **It did not randomize humans to different frequencies.** Histologic support is mainly preclinical. Authors reported no conflicts and funding SHDC2022CRT020; some outcome tables/text still reference46 despite attrition. Therefore 2.45 MHz is a research lead, not a universal home optimum. [S48](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11743280/), [lawful full-text XML](source_docs/Bai_Ni_2024_RF_thermal_stimulation_PMC11743280.xml).

###40.68 MHz: legitimate professional research, limited consumer transfer

The **POLARGEN pilot** involved ten women, four sessions two weeks apart, photos through two months and a biopsy from one participant. Satisfaction averaged4.5 on a six-point scale; major complications were not reported. No control arm and a tiny sample limit conclusions. Importantly, the primary abstract says **four sessions**; a retrieved review snippet describes a single treatment, so this archive follows the primary record. [S49](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28557650/).

A2026 prospective single-arm 40.68 MHz unipolar study reports modest measurable contour/elasticity changes after three sessions. Only abstract/search-level findings were extracted here; sample, exact thermal dose and conflicts need full-text confirmation. Neither paper validates Medicube Ultra Tune's hardware or shows 40.68 MHz beats1–3 MHz home RF. [S50](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13400472/).

### Mechanism: plausible, without a product ranking

The HSP47 RF/ultrasound study supports a heat-mediated matrix-turnover hypothesis; because energies are combined, it cannot assign a consumer RF-only effect. [S52](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32081434/). A2024 cell/aged-animal study describes HSP/Piezo1/TGF-beta and dermal–epidermal-junction changes. LIBON employment and research grants are disclosed. This supports biological plausibility, not a current-brand human wrinkle effect. [S53](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38791217/).

**What the clinical section found:** promising RF signals across disparate frequencies, but limited controlled current-model comparisons. Clinical improvement, ultrasound thickness and animal collagen staining should have separate evidence labels.

## Which technology is most promising?

For **home wrinkle maintenance**, focus on reliable **temperature feedback, contact/impedance control, even electrode coupling and protocol adherence**. Redundant sensing with a disclosed failure response is more informative than an unexplained “smart” label. Variable-frequency/multiple-ring systems are interesting when they improve uniformity, but require measurements to show it. Capacitive/gel-free designs could improve adherence; convenience is not proven superior remodeling.

For **professional tightening**, cooled monopolar RF has a different usable energy/volume envelope. Discuss exact device, measured/validated endpoints, anatomy and fat-preservation goals with the treating clinician. Professional bipolar/fractional platforms may suit different endpoints; microneedling RF is a separate invasive choice rather than a stronger home wand. The existing [home/professional comparison](index.html#doc4) and [clinical map](index.html#doc11) preserve the risk boundary.

For an actual product brief, see the [evidence-led home-RF specification proposal](index.html#doc15) and [interactive spec-fit visualizer](rf_spec_fit_visualizer.html). For this archive's routine, RF is optional when mild wrinkles/laxity are a defined unmet goal. More frequency or heat is not a reason to stack it on irritated skin or during active laser recovery. Exact model contraindications and clinician guidance after procedures outrank generic internet scheduling.

## Evidence requests before paying a premium

Ask for the exact model and IFU; RF output on a specified load and duty cycle; electrode map; coupling requirements; sensor location, cutoff and fault behavior; measured temperature-versus-time at multiple depths and facial sites; trial registration, sample/completers, comparator, blinded endpoint, duration, adverse events and sponsorship. “FDA registered,” a patent, CE logo or generic RF paper does not answer these questions.

**The decisive missing experiment:** a matched sham-controlled comparison of current consumer devices, with standardized temperature-time traces and six-to-twelve-month blinded outcomes. To isolate MHz,  compare carriers in otherwise matched hardware with controlled power/thermal dose; a high-MHz brand versus a low-MHz brand is insufficient.
