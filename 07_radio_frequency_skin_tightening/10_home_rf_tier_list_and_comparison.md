# Home RF Tier List & Device Comparison

*Compiled 2026-08-12. This is research orientation, not medical advice. Confidence labels: **verified** = FDA, IFU, official product page, or peer-reviewed source; **limited** = marketing, small/manufacturer-led study, or model-specific evidence; **inference** = reasoned comparison from the documented mechanism and safety controls.*

## 0. Bottom line

If mild wrinkles or laxity remain a defined unmet goal after the active Tria course and recovery are stable, **home RF is the most defensible distinct category to evaluate next**. That does not make it a current routine gap or an automatic purchase. It is not a substitute for Thermage, fractional laser, or RF microneedling, but it has a real OTC wrinkle-reduction pathway and repeated-use human evidence. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf)[[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)[[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm?ID=K232424)

My shortlist:

1. **CurrentBody Skin RF** if you want the clearest current FDA file, disclosed RF parameters, redundant temperature sensing, and a conservative home-use ceiling. [[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)
2. **NEWA / NEWA Plus** if you can verify the exact current model and want the strongest dedicated home-RF clinical/regulatory anchor. The original FDA De Novo file is strong evidence for the category, not a guarantee that every later model or seller is identical. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf)[[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm?ID=K232424)
3. **TriPollar STOP Vx / current STOP variants** if you value a more active, guided heat experience and accept more model-specific uncertainty around temperature and clearance claims. The brand's protocol is clear; some popular “55 °C” claims are not independently verified in the public materials reviewed here. [[4]](https://mytripollar.com/products/tripollar-stop-vx)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21401380/)

I would **not** make the Konmison LB056B or an unverified Alibaba RF wand the first RF device. The LB056B appears to be real 2 MHz bipolar hardware, but its published 55 W is total device consumption, not confirmed tissue RF output, and the reviewed materials do not disclose temperature sensing, thermal shutoff, a clinical study, or a US clearance. [[6]](https://www.konmison.com/product-item/3-in-1-rf-radio-frequency-facial-machine/)[[7]](02_initial_device_landscape.md)

## 1. How to read the tiers

The tiers rank **buying confidence for a home user**, not “how close to Thermage” a device is. Those are different questions:

| Tier | Meaning | What it does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| **A — home shortlist** | A documented home RF device with a credible safety/regulatory or human-evidence anchor | Not Thermage-equivalent; results remain modest and consistency-dependent |
| **B — plausible but verify** | Real RF is plausible and the product may be useful, but the exact model, indication, independent data, or regulatory chain is thinner | Not automatically unsafe or ineffective; simply lower confidence |
| **C — professional RF** | A higher-power or needle-delivered clinical category | Not a home-buying option |
| **D — avoid as a first purchase** | Missing the temperature/IFU/clearance/clinical information needed to judge the dose and risk | Not proof that no RF energy comes out |

The archive's earlier physics comparison is still useful for understanding depth, temperature, and the Thermage ceiling; this page adds the practical purchase ranking that the earlier landscape left open. [[8]](06_thermage_vs_everything_comparison.md)[[9]](09_laser_vs_rf_technology_comparison.md)

## 2. Tier list

### Tier A — home RF shortlist

| Rank | Device | Why it makes the shortlist | Main caveat | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | **CurrentBody Skin RF** | FDA 510(k) K232424; 1 MHz bipolar RF; 5 ± 1 W; two redundant thermistors; 40.5 ± 0.5 °C maximum allowed temperature; exact OTC mild-to-moderate facial-wrinkle indication | The clearance relies on substantial equivalence and did not add a new clinical trial; the conservative temperature cap limits the maximum thermal dose | Evidence-first buyer who wants a clearly documented, low-drama home device |
| **A2** | **NEWA / NEWA Plus** | FDA De Novo DEN150005 created the OTC RF category; the original study had a real wrinkle-severity endpoint and a 42 °C thermistor cutoff | Original De Novo evidence is model- and population-specific: adult women, Fitzpatrick I–IV, mild-to-moderate facial wrinkles; current product availability and model details must be checked | Buyer who prioritizes the strongest dedicated home-RF clinical/regulatory anchor |
| **A3** | **TriPollar STOP Vx / STOP Vx Gold 2 / related STOP variants** | Established home-RF lineage; official protocol uses contact across the electrodes, an orange “optimal temperature” indicator, and 2–3 treatments weekly for 6–8 weeks; older home TriPollar literature reports wrinkle endpoints | Current variant, regional clearance, temperature algorithm, and DMA/ELV add-ons are not interchangeable; the public product page is partly marketing evidence | Buyer willing to pay for a more guided and thermally assertive experience |

**A-tier conclusion:** for a single addition to this archive's current routine, I would start with **CurrentBody Skin RF** for documentation and safety transparency, then compare a legitimate NEWA or TriPollar offer if the user values clinical history or a more active heat protocol over conservative specs.

The MultiPlatform manual also describes an OTC facial-wrinkle indication. [[12]](https://medicube.us/products/age-r-ultra-tune-40-68) | The public page does not provide the same FDA/IFU evidence package as the Tier-A anchors, and the reported test is not an independent, peer-reviewed head-to-head trial. ### Tier C — professional RF, not home RF

| Category | What it adds | Why it stays separate |
|---|---|---|
| **Thermage FLX and other professional non-invasive monopolar RF** | A clinic platform can deliver a larger/deeper treatment volume with trained contact, energy selection, and cooling/endpoint management. It is the professional RF benchmark in this archive. [[8]](06_thermage_vs_everything_comparison.md)[[14]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18940540/) | Cost, operator dependence, and a different thermal-dose envelope. “Home RF” should not be marketed as a lower-cost Thermage replacement. |
| **RF microneedling: Morpheus8, Potenza, Genius, Vivace and similar** | Needle electrodes place RF at controlled depths and can target scars, texture, wrinkles, and selected laxity more aggressively than non-invasive home RF. [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31825296/) | This is an invasive medical procedure. FDA's current safety communication describes burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement, nerve damage, and cases requiring medical or surgical intervention; FDA says RF microneedling devices should not be used at home. [[16]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication) |

### Tier D — do not use as the first RF purchase

| Device/class | Why it lands here |
|---|---|

### What the table says about “hotter”

Temperature numbers are not interchangeable across devices. A surface thermistor, a claimed internal temperature, a marketing “optimal temperature,” and a tissue temperature at depth are different measurements. The public files support a safer conclusion: **temperature feedback and a model-specific IFU are more valuable than a large wattage or temperature number without measurement context.** [[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)[[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPMN/pmn.cfm?ID=K232424)[[6]](https://www.konmison.com/product-item/3-in-1-rf-radio-frequency-facial-machine/)

## 4. How RF fits with Tria, NIRA, and RLT

The supplied NIRA/Tria note is directionally right: NIRA and Tria are both optical laser treatments in the broad water-heating/dermal-remodeling family, but Tria is fractional while NIRA is non-fractional. RF adds a different energy-delivery mechanism: electrical impedance heating rather than optical absorption. [[9]](09_laser_vs_rf_technology_comparison.md)

That makes the stack look like this:

| Existing/add-on | Primary mechanism | Incremental role |
|---|---|---|
| **Tria Age-Defying / FRX** | 1440 nm fractional non-ablative laser; discrete microthermal zones | Texture and remodeling with a more obvious treatment/recovery cycle |
| **NIRA** | ~1450 nm non-fractional laser; repeated optical dermal heating | Gentler wrinkle-focused laser; overlaps Tria's broad optical lane more than RF does |
| **Home RF** | Electrical impedance heating; volumetric/local current paths | Firmness and mild-wrinkle maintenance through a non-optical thermal mechanism |
| **RLT/PBM** | Low-intensity red/NIR photobiomodulation | Adjunctive, non-ablative, low-downtime signaling; not a replacement for RF or Tria |

**Practical implication:** if the goal is incremental collagen support rather than another laser, RF is the more defensible next category. I would separate RF from Tria sessions and from any active-recovery decision until the skin is back to baseline; that spacing is a conservative routine inference, not a published universal interval.

## 5. Purchase checklist

Before buying any home RF device, require:

1. Exact model name and legal manufacturer—not just a brand family.
2. Frequency in MHz, RF output power in W, and the load/measurement condition.
3. Electrode geometry and treatment area.
4. Temperature sensor count/location, control behavior, and thermal shutoff or move signal.
5. A model-specific IFU with contraindications, treatment areas, gel/coupling instructions, and cadence.
6. A traceable FDA 510(k)/De Novo record if a US wrinkle-reduction claim is central to the purchase.
7. A clear statement that it is **non-invasive RF**, not a needle-delivered RF pen.

If a listing emphasizes “60 °C,” “body slimming,” “whitening,” or “professional results” while omitting those seven items, downgrade it immediately.

## 6. Safety boundary for this routine

Use only the manufacturer’s protocol for the exact model. Do not treat over a pacemaker/defibrillator or implanted electronic device, over metal hardware or fillers unless the IFU and clinician say it is appropriate, on active infection/open or inflamed skin, or on areas the manual excludes. Stop for persistent pain, blistering, burn-like redness, swelling, pigment change, or an unexpected contour change and seek medical advice.

Do not convert the absence of a visible reaction into proof that a device is safe. RF microneedling has a different risk profile, and FDA specifically says it should not be used at home. [[16]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication)

## Evidence gaps

- Current model availability and regional clearance chains change; verify the serial/model number before purchase.
- Home-RF studies often use brand-specific protocols and small samples; wrinkle-scale improvement is not the same as deep laxity correction.
- Public marketing does not consistently disclose internal tissue temperature, treatment depth, or delivered RF power.
- There is no good head-to-head randomized trial of CurrentBody vs NEWA vs TriPollar vs Medicube using the same endpoint and follow-up.
- “Thermage proximity” percentages in earlier notes are heuristic communication devices, not validated clinical scores.
