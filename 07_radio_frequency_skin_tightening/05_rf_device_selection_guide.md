# RF Device Quick Selection Guide

*Pick the right radiofrequency device (or decide RF isn't right for you) in 60 seconds. For full specs, evidence, and regulatory details, see the numbered docs this references.*

---

## Step 0: Is RF even what you need?

RF treats **laxity, mild wrinkles, and firmness.** It does NOT treat:

- ❌ Pigment / PIH / melasma → see laser/IPL or topicals
- ❌ Redness / rosacea → see vascular laser or topicals
- ❌ Acne bacteria → see blue light, topicals, or professional treatments
- ❌ Deep scarring → see fractional laser or RF microneedling (professional only)

**If your primary concern is pigment/PIH, RF is not your lead tool.** Sunscreen + topicals + possibly a pigment-specific device come first.

---

## Step 1: What category of RF are you looking for?

| Category | What it is | Risk level | Results | Price range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **FDA-cleared home RF** | OTC devices with temp sensing, 510(k)/De Novo clearance for mild-moderate wrinkles | Low | Modest, consistency-dependent | $200–$600 |
| **CE-only OEM RF** | Chinese-manufactured RF devices with CE marking, no FDA clearance | Medium (no temp sensing, no US regulatory oversight) | Unknown — no clinical data | $45–$150 |
| **Professional non-invasive RF** | Thermage, in-office monopolar RF | Medium (operator-dependent) | Stronger, fewer sessions | $1,000–$3,000/session |
| **RF microneedling** | Needles deliver RF into dermis. Morpheus8, Potenza, Genius, Vivace. | **High** — FDA warns of burns, scarring, fat loss, nerve damage | Strongest for scars/texture/laxity | $800–$2,500/session |
| **Fake RF / combo wands** | EMS/microcurrent/LED devices labeled "RF" with no real RF specs | Low (ineffective) but wastes money | None from RF | $20–$100 |

## §A — FDA-Cleared Home RF (Safe, Proven, Modest Results)

These have temperature sensing, thermal shutoff, and US regulatory clearance for wrinkle treatment.

| Pick | RF type | Key specs | Price | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **🥇 CurrentBody Skin RF** | Bipolar | 1 MHz, 5W RF output, 40.5°C max, 4 electrodes, K232424 | ~$350 | Best-documented home RF. Cleanest spec file. |
| **🥈 NEWA** | RF (proprietary) | FDA De Novo DEN150005, Fitz I-IV, mild-moderate wrinkles | ~$300 | The original home-RF regulatory anchor. |
| **🥉 TriPollar STOP Vx** | Multipolar | FDA-cleared lineage (K220322 for STOP U), multi-RF electrode switching | ~$400–$600 | Strong brand. Good clinical data in brand studies. Higher price. |

**Bottom line:** If you want home RF with real safety systems and US clearance, CurrentBody Skin RF is the current best-documented pick. All three are credible — pick by price, availability, and whether you prefer gel-based (CurrentBody) or gel-free (some TriPollar models).

---

## §B — CE-Only OEM RF (Budget Experiment, Accept the Unknowns)

These come from Chinese manufacturers (Alibaba, etc.) with CE marking but no FDA clearance, no temperature sensing, and no published clinical data. **You are the safety system.**

| Pick | RF type | Key specs | Price | Red flags |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Konmison LB056B** | Bipolar | 2 MHz, 55W total, 1–15 J/cm², 3 probes (face/body/eye), 5 levels | ~$45–50 (Alibaba) | No temp sensing, no FDA clearance, no clinical data, "whitening" claim is nonsense, no contraindication list |
| **Other Alibaba RF wands** | Usually bipolar | Varies wildly — often no frequency disclosed | $30–$100 | Assume worst case: verify frequency, output power, CE cert, and temp sensing before buying |

**Bottom line:** The Konmison LB056B is at least from a verified supplier with disclosed specs (2 MHz, bipolar, CE). It is the best-documented budget RF option in this project. But it is NOT equivalent to FDA-cleared devices. The missing temperature sensor is the critical gap.

**If you buy CE-only OEM RF:**
1. Verify the CE certificate names the actual manufacturer.
2. Request RF output power (not total consumption) and electrode specs.
3. Ask explicitly: "Is there a temperature sensor or thermal shutoff in the handpiece?"
4. Get the full user manual before paying.
5. Start at level 1 on a small test patch. Wait 48 hours. Check for burns/redness.
6. Always use conductive gel. Dry RF = hot spots = burns.

## §C — Professional RF (Strongest Results, Highest Cost)

These are in-office only. You're not buying a device — you're buying a treatment session.

| Treatment | Type | Best for | Sessions needed | Cost/session |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Thermage FLX** | Monopolar RF | Laxity, jawline, lower face tightening | 1 (results over 6 months) | ~$2,000–$3,000 |
| **Morpheus8** | RF microneedling | Acne scars, texture, tightening, submental fat | 1–3 sessions | ~$800–$1,500 |
| **Potenza** | RF microneedling | Similar to Morpheus8; multi-tip options | 1–3 sessions | ~$800–$1,500 |
| **Genius** | RF microneedling | Precision depth control | 1–3 sessions | ~$800–$1,500 |

**Bottom line:** If you have significant laxity or acne scarring and budget allows, professional RF (Thermage for laxity, RF microneedling for scars/texture) will outperform any home device dramatically. But these are medical procedures with real risk — choose your provider carefully.

---

## §D — RF Microneedling Warning

> **The FDA explicitly says RF microneedling devices should NOT be used at home.** (FDA Safety Communication, 2025)

Reported complications include burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement, nerve damage, and need for surgical repair. If you see a "home RF microneedling pen" on Alibaba/Amazon/TikTok — **do not buy it.** It is not a bargain Thermage. It is a dangerous product being sold into a regulatory gap.

## The 30-Second RF Picker

**1. Is RF even your problem?**
- Laxity, mild wrinkles, firmness → Yes, RF is relevant
- Pigment, redness, acne → No. RF won't help. Look elsewhere.

**2. Do you need temperature sensing and FDA clearance?**
- Yes, I want safety systems → Go §A (CurrentBody Skin RF is the pick)
- I'll accept the risk for a $50 experiment → Go §B (Konmison LB056B is the documented option)

**3. Do you want real results in fewer sessions?**
- Yes, and I can afford it → Go §C (professional Thermage or RF microneedling)
- I want maintenance/prevention at home → Go §A (FDA-cleared home RF)

**4. Are you considering RF microneedling?**
- Yes, at a professional clinic → Fine. Choose provider carefully.
- Yes, I found a cheap one online → **STOP. DO NOT BUY.** See §D.

## Quick Reality Check: What Home RF Can and Can't Do

| Claim | Realistic? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Mild wrinkle reduction with consistent use | ✅ Yes | Supported by NEWA and CurrentBody clinical data |
| Modest skin firming / texture improvement | ✅ Yes | Collagen remodeling takes weeks to months |
| "Non-surgical facelift" | ❌ No | Marketing exaggeration. Even Thermage isn't a facelift. |
| Replaces professional RF | ❌ No | Home RF is lower power, shallower depth, capped temperature |
| Fat loss / body contouring | ❌ No (home devices) | Requires much higher power. Professional RF only. |
| Works without consistent use | ❌ No | RF requires repeated sessions over weeks for results |
| Safe for all skin tones | ⚠️ Yes for RF mechanism, but... | Burns/PIH can still happen. Fitzpatrick V-VI: fewer home device studies. |
