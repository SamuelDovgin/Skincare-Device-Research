# Laser vs RF: Does the Technology Matter If the Temperature Is the Same?

*Compiled 2026-07-04. NIRA, NIRA Pro, and DermRays Revive are non-ablative lasers — different physics from RF. This document answers whether that difference matters for your goal of getting Thermage-like results at home.*

---

## 0. The Core Question

> "NIRA and DermRays Revive use lasers, not RF. But if they both heat dermis to ~43°C, does the technology actually matter? Is the end result the same?"

**Short answer:** The technology matters enormously. Same temperature ≠ same result. The *mechanism* of heating, *pattern* of heating, *depth* of heating, and *what gets heated* all differ fundamentally between RF and laser — even at identical tissue temperatures.

---

## 1. How RF Heats vs How Lasers Heat

### RF (Radiofrequency)
```
Electrical current → tissue impedance → Joule heating → EVERYTHING in the current path heats up

[Electrode] ──→ current flows through ALL tissue between electrodes ──→ [Electrode]
                ↑
                Water, collagen, fat, blood — everything heats equally
                No selectivity. Volumetric bulk heating.
```

### Laser (NIRA 1450nm, DermRays 1064nm)
```
Light photons → absorbed by specific chromophores → ONLY those structures heat up

[Laser] ──→ photons penetrate to specific depth ──→ absorbed by TARGET molecule
           ↑                                         ↑
           Wavelength determines depth         Chromophore determines WHAT heats

1450nm → absorbed by WATER (superficial dermis, ~0.5mm)
1064nm → absorbed by WATER + MELANIN + HEMOGLOBIN (deeper, ~3-4mm)
```

### The Critical Distinction

| Property | RF (TriPollar, NEWA, etc.) | Laser (NIRA, DermRays) |
|---|---|---|
| **What heats up** | Everything in the current path | Only structures containing the target chromophore |
| **Depth control** | Electrode geometry (crude) | Wavelength selection (precise) |
| **Heating pattern** | Volumetric — uniform across tissue | Selective — peaks where chromophore concentration is highest |
| **Can you cool the surface?** | Very difficult (current must pass through surface) | **Yes** — light passes through cooled epidermis to heat dermis below |
| **Selectivity** | None | Wavelength-dependent |

**Even at the same peak temperature, the volume and distribution of heated tissue is completely different.**

## 4. The Depth Problem: Why 1450nm (NIRA) Is Fundamentally Limited

```
Skin cross-section with penetration depths:

Epidermis ─────────── 0.0 mm  ← All devices pass through
                     │
Papillary dermis ─── 0.3 mm  ← NIRA peak heating zone (1450nm, ~0.5mm)
                     │
                     │  ← NIRA's limit — cannot heat deeper
Reticular dermis ─── 1.5 mm  
                     │
                     │  ← DermRays Revive peak heating (1064nm, ~2.8–4mm)
                     │  ← TriPollar claimed depth (3–5mm)
Deep reticular ───── 3.0 mm
                     │
SMAS fascia ──────── 4.0 mm  ← Thermage reaches this
                     │
Subcutaneous fat ─── 5.0 mm
```

**NIRA's 1450nm wavelength is absorbed very superficially** (~0.5mm). This means it heats the papillary dermis — great for fine lines and surface texture, but it physically cannot reach the deep reticular dermis where structural collagen for skin laxity lives. This is not a power limitation — it's a **physics limitation** of the wavelength. More power would just burn the surface, not reach deeper.

**DermRays Revive's 1064nm penetrates ~3–4mm** — reaching the reticular dermis where laxity-related collagen lives. This is comparable to TriPollar's claimed depth. Combined with active cooling, it can potentially deliver more thermal dose to the right layer than any uncooled RF device.

## 6. Answering the Question Directly

### "Does the actual tech matter if the temp is the same?"

**Yes, for four reasons:**

**1. Same temperature at different depths = completely different result.**
- 43°C at 0.5mm (NIRA) = fine line improvement, no laxity benefit
- 43°C at 3–4mm (DermRays, TriPollar) = structural collagen stimulation, possible laxity benefit
- The temperature number without the depth context is meaningless.

**2. Same temperature with different heating patterns = different biological response.**
- RF heats everything uniformly → fibroblasts, blood vessels, fat all get the same thermal dose
- Laser heats selectively → energy concentrated where the chromophore is, sparing other structures
- This affects side effect profile (RF can heat fat; laser typically doesn't at these depths)

**3. Active cooling changes what temperature is achievable.**
- DermRays Revive with cooling can potentially push dermal temperature higher than an uncooled device because the surface stays at 15–18°C
- This is the Thermage principle applied at home power levels
- No RF home device has this capability

**4. Lasers can do multiple things RF cannot.**
- 1064nm treats pigment (melanin absorption) AND vessels (hemoglobin absorption) AND wrinkles (water absorption → collagen heating)
- RF treats laxity/wrinkles only
- If you have pigment or redness concerns, a 1064nm laser delivers more value per session

### "Are they essentially the same?"

**No.** RF and laser are as different as a microwave and a spotlight. Both can heat things up, but how they do it, what they heat, how deep they reach, and what side effects they cause are all fundamentally different.
