# Home radiofrequency clinical evidence map

*Rapid evidence map updated 2026-08-23. It is not a systematic review, medical advice, or permission to use an RF device outside its exact instructions.*

## Bottom line

The topic now has a strong physics comparison and a practical home-RF tier list. The remaining high-value gap was to show **how much of that ranking rests on controlled human outcomes versus single-arm before/after studies and regulatory equivalence**.

- Home RF has a **repeatable modest rejuvenation signal**, but much of the device-specific literature is small, short, and uncontrolled.
- A randomized split-face study strengthens the category signal, yet it tested one device against an anti-aging cosmetic—not against sham RF or the current devices in the buying guide.
- NEWA has the best public regulatory/clinical package in this set, but its pivotal study was single-arm and limited to adult women with Fitzpatrick I–IV and mild-to-moderate wrinkles.
- CurrentBody K232424 is well documented technically, but its 510(k) is a substantial-equivalence file, not a new head-to-head outcome trial.

## Evidence ladder

| Tier | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A** | Randomized sham/untreated or active-controlled comparison with objective outcome |
| **B** | Prospective device-specific cohort with blinded/objective assessment and follow-up |
| **C** | Open-label/baseline comparison, small cohort, mixed-energy device, or indirect evidence |
| **D** | FDA equivalence, bench testing, ex vivo mechanism, or manufacturer material without comparative outcomes |

## 1. Device-specific human studies

| Device / study | Design and protocol | Main result | Critical limitation / tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NEWA, FDA De Novo DEN150005** | Prospective single-center home study; 69 enrolled, 62 completed; adult women 37–72, Fitzpatrick I–IV; ≥5 sessions/week for 4 weeks; 3-month follow-up | 59/62 met the prespecified ≥1-point Fitzpatrick improvement by agreement of ≥2/3 blinded dermatologists; no burns/scarring reported, mostly brief redness | No sham/untreated control; completer analysis; whole-face photos cannot assign benefit to each area. **Tier B** [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf) |
| **TriPollar STOP** | Home cohort, n=23 women; 6-week treatment + 6-week maintenance; 3D imaging and wrinkle grading | Reported significant perioral/periorbital reduction; average periorbital reduction 41% | Small uncontrolled study; model lineage may not equal current STOP variants. **Tier C** [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21401380/) |
| **NEWA 12-week study** | 47 enrolled, 45 completed; 3×/week for 4 weeks then 2×/week for 8 weeks; expert, image, Cutometer, SIAscope assessments | Significant changes across firmness, elasticity, texture, tone, lift, and related measures; mild transient erythema | No control; author affiliation includes EndyMed. **Tier C** [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27351303/) |
| **Silk’n HST RF + LED** | 33 enrolled, 30 completed; 21 alternate-day sessions over 6 weeks plus two maintenance sessions; baseline comparison, blinded photo review | Mean Fitzpatrick-score reduction 1.49 at three months; universal brief erythema and some short edema | Combination energy, no sham, completer cohort. **Tier C** [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27910259/) |
| **Home RF vs anti-aging cosmetic** | Randomized split-face trial, 33 enrolled/32 completed, 12 weeks | RF side improved wrinkles, radiance, color, and thickness more than cosmetic side | Active cosmetic control is not sham RF; one device/protocol and short follow-up. **Tier A/B** [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35249173/) |
| **Home RF in Chinese women** | Open-label intraindividual baseline-controlled trial, n=22, Fitzpatrick III–IV, 8 weeks | Improved evaluator wrinkle score, dermal thickness, and 3D perioral measures; no serious adverse event | No untreated/sham side despite “intraindividual” framing; small sample. **Tier C** [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37942722/) |
| **Multi-energy RF + light + microcurrent + ultrasound** | Randomized split-face trial, 36 Korean women, 8 weeks | Treated side improved several instrumental aging measures vs control side | Cannot isolate RF contribution and does not transfer to RF-only products. **Tier A for bundle, D for RF attribution** [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38236440/) |

## 2. What the FDA files add

### NEWA: direct clinical and thermal guardrail

DEN150005 created the Class II OTC RF aesthetic category for mild-to-moderate facial wrinkles in adult women with Fitzpatrick I–IV. The file documents 1 MHz RF, 10 W on a 360 Ω test load, and a thermistor that stops RF above 42 °C. It also states that safety/effectiveness were not established for several populations/conditions and lists treatment-area exclusions. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/reviews/DEN150005.pdf)

That makes NEWA unusually transparent, but the clinical result remains a single-arm baseline comparison. The very high “success” percentage should not be interpreted as a sham-adjusted probability of visible benefit.

### CurrentBody Skin RF: strong specifications, inherited clinical case

FDA K232424 cleared CurrentBody Skin RF under product code PAY in March 2024. The public file documents 1 MHz bipolar RF, 5 ± 1 W, two redundant thermistors, and a maximum allowed temperature of 40.5 ± 0.5 °C. The clearance is based on substantial equivalence; it does not supply a new randomized CurrentBody-vs-NEWA outcome trial. [[8]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)

This distinction strengthens the existing [home-RF tier list](index.html#doc10): CurrentBody can rank highly for documentation and conservative controls while NEWA retains the stronger direct subject-device clinical file.

## 3. Outcome interpretation

| Claim | Evidence verdict |
|---|---|
| “Home RF can modestly improve fine wrinkles/texture with consistent use.” | **Supported as a category signal**, with several device-specific cohorts and one useful split-face comparison |
| “A-tier devices are clinically proven equal.” | **Not supported**; no common-protocol head-to-head trial |
| “A 40–42 °C surface target guarantees collagen remodeling.” | **Not supported**; sensor location, time, depth, motion, coupling, and tissue impedance matter |
| “Home RF is a Thermage substitute.” | **Not supported**; professional energy delivery and treatment volume differ |
| “More heat or watts means better results.” | **Not supported** and potentially unsafe without validated feedback/control |
| “A before/after improvement percentage is the treatment effect.” | **Incorrect** unless a comparator removes natural change, measurement drift, co-interventions, and expectation |

## 4. Evidence-weighted buying rule

1. **Require exact-model regulatory identity and IFU.** Brand-family evidence does not automatically transfer.
2. **Prefer documented thermal feedback over headline power.** Sensor redundancy, cutoff behavior, electrode contact, movement, and coupling are safety-critical.
3. **Match the evidence population.** NEWA’s pivotal OTC evidence does not establish safety/effectiveness for Fitzpatrick V–VI, men, severe wrinkles, or excluded conditions.
4. **Expect subtle change.** Use fixed-light standardized photographs and one validated or objective endpoint at baseline, 4, 8, and 12 weeks.
5. **Stop for the safety endpoint.** Persistent pain, burn-like redness, blistering, swelling, pigment change, or unexpected contour/nerve symptoms outweigh a cosmetic target.

FDA’s current PAY total-product-life-cycle page lists a small number of adverse-event reports including superficial burns and skin discoloration. Passive reporting cannot estimate incidence, but it confirms that “OTC” is not “risk-free.” [[9]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfTPLC/tplc.cfm?id=PAY)

## 5. What the next decisive trial should look like

A useful head-to-head would randomize CurrentBody, NEWA, and a sham with matched gel, motion, sound, light, and surface warmth; enroll across skin types; measure actual temperature-time curves; use blinded standardized photography plus 3D wrinkle/elasticity outcomes; predefine a clinically meaningful difference; disclose manufacturer involvement; and follow participants six to twelve months after stopping.

## Evidence gaps

- No high-quality sham-controlled head-to-head compares the leading current home RF devices.
- Most device-specific cohorts are small and rely on baseline comparisons.
- Manufacturer sponsorship/affiliation is common and incompletely reported in abstracts.
- Fitzpatrick V–VI, men, severe laxity, and long-term repeated maintenance are underrepresented.
- Surface temperature does not reveal depth-resolved thermal dose.
- Passive adverse-event reports lack reliable denominators.
