# Older and generic home RF devices: used-market and value tiers

*eBay snapshot checked October 9, 2026. This is a technical-evidence and used-value triage, not a clinical efficacy ranking.*

> **My shortlist:** the corded NEWA Lift listing is the strongest conditional value because an exact original wired NEWA has a numeric FDA bench result; the listing’s revision still needs to match. A U.S. TriPollar STOP X / Model U is the next documented option if its label confirms the U.S. STOP U family. The low-cost generic devices are easy to find, but none in this scan has enough exact-model documentation to rank its heating output.

“Legacy candidate” here means an older-looking or used-market model. I did not find reliable evidence confirming that every listed model is no longer manufactured. eBay availability, seller counters and ended listings do not establish production status.

## How to read the tiers

RF watts describe electrical output at a stated load and mode. They do not directly compare skin temperature, depth, safety or results. The NEWA FDA bench figure is at 360 Ω; the TriPollar figure is specified at 200 Ω. Electrode spacing, current path, pulse timing, contact, gel and temperature control also matter. A larger watt figure from another load or a seller headline is not proof of stronger or safer tissue heating.

I rank used-market value by the combination of model identity, technical evidence, visible thermal safeguards, price signal and uncertainty. A conditional tier means the device moves up only after the buyer matches the seller’s unit label to the cited revision and confirms the correct adapter and included accessories.

### Heat-evidence bands

| Band | What qualifies | What it means here |
|---|---|---|
| **H1 — measured anchor** | Numeric bench output tied to a specific device and test load, with a documented control limit | Original wired NEWA: FDA record reports 10 W at 360 Ω and a 42°C sensor shutoff. This is a useful reference, not the confirmed output of every later “NEWA Lift” listing. |
| **H2 — rated, model-mapped** | A regulatory rating plus a defensible exact regional/model mapping | TriPollar STOP U: 5.7 W ±10% RMS at 200 Ω. U.S. STOP X maps to STOP U, but verify the label; do not transfer the figure to non-U.S. STOP X or the distinct VX family. |
| **H3 — manual exists, listing match unresolved** | A manual gives a number, but the used listing may be a different regional product or revision | Silk’n Titan H2111/H2112 manual says 10 W. A FaceTite listing with the same H numbers does not establish that its regional revision has that output. |
| **H-U — unknown / unranked** | No exact-model manual or comparable RF output and thermal-control evidence recovered | Toe Talk, IFAE A083, SC114 and the broad no-name 5-in-1 cohort. Their displayed prices are not a heating specification. |

## What I would actually shortlist

1. **First check the NEWA label.** Ask for a clear photo of the model plate, rear label and adapter. If it matches the original wired NEWA in DEN150005, the $171 delivered listing is the strongest evidence/value candidate here. If it does not, remove the 10 W and 42°C assumptions.
2. **Use STOP X as the second option only when it says U.S. Model U / STOP U.** Confirm the unit and regional manual before using the FDA 5.7 W rating. Inspect the treatment head and verify the correct adapter; eBay search asks are not completed-sale values.
3. **Treat Silk’n H2111/H2112 as an unpriced-output bargain hunt.** The shared H numbers are a lead to resolve, not proof that a Titan manual describes the FaceTite listing.
4. **Do not promote the generic group into a heating tier yet.** For Toe Talk, IFAE, SC114 or another generic handset to move up, archive a manual tied to the exact model/revision, label and power adapter, plus RF output test conditions and a documented temperature-control method. Until then, mark wattage unknown in the [power explorer](rf_power_explorer.html).
