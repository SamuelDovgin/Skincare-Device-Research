# Best Thermage equivalents and professional RF services

*Evidence checked October 2, 2026. This is an editorial service shortlist, not a measured efficacy league table or a treatment prescription.*

**My first alternative to investigate for a Thermage FLX-style service is Volnewmer.** It has a direct randomized split-face comparison against FLX, with similar short-term lifting/elasticity results and lower procedural pain. That evidence is small and short: 22 Asian women followed for eight weeks. **YOUMAGIC has the stronger large comparative trial**, but its comparator was older Thermage CPT. Neither establishes universal equivalence to FLX, superior collagen production, or equal long-term safety. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715870/)[[2]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10103-026-04841-4)

**No home RF device in this archive has demonstrated Thermage-equivalent results.** CurrentBody ST030 remains my practical home shortlist and original NEWA the direct home clinical anchor for modest wrinkle goals. Their home tiers do not place them in the same efficacy category as a professional cooled monopolar service. See the [home tier list](index.html#doc10) and [collagen evidence](index.html#doc16).

## 1. What would actually count as an equivalent?

A credible replacement needs a **human comparison against the exact Thermage generation**, similar treatment areas and baseline severity, an appropriate endpoint, a defensible non-inferiority margin, blinded assessment, adequate follow-up, and sufficiently large safety surveillance. Matching 6.78 MHz, console wattage, a return pad, or a regulatory predicate is only an architecture/authorization bridge. It does not prove equal wrinkle reduction, collagen gain, lifting, pain, durability or complication risk.

Separate the goal before comparing services: dermal firmness and mild laxity, wrinkles/texture, acne scars, substantial excess skin, and facial volume loss are different problems. A clinical result can improve appearance without directly demonstrating new collagen. A stronger heat sensation or immediate swelling cannot certify remodeling.

**What this section found:** the evidence supports two conditional comparator-based candidates. It does not support a universal best RF platform, a percentage conversion between devices, or a number of home sessions equivalent to one clinic treatment. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715870/)[[2]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10103-026-04841-4)

## 2. My service shortlist and tiers

These grades describe **confidence as a Thermage substitute**, independently of the home-device grades. Within a grade there is no proven efficacy ordering. Thermage FLX is the reference service, not automatically the most effective treatment for every face.

|---|---|---|---|

| **B: promising, no human Thermage comparison located** | **XERF** | 39-person prospective uncontrolled study, two sessions/90-day follow-up; 16-woman retrospective single-session study/three months; FDA ex-vivo thermal comparison to FLX | Reasonable consultation option, but I would not pay a premium solely for two frequencies, a “SMAS” diagram or claimed FLX superiority [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41930066/)[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13486139/)[[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251327.pdf) |

| **B: promising, no human Thermage comparison located** | **Density / Mono-Bi CrossLIFT** | 16-person single-arm series, one treatment/24 weeks, imaging and independent GAIS assessment | An alternative architecture with early human data; adding bipolar RF does not establish superiority or more collagen [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41090512/) |
| **C: substitution claim unresolved** | Other clinic bipolar/multipolar RF, newer revisions or combination packages | Device- and protocol-specific evidence needed | Ask for the exact study and model. Do not inherit the above grades because the treatment is also called RF |

Grades A− and B are recommendation judgments. A− deliberately retains uncertainty: neither candidate supplies large, independently replicated, long-term FLX equivalence evidence across populations. No alternative earns an unrestricted A/S-equivalent claim.

## 3. The evidence that changes the decision

### Volnewmer: closest direct FLX comparison, limited durability evidence

The 2025 Annals of Dermatology study randomized facial sides in **22 Asian women**, mean age 46±3 years, comparing Volnewmer continuous water cooling with Thermage FLX cryogen cooling. One session per side was followed for eight weeks, with quantitative imaging and three blinded dermatologists. Lifting, elasticity and several wrinkle/volume endpoints had no significant between-device difference; some density, pore and roughness measures favored Volnewmer. At the 300-shot assessment, mean pain was **4.55 versus 8.39/10** under this specific anesthetized protocol. Mild redness/edema resolved; no significant persistent adverse effects were reported. The authors declared no funding or conflicts. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715870/)

**Translation limit:** the paper frames its aim as non-inferiority but the reported statistical methods use paired significance tests; an explicit clinical non-inferiority margin and corresponding margin-based confidence-interval analysis were not identified in the inspected full text. Failure to detect a difference is not proof of equivalence. Eight weeks cannot establish equal one-year results or rare-event risk. Separate ex-vivo collagen assays were performed on tissue, not facial biopsies from these women, and did not compare both devices identically. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12715870/)

A separate Volnewmer-versus-CPT registry, **NCT06657365**, was marked completed, last updated May 4, 2026, but had **no posted results section** in the October 2 API snapshot. It adds no outcome claim here. [[11]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06657365)

### YOUMAGIC: better-sized comparison, older Thermage generation

The 2026 trial analyzed **107 YOUMAGIC and 105 Thermage CPT participants** after enrolling 230. The assessor-blinded multicenter study used a **−10 percentage-point non-inferiority margin** for its clinical response endpoint and followed participants for six months. At day 90, reported investigator GAIS response was **100% versus 98.10%**. It found no serious adverse events. The cohort was predominantly female; registration was retrospective, and the manufacturer supplied the device. [[2]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10103-026-04841-4)

**Translation limit:** these are clinical response proportions, not percentage tightening or collagen increases. Non-inferiority within the chosen margin is not superiority, identical effect magnitude, or proof against FLX. Regional regulatory status and local service availability must be verified before purchase.

### XERF: plausible delivery design, clinical equivalence still missing

The prospective study included **39 completers at four sites**, all receiving two sessions. Treating investigators, rather than independent blinded assessors, rated outcomes through 90 days after the final treatment. The manufacturer supplied equipment/consumables and research grants. A separate retrospective study included **16 women**, one treatment, three months; its lead author trains for the manufacturer, which supported IRB submission and statistical analysis. Imaging changes and patient satisfaction are encouraging, but neither study compared Thermage. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41930066/)[[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13486139/)

XERF’s FDA summary lists **2 and 6.78 MHz**, with respective maximum platform outputs of **300 and 400 W**, and describes an ex-vivo temperature-time comparison with FLX at 3 mm. It explicitly states no clinical tests were included in that submission. **Bench/ex-vivo equivalence is not human outcome equivalence.** The dual-frequency and anatomical target descriptions do not independently verify a precise treated depth or safe fat preservation in every patient. [[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251327.pdf)

### Oligio and Density: human signals, weaker substitution evidence

**Oligio:** 20 women received one treatment with follow-up to 24 weeks. Blinded raters found laxity improvement at 12 and 24 weeks, and firmness improved; automated pore/fine-wrinkle/skin-tone measures did not show significant changes. The paper’s abstract describes no pain, but its results report mean pain **0.4/10**; use the reported measurement. No external funding/conflicts were declared. No control group or Thermage comparison means this cannot establish equal effects. [[5]](https://doi.org/10.3390/cosmetics11030071)

**Density:** 16 adults with mild/moderate laxity received one sequential monopolar/bipolar treatment. At 24 weeks the abstract reported mean imaging tightening **0.82±0.36 mm**, with 15/16 rated improved or better. The body uses a slightly different SD for the tightening metric. These protocol-specific surface measurements are not biopsy collagen, a surgical lift, or fat-loss assays. The author declared no conflicts; a controlled comparison is still needed. [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41090512/)

## 4. The specifications that matter for a professional replacement

| Requirement | What to verify with the provider | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Exact identity | Manufacturer, model/revision, authentic applicator and applicable local authorization | Whether the cited evidence and labeling can transfer |
| Delivery architecture | Noninvasive monopolar/bipolar or needles; tip area and return electrode where required | How current is delivered, not an outcome ranking |
| Cooling and feedback | Cryogen/water/other cooling; contact/impedance checks; sensor location and response | Delivery controls; skin sensors do not measure the entire dermal dose |
| Studied treatment | Treatment area, baseline laxity, session count, operator technique and follow-up | Whether a clinic package resembles the evidence |
| Human endpoint | Blinded laxity/wrinkle assessment and standardized images; collagen-specific corroboration if claimed | Clinical appearance versus a directly established tissue mechanism |
| Operator and anatomy | Experience with this device and concern; assessment of volume loss, prior procedures and excluded areas | Whether the platform fits this face and treatment goal |
| Full-course quote | Included areas/tips, sessions, review visit and likely maintenance | Value; shot count alone cannot compare value or tissue dose |

**MHz and maximum W are descriptive specifications.** Oligio’s filing gives 6.78 MHz/145 W maximum; Volnewmer’s gives 6.78 MHz/115 W maximum. Those smaller maxima do not make their results a fixed fraction of a 400 W Thermage/XERF console. Actual pulses, tip area, feedback, cooling, delivered energy and treatment geometry differ. Neither surface cutoff nor a universal “65–75°C dermis” target can establish equivalent collagen remodeling. [[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251327.pdf)[[7]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K240248.pdf)[[9]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K221989.pdf)

**Regulatory distinction:** Thermage FLX’s filing explicitly includes noninvasive wrinkle and periorbital indications, with different RF-only and RF-plus-vibration wording. The inspected Volnewmer, Oligio and XERF U.S. filings list electrocoagulation/hemostasis indications; their use of FLX as a predicate does not copy all FLX cosmetic or eyelid indications. Read the exact current local labeling and intended treatment area. [[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K251327.pdf)[[7]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K240248.pdf)[[8]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf17/K170758.pdf)[[9]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K221989.pdf)

## 5. Which service would I actually investigate?

- **Closest FLX-style alternative:** obtain a Volnewmer consultation/quote from an experienced provider, compare the same treatment areas and follow-up, and accept that the best direct evidence is short-term.
- **Larger controlled evidence matters most:** investigate the exact YOUMAGIC trial platform where locally authorized; keep its CPT comparator explicit.
- **XERF/Oligio/Density is the accessible or cheaper offer:** consider it on operator quality, fit, full-course cost and early clinical evidence. Do not assume equal or better results because a clinic says “new Thermage.”
- **Mild home maintenance is the goal:** use the home shortlist on its own terms. No reviewed home-to-Thermage comparator trial or validated session conversion establishes equivalence.
- **Scars, substantial excess skin or volume loss is the goal:** obtain a diagnosis and compare appropriate procedures. More RF may not address the main anatomical problem.

No geography or budget was specified, so this page ranks platforms rather than named clinics and does not invent national prices. A cheaper platform is not necessarily a cheaper complete course. A comparison should use **total quoted course cost**, covered areas, visit count and an agreed follow-up endpoint; there is no defensible cost-per-collagen calculator from these studies.

RF microneedling services such as Morpheus8/Genius/Potenza belong in a separate invasive decision. FDA’s October 2025 communication describes reported burns, scarring, fat loss, disfigurement and nerve damage, and states that RF microneedling should not be used at home. This is a specific microneedling warning, not an incidence estimate or proof that all noninvasive RF shares those risks. [[12]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication)
