Build one documented calibration batch

This topic gives the vinegar-based HOCl route its own home. It starts with the exact generator manual, preserves the 2.00 g/L salt source ratio, and separates official one-liter anchors from volume, FAC, and pH sensitivity estimates.

Fast answer: the developer defaults to the exact 1,000 mL · 100 ppm · 8-minute source point. You can scale water from 500 to 1,000 mL, choose 40–500 ppm, and select a final-pH scenario. Salt scales at 2.00 g/L; vinegar stays below the manual's 5.0 mL/L ceiling. Measure final pH and FAC—the recipe and graphs do not make a home batch facial-grade.
PWPAM is intentionally excluded from this route. Its supplied manual says salt + water and calls the output sodium hypochlorite. Do not add vinegar before or after a PWPAM run. The old topic preserves that manual record separately.

What the two tools can—and cannot—tell you

  • They can build a device-compatible planning recipe, show FAC and illustrative pH paths over time, classify paired final readings, and produce a report for next-fresh-batch review in chat.
  • They cannot certify the liquid, make a medical/cosmetic/disinfection claim, predict shelf life, or approve face, eye, aerosol, or face-contact-bedding use.
  • They never tell you to acidify an existing chlorine-containing solution or a salt-only device output.
