Scale the batch, then test the actual output

The official Eco One page publishes production rates at a one-liter fill. This developer keeps those values visible, then applies a volume-aware constant-current estimate for smaller batches. It also provides a bounded vinegar/pH sensitivity scenario and graphs both FAC and pH over the planned run.

Default skincare plan: 1,000 mL · 100 ppm FAC · final-pH target 5.5. The source recipe is 2.00 g salt and up to 5.0 mL of 5% vinegar per liter. Final FAC and pH remain measured outputs; the time and pH traces are planning models, not product specifications.
Device gate: use this only with the Eco One or a generator whose manual permits vinegar before electrolysis and variable stopping. Never add vinegar to PWPAM, bleach, chlorine tablets, a salt-only machine, or a finished chlorine-containing batch.

4 · Make one fresh calibration batch

  1. Verify the exact device path. Confirm vinegar before electrolysis, the selected fill level, and whether manual stopping at the estimated time is allowed.
  2. Measure the water. Use the displayed volume and the same water source used for future calibration runs.
  3. Weigh salt and measure vinegar. Add the displayed 2.00 g/L salt amount and bounded pH-sensitivity vinegar amount before electrolysis. Do not use cleaning vinegar or concentrated acid.
  4. Run the displayed time as a planning estimate. Only the one-liter published anchors are official. A smaller-volume or between-anchor time is an extrapolation.
  5. Test immediately. Measure final FAC and pH with methods that cover the planned ranges. Optionally record starting pH before electrolysis.
  6. Record; do not rescue. Never add acid, salt, or other chemistry to the completed chlorine-containing batch. Use the paired result only to plan a separate fresh batch.
Skincare boundary: prefer a finished, appropriately labeled dermal product for actual face/skin use. This model does not establish HOCl identity, byproducts, purity, sterility, preservation, shelf life, eye safety, irritation safety, or regulatory status.

Model ledger, equations, and evidence classes

official 1 L anchors = (40 ppm, 3 min), (60, 5), (100, 8), (200, 16), (500, 40) base_time_1L = piecewise interpolation between official anchors planned_time = base_time_1L × planned_volume_mL / 1000 salt_g = 2.00 g/L × planned_volume_L published pH anchor = mean(4.58, 4.95) = 4.765 at 5.0 mL/L vinegar after 20 min effective_acid(pH) = max(0, 10^(-pH) − 10^(-starting_water_pH)) vinegar_rate_mL_L = min(5.0, 5.0 × effective_acid(target_pH) / effective_acid(4.765)) 5% vinegar acetic-acid concentration ≈ 0.833 mol/L estimated_starting_pH solves Ka = [H+]² / (C − [H+]), Ka = 1.75 × 10^-5 pH_over_time = illustrative eased bridge from estimated start to target/capped endpoint

Evidence classes: the 1 L production anchors and 2 g salt + 1 tsp vinegar recipe are manufacturer/manual values. Volume-time scaling is a constant-current, constant-efficiency sensitivity assumption. The starting-pH calculation is an acetic-acid equilibrium approximation. The vinegar-to-final-pH relationship and dashed pH trace are source-anchored illustrative scenarios—not a validated buffer/electrolysis model or confidence interval.

  • FDA 510(k) K180305 summary — finished 0.01% HOCl skin/wound product precedent; not evidence of DIY equivalence.
  • CDC chemical disinfectants guidance — chlorine speciation and acid/hypochlorite mixing hazard.
