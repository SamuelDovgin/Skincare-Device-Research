4 · Make one fresh calibration batch

  1. Verify the exact device path. Confirm the selected chemistry is allowed before electrolysis, electrode immersion is safe, and manual stopping at the displayed time is permitted.
  2. Measure the water. Use the displayed volume and the same source for future calibration runs.
  3. Weigh the selected salt. In the default Amazon/Chloe working recipe, use the displayed volume-scaled amount: 6.00 g/L non-iodized salt and 2.50 mL/L vinegar. The primary calibration point is 1.50 g salt plus 0.625 mL vinegar in 250 mL; using the original full review amounts at 250 mL would be a different recipe.
  4. Run the displayed program. The default program is the corrected observed-rate estimate scaled from 250 mL / approximately 300 ppm / 30 minutes. Use documented manual stopping and fresh FAC/pH measurement.
  5. Test immediately. Measure final FAC and pH with suitable-range methods. The observation is a working calibration, not a product certificate.
  6. Record; do not rescue. Never add acid, salt, or other chemistry to the completed chlorine-containing batch. Use the paired result only to plan a separate fresh batch.
Skincare boundary: prefer a finished, appropriately labeled dermal product for actual face/skin use. This model does not establish HOCl identity, byproducts, purity, sterility, preservation, shelf life, eye safety, irritation safety, or regulatory status.

  • Official Eco One product page — current 1 L production rates through 500 ppm, additional-cycle claim, cell materials/lifespan, and incomplete electrical specification.

  • Eco One user manual — one-liter recipe, fill marks, and 3/5/8/16-minute settings.
  • IUPAC electrochemical terminology — current is charge per time and electrochemical production relates to integrated charge through Faraday's laws.
  • Khalid et al., 2020 — controlled experiments showing NaCl concentration, voltage, and time jointly affect conductivity, current, and chlorine production in a different cell.
  • Choi, Shim & Yoon, 2013 — NaCl concentration materially affected electrochlorination current efficiency and power consumption; this supports direction-of-effect, not an Eco One conversion factor.
