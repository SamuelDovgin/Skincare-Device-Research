Evidence boundary

Layer finished products; do not turn the planner into a DIY mixing recipe

The checker evaluates sequential use of finished, preserved products as labeled. Mixing products in your palm or combining raw powders can change pH, concentration, preservation, solvent balance, oxidation, and delivery. Unless one product was formulated with both ingredients or a prescriber directs it, treat “mix together” as unknown formulation work and use the separate DIY formulation research.

Why there are no universal waiting-time numbers

Most topical pairs do not have clinical trials comparing a 5-, 20-, or 60-minute wait. Device and procedure windows vary by IFU, treatment intensity, site, skin response, and clinician protocol. The lab therefore uses “different session,” “different day,” and “resume when baseline comfort returns” instead of inventing precision.

What the catalog deliberately splits and groups
  • Conventional tretinoin is split from microsphere/optimized tretinoin because benzoyl-peroxide stability is formulation-dependent.
  • Pure low-pH L-ascorbic acid is split from vitamin C derivatives because pH, stability, and evidence are different.
  • Copper peptides are split from non-copper peptides because metal/redox chemistry creates a real formulation question, while clinical layering evidence remains sparse.
  • Low-evidence pigment actives are grouped because most pairwise “conflict” claims are not supported well enough to justify dozens of false rules.
  • Oral supplements are excluded: systemic dose duplication and drug interactions belong in the supplement checker, not a topical-layering matrix.
