# Search method and executive map

*Compiled 2026-07-14. This is a literature search record, not medical advice. The cutoff is 2026-07-14; online-first papers were eligible when PubMed recorded them by that date.*

## 0. Bottom line

The recent literature is not one breakthrough. It is a shift toward **integrated skincare**: photoprotection plus pigment control, acne treatment plus barrier support, procedures plus aftercare, and device comparisons that report recovery and pigment risk rather than efficacy alone. The strongest actionable themes are visible-light-aware sunscreen for pigment-prone skin, tolerability-focused acne care, and conservative interpretation of device trials.

## 1. Search boundary

The search was designed around the archive’s existing thirteen projects and the user’s request for the latest skincare research. Search families were run in PubMed/MEDLINE with publication dates `2025:2026` and combinations of:

- `skincare`, `skin care`, `cosmetic`, `dermocosmetic`, `moisturizer`, `ceramide`, `barrier`, `TEWL`;
- `acne`, `microbiome`, `prebiotic`, `probiotic`, `bacteriophage`, `hydrogel`, `topical delivery`;
- `melasma`, `hyperpigmentation`, `PIH`, `skin of color`, `sunscreen`, `visible light`, `photoaging`, `retinoid`, `peptide`;
- `laser`, `IPL`, `diode`, `LED`, `photobiomodulation`, `radiofrequency`, `microneedling`, `HIFU`, `microfocused ultrasound`, `hair removal`.

Included records had to be directly about human skin, a human clinical intervention, a clinically relevant systematic review/meta-analysis, a practice/consensus statement, or a clearly labeled emerging delivery/microbiome study. I excluded general oncology, wound, hair-growth, dental, and non-skin energy papers even when a broad keyword search retrieved them. The structured ledger contains 100 records, including a 35-paper high-prestige journal supplement; it is an inclusion set, not a claim that all 2025–2026 dermatology has been exhausted.

## 2. Evidence hierarchy used here

| Tier | Meaning | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| A | Randomized human clinical trial or robust systematic review/meta-analysis of clinical trials | Best basis for efficacy or comparative claims, still check sample size and endpoint |
| B | Prospective cohort, open-label clinical trial, or consensus/guideline | Useful for feasibility, tolerability, practice framing, or hypothesis generation |
| C | In-vitro, formulation, mechanistic, or industry product study | Explains plausibility or translation direction; does not prove consumer benefit |

Consensus documents are not upgraded to Tier A merely because they use Delphi methods. Industry-linked trials remain usable, but their conflict signal is retained in the ledger.

## 3. What changed in 2025–2026

### 3.1 Pigment is now a visible-light and recovery problem

The 2025 PIH systematic review found only 14 prevention studies involving 369 cases, with nearly all triggered by lasers and all participants reported as Asian; sunscreen was the only consistently successful preventive measure in that review. A 2025 tinted-versus-untinted melasma trial, a 2026 randomized sunscreen trial adding sclareolide and niacinamide, and a 2026 visible-light consensus statement all move the field toward iron-oxide/tint-aware protection, but they do not establish a single universal product or visible-light SPF standard. [1–4]

### 3.2 Barrier support is part of treatment adherence

Recent acne trials test moisturizers and adjuncts against irritation, hydration, TEWL, or microbiome endpoints—not only lesion count. A 2026 split-face trial found a bioactive moisturizer reduced treatment side effects, while 2025 studies evaluated salicylic-acid gel, prebiotic gel cream, and ceramide cleanser systems. These are meaningful because irritation is a pathway to discontinuation, but most are small, short, open-label, or product-linked. [5–9]

### 3.3 Microbiome products are promising but not mature

The 2026 Lactobacillus-based meta-analysis and probiotic meta-analysis point toward a possible adjunct signal, while the 2025 prebiotic acne study reported lesion reductions and shifts in *C. acnes* strains. However, heterogeneous strains, vehicles, endpoints, and funding make “microbiome balancing” too broad a conclusion. Prebiotic, probiotic, bacteriophage, and nanoformulation work belongs in an emerging lane beside—not above—retinoids, benzoyl peroxide, azelaic acid, and clinician-guided therapy. [7,10–13]

### 3.4 Comparative device evidence is better, but home translation is still weak

New reviews compare MFU-V with real-world outcomes, HIFU with RF, Er:Glass with CO₂, fractional CO₂ with RF microneedling, and lasers with chemical peels. A 2026 IPL-versus-diode RCT found both effective for axillary hair removal, with longer-term objective outcomes favoring diode but more discomfort; a 2025 home-versus-medical IPL trial found similar short-term efficacy after three months. Neither paper validates every home device, every body area, or pigment-treatment claims. [14–20]

### 3.5 The highest-impact gap is representation and parameter reporting

Trials commonly report Fitzpatrick group, but not always enough to support people at the darker end of the spectrum, and many procedural studies are single-center. Laser/RF studies also vary in device, pulse train, fluence, needle depth, pass count, cooling, and follow-up. A positive clinical result is therefore class- and protocol-specific, not a blank check for another device.

## 4. Archive decisions

| New finding | Existing destination | Why it stays here too |
|---|---|---|
| Home IPL vs clinic IPL and IPL vs diode | `01`, `02` | It updates the hair-removal decision surface and helps prevent cross-use of hair-removal evidence for rejuvenation |
| Fractional laser/RF microneedling comparisons | `03`, `07`, `10` | These are distinct procedure lanes with different depth and pigment-risk assumptions |
| MFU-V/HIFU vs RF | `07`, `11` | Comparative tightening evidence belongs to both device owners |
| Tinted sunscreen, visible light, PIH prevention | `01`, `03`, `08`, `13` | It cuts across pigment, laser aftercare, vitamin C/topicals, and regimen scheduling |
| Acne barrier and microbiome adjuncts | new cross-category lane here | No existing project owns acne as a disease-and-skincare evidence lane |
| Periprocedural skincare | `03`, `07`, `10`, `11`, `13` | The same aftercare principle applies across device categories but is easy to lose in a single topic |

## 5. Evidence gaps

- No single database search can guarantee all 2025–2026 cosmetic dermatology papers; Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, trial registries, and non-English databases remain follow-up work.
- Many papers report percentage improvement or global aesthetic scales without giving enough raw data for cross-study pooling.
- “Skin of color” is not one biological or clinical group; the studies often underrepresent Fitzpatrick V–VI.
- Most consumer-device studies are protocol-specific and do not disclose the complete output tuple needed for replication.
- Positive findings in a manufacturer-linked serum trial should not be generalized to an ingredient family or a retail product with unknown concentration.
