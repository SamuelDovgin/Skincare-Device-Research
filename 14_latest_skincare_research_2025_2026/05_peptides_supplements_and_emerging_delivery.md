# Peptides, supplements, and emerging delivery

*Compiled 2026-07-14. The word “peptide” covers topical signaling ingredients, oral collagen hydrolysates, experimental delivery systems, and product-specific blends; these are not interchangeable.*

## 0. Bottom line

The 2025–2026 peptide literature is active but easy to overread. A few small topical trials report improvements in wrinkles or barrier metrics, while the largest recent synthesis finds that the pooled signal is driven mainly by oral peptide studies and that high-quality topical evidence is sparse. The most defensible conclusion is **peptides may be reasonable low-irritation adjuncts, but they are not established substitutes for sunscreen, retinoids, or procedure-specific treatment**.

## 1. Evidence map

| Study | Intervention / design | Main finding | What keeps it from being a decisive retail conclusion |
|---|---|---|---|
| OS-01 / Peptide 14 ([PMID 40193112](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40193112/)) | 2025 randomized double-blind; 60 women aged 60–90; 12 weeks | Improved TEWL, hydration, pH-related barrier measures and reported appearance vs moisturizer control | Product-specific, small, and OneSkin-linked; systemic inflammation/biological-age interpretation is exploratory |
| Cyclized hexapeptide-9 ([PMID 40586182](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40586182/)) | 2025 randomized double-blind active/vehicle-controlled; 0.002% peptide vs 0.002% retinol for 56 days | Reported reductions in wrinkle count/area/roughness and larger effects than the retinol arm on several endpoints | Authors are linked to Shanghai Chicmax/Kans; short study and unusual active concentration; needs independent replication |
| PTPD-12 ([PMID 41044809](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41044809/)) | 2025 randomized split-face; 21 participants; 8 weeks | Reduced melanin index and improved investigator/patient ratings | Pigment peptide, not a general anti-aging peptide; small and short |
| Low-molecular-weight collagen peptide supplement ([PMID 40935395](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40935395/)) | 2025 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled; 70 adults; 1,650 mg/day product for 8 weeks | Reported improvements in wrinkles, hydration, elasticity, dermal density, pores, and sebum | Oral product study; company-linked research center/producer context; multiple endpoints |
| Bioactive collagen peptides ([PMID 41311286](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41311286/)) | 2025 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled; 77 women; 5,000 mg/day for 12 weeks + 4-week washout | Improved dermal density, hydration, TEWL and thickness, with some persistence after washout | Company employees among authors; molecular composition and dose are product-specific |
| Sakura + Rosa roxburghii supplement ([PMID 41215693](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41215693/)) | 2025 randomized placebo-controlled; 60 women; 8 weeks | Tests hydration, chromaticity, elasticity, collagen index, texture, and wrinkles | Oral botanical combination; Shiseido-linked authors; cannot isolate either ingredient |
| Oral/topical peptide meta-analysis ([PMID 41924746](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41924746/)) | 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of RCTs | Synthesizes hydration, elasticity, wrinkles, roughness, and brightness; only two high-quality topical studies | Pooled studies differ by route, peptide, dose, outcome, and industry involvement; topical inference is weak |
| Neuropeptide serum after botulinum toxin ([PMID 42299447](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42299447/)) | 2026 double-blind vehicle-controlled split-face study | Tests post-procedural serum for lines, roughness, and skin quality around toxin injection | SkinCeuticals-linked authors/product; post-BTX context does not generalize to untreated skin |
| Emerging acne nanocarriers ([PMID 41840253](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41840253/)) | 2026 translational review | Lipid/vesicular/polymeric systems may improve follicular delivery and tolerability | Mostly a delivery roadmap; clinical reproducibility, manufacturing, and long-term safety remain gaps |
| Probiotic-loaded alginate microspheres ([PMID 41034461](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41034461/)) | 2025 in-vitro formulation study | Encapsulated *L. casei* formulation showed antimicrobial and scratch-test signals | No human outcome; viability and real-world formulation stability need testing |
| Oral supplement photoprotection ([PMID 39804624](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804624/)) | 2025 systematic review | Finds nutritional supplement signals for photoprotection | Heterogeneous supplements and endpoints; none replaces sunscreen |

## 2. Topical versus oral evidence

Topical peptides face a delivery problem: the stratum corneum limits penetration, and cosmetic labels often disclose neither the active peptide concentration nor the stability/vehicle data. Oral collagen studies bypass that topical-delivery problem but introduce a different causal chain: digestion, absorbed fragments, systemic signaling, and a multi-ingredient product. The 2026 review’s pooled oral signal therefore cannot be copied into a topical serum claim.

## 3. How to read “outperforms retinol”

The cyclized hexapeptide-9 trial is interesting because it used an active retinol arm, but “outperforms” is still bounded by the trial:

- 56 days is short compared with long-term retinoid studies.
- The peptide and retinol were each 0.002%, which is not a general comparison of a peptide serum with standard retail or prescription retinoid practice.
- The study is product/company-linked and needs independent replication.
- The endpoint is a set of wrinkle measurements, not an all-purpose measure of skin health.

The correct archive label is **promising small product trial**, not “retinol replacement proven.”

## 4. Practical ranking for the existing peptide project

1. Use the existing `12_peptides_in_skincare` evidence tiers for product decisions.
2. Prefer a peptide product that discloses the peptide identity and, where possible, concentration or supplier.
3. Treat a low-irritation peptide as an adjunct when the routine already covers sunscreen, cleansing, moisturizing, and a target-matched active.
4. Do not pay a premium for “stem cell,” “senolytic,” “biological age,” or “collagen-boosting” language without a product-specific human trial.
5. Separate oral collagen research from topical peptide research in any future recommendation or cost model.

## Evidence gaps

- Independent, adequately powered topical peptide RCTs with vehicle control and at least 6–12 months of follow-up are rare.
- Product labels frequently hide dose, peptide purity, molecular weight, and stability.
- Oral supplement trials often include manufacturer employees or clinical research organizations tied to the product.
- Outcomes vary widely: wrinkle image scores, hydration, TEWL, dermal density, elasticity, pigmentation, and subjective satisfaction are not one endpoint.
