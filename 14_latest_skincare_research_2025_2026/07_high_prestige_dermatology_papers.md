# High-prestige dermatology papers: 2025–2026

*Compiled 2026-07-14. This is a curated supplement to the broader PubMed map, not a journal impact-factor ranking or a formal systematic review.*

## 0. Bottom line

The most useful upgrade is not simply adding more papers; it is separating **high-signal dermatology publishing venues and study designs** from small, product-linked cosmetic studies. This supplement adds 36 papers from JAMA Dermatology, the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology, the British Journal of Dermatology, JEADV, Dermatologic Surgery, and Lasers in Surgery and Medicine. Thirty-five are new to the main ledger; one was already present but is included here because it is a high-value consensus paper.

The strongest additions are:

1. **At-home LED acne evidence:** a JAMA Dermatology systematic review/meta-analysis and a later randomized home-device comparison improve the LED lane, but the total evidence base remains small.
2. **Photoprotection:** JAAD sunscreen reviews and tinted-sunscreen physics clarify mechanisms, regulation, safety, and visible-light limitations; they do not create a universal visible-light rating system.
3. **Barrier and cleanser biology:** BJD studies connect physiological lipids, mild cleansers, and microbiome networks to barrier care, while avoiding the stronger claim that every “microbiome” product is clinically equivalent.
4. **Pigment and skin of color:** JEADV consensus work plus Dermatologic Surgery/Lasers in Surgery and Medicine laser comparisons add direct management and safety context, but darker phototypes remain underrepresented in many trials.
5. **Procedure comparisons and aftercare:** high-quality reviews and randomized studies compare RF microneedling, fractional CO₂, picosecond lasers, IPL, peels, and MFU-V. The results remain protocol-specific and should not be generalized to unmeasured home devices.

## 1. Selection rule

Papers were eligible if PubMed recorded a 2025 or 2026 publication date and the paper came from one of the selected dermatology/procedure journals above. Priority went to randomized or controlled human studies, systematic reviews/meta-analyses, consensus statements, and clinically useful evidence reviews. Mechanistic or delivery papers were retained only when they directly inform topical delivery, barrier biology, or procedure translation.

Journal reputation is a **triage signal**, not a substitute for study quality. A small single-center study in a respected journal remains a small study; a consensus statement remains a consensus statement; and a manufacturer-linked trial remains product-specific.

## 3. What this changes in the archive

- **LED/PBM:** raise the priority of JAMA/JAAD reviews and consensus, but keep consumer-device claims below clinic PBM evidence unless wavelength, irradiance, fluence, geometry, and cadence match.
- **Sunscreen/pigment:** treat tinted products as a visible-light strategy with formulation and shade limitations, not as a universal “better sunscreen.”
- **Barrier/microbiome:** move mild cleansers and physiological lipids above speculative probiotic/nanocarrier claims, while retaining product-specific caveats.
- **Laser/RF:** use the randomized split-face comparisons and complication consensus documents as guardrails; do not rank device classes from one small trial.
- **Skin of color:** give explicit weight to the V–VI acne-scar study and the III–IV laser trials, while retaining the broader evidence gap because representation is still incomplete.

## Evidence gaps

- The set is journal-curated, not a formal impact-factor or citation-count ranking.
- Many 2026 records are online-first; issue metadata and corrections should be rechecked.
- Several high-prestige papers are reviews or consensus statements, so they improve interpretation more than they add new treatment-effect estimates.
- Most cosmetic and device studies still need independent replication, longer follow-up, standardized outcomes, and better inclusion of Fitzpatrick V–VI.
