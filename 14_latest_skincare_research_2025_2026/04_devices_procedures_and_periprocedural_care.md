# Devices, procedures, and periprocedural care

*Compiled 2026-07-14. Energy-based procedures can burn, scar, alter pigmentation, or injure deeper tissue; this page is an evidence map, not a home-treatment protocol.*

## 0. Bottom line

The latest device literature is most useful when read as **comparative and protocol-specific**. MFU-V/HIFU, RF, RF microneedling, fractional lasers, IPL, and diode lasers can each show positive outcomes in selected studies, but they are not interchangeable. Recovery, pain, PIH risk, skin type, operator control, and the exact endpoint often separate a defensible clinic result from a misleading home-device analogy.

## 2. Interpretation by device lane

### LED / photobiomodulation

The 2025 facial trial is one of the more useful recent PBM studies because it reports wavelength, irradiance, fluence, session duration, sham control, and frequency. It supports a conservative statement: **a defined red-LED mask protocol may improve satisfaction and wrinkle grading**, with two weekly sessions appearing sufficient in that trial. It does not establish a universal dose, and it should not be inserted into the handheld pain/recovery project without respecting geometry and endpoint differences.

### IPL and lasers

The 2025/2026 hair-removal trials are valuable for the existing `01` and `02` projects because they directly compare modalities. The 2025 home-vs-medical IPL trial found similar efficacy after three months, while the 2026 IPL-vs-diode trial found a longer-term diode advantage with more pain. These are useful category comparisons—not proof that any seller’s device reproduces the clinical protocol. Hair-removal evidence also cannot be used to justify off-label pigment treatment in melasma or PIH.

### RF and RF microneedling

The new RF literature has more comparative structure but still a manufacturer/conflict issue in several reviews. Positive results for professional RF microneedling should remain separated from home RF: needles, depth, thermal control, treatment density, operator training, and complication reporting change the risk surface. The archive’s existing FDA safety page for RF microneedling should remain the governing safety boundary for consumer decisions.

### HIFU/MFU

The 2025 HIFU/MFU reviews are more cautious than marketing language. Pooled improvement and satisfaction are high, but only a small subset of studies were randomized, pain is material, and RF comparisons did not establish a universal winner. Visualization, focal depth, coupling, line spacing, and anatomy matter more than a generic “ultrasound energy” label.

### Periprocedural skincare

The 2025 algorithms and 2026 integrated-skincare consensus align on a practical principle: **the greater the barrier disruption, the simpler and more bland the aftercare should be**. Ceramides, cholesterol, hyaluronic acid, niacinamide, and peptides were broadly accepted by the 2026 panel; retinoids and stronger acids were considered unsuitable on treatment day/early healing. These are consensus recommendations and should not override a procedure-specific clinician aftercare plan.

## 3. Safety translation

- Do not use a positive clinic trial to infer that a home device has the same output, depth, pulse timing, spot size, cooling, or clinical endpoint.
- Treat pigment risk as a first-class outcome, especially for FST III–VI or a history of PIH.
- Do not stack energy procedures or aggressive acids/retinoids during active barrier disruption without clinician guidance.
- RF microneedling is a medical procedure; the FDA’s 2025 safety communication in the existing `10` topic is more important than a favorable cosmetic review.
- “Non-invasive” does not mean risk-free, and “FDA-cleared” does not mean a device is cleared for every cosmetic claim.

## Evidence gaps

- Many studies use GAIS or satisfaction rather than blinded objective endpoints.
- Follow-up is often shorter than the time at which relapse, collagen remodeling, or late pigment change becomes clear.
- Home-device studies rarely publish enough output data for independent replication.
- Darker skin types and people with prior PIH remain underrepresented.
