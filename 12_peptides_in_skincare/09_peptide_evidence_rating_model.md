# Peptide Evidence Maturity and Formulation-Confidence Rating Model

*Compiled 2026-08-14. Research orientation, not medical advice. This page is an evidence-and-formulation ranking, not a price ranking, efficacy guarantee, or product recommendation.*

## 0. Bottom line

The current 25-formulation scan contains many peptide names for which a defensible **source price** or **independently stated amount** cannot be found. That is a pricing problem, not proof that the ingredient is useless. The right next question is whether each ingredient deserves attention based on its biology, human evidence, formulation identity, and research maturity.

My evidence-weighted ranking of the 36 named peptide ingredients and peptide technologies in the scan is:

1. **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4 / pal-KTTKS** — the strongest direct matched-vehicle topical wrinkle study in this scan, though still based mainly on one manufacturer-authored program.
2. **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 / Argireline** — the clearest direct expression-line signal, with a small randomized study and meaningful penetration limitations.
3. **Copper Tripeptide-1 / GHK-Cu** — unusually broad mechanistic and formulation literature, but less decisive objective facial evidence than the reputation suggests.
4. **sh-Oligopeptide-1 / recombinant EGF** and **sh-Polypeptide-1 / FGF2** — biologically defined growth factors with a broader topical growth-factor literature, but heterogeneous products, delivery constraints, and weak ingredient-specific attribution.
5. **Matrixyl 3000, Matrixyl Synthe'6, Tetrapeptide-21, and Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5** — identifiable, formulator-ready commercial technologies with plausible supplier data; they should not inherit the direct clinical evidence of pal-KTTKS or Argireline.
6. **Sodium DNA / PDRN** — an emerging repair/biopolymer lane with real clinical literature in procedures and newer topical work, but topical exposure cannot be assumed to match injection or microneedling.
7. **Progeline, ChroNOline, SYN-AKE, SNAP-8, Leuphasyl, Skinarch, Neøclair Pro, Relistase, X50, and the pigment peptides** — plausible or technically well-described, but mostly supplier-led, combination-formula, or insufficiently replicated evidence.
8. **Opaque growth-factor blends, Bio-Placenta, unbranded Tripeptide-2, and poorly documented peptide names** — not necessarily inactive, but not auditable enough to justify a high rank.

The ranking deliberately does **not** use retail price, supplier price, retail multiple, brand prestige, social-media popularity, or the amount of marketing material. The companion [ingredient-by-ingredient price research](index.html#doc8) answers the procurement question separately; this page answers **“how much confidence should this ingredient receive before I treat it as an important peptide?”**

## 1. Why this page exists

The earlier cost model exposed an evidence asymmetry: some commercial premixes have a public price ladder but no independently disclosed pure-peptide amount; other named ingredients have a plausible mechanism but no public quote or matched formula amount. A cost table cannot resolve that asymmetry.

This page adds a separate decision layer for all 36 named ingredients/technologies in the current data ledger. It combines:

- direct human topical outcomes;
- trial design, independence, replication, and comparator quality;
- mechanism and target specificity;
- identity, active-content, and finished-formula disclosure;
- practical topical delivery, stability, and formulation feasibility;
- a separate development-stage/novelty flag.

It does **not** attempt to estimate a brand's procurement price, pure-peptide percentage, profit, efficacy magnitude in a particular serum, or medical equivalence to tretinoin, botulinum toxin, lasers, RF, or injectables.

## 2. The score and rating scale

### 2.1 Score components

The total is a 100-point evidence-maturity score:

| Component | Points | What earns points | What does not earn points by itself |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **H — Human topical outcomes** | 0–30 | Randomized topical human results with relevant endpoints and a usable formula description | A cell study, animal study, injectable study, or an unquantified before/after claim |
| **R — Research quality, independence, and replication** | 0–20 | Blinding, matched vehicle, objective endpoints, adequate sample, independent replication, transparent conflicts | A supplier brochure, one tiny uncontrolled panel, or a finished multi-active result with no attribution |
| **M — Mechanism and target match** | 0–15 | Defined sequence/complex, credible target, skin-relevant pathway, and a plausible reason topical exposure could matter | A broad “boosts collagen” slogan or a mechanism borrowed from a different peptide |
| **F — Formulation identity and disclosure** | 0–15 | Exact INCI/sequence, active content, recommended use level, carrier/vehicle, stability or processing information | A trade name with an undisclosed blend, “peptide complex” with no active identity, or a label percentage that only describes the premix |
| **D — Delivery, stability, and finished-formula feasibility** | 0–20 | Evidence that the material survives formulation, remains available, has a plausible topical route, or has a documented delivery system | Assuming that a large protein or polynucleotide reaches the dermis simply because it works when injected |

**Score = H + R + M + F + D.** The companion CSV records every component so the ranking can be audited or changed without rebuilding the argument.

### 2.2 Rating bands

| Rating | Score | Meaning in this topical-cosmetic comparison |
|---|---:|---|
| **A — evidence-leading** | 60–100 | At least one meaningful direct human topical signal plus enough identity/formulation information to make the claim auditable. Still not equivalent to a prescription or procedure. |
| **B — promising / usable evidence** | 45–59 | Credible human, supplier, or category evidence, but replication, attribution, comparator strength, or delivery remains incomplete. |
| **C — plausible / supplier-led** | 30–44 | Defined or technically feasible ingredient with a coherent mechanism, but human evidence is narrow, indirect, manufacturer-led, or difficult to attribute. |
| **D — insufficiently auditable** | 0–29 | Identity, dose, delivery, source, or direct outcome evidence is too incomplete for a strong evidence-led ranking. |

These bands are intentionally conservative. A C-rated peptide may still be a reasonable low-irritation experiment. A-rated means **best-supported within this narrow peptide scan**, not “large effect,” “clinically proven anti-aging,” or “worth any price.”

### 2.3 Newness is a separate axis

Newness is recorded as **legacy**, **established**, **emerging**, or **uncertain**. It is not added to the score. A 2025 peptide with one well-controlled trial can be more interesting than a 2005 blend with only marketing data, but it is still unreplicated. Conversely, an old ingredient can have a mature identity and formulation record without having strong direct facial-outcome evidence.

### Important note on the order

The scores are close in the middle. Rank 8 versus rank 9 is not a meaningful claim that TEGO Pep 4-17 is better than SYN-COLL. It means both are in the same **B / promising but supplier-led** band. The useful separation is between:

- direct controlled human evidence;
- defined but supplier-led technologies;
- named ingredients with plausible mechanisms but weak attribution;
- opaque complexes where identity and dose cannot be audited.

## 4. What drives the top of the list

### 4.1 Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4: the strongest direct evidence anchor

The pal-KTTKS study is unusually useful because the same moisturizer vehicle was used with and without approximately 3 ppm peptide in a 12-week randomized, double-blind, split-face design involving 93 women. Fine-line and wrinkle measures favored the peptide side. [[2]](source_docs/pal-kttks-2005-rct-pubmed.xml)

That earns a high H and R score, not a perfect score. The program is manufacturer-authored, public reporting is not enough for a clean comparison with tretinoin or procedures, and independent replication remains limited. It is the best-supported **topical peptide signal** in this scan, not a large-effect anti-aging treatment.

### 4.2 Argireline: the best expression-line candidate, with a delivery ceiling

The Argireline study randomized 60 participants to peptide or placebo for four weeks and reported peri-orbital wrinkle improvement with clinical and replica-based measurements. [[3]](source_docs/argireline-2013-rct-pubmed.xml) A second, small Asian study tested Acetylhexapeptide-3 and Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4; that is not the same molecule as Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 and should not be merged into the Argireline evidence. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36909866/)

Lubrizol’s official formulation information helps with F and D: Argireline is available in water-based and oil-soluble delivery versions, and example formulas disclose the commercial solution and processing context. [[18]](https://www.lubrizol.com/Personal-Care/Products/Argireline-Peptide) That improves formulation confidence; it does not convert a cosmetic topical into botulinum toxin.

### 4.3 GHK-Cu: a high-mechanism, medium-human-evidence peptide

GHK-Cu has a long history of copper-binding, wound, matrix, and cellular-mechanism work. [[5]](source_docs/ghk-cu-skin-regeneration-review-2015.xml) But the directly auditable randomized post-CO2-laser study had only 13 completers and found no objective wrinkle, overall skin-quality, or erythema advantage, although satisfaction favored GHK-Cu. [[6]](source_docs/ghk-cu-post-laser-2006-trial-pubmed.xml)

That is why GHK-Cu ranks above most supplier-only peptides but below the two direct wrinkle anchors. Its M and D scores are strong; its H and R scores are deliberately restrained.

### 4.4 Growth factors: real category evidence, poor ingredient attribution

The topical growth-factor literature is broader than the ordinary cosmetic-peptide literature. A 2023 systematic review found 33 studies, including 9 randomized trials, across 23 preparations and 1,180 participants. The review reported generally modest improvements but emphasized heterogeneity in growth-factor source, number, additional ingredients, outcomes, and persistence beyond six months. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37222303/)

That supports a B-tier category read for defined sh-Oligopeptide-1/EGF and sh-Polypeptide-1/FGF2, not a free pass for every “growth factor complex.” A separate review specifically warns that clinical preparations using sh-Oligopeptide-1 are not automatically equivalent to a validated, stable, delivered recombinant human EGF preparation. [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37452558/)

### 4.5 Matrixyl family: do not transfer evidence between trademarks

Croda identifies Matrixyl 3000 as a commercial INCI blend containing Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 and Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7. [[14]](https://www.crodabeauty.com/en-gb/products/product/3007-matrixyl_1_3000) Croda identifies Matrixyl Synthe'6 as a different commercial solution containing Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38. [[15]](https://www.crodabeauty.com/en-gb/products/product/3046-matrixyl_1_synthe_1_6)

Those are useful formulator facts. They do not mean:

- Matrixyl 3000 is equivalent to original Matrixyl pal-KTTKS;
- Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 and Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7 each inherit the whole Matrixyl 3000 result;
- a serum percentage of “Matrixyl 10%” is 10% pure peptide;
- a supplier before/after image is an independent clinical replication.

### 4.6 TEGO Pep 4-17 and SYN-COLL: high formulation confidence, moderate evidence confidence

Evonik identifies TEGO Pep 4-17 as Tetrapeptide-21 and publishes the commercial vehicle and ECM-oriented use case. [[17]](https://www.evonik.com/en/products/cs/pr_52023004.html) DSM’s SYN-COLL documentation identifies Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5, its glycerin-based vehicle, active content, and recommended use range. [[16]](https://www.dsm.com/content/dam/protected/personal-care/en_US/peptides/peptides_distributor/syn-coll_formulation_guidelines_2023_09.pdf)

Both therefore score well on identity and formulation feasibility. They do not receive A-tier human scores because the public evidence is dominated by supplier studies and because a finished-formula claim cannot tell us how much of the visible result came from the peptide rather than the vehicle.

### 4.7 PDRN: emerging, but route matters

PDRN/polynucleotides have a legitimate clinical literature in tissue repair and aesthetic procedures. A systematic review describes promising results but also emphasizes that the literature is concentrated in medical or procedural use. [[11]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11845969/) Newer topical research is worth watching, including a randomized split-face study of 0.1% PDRN-850K eye cream versus 0.1% retinol over 28 days. [[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42430369/)

The correct current rating is B / emerging rather than “proven injectable effect in a bottle.” The molecule class, fragment length, purification, vehicle, and delivery method are central variables.

## 6. Formulator-facing interpretation

The formulation-confidence portion of the score is where the price model could not help. A formulator needs to know whether the material in the tank is the claimed active and whether the finished formula preserves it.

### A. Identity

Prefer an exact INCI and, where possible, the sequence, CAS number, molecular weight, active assay, and carrier composition. “Peptide complex,” “growth-factor blend,” and “bio-placenta” are not sufficient identities for a strong evidence claim.

### B. Active content versus premix percentage

Commercial percentages often refer to the supplier solution. For example, a Matrixyl 3000 use level describes the commercial blend containing glycerin, water, stabilizers, and the two peptide INCIs—not the mass of pure Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 plus Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7. The same distinction applies to SYN-COLL, TEGO Pep 4-17, X50, and most growth-factor systems.

### C. Stability and processing

Technical data that states pH range, temperature limits, order of addition, preservatives, storage, or a recommended use level improves D and F. It does not prove a wrinkle outcome. Progeline’s technical file, for example, gives a 2% clinical-study formulation, pH/stability guidance, and post-addition processing advice. [[19]](https://cms.safic-alcan.com/app/uploads/2026/01/tds_PROGELINE_TDS_2021_EN-58642.pdf)

### D. Delivery

Lipidation, encapsulation, a commercial carrier, or an oil-soluble version can improve the plausibility of topical exposure. It still needs finished-formula evidence. A “cosmetic drone” or targeted delivery claim is not equivalent to a measured human skin-concentration study. X50’s official documentation describes its targeted delivery system and active INCI, which earns formulation points, not an automatic A rating. [[22]](https://www.evonik.com/en/products/cs/pr_52058158.html)

### E. Identity of growth factors and PDRN

Growth factors are larger recombinant proteins, not interchangeable with short signal peptides. PDRN is a DNA-derived biopolymer, not a single short peptide. Their evidence and delivery constraints must be rated in their own lane.

## 8. Assumptions and hard boundaries

1. **No price input.** Retail price, raw-material price, cost per gram, bottle size, brand margin, and the earlier retail multiple are excluded from every score.
2. **No product-level halo effect.** A peptide does not inherit the result of a serum containing many other actives. A supplier’s in-vivo claim is marked as supplier-led.
3. **No trademark transfer.** Matrixyl 3000, Matrixyl Synthe'6, SYN-COLL, SYN-AKE, X50, and TEGO technologies are separate technologies. Shared “collagen” or “Botox-like” language does not merge their evidence.
4. **No mechanism-as-outcome shortcut.** Fibroblast, collagen, MMP, TGF-β, tyrosinase, receptor, or gene-expression findings are mechanistic evidence, not proof of visible human improvement.
5. **No route transfer.** Injectable GHK-Cu, PDRN, polynucleotide, or growth-factor evidence cannot be treated as equivalent to a normal leave-on serum. Microneedling-assisted delivery is a different exposure condition.
6. **No concentration invention.** If the formula discloses only the commercial premix level, the active mass remains unknown. This document does not back-calculate it.
7. **No false precision.** Mid-table scores are judgment bands. A difference of two or three points should not be read as a meaningful biological ranking.
8. **Safety is not efficacy.** A well-tolerated ingredient can still have weak efficacy evidence; a higher evidence score does not make an ingredient appropriate for damaged skin, disease, pregnancy, or post-procedure use.

## Evidence gaps

- Independent, peptide-only topical RCTs remain rare. The 2026 systematic review found 19 peptide RCTs, but only two high-quality topical studies; most pooled benefit was driven by oral formulations. [[1]](source_docs/nukaly-2026-oral-topical-peptides-systematic-review.pdf)
- Head-to-head trials against ordinary-strength retinol or prescription tretinoin are scarce.
- Finished products rarely disclose the pure active mass, vehicle-level stability, skin concentration, or intact-peptide recovery.
- Growth-factor and PDRN studies often mix route, delivery system, procedure, or co-actives.
- Many branded actives have supplier clinical data but no independent replication, and some supplier pages change or disappear.
- The current scan includes 25 retail formulations, not every peptide on the market. The benchmark section prevents newer evidence from being lost merely because it was not present in the original product list.

## Reproducible data

The machine-readable model is [peptide_evidence_rating_model.csv](data/peptide_evidence_rating_model.csv). It contains one row per named ingredient/technology, the five component scores, the separate novelty flag, the rating confidence, and the source-key trail. It is a research ledger, not a claim that the numbers are validated clinical measurements.
