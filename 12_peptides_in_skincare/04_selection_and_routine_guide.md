# Peptide Selection and Routine Guide

*Compiled 2026-07-12. Research orientation, not medical advice. Patch test new products; seek clinical guidance for persistent rash, pigment disorders, wounds, pregnancy-related treatment questions, or post-procedure care.*

## 0. Bottom line

Choose a peptide by **goal + INCI name + finished-formula evidence**, not by the number of peptides on the front label.

| Goal | First peptide to look for | Expectation |
|---|---|---|
| Fine lines / photoaging support | **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4** | Best-established modest topical peptide signal over about 8–12 weeks |
| Mild expression lines | **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8** | Possible peri-orbital/forehead softening; not Botox-equivalent |
| Gentle repair / maintenance | **Copper Tripeptide-1 (GHK-Cu)** | Plausible low-irritation adjunct; clinical certainty is limited |
| Emerging multi-domain wrinkle/barrier option | **Cyclized Hexapeptide-9** | Promising single study; verify exact ingredient and formula evidence |
| Barrier/hydration experiment | **OS-01 / Peptide 14** | Early proprietary-formula evidence; base moisturizer still matters |
| Pigment adjunct | **PTPD-12** | Early small split-face trial; not a replacement for photoprotection or established pigment care |

## 1. Thirty-second picker

1. **Are you already using sunscreen?** If no, start there; it has stronger prevention evidence than any peptide.
2. **Can you tolerate a retinoid and is photoaging the goal?** If yes, use the peptide as optional support, not the active anchor.
3. **Is the main issue expression at movement?** Look for Acetyl Hexapeptide-8.
4. **Is the main issue mild static fine lines?** Look for Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4.
5. **Is the main issue sensitivity/repair and you want a gentle experiment?** Consider Copper Tripeptide-1.
6. **Is the claim pigment, scar remodeling, or lifting?** Peptides are not first-line; use the cross-category comparison before buying.

## 2. INCI-to-marketing translation

| What the label may say | What to find in the ingredient list | Important caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Matrixyl / original Matrixyl | **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4** | Do not confuse with other Matrixyl families |
| Matrixyl 3000 | **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 + Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7** | Combination; evidence does not automatically equal pal-KTTKS evidence |
| Matrixyl Synthe'6 | **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38** | Different peptide and evidence base |
| Argireline | **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8** | “Botox-like” is mechanism marketing, not equivalence |
| SNAP-8 | **Acetyl Octapeptide-3** | Less direct clinical evidence than Argireline |
| Leuphasyl | **Pentapeptide-18** | Often paired with Argireline; contribution unclear |
| SYN-AKE | **Dipeptide Diaminobutyroyl Benzylamide Diacetate** | Waglerin-inspired marketing; limited independent human evidence |
| SYN-COLL | **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5** | Supplier-led evidence is common |
| Copper peptides / GHK-Cu | **Copper Tripeptide-1** | A blue color does not verify dose, purity, or efficacy |
| Peptide 14 / OS-01 | Proprietary peptide may not have a standard consumer-facing INCI synonym | Finished-formula study matters more than the nickname |

## 3. Product evidence checklist

Prefer products that answer more of these questions:

- Is the exact INCI peptide disclosed?
- Is the concentration disclosed as **pure active**, not merely percent of a supplier solution?
- Was the marketed finished formula studied, or only the raw peptide?
- Was the control the same vehicle without peptide?
- Were investigators blinded and outcomes objective?
- Are other strong actives—retinoid, niacinamide, acid, vitamin C—confounding the result?
- Is packaging appropriate for a water-based peptide formula and is stability documented?
- Is the claim appropriate: appearance/conditioning versus treatment of disease, wounds, or structural change?

More peptide names do not answer any of these.

## 4. Routine placement

### Simple morning

1. Gentle cleanse or rinse.
2. Peptide serum if using one.
3. Moisturizer as needed.
4. Broad-spectrum sunscreen; tinted/iron-oxide protection when pigment/visible light is relevant.

### Simple evening

1. Cleanse.
2. Goal-based treatment: retinoid on tolerated nights **or** peptide on recovery/non-retinoid nights.
3. Moisturizer.

There is no strong need to separate most ordinary peptides from niacinamide or moisturizers. Claims that copper peptides are universally “destroyed” by vitamin C or retinoids are often oversimplified; the more practical issue is **formula compatibility and irritation**. If a routine stings or pills, alternate AM/PM or alternate nights rather than building a chemically crowded layer stack.

## 5. A fair 12-week trial

### Before starting

- Keep sunscreen, cleanser, moisturizer, lighting, camera distance, and other actives stable.
- Take relaxed and expression photos in the same lighting.
- Pick one target: crow's feet, forehead fine lines, hydration, or a defined pigment area.

### Cadence

- Patch test for several days.
- Start once daily for one week.
- Increase to label directions if comfortable.
- Assess at weeks 4, 8, and 12; do not judge structural change from next-day plumping.

### Continue only if

- the formula is comfortable and cosmetically usable;
- standardized photos or a consistent measure show a meaningful benefit;
- the price remains sensible relative to a good moisturizer, sunscreen, niacinamide, or retinoid.

## 6. When peptides are the wrong primary buy

| Problem | Better next move |
|---|---|
| Moderate/severe dynamic glabellar lines | Discuss botulinum toxin with a qualified clinician if desired; topical Argireline will be subtler |
| Atrophic acne scars | Professional microneedling/fractional-resurfacing evaluation, not a peptide-only serum |
| Meaningful laxity/jowling | Professional assessment; peptide “firming” is not tissue lifting |
| Melasma or persistent/changing pigment | Diagnosis, strict photoprotection, and evidence-based pigment plan |
| Broken, infected, or freshly procedure-treated skin | Procedure-specific/medical aftercare; do not improvise peptide delivery |
| Irritated barrier | Simplify to a bland moisturizer/occlusive strategy before adding another active |

## 7. Safety and stop rules

Topical cosmetic peptides appear generally well tolerated in short trials, but the **whole formula** can cause irritation or allergy.

Stop and reassess for:

- persistent burning, swelling, hives, blistering, or worsening rash;
- new or worsening pigment after irritation;
- eye symptoms from peri-orbital use;
- use on infected, open, or unexpectedly slow-healing skin;
- a post-procedure reaction outside the clinician's expected course.

Avoid buying injectable GHK-Cu or other “research-use-only” peptides as if topical cosmetic evidence established injection safety. It does not.

## 8. Practical recommendations by user type

| User | Best peptide strategy |
|---|---|
| Retinoid-tolerant, prevention-focused | Sunscreen + retinoid remain anchors; peptide is optional recovery-night support |
| Retinoid-intolerant/sensitive | Pal-KTTKS or a simple GHK-Cu formula in a bland base is a reasonable 12-week experiment |
| Expression-line focused but injection-averse | Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 with modest expectations and standardized movement photos |
| Pigment-prone | Do not lead with peptide; prioritize photoprotection and established pigment agents; PTPD-12 is experimental adjunct |
| Device user | Use only bland, compatible aftercare specified for the device/procedure; do not enhance peptide penetration without combination evidence |

## Evidence gaps

- Consumer products rarely disclose meaningful active concentration and stability.
- Few products have matched-vehicle trials.
- Compatibility claims are not well standardized.
- There is little evidence that combining many peptide families is additive.
