# Peptide Science and Functional Taxonomy

*Compiled 2026-07-12. Research orientation, not medical advice. Confidence legend: high within peptide class / limited human evidence / mechanistic or marketing-led.*

## 0. Bottom line

Peptides are short amino-acid chains. In skincare they are usually intended to act as **signals**, **mineral carriers**, **neurotransmission modulators**, **enzyme inhibitors**, or **pigment/barrier modulators**. Those labels describe a proposed biological job; they do not prove that a topical formula reaches the target or produces a visible consumer outcome.

The most useful grouping is by **what the peptide is supposed to do**, then by the strength of direct finished-formula human evidence.

## 1. Why this page exists

Consumer labels mix INCI names, supplier trademarks, and broad “multi-peptide” claims. This page translates the names and separates established human signals from cell-culture plausibility. It does not cover injectable research peptides or treat oral collagen hydrolysates as equivalent to topical peptides. The 2026 systematic review found 19 randomized trials but only **two high-quality topical studies**, which is the central evidence constraint. [[1]](source_docs/nukaly-2026-oral-topical-peptides-systematic-review.pdf)

## 2. The skin-delivery problem

For a peptide to change dermal biology after normal topical use, the finished formula must keep it stable, release it, and move enough intact peptide across or through the stratum corneum. Size, charge, water solubility, lipid affinity, vehicle, and degradation all matter. Lipid conjugation—often the **palmitoyl-** prefix—is used to increase lipophilicity. Copper complexation, encapsulation, and physical delivery technologies are other strategies. [[2]](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijcs.2025.01.019)[[3]](source_docs/ghk-topical-antiwrinkle-review-2024.xml)

That creates three separate questions:

1. Does the peptide affect cells or enzymes in a model?
2. Does the finished topical formula deliver enough intact peptide in human skin?
3. Does a controlled human trial show a visible benefit beyond its moisturizer vehicle?

Marketing often answers question 1 and implies question 3.

## 3. Peptide families grouped by what they can do

### 3.1 Signal peptides / matrikines: collagen, texture, and fine-line support

These peptides mimic fragments or signals associated with extracellular-matrix turnover and are intended to encourage fibroblast or keratinocyte responses.

| INCI / common name | Trademark association | Intended job | Human evidence read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4** (pal-KTTKS) | Original Matrixyl | Signal collagen/ECM support; fine lines | **Best-established topical peptide signal.** One good 93-person split-face vehicle-controlled RCT; manufacturer-authored. [[4]](source_docs/pal-kttks-2005-rct-pubmed.xml) |
| **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-1 + Palmitoyl Tetrapeptide-7** | Matrixyl 3000 | ECM signaling plus proposed inflammatory/ECM modulation | Common and plausible, but direct independent peptide-only trials are thinner than marketing suggests. |
| **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-38** | Matrixyl Synthe'6 | Signal ECM components associated with the dermal-epidermal junction | Mainly supplier/formulation evidence; rank below pal-KTTKS. |
| **Palmitoyl Tripeptide-5** | SYN-COLL | TGF-beta-pathway/collagen signaling claim | Mechanistically plausible; limited transparent independent clinical evidence. |
| **Hexapeptide-9** | Various | Repair/ECM-signaling claim | Conventional form has limited product-specific evidence. |
| **Cyclized Hexapeptide-9 (CHP-9)** | Emerging proprietary peptide | Improve stability/permeability and signal repair | Promising single 96-person, 56-day RCT; beat vehicle and 0.002% retinol, but needs replication and a meaningful retinoid comparator. [[5]](source_docs/chang-2025-cyclized-hexapeptide-9-trial.xml) |
| **Tetrapeptide-21 / GEKG** | Various | ECM signaling and wrinkle/roughness support | Small/formulation-led studies; limited confidence. |

**What this family can reasonably do:** modest fine-line/texture support over roughly 8–12 weeks in a well-formulated moisturizer or serum. It is not proven to lift meaningful laxity or remodel scars like a procedure.

### 3.2 Carrier peptides: copper delivery and repair support

| INCI / common name | Intended job | Human evidence read |
|---|---|---|
| **Copper Tripeptide-1 / GHK-Cu** | Bind/deliver copper used in repair enzymes; support wound/ECM signaling | Rich mechanistic literature and small cosmetic signals, but limited decisive controlled facial data. A 13-completer post-laser RCT found no objective advantage for erythema, wrinkles, or overall quality. [[6]](source_docs/ghk-cu-skin-regeneration-review-2015.xml)[[7]](source_docs/ghk-cu-post-laser-2006-trial-pubmed.xml) |
| **Tripeptide-1 / GHK** | Signal/repair peptide without the copper complex; may be palmitoylated as Pal-GHK | Plausible, but formula delivery is decisive. |
| **Manganese Tripeptide-1** | Carrier/antioxidant-enzyme cofactor claim | Sparse consumer-outcome evidence. |

**What this family can reasonably do:** serve as a low-irritation repair/maintenance adjunct. GHK-Cu is not currently proven equivalent to tretinoin, standard wrinkle devices, wound-care therapy, or injectable use.

### 3.3 Neurotransmitter-modulating / “expression line” peptides

These are marketed as reducing the appearance of dynamic lines by interfering with steps related to neurotransmitter release or muscle signaling. A topical cosmetic is not equivalent to injected botulinum toxin, which reaches the neuromuscular target directly.

| INCI / common name | Trademark association | Intended job | Human evidence read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acetyl Hexapeptide-8** | Argireline | Modulate SNAP-25/SNARE-related signaling; expression lines | Best-supported in this family: 60-person randomized placebo-controlled trial reported peri-orbital improvement after 4 weeks. Penetration remains a major constraint. [[8]](source_docs/argireline-2013-rct-pubmed.xml)[[9]](source_docs/acetyl-hexapeptide-8-permeability-efficacy-review-2025.xml) |
| **Acetyl Octapeptide-3** | SNAP-8 | Similar “expression line” positioning | Mainly supplier/formulation evidence; not equally proven just because the mechanism sounds similar. |
| **Pentapeptide-18** | Leuphasyl | Enkephalin-receptor / muscle-contraction modulation claim | Limited independent clinical evidence; often combined with Argireline. |
| **Dipeptide Diaminobutyroyl Benzylamide Diacetate** | SYN-AKE | Waglerin-inspired neuromuscular-modulation claim | Limited independent topical human evidence. |
| **Acetyl Hexapeptide-1** | Argirelox-related blends may vary | Expression/pigment signaling depending exact peptide | Verify the exact INCI; similar names are not interchangeable. |

**What this family can reasonably do:** possibly soften the look of mild peri-orbital or forehead expression lines. It cannot match the magnitude, reliability, or target access of botulinum toxin. [[10]](source_docs/tretinoin-and-botulinum-comparator-trials-pubmed.xml)

### 3.4 Pigment-modulating peptides

| INCI / common name | Intended job | Human evidence read |
|---|---|---|
| **Decapeptide-12** | Tyrosinase/melanogenesis modulation | Open-label melasma systems combined it with glycolic acid, cleanser, and sunscreen, so the peptide contribution cannot be isolated. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23839199/) |
| **Pentasodium Tetracarboxymethyl Palmitoyl Dipeptide-12 (PTPD-12)** | Autophagy-related melanin handling | One 21-person randomized split-face controlled study reported lower melanin index over 8 weeks; early and not yet replicated. [[12]](source_docs/ptpd12-pigment-2025-rct-pubmed.xml) |
| **Nonapeptide-1 / Melanostatine-5** | Alpha-MSH/melanogenesis pathway claim | Mostly mechanistic/supplier evidence. |
| **Oligopeptide-34** | Multi-pathway brightening claim | Limited transparent controlled human evidence. |
| **Tetrapeptide-30** | Uneven-tone/inflammation-related claim | Usually tested in multi-ingredient systems; attribution is weak. |

**What this family can reasonably do:** act as an optional adjunct for uneven tone. It should not displace broad-spectrum/tinted sunscreen or better-established pigment agents, and melasma deserves clinician-guided management.

### 3.5 Barrier, hydration, inflammation, and senescence-targeting peptides

| Peptide | Intended job | Human evidence read |
|---|---|---|
| **OS-01 / Peptide 14** | Senescence-associated signaling; barrier/hydration | A 60-woman randomized double-blind 12-week trial reported improved TEWL and hydration versus a commercial moisturizer; proprietary finished-formula result, not yet broad replication. [[13]](source_docs/os01-peptide14-2025-rct-pubmed.xml) |
| **Acetyl Tetrapeptide-5** | Eye puffiness/edema and hydration claim | Common in eye products; limited independent controlled evidence. |
| **KPV (Lys-Pro-Val)** | Anti-inflammatory signaling | Interesting preclinical/therapeutic literature; insufficient evidence for routine cosmetic facial outcomes. |

**What this family can reasonably do:** potentially improve comfort, hydration, or barrier measures in a good moisturizer. Ceramides, glycerin, petrolatum, and established moisturization are still the first-line formulation anchors.

### 3.6 Enzyme-inhibitory peptides

This category includes soy-, rice-, silk-, and other plant/animal-derived peptide fractions proposed to inhibit collagenase, elastase, proteases, or pigmentation enzymes.

**Evidence read:** broad category plausibility, but ingredient identity, hydrolysate composition, dose, allergenicity, and finished-formula evidence vary substantially. Do not infer that a “rice peptide” or “soy peptide” has the same activity as a defined sequence.

### 3.7 Antimicrobial and wound-healing peptides

Antimicrobial peptides such as defensin-related or synthetic sequences are a legitimate therapeutic research field. That does not make a cosmetic peptide serum an infection treatment or validated post-procedure product. Freshly injured skin changes exposure and safety. Follow the treating clinician's aftercare rather than using a cosmetic peptide as a wound-healing drug.

## 4. Functional map: which family fits which goal?

| Goal | Most relevant peptide family | Best current candidates | Evidence ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fine lines / photoaging support | Signal peptides | Palmitoyl Pentapeptide-4; emerging CHP-9 | Modest adjunct |
| Dynamic expression lines | Neurotransmitter-modulating | Acetyl Hexapeptide-8 | Modest topical effect; far below botulinum toxin |
| Repair / low-irritation maintenance | Carrier peptides | GHK-Cu / Copper Tripeptide-1 | Plausible adjunct; inconsistent objective clinical proof |
| Uneven tone / pigment | Pigment-modulating | PTPD-12 emerging; Decapeptide-12 systems | Early/combination evidence |
| Barrier / hydration | Barrier/senescence targeting | OS-01 emerging | One proprietary controlled trial; moisturizer base matters |
| Scar, deep texture, meaningful laxity | None as primary | Peptides only as adjuncts | Devices/procedures have the more relevant mechanism |

## 5. Practical takeaways

- Search for the **INCI name**, not only a trademark.
- Prefer a disclosed finished-formula human trial over an ingredient-supplier graph.
- Treat pal-KTTKS and acetyl hexapeptide-8 as the best-established starting points for fine lines and expression lines, respectively.
- Treat GHK-Cu as plausible and gentle, not as a retinoid replacement proven by strong trials.
- Treat CHP-9, OS-01, and PTPD-12 as emerging single-program evidence, not category winners yet.
- Give a tolerable topical peptide 8–12 weeks and use standardized photos. If there is no visible change, do not assume a higher price or more peptide names will fix delivery.

## Evidence gaps

- Few independent replications and few head-to-heads against established active concentrations.
- Finished formulas often contain niacinamide, gluconolactone, humectants, antioxidants, or botanicals that confound attribution.
- Skin penetration is rarely demonstrated in the marketed finished product.
- Trademark blend percentages and pure-peptide concentrations are routinely confused.
- Durability after discontinuation is largely unknown.
