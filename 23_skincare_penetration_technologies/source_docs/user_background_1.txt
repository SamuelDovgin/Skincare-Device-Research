2. The ingredient-penetration study: what was actually tested
Published June 2023 · Journal of the Society of Cosmetic Chemists of Japan

24 Japanese men, ages 20–40, with usable comparison groups of four to six participants.

Researchers applied magnesium ascorbyl phosphate (a vitamin C derivative) and niacinamide to the forearms, delivered different electrical waveforms, then used adhesive tape stripping and HPLC analysis to measure how much ingredient entered the outer skin. The niacinamide exposure lasted 60 seconds and the vitamin C derivative exposure lasted 90 seconds.

3. Human clinical evidence for RF and EMS
RF — Bloom WR (2021). In a four-week study, 14 women used YA-MAN's Bloom WR with its accompanying serum on one side of the face, while applying serum alone to the other. The device-treated side had significantly better results for crow's-feet scores, hydration and several facial-angle measurements. The difference in nasolabial-fold width narrowly missed conventional statistical significance (p = 0.054). This is direct evidence for an older Bloom model, not proof of identical results from today's Photo Plus Deep Lift.

CERTEC — high-frequency EMS (2024). Researchers at the University of Tokyo studied 24 women using a prototype YA-MAN device for eight weeks, with treatment on one side of the face and skincare alone on the other. Dermatologists' average crow's-feet grades were 2.67 on the treated side versus 3.50 on the control side after eight weeks (p = 0.002). Several objective measurements of elasticity and facial contours also improved. However, the treated side was not randomized, and the device included other functions that may have contributed to the results.

4. What the marketing multipliers don't establish
Panasonic's collagen claim is not collagen banking. Its EH-SS85 penetration test used collagen molecules weighing approximately 1,000–5,000 Da, substantially smaller than intact collagen. The result concerns penetration of that ingredient into the stratum corneum, not an increase in newly synthesized dermal collagen. 

Panasonic

Panasonic's hyaluronic-acid tests likewise used 5,000–10,000 Da material. Results with that molecular-weight range cannot automatically be applied to every hyaluronic-acid serum, especially products containing much larger molecules. 

Panasonic
+1

For the smaller active ingredients, the comparison is much closer. Panasonic's EH-SS85 reports 1.6× vitamin C penetration in 12 people, while YA-MAN's Deep Lift reports 1.7× penetration of a vitamin C derivative in 10 men. The different formulations and testing conditions prevent a direct comparison, but they illustrate why Panasonic's 12× hyaluronic-acid figure shouldn't be treated as a universal 12× absorption improvement.
