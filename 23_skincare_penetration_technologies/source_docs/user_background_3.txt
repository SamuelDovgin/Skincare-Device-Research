2. What the Japanese categories actually mean
Lift care (リフトケア): Moving the device across the face in an upward, lifting motion. The footnote defines the technique, not proven long-term lifting.

Moisture care (うるおいケア): Hydration and helping moisturizing ingredients reach the outer skin layers.

Pore care (毛穴ケア): Improving the appearance of pores.

Firmness care (ハリケア): Skin firmness, elasticity and the appearance of plumpness.

Clarity care (透明感ケア): A clearer-looking complexion resulting from smoother skin texture. This doesn't necessarily mean reducing melanin or treating pigmentation.

Muscle training (トレーニング): Transmitting electrical stimulation to muscles to produce contractions, generally through EMS.

The important distinction for your interest in ingredient penetration is that the chart combines fundamentally different technologies and outcomes. Moisturization, muscle contractions, visible lifting and increased collagen production are not interchangeable measures of effectiveness.

There are two different mechanisms to distinguish. Sustained, moderate heating around 43–45°C has been associated with fibroblast stimulation and extracellular-matrix production in laboratory experiments. More immediate collagen contraction generally involves substantially higher tissue temperatures, often around 57–61°C or above. Those higher temperatures are not appropriate targets for unmonitored home treatment. 

Wiley Online Library
+1

Crucially, skin-surface temperature is not the same as dermal temperature. Professional RF equipment can heat deeper tissue more strongly while protecting the epidermis. A 2024 animal study, for example, measured approximately 50–60°C in the dermis while keeping the skin surface under approximately 42–45°C. That result cannot be assumed for a consumer device without equivalent measurements.
