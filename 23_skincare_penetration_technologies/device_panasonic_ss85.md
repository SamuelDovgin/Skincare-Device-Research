# Panasonic EH-SS85 Ion Boost Multi EX: research and buying assessment

[Company overview](index.html#company_panasonic) · [All-device comparison](index.html#doc3) · [Final recommendation](index.html#decision)

Reviewed October 2, 2026. **Delivery research grade: C**. This is the [public-evidence rubric](index.html#doc3), not a potency score. No reviewed model reaches A/B for this exact routine.

## What you are buying

Dedicated BOOST and ion delivery; EMS; warm/cool; no RF. Specifications describe capability; they do not establish the amount of ingredient reaching a therapeutic target.

## Research supporting this exact model

Manufacturer uptake tests: HA 12×, n=8; hydrolyzed collagen6.3×; vitamin C1.6×, n=12, in BRIGHT UP. The ingredient, mode and cohort differ.

Manufacturer hydration evaluation, n=10, measures three-hour hydration; four-week n=26 half-face evaluation concerns dry fine lines with the full multimode course. It does not isolate increased vitamin-C delivery as the cause.

**Evidence trail:** [S09: Panasonic SS85](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SS85/feature.html). ## What this means for your skincare

My higher-budget pick for a dedicated compatible-vitamin-C delivery device. Its 12× HA claim is not your vitamin-C result.

For your vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid, Thiamidol and tazarotene, only a compatible vitamin-C/niacinamide product is a plausible optional delivery experiment. The latter three do not have a located consumer-device trial showing useful added benefit. Tazarotene already has irritation risks; this archive does not recommend deliberately enhancing its delivery. Your exact vitamin-C form, pH and vehicle remain unknown. See [ingredient-specific evidence and labels](index.html#doc4).

## What remains unproven

No head-to-head study in this review establishes this device as the clinical winner against another brand. A trial of another generation, generic electroporation study, RF tightening result or high-molecular-weight ingredient assay does not fill the exact-model/formula gap. Where no complete study was recovered, that means limited accessible evidence, not proof of zero effect.

To change this recommendation, the useful evidence would be a randomized, masked, exact-device comparison using the same finished topical on both sides, with irritation and meaningful pigment/acne outcomes over adequate follow-up.
