# Medicube: company evidence and device directory

Medicube has the lowest sampled entry prices and more recoverable human evidence than the initial review suggested. Exact Booster Pro has two preliminary published studies; older Booster-H has four manufacturer reports including a matched-vitamin-C comparison. Mini Plus has a caffeine tracer excerpt. These are different models and endpoints.

## How strong is the company’s case?

The best evidence here is a model-specific matched-topical human comparison or a disclosed ingredient uptake test. A named laboratory, patent, voltage figure, subjective satisfaction percentage or general mechanism bibliography has a different evidentiary role. The pages identify which support actually belongs to each device and which ingredient was tested.

For your vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid, Thiamidol and tazarotene, only a compatible vitamin-C/niacinamide product is a plausible optional delivery experiment. The latter three do not have a located consumer-device trial showing useful added benefit. Tazarotene already has irritation risks; this archive does not recommend deliberately enhancing its delivery. Your exact vitamin-C form, pH and vehicle remain unknown. See [ingredient-specific evidence and labels](index.html#doc4).

## Coverage and limits

This directory covers the relevant delivery models and adjacent device families identified in this comparison, with older models retained where they matter for used purchases. It is not a claim to cover every device ever sold by the company, all regional/color SKUs, or inaccessible/private research. Older unreviewed catalogue variants remain unranked. No statement that a study was not recovered means that no study could exist.

Standalone RF/EMS/HIFU products are covered by [the RF archive](../07_radio_frequency_skin_tightening/index.html), where applicable. Their outcomes are not ingredient-delivery evidence.
