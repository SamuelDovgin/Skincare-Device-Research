# What the evidence actually supports

A useful purchasing trial would randomize the **same finished skincare formulation**, used with the exact consumer device or without it, and measure pigment, acne, wrinkles or tolerability over months. Ingredient delivery into the outer barrier is a surrogate. The studies below do not provide that missing comparison for your routine.

| Source | Design and finding | Purchasing limitation |
|---|---|---|
| [Huh 2003, vitamin C iontophoresis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12771472/) | Randomized double-blind split-face study, 29 women with melasma, 12 weeks; vitamin C versus water, both delivered by iontophoresis. Colorimetry improved on the vitamin-C side. | Supports the combined treatment; lacks vitamin C applied ordinarily as a control. Cannot isolate the device increment. |
| [Guo 2024, TXA iontophoresis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39185080/) | 30 participants, active versus placebo with iontophoresis, twice weekly for three months; melasma severity/luminance outcomes reported. | Also lacks matched TXA without the device; not a consumer-brand comparison or evidence for your Thiamidol. |
| [Taylor 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23377327/) | 35 patients, uncontrolled vitamin C mask/iontophoresis regimen with acids and sun protection; pigment improvement reported. | Multiple interventions and no control; the headline improvement cannot be assigned to current or vitamin C alone. |
| [Sobhi 2012](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22360337/) | 14 patients, split-face vitamin C iontophoresis versus glycolic peel over six sessions. | A different treatment comparator, not vitamin C alone; very small study. |
| [Niacinamide 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12100180/) and [2005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/) | Controlled topical niacinamide studies found pigment/skin-quality benefits without a penetration device. | Supports ordinary topical use; does not exclude an additional effect from devices, but establishes a reasonable baseline. |
| [Thiamidol 2026](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41566113/) | Vehicle-controlled study, 200 participants; 0.2% Thiamidol, 12-week mMASI reduction reported as 36.1% versus 16.1%. | Formula-specific benefit without a booster; no evidence here for electrically enhancing that product. |

## Corrections to the pasted comparison

- ST0A’s 1.6× BOOST output is a manufacturer engineering claim relative to its predecessor. It is not a measured 1.6× improvement in your clinical results.
- ST99’s earlier pasted peptide 6.3× figure was not reconfirmed in a primary source. The official regional catalog supports HA 5.7× and collagen 5.0×; keep the ingredient identity clear. [Panasonic catalog](https://pstw.panasonic.com.tw/catalog/files/1_consumer/hair/202509_Panasonic_%E7%BE%8E%E5%AE%B9%E5%95%86%E5%93%81.pdf).
- YA-MAN “more warmth” or “stronger EMS sensation” comparisons are not RF wattage or penetration measurements.
- Older Medicube “785%” marketing should not be ranked against Panasonic’s HA multiple without the underlying assay and denominator. It was not reconfirmed on the current official Pro page used here.
- High-output delivery tests do not prove dermal collagen stimulation; additional RF or EMS features require their own evidence.

**What this section found:** small clinical studies support certain active-plus-ion regimens; no located trial establishes the incremental value or best consumer model for the user’s complete routine. Industry tests provide narrower delivery endpoints. S04–S15, S21–S24.
