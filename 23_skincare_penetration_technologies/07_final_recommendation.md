# My final recommendation for your skincare

**If I were buying a device for you, I would choose the original Medicube Booster Pro at roughly $110 delivered for a tested, authentic used unit.** It is my best balanced paid choice for both value and overall usefulness. If you want only a simple penetration experiment, Mini Booster Pro Plus at roughly $77 is the cheaper choice. These are purchase judgments under uncertainty, not a demonstrated clinical efficacy ranking.

Your routine is vitamin C, niacinamide, azelaic acid, Thiamidol and tazarotene. I assume “vitamin” means vitamin C; its exact form/product is still unspecified. Because you rarely use high-molecular-weight ingredients, I give Panasonic’s HA/peptide headline multiples little weight in your buying decision.

## Which will likely work best overall?

**My overall paid pick remains Booster Pro.** This is a prediction about the most sensible purchase for your situation, with low confidence about added skin improvement. It combines affordable entry with preliminary exact-model human evidence. I cannot responsibly predict that SS85, Deep Lift or a premium Prestige will give you greater clinical benefit just because it costs more, has higher voltage, or reports a larger uptake ratio.

If price did not matter and you wanted a dedicated vitamin-C delivery tool, I would lean toward **SS85** for the disclosed test methods. If niacinamide delivery plus RF were your main goals, **Deep Lift** is the more directly ingredient-matched option. Neither preference establishes a winner for pigment, acne or antiaging in your actual routine. [Panasonic methods](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SS85/feature.html), [YA-MAN ingredient tests](https://www.ya-man-tokyo-japan.com/products/forface/photo-plus-deep-lift.html), [exact-Pro EP study](https://doi.org/10.52660/JKSC.2023.29.6.1496).

The old Booster-H now has a more directly relevant vitamin-C report than Pro: a small manufacturer split-face study reported an added brightness effect with the same ampoule. That strengthens the **family’s rationale**, but is not an exact-Pro vitamin-C trial and does not make an unpriced, older used H a better purchase automatically. [Booster-H report review](index.html#medicube_booster_h).

## When I would change the pick

Choose Mini Plus if you only want Booster use and can verify the sealed-claim offer. Choose Panasonic if you prefer its specific ion-mode workflow or seller support. Choose Neo if RF is genuinely a separate priority. Choose no device if your routine is already working, irritated, or hard to keep consistent.

For the approximately $110 Pro offer, confirm exact model, working modes, battery condition, cleanliness and return terms. The unusually cheap X2 “new other” offer is not my default pick: its authenticity/condition are unverified and no exact-X2 trial demonstrates superior results.
