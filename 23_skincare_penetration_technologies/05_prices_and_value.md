## Buying order for your routine

1. **No device: $0 incremental cost.** Most defensible for the five actives you actually use. Spend only if you want an experiment or another independently useful mode.
2. **Low-cost delivery experiment: Mini Plus about $77 or original Mini about $68.** Either is enough to test whether you enjoy the process with basic skincare. Evidence does not establish that one gives better pigment outcomes.
3. **Full Medicube Pro about $110, or X2 about $108 if the unusually inexpensive offer is authentic and complete.** Similar prices make features/condition the decision. New-sealed X2 around $190 is another observed offer, but more power is not a reason to pay more for your small-molecule routine.
4. **Panasonic ion preference: ST99 $150 versus ST0A $183–186.** A $33–36 premium can be reasonable for newer generation and good condition. It buys updated hardware, not demonstrated better niacinamide/Thiamidol outcomes. Compared with the cheap Minis, the premium needs a preference for its ion protocol, warm/cool comfort, documentation or quality.
5. **If RF is also wanted: YA-MAN Shiny Neo about $236.** Assess its RF value in the [RF topic](../07_radio_frequency_skin_tightening/index.html). Deep Lift at $552–587 and SS85 at $348 are hard to justify for penetration alone.

These are decision tiers based on evidence fit and the sampled offers, **not measured efficacy rankings**. No evidence-based dollar threshold for “effective ingredient penetration” exists.

## What to check before paying

Confirm the exact SKU (letter O versus number 0 in ST0A), genuine device, compatible charger, complete heads/cotton ring or accessories required by the manual, battery runtime, every mode functioning, and a usable return policy. “New other,” “tested,” “sealed,” and seller feedback are claims to investigate. Deep Lift’s cheap sealed listing has a seller with limited history; the Panasonic “new other / tested” description is not the same as factory-new. Keep electrical pacemaker/implant restrictions and treated-area limitations in the exact manual in view.

Do not mistake “EMS” in a reseller ST0A/ST99 title for a real specification: these models do not have RF/EMS. An X2-looking listing at a bargain price is not authentication. The cost worksheet excludes taxes, consumables, replacement batteries, failures and resale value, and its hypothetical session count is not a treatment recommendation.

**What this section found:** low-cost Medicube offers make optional experimentation inexpensive; Panasonic’s newer large-molecule performance does not establish a premium worth paying for this routine; YA-MAN premiums principally need a separate RF/feature rationale.
