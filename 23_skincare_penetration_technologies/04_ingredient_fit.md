# Your ingredients: where a booster may help, and where it is poor value

Your answer listed “vitamin,” niacinamide, azelaic acid, Thiamidol and a retinoid interpreted as **tazarotene**. Vitamin C is assumed; vitamin E or another vitamin would require a different formula-specific assessment.

| Ingredient | Plausible delivery benefit | Evidence and practical verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin C | Most plausible optional ion-delivery experiment here, especially an aqueous, compatible derivative formulation. | Small melasma ion studies support combined treatments; none proves your serum improves more than ordinary use. Panasonic’s separate BRIGHT UP mode is more relevant than HA BOOST multiples. Acidic L-ascorbic acid may sting more; formula, pH, stability and tolerability come first. |
| Niacinamide | Aqueous small-molecule delivery can increase in a manufacturer assay, including YA-MAN’s test. | Ordinary topical niacinamide already has controlled human evidence. Optional basic-skincare experiment, with low expected incremental value; no justification for an expensive upgrade solely for it. |
| Azelaic acid | Mechanistically possible depending on vehicle and solubility. | No located evidence for a worthwhile increment with these consumer devices. Burning/stinging is already common with prescription gel; leave it to ordinary topical use. |
| Thiamidol / isobutylamido thiazolyl resorcinol | No established need for electrical enhancement. | Direct vehicle-controlled topical evidence exists; no located device trial with the finished product. Follow its formulation instructions. More delivery has unestablished benefit and tolerability. |
| Tazarotene / other prescription retinoids | Not a useful goal to maximize an already active prescription exposure. | Do not use it as the booster serum. No located controlled consumer-device benefit; irritation is a label concern. Any deliberate enhancement of prescription delivery belongs with the prescriber. |
| HA / hydrophilic peptides, which you rarely use | This is where many large-molecule BOOST claims are concentrated. | Better marketing-test fit, but outer-barrier delivery still does not prove deeper wrinkle benefit. Those ratios should receive little weight in your purchase. |

**The avoid-enhancement judgments are clinical risk/value inferences**, based on the absence of demonstrated incremental benefit and known irritation, rather than a label explicitly naming every wand. ## What I would do with this routine

Keep the active routine stable and put budget toward a tolerable, reliable vitamin C formulation, consistent pigment protection, and adherence. A device is an optional comfort/hydration experiment, not a required step to make these actives work.

If trying one, first use only a bland compatible moisturizer or an already tolerated simple niacinamide product, on intact comfortable skin, following the exact device manual. Keep tazarotene, azelaic acid, Thiamidol and irritating acidic vitamin C out of that first experiment. An initially bland product can reveal tolerability; it cannot validate subsequent use with a prescription. Do not increase settings to chase a manufacturer absorption figure, or stack every device mode on an irritated barrier.

For an eventual vitamin C experiment, identify the **exact product and vitamin C form** and check the device’s instructions. Panasonic BOOST is a bare-skin pretreatment, whereas its subsequent ion modes have their own cotton/product instructions; Medicube Air Shot has different instructions from Booster. Avoid improvising a universal protocol.

Useful comparisons without a device: [L-ascorbic acid delivery study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11207686/) (pig skin, formulation-dependent), [niacinamide clinical study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/), [Thiamidol controlled study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41566113/), [official Thiamidol information](https://www.eucerin.co.uk/skin-concerns/uneven-skin/thiamidol). Consult [retinoid research](../17_tazarotene_vs_tretinoin/index.html) and [pigmentation research](../22_body_hyperpigmentation_long_term/index.html) for the larger routine.
