# 07 — Blind Spots and Opportunity Map

*Compiled 2026-07-10. Research orientation, not medical, legal, or purchasing advice. Confidence: **verified** = primary regulatory/patent source; **signal** = real filing/product but insufficient outcome evidence; **inference** = reasoned market interpretation.*

## 0. Bottom line

The archive already covers the obvious energy categories well: IPL, diode/fractional/non-fractional lasers, LED, RF, microneedling, and focused ultrasound. Its biggest blind spot was not another laser. It was the **convergence layer**: inexpensive home devices combining microcurrent, red/blue LED, heat, ultrasound/sonophoresis, iontophoresis/electroporation, sensors, and apps.

The most important correction is regulatory. The statement “home light devices are cleared only for hair removal” is too broad. It should be: **home IPL and laser devices in this archive are generally hair-removal clearances; home LED devices can have wrinkle and acne indications.** Recent FDA letters for K241718, K252142, K252187, K252642, and K253833 document home microcurrent/LED systems with red-light wrinkle, blue-light acne, or facial-stimulation indications. ## 1. Why this page exists

The popular-product map—IPL, Ulike, Tria, Clear + Brilliant, MiMi Silk—captures what shoppers see, but it can miss technologies that enter through OEM combinations, ingredient-delivery claims, diagnostics, or adjacent medical uses. This audit asks three separate questions:

1. Is a product actually shipping?
2. Does a regulator support the marketed indication?
3. Does a patent merely reveal technical intent?

Those are not interchangeable.

## 3. What the patent clusters imply

### 3.1 The moat is moving away from the energy source

Expired foundational light and RF patents make basic energy delivery easier to copy. Newer defensible claims increasingly cluster around **contact detection, skin classification, safety interlocks, combination geometry, consumable compositions, and software-guided protocols**. That is a market inference from the cited families, not a patent-count study.

### 3.2 Combination devices need a “weakest modality” audit

A six-in-one patent proves integration, not adequate dose. Each mode needs its own disclosed output, treatment area, duty cycle, contact logic, contraindications, and evidence. A device that lists RF + LED + EMS + ultrasound can still be mostly a low-output warming/massage tool.

### 3.3 Delivery enhancement deserves more caution than the market gives it

Electroporation, iontophoresis, and sonophoresis are often marketed as benign “absorption boosters.” Their purpose is to alter transport across the barrier. That makes the paired formulation, molecular size, pH, current density, waveform, contact time, and irritation testing central—not optional accessories.

## 4. Devices and trends that deserve new archive lanes

Priority is based on user relevance × evidence availability × how poorly the current archive covers the question.

1. **Home acne devices (highest priority):** blue/red LED, heat, and combination microcurrent devices; map exact FDA indications, wavelengths, dose, acne severity, and photosensitizing-drug cautions.
2. **Microcurrent / EMS / neuromuscular facial devices:** separate transient stimulation/appearance claims from dermal-remodeling claims; trace predicate families and measure current/waveform disclosure.
3. **Enhanced-delivery devices:** electroporation, iontophoresis, sonophoresis, and the paired ampoule/serum business model; build a cargo-specific safety map.
4. **Skin diagnostics and closed-loop dosing:** distinguish photography/recommendation apps from devices that actually constrain emitted energy using measured skin state.
5. **Cold plasma:** monitor FDA product codes, trials, and device-specific acne/wound evidence before treating it as a consumer skincare category.
6. **Multimodal OEM platforms:** identify the actual manufacturer, cleared model, power budget, and whether every advertised mode exists in the cleared configuration.

## 5. Patent-to-product diligence checklist

For every “cutting edge” product, record:

- exact model and manufacturer—not only the storefront brand;
- shipping status and date observed;
- patent family, assignee, priority date, claims that plausibly map to the device, and current legal status;
- FDA K-number/De Novo/product code and the exact indication wording;
- wavelength, fluence/irradiance, pulse width, current density, RF frequency, ultrasound frequency, temperature, treatment area, and duty cycle as applicable;
- independent human evidence using the same device or a clearly identified predecessor;
- whether the “AI” controls energy, merely recommends a program, or only logs adherence;
- consumables, subscription lock-in, warranty, replacement head life, and privacy/data-retention terms.

## Evidence gaps

- This is a targeted blind-spot audit, not a patent-count landscape; filing volume and assignee rankings require a reproducible bulk export.
- FDA clearance does not establish comparative superiority, retail popularity, or that every white-label variant shares the clearance.
- Patent legal status must be confirmed in USPTO Patent Center or Espacenet before freedom-to-operate conclusions.
- Product popularity needs retailer sell-through, search-interest, review-velocity, return-rate, and revenue data—not roundup placement.
- No product-specific conclusion should be drawn for cold plasma from a wound-care patent alone.
