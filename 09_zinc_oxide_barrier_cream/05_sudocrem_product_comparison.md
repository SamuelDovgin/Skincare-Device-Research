## Bottom Line

Sudocrem is a useful product comparison because it is **15.29% zinc oxide**, which puts it near the first-pass facial experiment range. It is not a cleanest-possible face pick, though, because the U.S. label includes several ingredients that add allergy or irritation variables.

My read:

> Sudocrem is a credible mid-strength zinc oxide skin protectant, but I would treat it as a second-tier facial experiment behind Triple Paste Multi-Purpose 12.8% or Pipette 14%.

This is a classic protective cream logic: mineral protectant plus waxy/occlusive base. For the face, the issue is not the zinc concentration. It is the extra sensitization surface.

## When Sudocrem Makes Sense

| Scenario | Sudocrem fit |
|---|---|
| You already own it and tolerate it | Reasonable to patch test on a small irritated facial area |
| You want a classic mid-strength zinc cream | Reasonable, especially for non-face chafing or body irritation |
| You are sensitive to lanolin/fragrance | Skip |
| You are acne-prone or get closed comedones under occlusives | Use caution; start with a tiny patch |
| You want the cleanest possible facial experiment | Choose Triple Paste Multi-Purpose or Pipette first |

## Final Ranking

For facial barrier experimentation:

1. **Triple Paste Multi-Purpose Rash Ointment with Sheer Zinc, 12.8%** - best first pick.
2. **Pipette Diaper Rash Cream, 14%** - best if avoiding petrolatum/lanolin.
3. **Sudocrem Skin Protectant Cream, 15.29%** - useful comparator; good zinc level but less clean ingredient story.
4. **Desitin Maximum Strength, 40%** - small-patch rescue, not a default face product.
