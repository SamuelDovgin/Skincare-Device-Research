# 03 - Safety, Routine Fit, and Protocol

## High-Level Safety Read

Topical zinc oxide itself has a reassuring dermal-safety profile. The practical risks of using diaper rash cream on the face are usually not "zinc poisoning" or particle penetration. They are:

- clogged pores or acneiform bumps from occlusion,
- irritation from fragrance, botanicals, lanolin, balsam/resins, preservatives, or essential oils,
- worsening perioral dermatitis, rosacea, seborrheic dermatitis, or fungal-looking rashes,
- trapping irritating actives under an occlusive layer,
- using a barrier paste when the real issue is infection, allergy, or a diagnosis that needs treatment.

## Who This Fits Best

| Good candidate | Why |
|---|---|
| Retinoid dryness or stinging on recovery nights | Barrier is irritated; a thin protectant layer may reduce TEWL and friction |
| Around-nose irritation from wiping/blowing | Chafing/wetness/friction pattern matches zinc oxide's strengths |
| Mouth-corner/chin chapping from saliva or masks | Barrier-protectant logic is strong, provided it is not infected/angular cheilitis needing treatment |
| Windburn or cold-weather irritation | Physical shield plus occlusive base can help short term |
| Over-exfoliation recovery | A temporary bland barrier can reduce further irritation |

## Use Caution Or Avoid

| Situation | Why |
|---|---|
| Active acne flares or frequent closed comedones | Occlusive pastes can worsen bumps in some people |
| Perioral dermatitis | Heavy occlusion and rich products can aggravate it; get clinician guidance |
| Rosacea that flares with heat or occlusion | A thick paste may trap heat or trigger flushing |
| Seborrheic dermatitis or fungal-looking scale | Occlusion and rich bases can worsen some yeast-associated patterns |
| Known lanolin/fragrance/balsam allergy | Many diaper creams contain common contact-dermatitis variables |
| Yellow crusting, pus, spreading warmth, severe pain, blisters | Do not self-treat possible infection or severe dermatitis with diaper paste |
| Fresh post-device heat injury | Do not trap heat after IPL, laser, RF, microneedling, or burns unless directed by a clinician |

## Patch Test

Do this before putting it on irritated facial skin:

1. Apply a rice-grain amount behind the ear or along the jaw.
2. Leave it overnight.
3. Repeat for 2 nights.
4. Watch for itching, swelling, burning, hives, rash, pustules, or a delayed dermatitis flare.

If your skin is highly reactive or you have a history of allergic contact dermatitis, patch testing on the inner arm first is safer than starting on the face.

## Basic Facial Protocol

Use this like a rescue layer, not a moisturizer replacement.

1. Cleanse gently.
2. Pat dry and wait until skin is calm.
3. Apply a bland moisturizer first.
4. Apply a very thin zinc oxide layer over irritated areas.
5. Use 1-3 nights/week, or 2-5 consecutive rescue nights after irritation.
6. Remove gently in the morning with cleanser, oil cleanser, or micellar water. Do not scrub.

For the whole face, start around a pea-sized amount total. For patches, use less than you think. The goal is a thin protective film, not a thick white mask.

## With Differin / Adapalene

The conservative approach is to separate zinc oxide paste from retinoid nights at first.

| Night type | Routine |
|---|---|
| Differin night | Cleanse -> dry fully -> moisturizer buffer if needed -> Differin/adapalene -> moisturizer |
| Zinc recovery night | Cleanse -> moisturizer -> thin zinc oxide layer on irritated areas |

Why separate them:

- occlusion can increase penetration/irritation from some actives,
- zinc pastes can make it harder to judge whether Differin is irritating you,
- the point of zinc nights is recovery, not intensifying a retinoid.

Once skin is stable, tiny spot use over moisturizer may be reasonable, but do not start by sealing adapalene under diaper paste.

## With Acids, Benzoyl Peroxide, or Vitamin C

Do not turn zinc paste into a seal over an irritating active routine.

| Active | Zinc oxide fit |
|---|---|
| AHAs/BHAs | Use zinc on off nights if over-exfoliated; do not occlude acids at first |
| Benzoyl peroxide | Avoid sealing large areas; dryness and irritation can intensify |
| L-ascorbic acid vitamin C | Usually AM active; zinc paste at night is separate |
| Azelaic acid | Can be barrier-friendly for some, irritating for others; separate at first if inflamed |
| Hydroquinone / strong pigment actives | Keep simple and clinician-led if irritation or PIH risk is high |

## With Devices

This repository includes IPL, lasers, RF, red/NIR light, and related device research. Zinc oxide paste has a role only after the skin is calm.

Do not apply thick occlusive paste:

- immediately after IPL/laser/RF while skin feels hot,
- over burns, blisters, open wounds, or swelling,
- before using a device, because residue can interfere with contact, optics, heating, or hygiene,
- after microneedling or ablative procedures unless aftercare explicitly says to use a barrier ointment.

For non-thermal LED/red light, zinc paste is still not ideal before treatment because it can block light and make dosing inconsistent. Use it after cleansing, when the light session is done.

## Stop Rules

Stop using it and reassess if you get:

- new clusters of closed comedones,
- pustules or acne flare,
- persistent burning,
- itchy rash,
- swelling,
- hives,
- worsening redness after 2-3 uses,
- flaking that becomes more inflamed,
- yellow crusting or drainage,
- rash around mouth/nose/eyes that keeps recurring.

If symptoms last more than a week, recur repeatedly, or look infected, stop treating it like a skincare trend and get medical input.

## Formula Risk Checklist

For facial use, prefer:

- fragrance-free,
- essential-oil-free,
- talc-free,
- low botanical load,
- no balsam tolu/peru-style resin,
- no lanolin if you have sensitive/allergy-prone skin,
- cream/ointment, not loose powder or spray.

Ingredients to flag:

| Ingredient/category | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Fragrance/parfum | Common irritant/allergen category |
| Essential oils | More sensitization variables than needed |
| Lanolin | Useful emollient but allergy-prone on damaged skin |
| Balsam/resin botanicals | Contact-allergy risk, especially around mouth/nose |
| Cod liver oil | Classic in some diaper pastes; odor/allergen/texture downside for face |
| Talc | Not useful for a face experiment and can make removal messier |
| Zinc oxide powder | Inhalation and uneven dosing concerns; not an OTC finished product |

## When Plain Petrolatum May Be Better

If the issue is dryness without inflammation, start simpler:

- plain petrolatum,
- CeraVe Healing Ointment,
- Aquaphor Healing Ointment if lanolin does not bother you,
- a bland ceramide cream.

Zinc oxide adds a mineral barrier and mild drying/astringent behavior. That is helpful for chafed or wet irritation, but not always for dry tightness.

## Minimal Experiment Design

If you want a clean personal test:

1. Pick one formula.
2. Patch test.
3. Use only on one irritated area for 3 nights.
4. Keep the rest of the routine unchanged.
5. Track stinging, redness, flakes, bumps, and morning comfort.
6. Stop if acne/itching/burning increases.

This keeps the experiment interpretable. Changing moisturizer, retinoid frequency, exfoliation, cleanser, and zinc paste all at once makes the result impossible to read.
