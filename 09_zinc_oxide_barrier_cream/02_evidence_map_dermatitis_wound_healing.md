# 02 - Evidence Map: Dermatitis, Barrier, Wound Healing

## How To Read This Evidence

There is not a clean trial showing "adult facial diaper cream overnight improves retinoid irritation, wrinkles, acne, pigment, and barrier function." The evidence is indirect. It comes from four buckets:

1. OTC regulatory recognition of zinc oxide as a skin protectant.
2. Diaper dermatitis and infant barrier-function studies.
3. Wound and periwound topical zinc literature.
4. Dermal penetration/safety studies from zinc oxide sunscreen contexts.

The best conclusion is conservative: zinc oxide cream is evidence-plausible for **short-term barrier protection and irritation rescue**. It is not a proven cosmetic treatment for the usual facial endpoints.

## Study Notes

### FDA OTC Monograph M016

The monograph is the cleanest anchor for concentration ranges. It recognizes:

- zinc oxide 1-25% as a skin protectant,
- zinc oxide above 25-40% in an ointment dosage form.

This supports a formulation range. It does not prove that 40% is "stronger and therefore better" on the face.

Source: [FDA OTC Monograph M016](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/omuf/monographs/OTCMonograph_M016SkinProtectantDrugProductsforOTCHumanUse09242021.pdf)

### Baldwin/Odio/Haines/Lane Zinc Oxide/Petrolatum Diaper Study

This is one of the better direct zinc oxide/petrolatum data points. The abstract describes three independent blinded randomized trials:

- **Study A:** transfer of zinc oxide/petrolatum formulation from diaper to skin was confirmed.
- **Study B:** in an adult sodium lauryl sulfate irritant model, zinc oxide-containing formulations produced the greatest reductions in barrier damage and erythema.
- **Study C:** in 268 infants over 4 weeks, the zinc oxide/petrolatum diaper reduced erythema and diaper rash versus control.

Interpretation for this repo: good support for zinc oxide/petrolatum as an irritant-barrier system. Still not direct proof for adult facial nightly use.

Source: [PubMed PMID 11720074](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11720074/)

### Garcia Bartels Infant Barrier-Function RCT

This prospective RCT randomized 89 healthy infants to three diaper-care approaches:

- water-moistened washcloth,
- diaper cream twice daily,
- wet wipes plus diaper cream twice daily.

Measured outcomes included TEWL, stratum corneum hydration, pH, IL-1 alpha, microbiology, and diaper dermatitis occurrence. Key takeaways:

- diapered skin differed from non-diapered skin in barrier markers,
- diaper cream groups had lower hydration and higher pH in the diapered area,
- TEWL was reduced in the diaper-cream-only group,
- diaper dermatitis incidence and course were comparable across groups.

Interpretation: diaper cream can alter measurable barrier parameters, but it is not a guaranteed dermatitis-prevention magic shield.

Source: [PubMed PMID 25424209](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25424209/)

### Petrolatum Barrier-Recovery Study

The petrolatum study matters because many zinc oxide creams are petrolatum-heavy. It found petrolatum accelerated, rather than delayed, barrier recovery after acetone-induced barrier perturbation and was present in stratum corneum interstices.

Interpretation: a zinc oxide paste's base is not a passive delivery vehicle. It may be a major part of the barrier-repair effect.

Source: [PubMed PMID 1564142](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1564142/)

### Pilonidal Wound RCT

This trial compared topical zinc oxide with placebo mesh on secondary healing after pilonidal disease excision. It is useful because it is randomized, double-blind, and placebo-controlled.

Key reported results:

- median healing time: 54 days zinc oxide vs 62 days placebo,
- healing-time difference was not statistically significant,
- zinc oxide increased wound-fluid zinc,
- *S. aureus* presence decreased,
- fewer zinc-group patients needed postoperative antibiotics.

Interpretation: zinc oxide may improve some wound-environment markers without necessarily accelerating closure in every model. This should make us cautious about "heals skin faster" claims.

Source: [PubMed PMID 17014663](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17014663/)

### 2021 Human Wound-Model Trial

This trial tested 1.1% zinc oxide in two standardized human wound models: lancet wounds and fractional CO2 laser wounds. It found:

- slight erythema reduction around lancet/incisional wounds,
- no matching erythema effect in laser-induced wounds,
- no accelerated complete wound closure.

Interpretation: controlled human data are mixed. Zinc oxide may have anti-inflammatory signal in some wound contexts, but it did not produce a broad wound-healing acceleration.

Source: [PubMed PMID 34003296](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34003296/)

### Wound Reviews and Meta-Analysis

The older wound-healing review describes zinc's roles in matrix metalloproteinases, keratinocyte migration, cytoprotection, antioxidant defense, infection control, and epithelialization. A newer 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis suggests zinc treatment may be associated with improved ulcer healing at final endpoint, with limitations and heterogeneous included trials.

Interpretation: zinc has a credible wound biology story. But wound papers mix oral/topical routes, zinc compounds, chronic ulcers, surgical wounds, and deficiency states. This is background support, not direct adult facial guidance.

Sources: [PubMed PMID 17244314](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17244314/), [PubMed PMID 40771531](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40771531/)

## What The Evidence Supports For This Project

Supported:

- Zinc oxide is a legitimate OTC skin protectant active.
- Petrolatum/dimethicone/ointment bases can reduce TEWL and protect damaged barrier.
- Zinc oxide/petrolatum systems can reduce irritant-model barrier damage and diaper-area erythema/rash.
- Topical zinc has credible wound/periwound biology and some supportive clinical signals.
- Dermal zinc oxide particle penetration appears low in human topical studies.

Not established:

- Nightly zinc oxide diaper cream improves adult facial aging, texture, acne, pigment, or rosacea.
- 40% is better than 10-15% for facial skin.
- Zinc oxide paste is better than plain petrolatum or a bland ceramide moisturizer for retinoid irritation.
- Barrier cream is appropriate for unexplained rashes, infections, perioral dermatitis, or active inflammatory flares.

## Practical Evidence Grade

| Use case | Evidence grade for zinc oxide cream |
|---|---|
| Diaper rash / moisture-associated irritant dermatitis | Moderate support |
| Chafing/friction/wetness barrier | Moderate support |
| Retinoid irritation rescue on face | Plausible extrapolation |
| Post-device recovery after heat/light injury | Use caution; do not occlude hot, burned, swollen, or broken skin without clinician guidance |
| Acne treatment | Weak for zinc oxide diaper paste |
| Rosacea treatment | Weak; occlusion may worsen some people |
| Anti-aging | Weak direct evidence |
| Hyperpigmentation/PIH | Indirect only: reducing irritation may reduce PIH triggers, but it is not a pigment active |

## Repository Recommendation From The Evidence

Use zinc oxide cream as a **limited barrier experiment**:

- choose 10-15% zinc oxide first,
- choose fragrance-free and low-allergen formulas,
- apply thinly over moisturizer,
- separate from retinoid nights at first,
- use for 1-5 rescue nights or 1-3 nights/week,
- stop if it worsens bumps, burning, itching, or redness.

That recommendation is boring in exactly the right way: it respects the evidence without pretending the internet trend has been proven.
