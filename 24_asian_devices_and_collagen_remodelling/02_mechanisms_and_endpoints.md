# Understand the technologies before comparing devices

*October 2, 2026. Mechanism is not a guaranteed outcome. Illustrative tissue descriptions below are not instructions for setting energy or needle depth.*

## Four different routes to a similar-looking result

Think of the dermis as a living scaffold. Some technologies create controlled injury and repair; some provide noninjury stimulation; some primarily affect muscle, barrier delivery or superficial appearance. A device may combine routes. Its combined before/after result does not identify which component did the work.

| Route | Examples | Main intended process | Time and endpoint to distinguish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Controlled injury/repair | Needling, fractional RF, clinical thermal ultrasound | Wound-healing and matrix turnover | Changes over weeks/months; tissue and clinical follow-up |
| Thermal stimulation | Surface RF | Tissue heating with matrix-response pathways | Immediate appearance versus delayed, durable improvement |
| Photobiomodulation | Red/NIR light | Light-dependent signaling; a full mechanism is more complex than an ATP slogan | Repeated use and delayed wrinkle outcomes |
| Adjacent cosmetic functions | EMS, ion delivery, electroporation, cooling, steam, rollers | Muscle stimulation, transport, hydration, cleansing or sensory effects | Often quick changes; need separate proof of long-term collagen |

**Finding:** categories overlap in appearance, not in dose or evidence. S02–S07, S13–S20 support this distinction.

## Radiofrequency: the thermal collagen lane

RF drives alternating electrical fields/current through tissue, generating heat according to the configuration. **Monopolar, bipolar and multipolar** describe current/electrode arrangements; they are not simple strength ratings. Heat distribution depends on electrode spacing, return path, tissue properties, power, contact, movement and time. A professional monopolar treatment cannot be reconstructed by buying a home device with a nearby carrier frequency. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21315951/)[[14]](https://www.ya-man.com/en/products/bloom-6.php)[[24]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)

A device's surface thermistor can provide useful feedback. It is not a measurement of every point in the dermis, nor a guarantee that there are no local hot spots. Peak degrees Celsius alone omit duration and location. Do not turn a professional collagen-denaturation temperature into a target for a home device. [[24]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K232424.pdf)[[25]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf25/K250341.pdf)

The engineering questions worth asking are: is contact monitored, is output stopped when contact is lost, is temperature controlled, what happens if movement stops, and are power/geometry and the compatible medium documented? The [RF specification archive](../07_radio_frequency_skin_tightening/index.html#doc15) discusses those questions in much more detail.

## LED and near-infrared: stimulation without a thermal-injury goal

Red/NIR skin studies support wrinkle improvement and, in selected research settings, tissue changes. But “red light stimulates collagen” is a category claim. To translate it into a device decision, you need wavelength, irradiance **at the skin**, illuminated area, exposure, fit/distance and clinical validation. Wattage at the plug and LED count do not determine the useful dose. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17566756/)[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3926176/)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39960921/)

An LED ring on a moving RF wand has a different coverage history from a stationary mask or panel. Matching the color is insufficient. More colors do not necessarily increase remodelling; blue-light acne use is a different endpoint. NIR being invisible does not establish its output or safety. A larger exposure is not automatically more effective. The [LED archive](../04_red_light_therapy_handheld/index.html) contains dose limitations; this guide does not invent a universal optimum.

**Evidence finding:** the sham-controlled LED studies matter because they test people, rather than extrapolating solely from fibroblast cultures. They still do not validate every consumer mask. S04–S06.

## Conventional microneedling: mechanical repair

Professional microneedling creates controlled punctures intended to trigger healing. Human biopsy evidence supports matrix changes. The important distinction is penetration and a medically managed procedure, rather than a roller that only affects the outer surface. Hygiene, anatomical site, skin condition and healing response matter. A study's needle setting is not a prescription for home use. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26096653/)

A needle-assisted delivery protocol can also change topical exposure. That is a separate problem from mechanical collagen induction, with separate compatibility and safety questions. See [microneedling evidence and provider assessment](../10_microneedling_collagen_induction/index.html).

## RF microneedling: focal heat added to injury

Needle electrodes place energy into or beneath skin. Insulated/noninsulated tips, pulse timing, needle arrangement and depth matter; devices vary considerably. Stronger stimulation can mean more tissue injury, not a better risk-adjusted cosmetic result. Positive trials do not erase adverse-event reports. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34287975/)[[10]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication)

The FDA warning is specific and relevant: reported complications include unwanted fat loss and nerve injury as well as burns and scars. It does not establish the frequency of those outcomes or mean all surface home RF has the same risk profile. [[10]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/safety-communications/potential-risks-certain-uses-radiofrequency-rf-microneedling-fda-safety-communication)

## Ultrasound: three names that should not be merged

1. **Cleansing/sonophoresis ultrasound:** used for cleaning or delivery. A vibration/ultrasound feature does not establish collagen coagulation.
2. **Medical dermal ultrasound:** systems such as Sofwave use a particular treatment geometry with professional indications.
3. **Focused therapeutic ultrasound/MFU:** uses focal thermal zones; some systems visualize tissue to help target and couple treatment.

Depth labels such as 1.5, 3 or 4.5 mm are device/anatomy-specific intended targets, not a ranking of benefit. Deeper is not better for thin skin. Korean focused-ultrasound data support a clinical lane; they do not establish that an unverified home “HIFU” wand recreates it. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21806707/)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27047630/)[[20]](https://www.lg.com/hk_en/about-lg/press-and-media/lg-pra-l-2024/)[[26]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf21/K211483.pdf)

## EMS, microcurrent and proprietary pulse names

EMS causes muscle stimulation/contraction. Microcurrent generally refers to low-amplitude currents, but retail naming is inconsistent: some pages label a contraction-producing mode “microcurrent (EMS).” Interpret the disclosed waveform and endpoint, not the label alone. [[18]](https://www.refa.net/en/item/refa_carat_lift/)[[22]](https://amirobeauty.com/en-gb/products/amiro-high-radiofrequency-skincare-device)

Muscle stimulation can be relevant to contour appearance. A contraction is not proof of fibroblast synthesis. Claims about ATP, circulation or a tighter look need actual human evidence to become a remodelling recommendation. Treat proprietary names such as MFIP, PI, DWHP and RF-Wpulse as descriptions to unpack. Determine which outputs exist in that exact model; do not assume an unfamiliar acronym equals RF. [[17]](https://artistic.co.jp/zeus4-news/)[[19]](https://refa.com.sg/pages/re-aa02a)

## Ingredient delivery and “glass skin”

Iontophoresis and electroporation can affect transport under particular conditions. An uptake ratio for vitamin C, glycerin or a test molecule is not a multiplier of collagen production or clinical benefit. “Into the stratum corneum” means the outer barrier, not delivery to the dermal fibroblast layer. Hydration can visibly plump fine lines without rebuilding the scaffold. [[13]](https://panasonic.jp/face/products/EH-SR90/feature.html)[[16]](https://medicube.us/products/age-r-booster-pro)

The [penetration-device archive](../23_skincare_penetration_technologies/index.html#research) assesses assays and formula-specific translation. Increasing prescription-retinoid exposure is not the default recommendation.

## What counts as convincing remodelling evidence?

| Measurement | What it helps show | Main interpretive trap |
|---|---|---|
| Human dermal biopsy | Local matrix composition/structure | Tiny sampled areas, small cohorts; biological significance versus visible value |
| Ultrasound density/thickness | A noninvasive tissue proxy | Water, inflammation and processing can affect measurements |
| Blinded standardized wrinkle scores | Visible clinical change | Timing, lighting, expression and baseline severity |
| Elasticity/cutometry | Mechanical response | Hydration and assay conditions; not collagen-specific |
| Immediate lift/photo | Aesthetic appearance at that moment | Swelling, muscle position, massage, hydration or photography |
| Uptake assay or cell culture | Mechanism/transport plausibility | Cannot directly predict finished-product clinical benefit |

Sources S01–S12 establish why these endpoints should remain separate. Ask what was measured, where, against what comparator, and how long after the last treatment.

## Open questions

Many public product pages omit tissue-level dose, complete assays, absolute effect size or long-term follow-up. A bundle can work without each add-on adding value. The optimal number of modes and best carrier frequency remain unestablished in a common comparative protocol.
