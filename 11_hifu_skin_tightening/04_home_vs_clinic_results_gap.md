# Home vs Clinic Results Gap

## 1. The short answer

Home HIFU/MFU is **not** to clinic HIFU what Tria FRX is to Clear + Brilliant.

The fractional-laser analogy works because both sides can often be compared as fractional optical injury with lower density, lower energy, smaller coverage, slower cumulative dosing, and less downtime. HIFU's key clinic advantages are not just "more energy" or "more shots." They are target-layer verification, acoustic coupling, focal geometry, line spacing, operator anatomy, and complication management.

## 2. Comparison with the other device lanes

| Device class | Home-vs-clinic analogy | Why it works or fails |
|--------------|------------------------|-----------------------|
| Fractional lasers | Strongest "lower-density clinic analog" case | Same optical microbeam concept can be compared by wavelength, spot size, density, coverage, and energy |
| Home RF | Clinically real but not Thermage/RF-microneedling equivalent | Broad heating can be lowered for home use, but depth/temp/control gap stays large |
| Microneedling | Superficial cosmetic tools are not SkinPen/Exceed | Mechanism overlaps, but sterile cartridges, depth control, indication, and provider protocol define results |
| HIFU/MFU | Weakest home-clinic analogy | Focal depth, coupling, anatomy, and verification are central; shots do not add up like laser density |

## 3. Clinic-side advantages

### Ulthera / Ultherapy

Clinic Ulthera has a stack that home devices do not currently reproduce in this archive:

- prescription-device classification and trained-user labeling [1,2];
- DeepSEE imaging to visualize tissue up to 8 mm below the skin [2,3];
- transducers at 1.5/3.0/4.5 mm depths [2];
- treatment lines with defined length and TCP spacing [2];
- ability to plan dermal versus SMAS/fibrous layer treatments in SPT mode [2];
- explicit warnings about thyroid, bone, coupling, line spacing, gel pockets, burns/scarring, and nerve effects [2].

That stack makes Ulthera a procedure, not a gadget.

### Sofwave

Sofwave's clinic advantage is different. FDA summaries describe a professional ultrasound system with cooling, treatment depth around 1-2 mm, and clinical evidence for facial wrinkles plus eyebrow/submental/neck lifting indications [4,5]. It is a clinic system with FDA-reviewed performance data, not a user-guided home shot counter.

## 4. Home-side limitations

The strongest home page captured here, Medicube High Focus Shot, publishes useful caution language: 600-1200 shots, weekly or 1-2 times weekly recommendations, avoid repeated/overlapping shots, use full contact, and avoid eye/jawbone/Adam's apple/thyroid-type areas [6]. That is good as consumer safety language.

It is not the same as showing:

- real focal depth in living facial tissue;
- target-layer confirmation;
- equivalence to 1.5/3.0/4.5 mm Ulthera transducers;
- matching TCP geometry;
- clinical outcome trials against baseline photos with adverse-event tracking;
- FDA-cleared OTC indication.

## 5. Expected result gap by goal

| Goal | Clinic expectation | Home HIFU/MFU expectation | Archive recommendation |
|------|--------------------|---------------------------|------------------------|
| Brow lift | Subtle but plausible with Ulthera/Sofwave provider treatment | Not established | Use clinic consultation if this is the goal |
| Submental/neck laxity | Plausible for mild/moderate laxity; provider-dependent | Not established | Clinic lane, not DIY |
| Fine lines / superficial texture | Sofwave or other clinic options may fit | Mild cosmetic maintenance possible but unproven | Consider topical/LED/RF/fractional alternatives first |
| Facial fat reduction | Should be treated as a risk, not a home goal | Avoid | Do not chase slimming with facial HIFU |
| Acne scars | Sofwave has acne-scar-related FDA file, but this is not home HIFU territory | Not established | Use microneedling/fractional/acne-scar evidence map instead |
| Maintenance after clinic procedure | Possible adjunct if provider approves | Plausible but unproven | Conservative only; avoid overlap and risk zones |

## 6. Why "more sessions" does not solve it

For fractional lasers, repeated low-density passes can sometimes be a sensible cumulative-dose model. For HIFU, repeating shots without knowing focal depth and line placement can multiply the wrong risk:

- repeated shallow heating can irritate skin without lifting;
- repeated focused injury over facial fat can risk depression or volume change;
- repeated shots near nerves or bony contours can raise complication risk;
- repeated coupling errors can turn "maintenance" into burns.

Medicube's own page warns that repeated use on the same area can cause skin depression or burns [6]. That warning is exactly why this folder does not frame home HIFU as a simply slower Ultherapy.

## 7. Decision rule

If the goal is a true lift, submental/neck tightening, or deeper laxity change, treat HIFU/MFU as a **clinic decision**.

If the goal is at-home skin quality, this archive would usually rank better-supported or lower-anatomy-risk lanes first:

- pigment/redness: tinted SPF, topicals, LED;
- collagen/texture: fractional laser or professional microneedling depending goal;
- mild home tightening/wrinkles: home RF with realistic expectations;
- barrier/recovery: topical support.

Home HIFU/MFU can sit as an experimental conservative maintenance tool, but it should not be the main plan for clinic-grade lifting.
