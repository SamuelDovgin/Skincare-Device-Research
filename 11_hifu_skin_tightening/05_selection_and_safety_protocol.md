# Selection and Safety Protocol

This is a research decision guide, not medical advice. HIFU/MFU is an anatomy-sensitive procedure category. If the goal is true lift or laxity correction, this archive treats it as a provider conversation, not a DIY project.

## 1. Pick by goal

| Goal | Best first lane | Why |
|------|-----------------|-----|
| Brow lift / lower-face lift / submental-neck laxity | Clinic Ulthera or Sofwave consultation | FDA-cleared clinic systems exist; results are subtle and provider-dependent [1,2] |
| Deeper SMAS/fibrous-layer targeting | Ulthera/Ultherapy consultation | Ulthera has 3.0/4.5 mm transducers and DeepSEE visualization in the captured IFU [3] |
| Shallower wrinkles or mild laxity | Sofwave or other clinic options | Sofwave's captured summaries describe 1-2 mm treatment depth, cooling, and human clinical data [2,4] |
| Waist/body fat disruption | Body-contouring HIFU lane only | SCIZER/LipoSonix-type devices are not facial lifting analogs [5] |
| At-home maintenance | Conservative home RF/LED/topicals first; home HIFU only experimental | Home HIFU/MFU clinic-equivalence evidence was not found in this pass [6,7] |

## 2. Provider questions for clinic HIFU/MFU

Bring these to a consultation:

1. Which exact device and model are you using?
2. What is the FDA-cleared indication you are treating under?
3. For Ulthera: which transducer depths will be used, and where?
4. Will imaging/visualization be used to confirm coupling and treatment depth?
5. How do you avoid thyroid, trachea, major vessels, orbital zones, bone, and nerve-sensitive areas?
6. What is the line count or treatment-map plan by region?
7. How do you adjust for lean faces, existing volume loss, fillers, implants, dental hardware, or prior procedures?
8. What adverse events do you see most often, and what is the plan for burns, numbness, asymmetry, pain, or suspected fat loss?
9. What photo documentation and follow-up timing will be used?
10. What result would you call realistic for my anatomy, and what would require surgery instead?

## 3. Red flags

- The provider sells HIFU as a facelift replacement.
- The device name is emphasized but the exact FDA indication/model is vague.
- The provider cannot explain treatment depths or anatomy exclusions.
- Everyone gets the same number of shots in the same map.
- Pain is framed as proof of effectiveness.
- The clinic offers aggressive facial slimming with HIFU without discussing volume-loss risk.
- The device is a salon/prosumer HIFU system with unclear clearance and no medical oversight.

## 4. When to be extra cautious or skip

Use extra caution and ask a qualified clinician before HIFU/MFU if any of these apply:

- active wounds, lesions, severe/cystic acne, or active infection in the area;
- active implants, electrical/mechanical devices, or implant-related uncertainty in the treatment field;
- pregnancy, anticoagulants, neuropathy, or impaired healing;
- recent fillers, threads, surgery, laser/RF/microneedling, or other procedures;
- very lean face, visible volume loss, or strong concern about fat loss;
- history of keloids, abnormal scarring, pigment complications, or nerve symptoms.

Ulthera's IFU includes formal contraindications/precautions and trained-user warnings; defer to device labeling and clinician judgment over this simplified checklist [3].

## 5. If experimenting with a home HIFU/MFU device

This archive does not endorse DIY clinic-grade HIFU. If someone experiments anyway, the conservative boundary is:

- do not use around eyes/eyeballs, between eyebrows, lips, philtrum, around the mouth, nose, jawbone, Adam's apple, or thyroid area; these are also named on Medicube's High Focus Shot page [6];
- do not overlap shots or repeatedly treat the same spot;
- use the required conductive gel/product and maintain full contact;
- stop for severe pain, burning, numbness, blistering, swelling that seems abnormal, asymmetry, muscle weakness, or any visual/neurologic symptom;
- do not chase heat or facial slimming;
- do not use a salon/pro cartridge HIFU machine on yourself;
- do not combine with recent procedures unless a clinician clears it;
- photograph baseline and wait long enough to judge before repeating.

The key rule: **a home device that is too weak may disappoint; a device strong enough to mimic clinic HIFU without verification may injure.**

## 6. Where home HIFU fits in this archive

| Evidence tier | Device lane | Archive action |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| Stronger home evidence | Some home RF wrinkle devices, home fractional laser analogs | Consider with realistic expectations and device-specific docs |
| Plausible but under-proven | Home HIFU/MFU such as Medicube High Focus Shot | Conservative maintenance only; do not expect Ultherapy |
| Procedure-only | Ulthera, Sofwave, RF microneedling, medical microneedling, body-contouring HIFU | Provider/clinic decision |
| Avoid for DIY | Facial fat reduction, near-eye/thyroid/jawbone HIFU, prosumer cartridge HIFU | Risk outweighs evidence |

## 7. Follow-up tracking

If pursuing clinic HIFU/MFU, track:

- standardized photos at baseline, 3 months, and 6 months;
- pain score and immediate reactions;
- numbness, tingling, weakness, asymmetry, burns, blistering, nodules, prolonged swelling, or contour changes;
- weight changes and other procedures, so results are not misattributed;
- whether any improvement is visible in blind side-by-side photos, not just in mirror memory.
