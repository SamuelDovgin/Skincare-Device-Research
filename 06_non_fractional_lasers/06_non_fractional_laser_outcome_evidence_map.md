# Non-fractional laser outcome evidence map

*Rapid evidence map updated 2026-08-23. It separates device-specific human outcomes from FDA equivalence, bench testing, and professional analogs. It is not medical advice or a dosing guide.*

## Bottom line

The topic already compares NIRA, DermRays Revive, professional 1064 nm systems, dose geometry, and patents. The crucial missing synthesis was **which outcome claim has direct human evidence for the exact consumer device**.

| Device / claim | Direct subject-device human outcome evidence in the public file? | Best defensible conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| **Original NIRA Beauty Skin Laser, 1450 nm, periorbital wrinkles** | **Yes:** 76-subject open-label comparison to baseline | A device-specific wrinkle signal exists, but no sham/untreated control isolates effect size [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf16/K163137.pdf) |
| **NIRA Model 2, 1450 nm, periorbital wrinkles** | **No separate clinical trial:** FDA summary bridges to original NIRA using similar fluence/intracutaneous temperature | Regulatory equivalence supports the indication; clinical effect is inherited, not independently replicated [[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K222685.pdf) |
| **DermRays Revive, 1064 nm, dermatologic procedures** | **No:** public 510(k) says no clinical testing was performed | Clearance and high output establish a medical-device lane, not wrinkle-rejuvenation efficacy [[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K231910.pdf) |
| **Professional 1064 nm rejuvenation** | Human studies exist for other laser architectures/protocols | Mechanism/indication context only; not transferable to DermRays home use |

## 1. NIRA: what the direct study shows

FDA K163137 describes a 76-subject, open-label, baseline-controlled safety/effectiveness study for periorbital wrinkles. The prespecified endpoint was met with a median one-unit reduction on the Fitzpatrick Wrinkle Scale; 69% had at least a one-score improvement on the overall facial-wrinkle assessment. Among users who achieved a full one-unit reduction, 68% maintained some improvement for at least three months after stopping. Reported events included warmth/stinging, dryness, and temporary darkening; no unanticipated or severe events were reported. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf16/K163137.pdf)

### Evidence grade: **moderate directness, low causal certainty**

Why it matters:

- exact consumer device, wavelength, home users, labeled periorbital endpoint;
- validated wrinkle scale and persistence observation;
- actual denominator and adverse-event summary.

Why confidence stops short:

- no sham or untreated split-face comparator;
- open-label use and baseline photography are vulnerable to lighting, regression to the mean, skincare changes, and expectation;
- subgroup outcomes by skin type are not sufficiently detailed in the public summary;
- responder-maintenance percentage conditions on response and should not be read as 68% of all enrolled participants.

## 2. NIRA Model 2: an evidence bridge, not a replication

K222685 describes Model 2 as a 1450 ± 20 nm OTC non-fractional diode laser with 2.1–3.8 J/cm² fluence and a 2.0–3.1 second pulse train. FDA’s summary says no separate Model 2 clinical trial was performed because thermal modeling/testing showed intracutaneous temperature elevation similar to the predicate original NIRA. [[2]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf22/K222685.pdf)

That is meaningful regulatory evidence for substantial equivalence. It does **not** answer whether the larger spot, longer pulse train, workflow, adherence, or newer marketed variants produce the same real-world response distribution. Any current product claim should name the cleared model and avoid presenting the 2016 study as a direct Model 2 randomized trial.

## 3. DermRays Revive: output is not an outcome

The public K231910 file describes DermRays Revive (LHR-S5-1064), a prescription 1064 nm device, and explicitly states: **“No clinical testing has been performed.”** Its conclusion rests on bench/external-laboratory testing and substantial equivalence. [[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K231910.pdf)

The archive’s comparison dataset reports a 1.766 cm² spot, 5–10 J/cm² fluence, roughly 400 ms pulse, and about 8.83–17.66 J per pulse—dramatically different geometry and pulse energy from NIRA. Those numbers explain why the devices should not be collapsed into “non-fractional laser,” but they still do not demonstrate a wrinkle effect.

### Decision boundary

- **Regulatory conclusion:** the device may be marketed for its exact cleared prescription indication.
- **Clinical conclusion:** the public file does not provide a DermRays subject-device wrinkle trial.
- **Consumer conclusion:** professional-like output increases the importance of exact labeling, training/oversight, eye protection, skin-type selection, and adverse-event monitoring; it is not a reason to improvise facial rejuvenation.

## 4. Why professional 1064 nm studies are indirect

A prospective randomized split-face study of long-pulsed 1064 nm Nd:YAG in 20 Korean participants used 20–24 J/cm², 12 ms pulses, a 12 mm spot, three passes, and three monthly sessions. It reported a 45.1% reduction in average wrinkle grade on treated sides plus elasticity/histology changes. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25515708/)

That study supports a clinical signal for **that professional protocol**. It does not validate DermRays because:

- Nd:YAG and diode architectures are not equivalent merely because both emit 1064 nm;
- 12 ms at 20–24 J/cm² and ~400 ms at 5–10 J/cm² create different peak power and heat flow;
- three clinic passes with operator endpoints are not a home-use cadence;
- skin type, cooling, spot overlap, and treatment area differ.

Similarly, newer fractional picosecond 1064 nm trials belong to a fractional/high-peak-power lane and should not be used as evidence for a long-pulse non-fractional handheld.

## 5. Evidence-weighted decision aid

| Question | Answer supported by this map |
|---|---|
| Which device has the strongest direct home wrinkle evidence here? | **Original NIRA**, but from one uncontrolled device-specific study |
| Does Model 2 have its own replicated clinical trial in K222685? | **No**; FDA relies on substantial equivalence and similar thermal effect |
| Does DermRays’ FDA file prove wrinkle reduction? | **No**; no subject-device clinical testing is reported |
| Can professional 1064 nm results supply a DermRays protocol? | **No**; they are indirect and physically non-equivalent |
| Does higher joules/pulse mean better rejuvenation? | **Not established**; output and outcome are different evidence domains |

## Evidence gaps

- No sham-controlled published trial for original NIRA was located.
- No separate public subject-device clinical trial for NIRA Model 2 or DermRays Revive is in the reviewed 510(k) files.
- No randomized head-to-head compares NIRA and DermRays on the same outcome, population, and follow-up.
- The public record is thin on skin-type-stratified benefit and pigment risk.
- Long-term durability beyond the reported NIRA observation window is uncertain.
- Current product-family names and later variants should not inherit old evidence without an exact regulatory/model bridge.
