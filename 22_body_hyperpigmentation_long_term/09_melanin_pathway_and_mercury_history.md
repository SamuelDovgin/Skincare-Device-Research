# Melanin-pathway visual map and the mercury history

*Compiled 2026-09-04. The pathway is a teaching model: human skin is a feedback system, not a linear factory. Labels such as “direct inhibitor” identify a proposed molecular target; they do not prove that combining products will add their effects.*

## Bottom line

Pigment becomes visible through several linked steps: a stimulus activates keratinocyte and melanocyte signaling; MITF turns on tyrosinase-family enzymes; tyrosinase makes dopaquinone; melanosomes mature and transfer to keratinocytes; pigment persists until those cells move out or dermal melanophages clear it. Effective treatment can therefore target prevention, upstream signals, enzyme activity, precursor chemistry, transfer, or turnover. The strongest practical plan covers more than one category while keeping the barrier intact.

Mercury creams are shown here only as a **historical/regulatory red branch**. Mercury salts can inhibit tyrosinase and bind protein sulfhydryls, but the visible lightening also reflects cellular injury, inflammation, and sometimes permanent melanocyte loss. Kidney, neurologic, gastrointestinal, developmental, household-contamination, and environmental harms make this an unacceptable cosmetic pathway. WHO and FDA warn against mercury-containing lighteners; the Minamata Convention limit for cosmetics is 1 ppm. [[1]](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-CED-PHE-EPE-19.13)[[2]](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/skin-facts-what-you-need-know-about-skin-lightening-products)

## The pathway in plain language

1. **Stimulus:** UVB, UVA/visible light, friction, shaving/ingrowns, acne/eczema, heat, hormones, and some medicines activate keratinocytes and/or melanocytes. UVB DNA damage can increase p53 → POMC → α-MSH. Inflammatory keratinocytes release mediators such as PGE2, endothelin-1, stem-cell factor, and basic FGF.
2. **Melanocyte switch:** α-MSH binds MC1R, increasing cAMP → PKA → CREB and the transcription factor MITF. MITF increases tyrosinase (TYR), TYRP1, and DCT/TYRP2 expression. MC1R signaling tends to favor eumelanin; low MC1R/cysteine-rich conditions favor more pheomelanin.
3. **Pigment chemistry:** TYR catalyzes tyrosine → L-DOPA → dopaquinone. Dopaquinone either proceeds through DOPAchrome and DCT/TYRP2/TYRP1 toward eumelanin or reacts with cysteine to form pheomelanin.
4. **Packaging and transfer:** The melanosome matures through stages, travels along the melanocyte dendrite, and is transferred to surrounding keratinocytes. PAR-2-related transfer and epidermal retention determine how much pigment is visible at the surface.
5. **Persistence:** Pigmented keratinocytes migrate upward over weeks. Inflammation can drop pigment into the dermis, where melanophages retain it for months or years. Treating the trigger and preventing new transfer is as important as inhibiting a single enzyme.

## Ingredient-to-node matrix

| Node | What the ingredient is thought to do | Examples / evidence class | What the evidence does **not** establish |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stimulus/recurrence | Absorb or reflect UV; reduce visible-light contribution; remove friction, shaving trauma, and active dermatitis | Broad-spectrum sunscreen and trigger control (A/C) | Sunscreen does not erase established dermal pigment; compliance and quantity determine real-world effect |
| Plasminogen → plasmin | Reduce plasmin-mediated release of melanogenic keratinocyte signals and UV/inflammation amplification | Topical TXA (A/B); mechanism review [[3]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11439988/) | Topical TXA is not equivalent to oral or injected TXA; body pharmacokinetics are unknown |
| Inflammatory/mitochondrial tone | Reduce inflammation and some TYR activity; azelaic acid also affects abnormal keratinization | Azelaic acid 15–20% (A); niacinamide (A/H) | More irritation can reverse the benefit by adding PIH |
| MC1R/cAMP/MITF | Lower melanocyte transcriptional drive or MITF signaling | Retinoids indirectly; phenylethyl resorcinol p44/42–MITF mechanism (C); anti-inflammatory care | A signaling change in a cell assay is not a measured human body result |
| TYR catalytic step | Compete with or inhibit the copper-containing enzyme that oxidizes tyrosine/DOPA | HQ (A), Thiamidol/ITR (A/C), 4-BR/rucinol (A/B), kojic (A), arbutin (B), hexyl/phenylethyl resorcinol (B/C) | Assay IC50 values from different preparations cannot be sorted into a clinical league table |
| Dopaquinone / reactive precursors | Trap or chemically modify intermediates before polymerization | Melasyl/2-MNG (A/B); cysteamine (A); vitamin C/glutathione/resveratrol (B/C) | “Downstream” does not mean safer or more effective at every dose; formulation controls exposure |
| Melanosome transfer | Reduce transfer from melanocytes to keratinocytes and improve barrier signaling | Niacinamide 2–5%; NAG + niacinamide (A) | Transfer inhibition does not remove pigment already in the dermis |
| Epidermal turnover | Move pigmented keratinocytes out and smooth retained scale | Tretinoin/adapalene, glycolic/lactic acid, clinician peels (A/B) | Peels and retinoids are not interchangeable with daily body lotion; irritation can worsen PIH |
| Dermal persistence | Prevent new spillover; time and diagnosis determine clearance | Trigger control, sunscreen, anti-inflammatory care; procedures only with diagnosis | No topical can promise rapid clearance of melanophages |
| Destructive/toxic branch | Inhibit TYR while injuring melanocytes/keratinocytes or causing systemic exposure | Mercury salts, monobenzone, chronic steroid mixtures (R/H) | The lighter appearance is not a controlled, reversible therapeutic endpoint |

## Direct-enzyme potency: how to read the numbers

The 2025 KT-939 paper compared compounds in one human-tyrosinase assay: KT-939 0.07 μM, Thiamidol 0.33 μM, resveratrol 6.04 μM, 4-BR 7.09 μM, and phenylethyl resorcinol 21.28 μM. The earlier Thiamidol screen reported about 1.1 μM for ITR using a different recombinant-enzyme setup. These values are useful for **mechanism hypotheses**, not for predicting that a product will be four times more effective. Penetration, stability, dose at the melanocyte, vehicle, adherence, endpoint, and trigger control dominate the clinical result. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41159291/)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29427586/)

## Mercury creams: historical use and why they looked powerful

### What was used

Historical lighteners used inorganic mercury salts including calomel (mercurous chloride), mercuric chloride, mercurous oxide, and ammoniated mercury. Products were sold as creams, soaps, and powders long before modern pigment biology was understood. Contemporary reviews document the practice and the resulting poisonings. [[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10073381/)

### The molecular explanation

Mercury can bind thiol (–SH) groups in proteins and interact with the copper/histidine catalytic environment of tyrosinase. A human-tyrosinase study reported HgCl₂ IC50 values of about 29.97 μM for monophenolase and 77.93 μM for diphenolase and described irreversible, noncompetitive inhibition with histidine involvement. The immediate consequence is less DOPA/dopaquinone formation. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32210794/)

That is only one part of the apparent “efficacy.” Mercury salts also irritate and injure keratinocytes, disrupt melanocyte function, alter the barrier, and can produce mottled hypopigmentation or depigmentation. A cream can therefore look dramatically lightening while causing disease rather than delivering selective, reversible pathway control. The visualizer marks this branch in red to make that distinction explicit.

### Historical case signal

A published case described a woman who used a cream containing 17.5% ammoniated mercury for years. She developed facial mercury pigmentation, elevated blood/urine mercury, and possible neuropsychiatric toxicity. This is a case report, not a safe dose or a recipe. [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2215312/)

### Why modern guidance excludes it

- Dermal absorption increases with concentration, hydration/occlusion, damaged skin, and some vehicles. Mercury distributes beyond the application site and can contaminate clothing, bathrooms, household dust, and family members.
- Reported outcomes include nephrotoxicity/proteinuria, neurologic and gastrointestinal symptoms, dermatitis, pigmentary disorders, developmental risk, and persistent environmental contamination. A toxicology review identified kidney/CNS/GI harms and a skin/household contamination pattern. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22070559/)
- WHO states that mercury-containing skin-lightening products are hazardous and banned or restricted in many countries. The Minamata Convention sets a 1 ppm limit; WHO reported that around 10% of tested products exceeded it, some by roughly 100-fold. [[1]](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/WHO-CED-PHE-EPE-19.13)[[2]](https://www.who.int/news/item/14-02-2023-countries-unite-to-remove-mercury-from-hazardous-skin-lightening-products)
- FDA states that mercury is not permitted in U.S. skin-lightening products and advises consumers not to use products without a complete ingredient label. [[2]](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/skin-facts-what-you-need-know-about-skin-lightening-products)

**If an unlabeled or suspicious “whitening” product may contain mercury:** stop using it, avoid sharing towels or containers, wash hands, keep it away from children/pets, and contact a clinician or local poison center for exposure advice. Do not try to neutralize it at home or put it into household drains.

## What the pathway means for a maximal plan

The pathway suggests a **coverage model**, not an “everything at once” instruction:

1. Block the stimulus and the trigger.
2. Use one upstream signal lane (TXA) and one transfer/barrier lane (niacinamide or NAG/niacinamide).
3. Choose one direct inhibitor (Thiamidol, azelaic acid, 4-BR, HQ for a finite clinician-guided course, or a defined alternative).
4. Add a precursor/redox lane (Melasyl or cysteamine/vitamin C) only if there is a specific reason and the formula is understood.
5. Add one turnover lane for a texture/follicular indication, not merely because another enzyme label exists.
6. Keep barrier recovery in the loop and measure the target area over time.

The full theoretical coverage is displayed in the [melanin pathway visualizer](melanin_pathway_visualizer.html). The implementation-ready schedule, site gates, pregnancy branch, and stop rules remain in [Maximal routine and site-specific protocols](index.html#doc4). No study validates the complete stack; mercury and melanocyte-destroying agents never enter the practical branch.

## Model limits and unresolved biology

- The pathway is simplified: melanocyte–keratinocyte crosstalk, immune cells, hormones, microbiome, and dermal matrix create feedback loops.
- “Inhibits TYR” can mean competitive, noncompetitive, copper chelation, expression reduction, or assay interference; these are not interchangeable.
- Epidermal PIH and dermal melasma behave differently. A surface-turnover product cannot rapidly clear dermal melanophages.
- A controlled human melasma result does not establish large-surface body absorption, fold exposure, or years-long safety.
- Product labels may omit the exact concentration or use a derivative (for example, cetyl tranexamate mesylate rather than free TXA). The ingredient category and product performance should not be conflated.
