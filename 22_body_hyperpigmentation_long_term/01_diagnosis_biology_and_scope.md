# Diagnosis, biology, and the correct lightening target

*Compiled 2026-09-02. Research orientation, not diagnosis or treatment. Confidence labels: **verified** = directly supported by human/regulatory evidence; **limited** = small, short, indirect, or product-specific; **inference** = conservative translation to a body site or routine not directly studied.*

## 0. Bottom line

The safest long-term target is **acquired pigment above the person's own baseline**, not melanocyte destruction and not indefinite suppression of normal pigmentation over large body areas. A good protocol asks why the area is darker, removes that signal, and then fades residual epidermal pigment with the least irritating set of actives that can be sustained.

## 1. Why this page exists

The earlier combined-regimen page correctly separated acquired marks from natural tone, but the expanded ingredient request creates a bigger diagnosis problem: the same “dark underarm” description can represent PIH from shaving, inflammatory dermatitis, follicular disease, acanthosis nigricans (AN), infection, or normal variation. Those lanes do not share one maximal routine.

## 2. Decision gate before any depigmenter

| Pattern | Likely lane | First move | Why maximal actives are wrong initially |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat brown marks after shaving, ingrowns, acne/folliculitis, eczema, bites, burns, picking, or chafing | PIH | Treat/avoid the inflammatory trigger; then add a low-irritation pigment plan | Repeated irritation can create more PIH |
| Thick, velvety, symmetric neck/axilla/groin/knuckle darkening | Possible AN | Clinical evaluation for insulin resistance/diabetes, medication, endocrine, or other causes | Topicals can improve surface appearance while missing the driver |
| Itchy, scaly, painful, oozing, ring-shaped, malodorous, rapidly spreading, unilateral, or mucosal | Dermatitis, infection, or another diagnosis | Stop experiments and obtain medical evaluation | Pigment actives can mask/worsen the disorder |
| Rough follicular bumps on upper arms/thighs with brown marks | Keratosis pilaris/follicular plugging plus PIH | Urea/lactic-acid texture lane and inflammation control | A direct pigment inhibitor alone will not remove the plug/trigger |
| Uniform normal deeper tone without a distinct acquired darker area | Baseline pigmentation | No depigmenting routine indicated | Generalized bleaching has poor evidence and a worse risk/benefit ratio |
| Blue-gray/black reticulate darkening after prolonged lightener use | Possible exogenous ochronosis | Stop the suspected product and see dermatology | More lightener can worsen a difficult-to-treat adverse effect |

AN deserves special emphasis. A 2025 systematic review of seven randomized trials found short-term signals for urea, tretinoin, salicylic acid, and clinician peels, but the literature still treats correction of associated disease as the priority. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41140645/)

## 3. What changes pigment

Acquired hyperpigmentation is not just “too many melanocytes.” Several levers can raise visible pigment:

1. **Inflammatory signaling:** shaving, dermatitis, folliculitis, friction, acne, and procedures can stimulate melanogenesis.
2. **Melanin synthesis:** tyrosinase and related melanogenic steps increase pigment output.
3. **Melanosome transfer:** pigment is transferred from melanocytes into keratinocytes; niacinamide can reduce this transfer.
4. **Epidermal retention/turnover:** pigment-containing keratinocytes remain visible until they are shed; retinoids and hydroxy acids change turnover.
5. **Light signaling:** UV, and in susceptible exposed sites visible light, can maintain or deepen pigmentation.
6. **Skin thickness/texture:** AN and keratotic disorders can look darker partly because of epidermal thickening and surface optics.

This is why a rational maximal routine uses **different categories** rather than multiple copies of the same tyrosinase-inhibitor category.

## 4. Epidermal versus dermal pigment

Brown epidermal pigment is generally more responsive to topicals. Gray-brown or blue-gray pigment may sit deeper or represent another diagnosis and is less predictably responsive. A phone photograph cannot reliably classify depth, and a treatment-resistant area should not automatically trigger stronger home peels or a pigment laser.

The 2024 PIH treatment systematic review found partial response was much more common than complete response; laser/device treatment also worsened PIH in a small subset. Most included PIH followed acne, limiting translation to axillae, thighs, or large body areas. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37843491/)

## 5. Why destroying melanocytes is the wrong endpoint

The user-supplied discussion asked whether permanent melanocyte destruction could create a durable result. The answer is biologically yes but clinically no for cosmetic shade matching:

- FDA-labeled monobenzone can destroy melanocytes and permanently depigment skin, but it is indicated for final depigmentation in extensive vitiligo, not ordinary hyperpigmentation. [[3]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2003/08173slr015_benoquin_lbl.pdf)
- The effect can be delayed, erratic, excessive, and distant from the application site. White/depigmented skin lacks normal melanin protection.
- Destruction does not provide a controllable “one shade lighter” dial. It creates leukoderma/vitiligo-like pigment loss that may be difficult or impossible to reverse.
- Hydroquinone-associated chemical leukoderma is an adverse event, not a desirable long-term mechanism.

Thiamidol's reversible suppression in a melanocyte model is therefore a feature, not a weakness: the goal is to normalize excessive pigment production while preserving viable melanocytes. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29427586/)

## 6. Practical takeaways

- Photograph the same area, distance, lighting, and exposure every four weeks; day-to-day visual judgment is noisy.
- Establish a calm-skin baseline for one to two weeks before adding actives if the area is irritated.
- Keep products off mucosa, broken skin, and immediately post-shave/wax skin.
- Treat new bumps, dermatitis, and ingrowns before chasing old marks.
- If the area is thick/velvety, rapidly changing, symptomatic, or atypical, diagnosis outranks the routine.

## Evidence gaps

- No biomarker or photograph can reliably identify the cause without clinical context.
- Most pigment trials are facial, short, and female-heavy; body sites differ in thickness, friction, occlusion, follicles, and product transfer.
- There is no trial showing that permanent melanocyte injury produces safe, even, cosmetic body-tone correction.
