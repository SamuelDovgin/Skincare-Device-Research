# Emerging and proprietary depigmenting active map

*Compiled 2026-09-04. Current through the stated date. This page separates exact molecules from brand families and enzyme potency from clinical proof. Retail formulas and concentrations can change. The newer study census and pathway page now carry the broader 2025–2026 watchlist (KT-939, PTPD-12, malassezin, oxyresveratrol, and additional Melasyl/Thiamidol records).* 

## 0. Bottom line

Among newer OTC actives, **Thiamidol/ITR has the deepest human clinical record and strongest human-tyrosinase mechanistic case**. **Melasyl/2-MNG** is the most interesting different-mechanism competitor. **4-n-butylresorcinol** is the best-established non-Thiamidol resorcinol alternative. None has multi-year large-body evidence, and stacking them is not evidence-based.

## 2. Thiamidol / ITR

### What is verified

- The 2018 screen tested roughly 50,000 compounds against recombinant human tyrosinase and reported ITR IC50 around 1.1 μM, versus 21 μM for 4-n-butylresorcinol and much weaker values for kojic acid, hydroquinone, and arbutin in that assay. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29427586/)
- In the melanocyte-culture experiment, Thiamidol's suppression reversed after withdrawal; hydroquinone's suppression did not fully recover in the observation window. This is not proof of permanent cell destruction by hydroquinone. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29427586/)
- A 50-woman 90-day trial found statistically similar improvement with 0.2% Thiamidol twice daily and 4% hydroquinone nightly; two Thiamidol participants had allergic contact dermatitis. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33988887/)
- A 200-person double-blind vehicle RCT found a 36.1% mMASI reduction at 12 weeks with 0.2% Thiamidol versus 16.1% with vehicle; physician assessment favored ITR, while patient global assessment and digital color analysis did not significantly differ. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41566113/)
- A 2024 systematic review found 14 clinical studies but called for better dose/schedule and comparative research. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39496126/)

### Evidence caveats

Much of the evidence has Beiersdorf involvement, and the active is patented. Retail Eucerin and NIVEA Luminous630 formulas can list the same INCI molecule, but the surrounding vehicle, fragrance, concentration, claims, and intended site can differ. Same active does not mean identical product performance.

### Long-term conclusion

Reasonable maintenance candidate based on reversible mechanism and short-term tolerability; **not proven safe for uninterrupted decades**, large surface areas, pregnancy, or damaged folds.

## 3. Melasyl / 2-MNG

Melasyl acts downstream by intercepting/reacting with melanin precursors rather than directly inhibiting tyrosinase. A 2024/2025 publication on UV-induced pigmentation used 0.5–1% 2-MNG on melanin-rich skin, supporting prevention biology. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38372022/)

A randomized investigator-blind non-inferiority study compared a 0.5% 2-MNG serum twice daily with 4% hydroquinone nightly for 84 days in 109 women with facial melasma; both groups also used tinted SPF 50+ sunscreen. The 2-MNG formula met the prespecified non-inferiority criterion and had fewer early local reactions, but authors/company involvement and the full formulation limit ingredient-only inference. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40586974/)

**Position:** choose instead of Thiamidol when the formula/site/tolerance fits; do not assume combining both has been proven better.

## 4. 4-n-butylresorcinol and related resorcinols

A 20-person randomized double-blind split-face trial found 0.1% 4-n-butylresorcinol twice daily reduced melanin index versus vehicle by weeks 4 and 8, with mild transient adverse effects. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20548876/) Another comparative line of research found 4-BR more active than hexylresorcinol and phenylethyl resorcinol, but these are mostly small facial/formulation studies.

**Position:** credible fallback when Thiamidol/Melasyl is unavailable or poorly tolerated. Use as the single inhibitor lane.

## 5. Kojic acid and alpha-arbutin: established, not equivalent to the newer evidence

Kojic acid and alpha-arbutin are legitimate inhibitors, but the clinical evidence is thinner and more formula-dependent than social-media rankings suggest.

- SCCS's **1% kojic acid** and **0.5% alpha-arbutin body lotion** conclusions are safety assessments, not proof these concentrations equal hydroquinone or Thiamidol clinically. [[8]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/kojic-acid_en)[[9]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/safety-alpha-arbutin-and-beta-arbutin-cosmetic-products_en)
- A 2025 pilot comparing 5% alpha-arbutin + 2% kojic acid with a triple combination was facial, small, and used concentrations above the conservative SCCS body-lotion/kojic ceilings; it should not be converted into an indefinite fold routine. [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555866/)
- Kojic acid can cause allergic contact dermatitis, which is particularly counterproductive in PIH-prone folds.

## 6. KT-939: promising does not mean ready

A 2025 paper reported very strong human-tyrosinase inhibition and an early 28-day lotion study. The evidence is insufficient for a long-term body recommendation because independent replication, mature randomized comparisons, retail identity/concentration, sensitization surveillance, and long-term outcomes are missing.

## 7. Product-selection rules without brand hype

1. Find the exact INCI active, not only the marketing family name.
2. Prefer disclosed concentration when the clinical evidence is concentration-specific.
3. Prefer fragrance-free, low-irritation vehicles for axillae/folds.
4. Do not count three tyrosinase inhibitors as three independent evidence categories.
5. Treat manufacturer-funded trials as real but conflict-limited evidence, not as invalid or independent proof.
6. Recheck current official ingredient lists before purchase; formulas change.

## Evidence gaps

- No independent long-term axillary/body RCT compares these proprietary actives.
- Retail concentration is often undisclosed.
- Allergy surveillance and large-area exposure data are sparse.
- Enzyme IC50 values vary by assay and do not translate linearly into percent clinical lightening.
