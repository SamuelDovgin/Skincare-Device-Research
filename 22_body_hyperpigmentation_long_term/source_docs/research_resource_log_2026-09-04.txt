HYPERPIGMENTATION RESEARCH RESOURCE LOG
========================================

Topic: Body hyperpigmentation and long-term tone-evening
Compiled: 2026-09-04 (America/Chicago)
Purpose: Durable recovery notes for the comparative study census, category map,
product crosswalk, melanin-pathway visualizer, mercury history, and routine pages.

This file is intentionally plain text. It records what was actually consulted,
what each item supports, and whether a lawful local copy exists. It is not a
replacement for the rendered research pages and it is not a treatment protocol.
The topic uses “acquired hyperpigmentation” to mean darker-than-baseline marks
from PIH, friction/shaving/follicular inflammation, rough texture, or selected
melasma-like patterns. It does not recommend generalized baseline-tone change,
melanocyte destruction, mercury, monobenzone, or unlabelled bleaching creams.

* Product prices, stock, formulas, sellers, and marketing claims are snapshots.
  Recheck an official page or authorized retailer before purchase.
* Short paraphrases are used instead of long quotations. The log is a research
  index, not a redistribution of copyrighted full text.

EVIDENCE LABELS
---------------

A  Randomized/controlled human trial or systematic review with a relevant
   endpoint.
B  Open-label, split-face, small, or combination-formula human signal.
C  Human enzyme/cell, animal, or mechanistic plausibility evidence.
R/H Regulatory, toxicology, or historical evidence; not a benefit claim.
M  Official product/marketing/consumer source; useful for label, INCI, price,
   availability, and claim capture, not equivalent to an independent RCT.
L  Local archive/data/tool file.

[S01] Chang et al. 2023 topical depigmenting meta-analysis — PMID 36566490
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36566490/
Accessed: 2026-09-04
Class: A; systematic review/meta-analysis
Used in: README TL;DR; 08 bottom line; 08 pooled-effect section; 08 CSV rows
Supports: comparative class-level effect and irritation context.
Key record: 45 efficacy studies; 2,359 participants; pooled SMD approximately
HQ monotherapy -1.3, HQ combinations -1.4, cysteamine -1.6, topical TXA -1.5,
azelaic acid -1.3, and kojic acid -0.9. Approximate irritation incidence was
50.9% for HQ combinations, 42.2% cysteamine, 18.7% azelaic acid, 5.3% kojic
acid, and 0.8% TXA.
Finding: The pooled signal favors several active classes, but the estimates mix
facial melasma/PIH, formulas, comparators, and durations; they are not a retail
product league table or a body-skin dose recommendation.
Limits: High heterogeneity; mostly facial studies; standardized mean difference
does not say how many shades a person will change. Irritation estimates are not
directly transferable to folds or large body areas.
Local preservation: URL-only — no lawful local full-text copy saved; citation,
abstract metadata, and the numeric summary are preserved here and in the doc8 CSV.
Status/recheck: Stable PubMed record; recheck if a newer meta-analysis changes the hierarchy.

[S02] Alirezaei et al. topical TXA versus azelaic acid — PMID 37213446
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37213446/
Accessed: 2026-09-04
Class: A; randomized comparative human trial
Used in: 04 routine; 08 bottom line/ranking; 08 TXA and azelaic rows; visualizer TXA/azelaic links
Supports: direct comparison of topical 5% TXA and 20% azelaic acid for acne PIH.
Key record: 60 completers; 12 weeks; facial acne-induced PIH; both arms improved,
with more early adverse effects in the azelaic arm.
Finding: Both ingredients are credible anti-pigment options, but this is a
facial PIH comparison and the concentrations differ from common 10% retail
azelaic products.
Limits: Small, short, facial, and not a body-surface or long-term trial; exact
between-arm superiority was not established.

[S03] Castanedo-Cazares et al. 4% niacinamide axillary RCT — PMID 23355788
URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/
Accessed: 2026-09-04
Class: A; direct body-site randomized clinical study
Used in: README TL; 02 atlas; 04 fold protocol; 08 ranking; visualizer niacinamide lane
Supports: the clearest direct body/axillary topical niacinamide anchor.
Key record: 24 women, phototypes III–V; 4% niacinamide versus 0.05% desonide/
placebo comparator; 9 weeks; axillary hyperpigmentation; colorimetry improved,
but good/excellent response was only about 24%.
Finding: Niacinamide is a sensible, relatively low-cost transfer/barrier lane,
but the body response is gradual and far from universal.
Limits: Small sample, short duration, axilla-specific, and comparator/formula
details limit extrapolation to diffuse body marks or higher percentages.

and source_docs/PMC3552481_niacinamide_axillary_RCT.xml preserve bibliographic/
