# Non-topical strategy and escalation ladder

*Compiled 2026-09-02. Research orientation, not a procedural recommendation. Device choice depends on diagnosis, skin type, wavelength, fluence, pulse structure, operator, endpoint, test spot, and aftercare.*

## 0. Bottom line

The highest-value “non-topical” intervention is often **stopping new injury**. Hair reduction can help when shaving/ingrowns are the engine; professional peels can be an adjunct for small stable epidermal targets; pigment lasers/IPL are last-line because the same inflammation used to clear pigment can create new PIH.

## 1. Escalation ladder

| Level | Strategy | Best fit | Main boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Diagnose and treat driver | Thick/velvety, rash, infection, active folliculitis, rapid/spreading pigment | Cosmetic fading cannot substitute for metabolic/inflammatory/infectious care |
| 1 | Friction and shaving redesign | Axillae, inner thighs, bikini line, beard/neck, ingrowns | Requires weeks/months; does not instantly remove existing pigment |
| 2 | Hair reduction | Recurrent pseudofolliculitis/ingrown-hair PIH | Device must match skin type; test spot and pigment-risk plan required |
| 3 | Clinician superficial peel | Small, calm, stable epidermal PIH or keratotic target | Published peel concentrations are procedures, not home recipes |
| 4 | Pigment laser/light consultation | Refractory focal diagnosed pigment | Recurrence, dyspigmentation, PIH worsening, cost, operator dependence |
| 5 | Prescription systemic therapy | Narrow diagnosed conditions such as melasma, not generalized body lightening | Oral TXA and other systemic treatments require medical screening |

## 2. Friction, deodorant, and hair-removal redesign

- Use soft, breathable clothing and reduce repeated rubbing where practical.
- Stop abrasive scrubs, rough washcloths, and “exfoliating” brushes.
- Change only one deodorant/antiperspirant variable at a time; fragrance and repeated irritation can matter more than the marketing category.
- Use guarded trimming during active inflammation. If shaving, use lubrication, a sharp clean blade, fewer passes, and the direction that minimizes bumps for that site.
- Do not place acids/retinoids on freshly abraded skin.

## 3. Hair reduction as trigger prevention

Hair reduction does not directly erase PIH; its value is reducing future follicular inflammation. In richly pigmented skin, professionally used long-pulsed 1064-nm Nd:YAG is often selected because deeper penetration and lower epidermal melanin absorption can improve the safety margin relative to shorter wavelengths, but burns and dyspigmentation remain possible.

The right consultation asks:

- what wavelength/device is used for this skin type;
- whether the operator routinely treats similar skin tones;
- how the test spot is chosen and reviewed;
- what active folliculitis, tan, photosensitizer, or pigment changes delay treatment;
- how new PIH will be managed.

Read the separate diode/IPL topics before using a home hair-removal device; a hair-removal clearance does not establish pigment-treatment efficacy.

## 4. Peels

Professional glycolic, salicylic, lactic, mandelic, or trichloroacetic-acid peels may help selected epidermal PIH or AN, but the literature is small and protocol-specific. A 2024 randomized AN trial used **70% glycolic acid every two weeks in clinic**, not a home leave-on product; tretinoin performed better for the axillary subgroup. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38362706/)

Home rules:

- do not reproduce study peels;
- do not neutralize or mix concentrated acids without a professional protocol;
- do not peel active dermatitis, infection, broken skin, or recently shaved/waxed folds;
- do not use increasing burn as a surrogate for efficacy.

## 5. Lasers and IPL

The 2025 axillary review found small studies of Q-switched Nd:YAG and IPL with reported improvement, but only 10 total axillary studies existed across all modalities. This is not a strong enough evidence base to make a device first-line. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40843925/)

The broader PIH systematic review reported complete responses in a minority, partial responses more often, and PIH worsening in 2.6% of laser/energy-treated patients; most cases were acne-related. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37843491/)

Before a pigment device:

1. confirm the diagnosis and depth;
2. control the trigger and use photoprotection where relevant;
3. complete a conservative topical trial;
4. require a test spot and an objective waiting interval;
5. ask what the plan is for paradoxical darkening or hypopigmentation.

## 6. Systemic treatment boundary

Oral TXA has evidence for facial melasma but carries systemic contraindications and thromboembolic screening requirements. It is not an ingredient to add to a “maximal body-lightening stack.” Oral glutathione evidence is inconsistent and product-specific; injectable glutathione adds sterility/endotoxin and systemic risks and is excluded. [[4]](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/fda-highlights-concerns-using-dietary-ingredient-glutathione-compound-sterile-injectables)

## 7. Provider escalation checklist

Bring standardized photographs and ask:

- What is the diagnosis—PIH, AN, melasma, dermatitis, infection, fixed drug eruption, or something else?
- Is pigment epidermal, dermal, mixed, or uncertain?
- Which active or procedure has direct evidence for this site and skin type?
- What is the planned endpoint and time horizon?
- What area is being treated, and does surface area change systemic exposure?
- What are the stop rules for irritation, leukoderma, ochronosis, or paradoxical PIH?
- What maintenance plan replaces any finite hydroquinone/prescription phase?

## Evidence gaps

- No device has a robust long-term comparative trial for generalized body-tone evening.
- Axillary laser/IPL studies are small and heterogeneous.
- Hair-reduction benefit for PIH is usually inferred through trigger prevention rather than measured pigment endpoints.
- Procedure evidence is highly operator- and parameter-dependent.
