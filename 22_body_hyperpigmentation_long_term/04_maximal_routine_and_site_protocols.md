# Maximal evidence-based routine and site-specific protocols

*Compiled 2026-09-04. Educational framework, not a personal prescription. The complete combination has not been tested as one regimen. “Maximal” here means maximum useful mechanism coverage with a deliberately limited irritation burden. For the full category map—including a deliberately theoretical all-lane thought experiment—see the [comparative study census](index.html#doc8) and [melanin-pathway visualizer](melanin_pathway_visualizer.html).* 

## 0. Bottom line

The best maximal routine is **four jobs, not ten products**:

1. stop the trigger and repair the barrier;
2. reduce pigment signaling/transfer with niacinamide or topical TXA;
3. use one direct pigment-production inhibitor;
4. add one turnover method only if texture, follicular plugging, or plateau justifies it.

The default plan below reaches all four jobs while never scheduling a retinoid and AHA on the same night.

## 1. The maximal long-term architecture

### Morning

1. Gentle rinse or cleanse only if needed.
2. **Signal/transfer layer:** niacinamide 4% **or** topical TXA 2–5%. A well-tolerated combination formula can cover both; separate high-strength serums are not required.
3. **One inhibitor lane:** Thiamidol/ITR product **or** Melasyl/2-MNG product **or** 4-n-butylresorcinol product. Follow the product label; do not layer all three.
4. Bland moisturizer if needed.
5. Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ on exposed arms, legs, chest, neck, hands, or other exposed sites. A shade-matched iron-oxide tint is especially relevant when visible light maintains facial/upper-chest pigment. Covered axillae do not need a ceremonial sunscreen layer.

### Evening weekly rotation

| Day | Default long-term plan | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Monday | Azelaic acid 10–20% → moisturizer | Anti-inflammatory + melanogenesis |
| Tuesday | Moisturizer only | Recovery/barrier |
| Wednesday | Azelaic acid → moisturizer | Core treatment |
| Thursday | **One turnover lane:** glycolic/lactic acid **or** retinoid → moisturizer | Retention/texture/follicular plugging |
| Friday | Moisturizer only | Recovery |
| Saturday | Azelaic acid → moisturizer | Core treatment |
| Sunday | Moisturizer only; assess irritation | Recovery/decision point |

After four calm weeks, a non-fold site may add a fourth azelaic night. Do not increase turnover frequency and azelaic frequency in the same week.

## 2. Four-phase ramp

### Phase 0 — calm and classify (1–2 weeks)

- Stop scrubs, fragranced irritants, harsh deodorants, and close shaving over inflamed skin.
- Use bland cleanser/rinse + moisturizer.
- Photograph the area and identify the driver.
- Obtain medical evaluation first for thick/velvety, sudden, spreading, symptomatic, or atypical pigment.

### Phase 1 — core signal control (weeks 1–4)

- AM: niacinamide 4% **or** topical TXA 2–5%.
- PM: azelaic acid two nights/week; moisturizer on other nights.
- No AHA, retinoid, kojic acid, or extra proprietary inhibitor yet.

### Phase 2 — add one direct inhibitor (weeks 5–8)

- Add one Thiamidol, Melasyl, or 4-n-butylresorcinol product at label frequency, initially every other morning or once daily.
- Keep the Phase 1 schedule unchanged for two weeks.
- If itch/rash develops, stop the newest product and return to the prior tolerated routine.

### Phase 3 — add turnover only if needed (week 9+)

Choose one:

- **AHA branch:** glycolic 5–8% or a lactic-acid body lotion one night/week for rough/retained pigment.
- **Retinoid branch:** prescription tretinoin 0.025% or adapalene 0.1% one night/week when acne, ingrowns, follicular plugging, or clinician-confirmed AN makes the tradeoff worthwhile.

Do not use both branches initially. Retinoids are avoided in pregnancy/trying-to-conceive lanes.

### Phase 4 — maintenance after a plateau/goal (month 4+)

- Keep trigger control permanent.
- Reduce the optional inhibitor or azelaic frequency one step at a time every four weeks.
- Keep the lowest frequency that preserves improvement without dermatitis.
- A mild rebound after stopping a reversible inhibitor is expected biology, not “dependence.”

## 3. Underarms, groin-adjacent folds, and inner thighs

These areas are warm, occluded, friction-prone, and easily irritated.

- Start with niacinamide 4% once daily and moisturizer.
- Add azelaic acid only twice weekly initially.
- Wait at least 24 hours after shaving/waxing, longer if stinging or inflamed.
- Prefer guarded trimming or a less traumatic hair-removal method while inflammation is active.
- Use an inhibitor once daily at most initially; fragrance-free body/fold formulations are preferable.
- Keep AHA at once weekly maximum until several weeks of calm tolerance.
- Avoid applying on mucosa or immediately inside the groin/genital area.

The direct axillary niacinamide trial supports the category but produced good-to-excellent improvement in only about one quarter of niacinamide-treated cases, so set expectations around gradual partial improvement. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/)

## 4. Ingrown-hair / folliculitis-driven marks

The sequence is prevention first:

1. control active folliculitis/acne with an appropriate medical/acne regimen;
2. reduce close shaving and picking;
3. consider professionally managed hair reduction when recurrent coarse-hair injury is the dominant driver;
4. use niacinamide/TXA + azelaic acid for residual marks;
5. choose a retinoid turnover branch only if follicles tolerate it.

If benzoyl peroxide or another acne active is needed, do not add it casually to the same maximal pigment night; use the combined-regimen Stack Lab to schedule irritation and formulation conflicts.

## 5. Rough elbows, knees, upper arms, thighs, and keratosis pilaris

Texture may contribute more than melanogenesis.

- Use urea 10–20% or lactic acid around 5–12% as the first texture intervention.
- Add niacinamide or azelaic acid only after the barrier is calm.
- Do not scrub mechanically on top of an acid lotion.
- A keratosis-pilaris trial found both 10% lactic acid and 5% salicylic acid improved lesions over three months; this supports texture treatment, not guaranteed pigment erasure. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25802513/)

## 6. Exposed arms, legs, chest, neck, and hands

- Photoprotection becomes a core treatment category, not an optional afterthought.
- Broad-spectrum SPF 30+ and clothing reduce new UV-driven pigment; visible-light protection is most relevant to exposed pigment-prone sites and melasma-like patterns.
- The 2025 PIH-prevention systematic review found sunscreen was the only measure that consistently prevented procedure-induced PIH across included skin-of-color studies, although the evidence was mostly facial/laser-related. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39953770/)

## 7. Sensitive/eczema-prone lane

Use a deliberately non-maximal routine:

- AM: moisturizer; optional 2–4% niacinamide if tolerated.
- PM: moisturizer only for two weeks, then azelaic acid once weekly if the skin is fully calm.
- Skip AHA, retinoid, kojic acid, cysteamine, and proprietary inhibitor until stability is demonstrated.
- Treat eczema itself; pigment treatment on active dermatitis is backwards.

## 8. Pregnancy/trying-to-conceive lane

- Trigger control, moisturizer, and photoprotection.
- Consider niacinamide or azelaic acid only after clinician/product-label review.
- No topical retinoid.
- Do not build a maximal proprietary-active/TXA/hydroquinone/cysteamine routine without obstetric/dermatology input.

## 9. Escalation after 12–16 weeks

If photographs show no improvement and the skin is calm:

1. re-check the diagnosis and trigger;
2. confirm adherence and actual active concentration/label;
3. swap—not stack—the direct inhibitor lane;
4. consider a dermatologist-guided cysteamine or finite hydroquinone course for focal disease;
5. consider procedures only after diagnosis, trigger control, test spot, and PIH-risk planning.

## 10. What not to put in the maximal routine

- multiple tyrosinase inhibitors layered for mechanism-count vanity;
- retinoid + AHA on the same fold night;
- home 20–70% peels;
- chronic corticosteroid lightening mixtures;
- oral TXA without medical indication/screening;
- injectable glutathione;
- mercury/unlabeled creams;
- monobenzone or any strategy designed to destroy melanocytes.

## Evidence gaps

- The full schedule is not a tested fixed combination.
- “Three nights of azelaic + one turnover night” is a conservative synthesis, not an optimized dose established by a trial.
- Retail formulas may combine actives, fragrances, exfoliants, and solvents that change tolerability.
- Folds, groin-adjacent skin, and large body surfaces have especially weak long-term evidence.
