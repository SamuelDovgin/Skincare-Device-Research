# Ingredient evidence atlas for long-term body hyperpigmentation

*Compiled 2026-09-02. This is an evidence map, not a prescription. “Long-term compatible” means a reasonable maintenance candidate based on present evidence and mechanism—not proof of daily lifetime safety.*

## 0. Bottom line

For long-term body PIH, the best-supported practical core is **niacinamide or topical tranexamic acid + azelaic acid + trigger control**, with **one** optional human-tyrosinase/proprietary inhibitor and **one** optional turnover lane. Hydroquinone is highly effective but finite/supervised; monobenzone, mercury, potent steroid mixtures, injections, and unlabeled bleaches are excluded.

## 2. Direct underarm evidence: what is actually known

The 2025 axillary systematic review found only **10 studies** across topicals and devices; there is no standard treatment. It lists niacinamide, desonide, hydroquinone/AHA and several botanical/product-specific approaches, plus Q-switched Nd:YAG and IPL studies. The evidence is too sparse and heterogeneous to declare one universal axillary winner. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40843925/)

The most useful direct topical RCT randomized 24 women with phototypes III–V to 4% niacinamide, 0.05% desonide, or placebo for nine weeks. Both active arms improved colorimetry, but good-to-excellent responses occurred in only 24% with niacinamide. Desonide is not a long-term cosmetic maintenance recommendation because fold steroids can cause atrophy/striae and other steroid harms. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/)

## 3. Strongest non-hydroquinone categories

### Niacinamide

Niacinamide reduces melanosome transfer and supports barrier/inflammation control. It is the rare active with a direct axillary placebo-controlled trial. A separate vehicle-controlled facial trial found a 2% niacinamide + 2% TXA moisturizer outperformed vehicle over eight weeks, supporting complementary categories without proving either ingredient alone at those doses. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23355788/)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24033822/)

**Use role:** morning/core, especially folds and shaving-related PIH. **Do not chase 10–20%** when the body-site clinical anchor is 4%; higher concentration may add irritation without proven extra benefit.

### Azelaic acid

Azelaic acid combines antimelanogenic and anti-inflammatory effects. A 24-week randomized vehicle-controlled trial of 20% cream in phototypes IV–VI improved facial hyperpigmentation. A 12-week PIH trial found twice-daily 20% azelaic acid and 5% topical TXA both improved scores, with more early side effects in the azelaic arm. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9829446/)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37213446/)

**Use role:** evening/core. Start lower-frequency in folds; 15–20% is usually prescription depending on jurisdiction.

### Topical tranexamic acid

Topical TXA acts through plasmin/plasminogen-related pigment signaling rather than directly poisoning melanocytes. Evidence includes 2% TXA + 2% niacinamide versus vehicle and 5% TXA versus 20% azelaic acid. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24033822/)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37213446/)

**Use role:** morning signaling option or azelaic alternative. **Do not translate topical use into oral TXA**; systemic TXA has materially different clotting/medical screening requirements.

## 4. Human-tyrosinase and newer proprietary inhibitors

Thiamidol/ITR was identified by screening recombinant human tyrosinase, with a reported IC50 of 1.1 μM versus far weaker activity for hydroquinone/arbutin and kojic acid in that assay. In cultured melanocytes, suppression was strong and reversible after withdrawal. Enzyme potency does not directly equal clinical superiority because dose, vehicle, penetration, endpoint, and adherence differ. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29427586/)

A 50-woman evaluator-blinded RCT found 0.2% Thiamidol twice daily and 4% hydroquinone nightly produced statistically similar 90-day melasma improvement; two Thiamidol participants developed allergic contact dermatitis. A later 200-participant double-blind vehicle RCT found a larger 12-week mMASI reduction with 0.2% Thiamidol than vehicle, while patient global assessment and digital color analysis did not significantly differ. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33988887/)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41566113/)

**Use role:** choose Thiamidol **or** 4-n-butylresorcinol **or** Melasyl as one inhibitor lane. Do not stack all three.

## 5. Turnover lane

The systematic review of topical PIH treatments judged retinoids, hydroxy acids, and broad-spectrum sunscreen to have the greatest number of high-quality studies. That does not mean they should all be used every night. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34525885/)

- **Retinoid:** best when acne, ingrowns, follicular plugging, or thickened AN is part of the driver. In a short split-body AN trial, tretinoin 0.05% every other night outperformed clinician 70% glycolic peels for axillary lesions. That is AN evidence, not a mandate for 0.05% tretinoin on simple underarm PIH. [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38362706/)
- **AHA:** best when roughness/retention is prominent. Home 5–10% glycolic or a gentler lactic-acid body lotion is a different exposure from 35–70% professional peels.
- **Rule:** start with one turnover product once weekly. Add frequency, not a second turnover family, only if the skin remains calm for several weeks.

## 6. Lower-priority but legitimate adjuncts

- **Kojic acid:** SCCS considers up to 1% safe for cosmetic skin-lightening use under its assessment, while specifically flagging weakened-barrier/peeling contexts. Efficacy exists, but contact allergy and weaker human-tyrosinase performance lower its long-term fold priority. [[11]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/kojic-acid_en)
- **Alpha-arbutin:** SCCS considers up to 0.5% in body lotions safe, with hydroquinone impurities kept as low as possible. This is a safety ceiling, not evidence that 0.5% produces a large effect. [[12]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/safety-alpha-arbutin-and-beta-arbutin-cosmetic-products_en)
- **NAG/niacinamide:** a 10-week facial randomized study used 2% NAG + 4% niacinamide and improved pigmentation versus vehicle; attribution to NAG alone is impossible. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19845667/)
- **Vitamin C/licorice/soy/botanicals:** plausible or supported by small/formula-specific studies, but formulation and evidence maturity are weaker than the core. Use them as substitutions when better tolerated, not extra mandatory layers.

## 7. What “clinically proven” does not mean

- A positive facial melasma RCT does not prove an underarm protocol.
- A significant mean score change does not mean every user gets a visible result.
- An IC50 ranking is not a clinical ranking.
- A safety opinion's maximum concentration is not an efficacy target.
- A combination formula does not prove every ingredient contributed.
- Twelve or 24 weeks of tolerability is not proof of lifetime daily safety.

## Evidence gaps

- No head-to-head trial compares a long-term niacinamide/TXA/azelaic regimen with Thiamidol, Melasyl, or 4-n-butylresorcinol on axillae or thighs.
- Nearly all proprietary-active treatment evidence is facial and many studies have manufacturer involvement.
- Exact retail concentrations of patented actives are often undisclosed.
- Large-body-surface and occluded-fold exposure are not well studied.
