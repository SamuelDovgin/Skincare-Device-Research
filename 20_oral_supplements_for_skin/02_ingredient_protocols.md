# Ingredient protocols: what the trials actually used

*Compiled 2026-08-15. These are evidence-translation ranges, not prescriptions or universal recommendations. “Studied” does not mean “optimal,” and a dose below an adult UL is not automatically appropriate for a specific person.*

## 0. Bottom line

If you want a low-complexity experiment for general photoaged/dry skin, the most defensible sequence is one standardized product at a time for 12 weeks: either hydrolyzed collagen peptides or oral HA, while keeping diet, sunscreen, skincare, and sun exposure stable. Add a second ingredient only if the first has a defined benefit and no adverse effects. Vitamin C can support normal collagen biology, but the evidence does not justify high-dose oral vitamin C as the primary anti-aging intervention.

## 1. Protocol translation table

| Lane | Trial range | Conservative research translation | Endpoint and checkpoint |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Collagen peptides | 1–10 g/day; many trials 2.5–5 g/day | Choose a product that states grams of hydrolyzed collagen peptides, source, lot/expiry, and third-party testing. A 2.5–5 g/day, 12-week trial is within the common literature range. | Hydration/TEWL, elasticity, standardized photos at baseline and week 12. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40826844/)
| Oral HA | 60–120 mg/day for 6–12 weeks | Use the actual HA/sodium-hyaluronate mass, not “matrix” mass. Do not combine multiple HA products while testing. | Hydration, TEWL, dryness, wrinkle area/depth at weeks 6 and 12. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41422283/)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28761365/)
| Wheat ceramide extract | 350 mg/day for 84 days | Only if the label identifies the studied extract or a comparable standardized wheat-oil/ceramide preparation; skip with wheat allergy. | Corneometry/dryness, especially limbs, at week 12. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20646083/)
| Astaxanthin | 3–6 mg/day, 8–16 weeks | Treat as an optional adjunct, preferably with food containing fat. Avoid stacking multiple carotenoids if you want attribution. | Hydration/elasticity; do not use wrinkle change as the sole expected outcome. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34578794/)
| Lycopene / carotenoid food strategy | 16 mg/day lycopene in a 12-week tomato-paste trial; mixed carotenoid trials ran 12 weeks | Prefer food sources (tomato products, colorful produce) before pills. If supplementing, use a disclosed dose and avoid high-dose beta-carotene in current/heavy smokers. | UV-provocation or erythema is a research endpoint; ordinary sunscreen use remains unchanged. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20854436/)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32072695/)
| Polypodium leucotomos | Often 240–480 mg/day, product-specific | Only standardized extracts with an exact dose and botanical identity; consider this a photosensitivity/UV adjunct, not a general anti-aging capsule. | MED/erythema or clinician-managed photodermatosis; 8–12 weeks is a reasonable study window. [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40196953/)
| Vitamin C | RDA 75 mg/day women, 90 mg/day men; adult UL 2,000 mg/day | Use food first. If diet is poor, a modest supplement that brings total intake near adequacy is more evidence-aligned than a gram-dose beauty protocol. | Deficiency/adequacy, wound healing, general nutrition—not a guaranteed wrinkle result. [[9]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/)
| Vitamin D | RCT doses vary | Test or discuss risk factors when treating deficiency/AD. Do not use a cosmetic target serum level or chronic high dose without clinician input. | 25(OH)D and diagnosed condition; not facial brightness. [[10]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)
| Zinc for acne | Trial salts/doses vary | Use only for a defined acne plan, preferably if intake/status or clinician assessment supports it; keep total daily zinc below 40 mg unless medically supervised. | Inflammatory lesions and GI tolerance over 8–12 weeks. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32860489/)[[12]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)
| Probiotic for acne | Strain-specific; 12-week signal in 2025 meta-analysis | Record genus/species/strain, CFU through expiry, storage, and dose. Do not equate any “probiotic blend” with the studied strains. | Lesion count and validated severity at 4 and 12 weeks. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832719/)
| Nicotinamide | 500 mg twice daily for 12 months in ONTRAC | This is a dermatologist-directed chemoprevention protocol for patients with recurrent keratinocyte cancers; do not self-start it for wrinkles. | New NMSC/AK counts, not texture or brightness. [[14]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26488693/)
| Glutathione | 250–500 mg/day, 4–12 weeks in small trials | Consider evidence inconclusive and effect unsustainable/uncertain. Never use IV glutathione for cosmetic purposes. | Melanin index only; stop if adverse effects and do not promise permanent lightening. [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30895708/)

## 2. Timing and co-factors

- **Timing is rarely proven.** A consistent time of day that improves adherence is usually more defensible than chasing an untested absorption schedule.
- **Fat-soluble ingredients** such as astaxanthin and carotenoids are commonly taken with a meal containing fat; this is a pharmacokinetic practicality, not evidence of a superior skin outcome.
- **Collagen does not need to be taken at a special “anabolic window.”** The human skin trials generally used daily dosing over weeks, not timed peri-workout dosing.
- **Vitamin C with collagen is biologically plausible but not proven to amplify oral collagen outcomes.** Most collagen trials already used products with varied co-ingredients, and no robust head-to-head trial establishes synergy.
- **Do not change five variables at once.** If you add collagen, HA, astaxanthin, a multivitamin, and a new skincare routine together, a before/after result is uninterpretable.

## 3. Measurement protocol for a personal experiment

1. Photograph the same face, lighting, distance, expression, and camera settings at baseline, week 4, week 8, and week 12.
2. Pick one primary endpoint: dryness/tightness, acne inflammatory lesions, or a standardized wrinkle region. Treat hydration meter changes as secondary unless measured consistently.
3. Keep sunscreen, retinoid use, cleanser, moisturizer, diet, and tanning/sun exposure as stable as realistically possible.
4. Record GI symptoms, headache, rash, flushing, bruising, menstrual changes, and medication changes weekly.
5. Stop the experiment for allergic symptoms, severe GI symptoms, unusual bruising/bleeding, jaundice, severe headache, or any clinically concerning change; seek medical care for serious symptoms.

## Evidence gaps

- No dose-response study shows that 10 g collagen is better than 2.5–5 g for facial skin.
- Product-specific peptides cannot be assumed interchangeable even when the front label says “collagen peptides.”
- There is no validated oral HA or collagen maintenance schedule after week 12.
- “With vitamin C” on a label does not prove the vitamin C contributed to the outcome.
