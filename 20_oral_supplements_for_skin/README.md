# Oral supplements for skin

*Compiled 2026-08-15. Research orientation, not medical advice. This topic covers oral nutrients, peptides, lipids, carotenoids, botanicals, probiotics, and “nutricosmetics” studied in humans for skin endpoints. It does not replace diagnosis, sunscreen, prescribed dermatology treatment, or nutrition care.*

## What this topic adds

The archive’s [vitamin C topic](../08_vitamin_c_serums/index.html) is about topical ascorbic-acid delivery. This topic asks a different question: what happens when an ingredient is ingested, which skin metric changes, at what dose and duration, and in which population? A product containing ten ingredients is not evidence that each ingredient contributed.

## TL;DR

1. **The most reproducible oral signal is modest hydration/elasticity improvement from hydrolyzed collagen peptides, usually after 8–12 weeks.** But the 2025 meta-analysis found that apparent benefits disappeared in non-industry-funded and high-quality-study subgroups, so collagen is promising rather than settled. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40324552/)
2. **Oral hyaluronic acid and phytoceramide-rich wheat extracts have human RCT signals for dry-skin metrics**, commonly around 60–120 mg/day HA or 350 mg/day wheat-oil extract for 6–12 weeks. These are ingredient-specific results, not proof that every capsule works. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41422283/)[[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20646083/)
3. **Carotenoids, lycopene-rich foods, astaxanthin, and Polypodium leucotomos can raise UV-provocation thresholds or reduce erythema in small studies.** They are adjuncts; they do not have an SPF rating and must not replace sunscreen, clothing, or exposure reduction. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804624/)
4. **Vitamin C is essential for collagen formation, but standalone oral vitamin C has weak direct anti-aging trial evidence.** Aim for adequacy; do not assume 1–2 g/day improves wrinkles. The adult UL is 2,000 mg/day, with GI and kidney-stone concerns in susceptible people. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28805671/)[[6]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/)
5. **Zinc, vitamin D, omega-3s, and probiotics are condition-specific tools, not generic beauty supplements.** The best use case is a documented deficiency or a diagnosed acne/eczema phenotype, with strain/formulation and duration specified. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37878272/)[[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39683522/)
6. **Nicotinamide 500 mg twice daily is a medical chemoprevention protocol for selected high-risk patients, not an anti-aging routine.** Benefit stopped after discontinuation, and transplant-recipient evidence is mixed. [[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26488693/)[[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37838527/)
7. **Avoid “everything stacks” logic.** The studies mostly test one product for 8–16 weeks, not a large stack. Start one change at a time, use a defined endpoint, and do not exceed nutrient upper limits without clinician oversight.

## Status / open items

- **Strong:** current human anchors were assembled for collagen, HA, wheat ceramides, carotenoids, astaxanthin, Polypodium, nicotinamide, vitamin D/eczema, zinc/acne, probiotics/acne, vitamin C, glutathione, and biotin.
- **Moderate:** many nutricosmetic RCTs are small, short, female-heavy, product-specific, and industry-funded; exact effect sizes and clinically visible magnitude are often less useful than the direction of the biomarker signal.
- **Open:** no universal “maximum skin” stack has been tested head-to-head. Formulation quality, contaminant testing, actual ingredient assay, diet, UV exposure, baseline nutrient status, and adherence remain major moderators.
- **Added decision aid:** the stack audit closes the practical arithmetic gap for overlapping multivitamins, beauty blends, and single-nutrient products; it deliberately excludes “optimal dose” advice and nutrients without a general UL.

10. [Goh et al., transplant-recipient nicotinamide pilot](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37838527/) — small negative/mixed follow-up trial.
