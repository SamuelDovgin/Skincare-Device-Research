# Oral supplement evidence map

*Compiled 2026-08-15. Confidence labels: **higher** = replicated human synthesis or a reasonably designed human RCT; **moderate** = signal but small, heterogeneous, product-specific, or industry-funded; **low** = sparse, inconsistent, or only surrogate evidence.*

## 0. Bottom line

There is no single oral ingredient that reliably “maximizes skin.” The highest-value decision is matching the ingredient to the endpoint. Oral collagen/HA/wheat-ceramide products are mostly hydration/elasticity experiments; carotenoids and Polypodium are UV-response adjuncts; zinc, vitamin D, omega-3, and probiotics are condition-specific; vitamin C is foundational nutrition rather than a proven oral wrinkle treatment.

| **Oral sodium hyaluronate / HA** | Hydration, TEWL/barrier, elasticity; some wrinkle depth | 60–120 mg/day, 6–12 weeks | **Moderate** | Recent 150-person RCT is encouraging but company-linked; oral HA is not the same as injectable or topical HA. | **Wheat-oil / phytoceramide-rich extract** | Arm/leg hydration, dry-skin scores | 350 mg/day, 84 days | **Moderate-low** | Small, branded extract; wheat allergy and product standardization matter. [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20646083/)
| **Astaxanthin** | Hydration, elasticity; UV-damage markers; wrinkle depth inconsistent | 3–6 mg/day, 8–16 weeks | **Moderate-low** | Meta-analysis improved moisture/elasticity but not wrinkle depth; trials are small and often Japanese/industry-linked. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34578794/)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32202443/)
| **Lycopene / mixed carotenoids / lutein** | UVB erythema threshold, UVA pigmentation threshold, molecular UV response | Food-equivalent lycopene ~16 mg/day; mixed carotenoids for 12 weeks | **Moderate for UV surrogate** | This is photoprotection augmentation, not a sunscreen replacement; beta-carotene pills are unsafe in high-risk smokers. [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20854436/)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32072695/)[[10]](https://www.cancer.gov/types/lung/patient/lung-prevention-pdq)
| **Polypodium leucotomos extract** | UV-provoked erythema/MED, photodermatoses; some actinic-keratosis adjunct data | Product-specific, often 240–480 mg/day in human studies | **Moderate-low** | Standardized extract and indication matter; sunscreen and exposure control remain primary. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40196953/)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28341348/)
| **Vitamin C** | Deficiency correction, collagen biology, wound healing; limited direct oral anti-aging evidence | Meet RDA: 75–90 mg/day for adults; trials of photoprotection used 2 g/day with vitamin E | **Higher for adequacy; low for cosmetic megadose** | Absorption saturates; excess causes GI symptoms and may increase oxalate/kidney-stone risk. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28805671/)[[14]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminC-HealthProfessional/)
| **Vitamin D** | Atopic-dermatitis severity in selected populations | Lab-guided repletion; RCT regimens vary | **Moderate for AD, not general anti-aging** | Association is not causation; avoid high-dose self-treatment and monitor calcium/25(OH)D where indicated. [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39683522/)[[16]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminD-HealthProfessional/)
| **Zinc** | Acne inflammatory papule/lesion count, especially if deficient | Salt and dose vary; usually short-term, clinician-guided | **Moderate for acne; not beauty maintenance** | Nausea and chronic copper depletion; adult UL is 40 mg/day total intake. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32860489/)[[18]](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Zinc-HealthProfessional/)
| **Probiotics / postbiotics** | Acne lesion counts after 12 weeks; AD severity is strain/age dependent | Strain-specific, generally 8–12 weeks | **Moderate-low** | CFU alone is not a protocol; strains, viability, diet, and disease phenotype differ. [[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40832719/)[[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37459239/)
| **Omega-3 fatty acids** | Possible acne inflammatory outcomes; some UV/photoaging signal | Product-specific EPA/DHA; no validated cosmetic dose | **Low-moderate for disease adjunct** | Bleeding and medication interactions; do not infer a wrinkle dose from fish-oil studies. [[21]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37878272/)[[22]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39804624/)
| **Soy isoflavones / S-equol** | Crow’s-feet/under-eye wrinkles and hydration in peri-/postmenopausal women | 40 mg/day isoflavone aglycone; 10–30 mg/day S-equol in trials | **Moderate-low, population-specific** | Estrogen-sensitive conditions, medication context, and equol-producer status matter. [[23]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17484381/)[[24]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21934634/)
| **Glutathione** | Melanin index/brightness, not proven durable disease treatment | 250–500 mg/day for 4–12 weeks in small RCTs | **Low / inconclusive** | Short follow-up, inconsistent findings, limited long-term safety; IV glutathione is outside this topic and unsafe to self-administer. [[25]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30895708/)[[26]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20524875/)
| **Nicotinamide** | New nonmelanoma skin cancers and actinic keratoses in high-risk patients | 500 mg twice daily for 12 months under dermatology care | **Higher for narrow chemoprevention** | Not a general anti-aging dose; benefit did not persist after stopping, and transplant data are mixed. [[27]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26488693/)[[28]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37838527/)
| **Biotin** | Deficiency-related hair/nail disease, not normal skin | No evidence-based cosmetic dose | **Low for healthy people** | High doses can interfere with lab tests; use only when deficiency/indication exists. [[29]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39148962/)[[30]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28879195/)

## 2. How to interpret “significant”

Most endpoints are corneometer hydration, TEWL, cutometer elasticity, wrinkle replicas, melanin index, or MED after a controlled UV exposure. These are legitimate research outcomes, but a statistically significant change may be small, temporary, device-dependent, or invisible in ordinary photographs. A supplement study is strongest when it has placebo control, preregistration, independent funding, a clinically meaningful endpoint, adequate sample size, and follow-up after discontinuation; this literature often has only some of those features.

## 3. What is actually “most researched”

By synthesis volume, collagen, photoprotective carotenoids/plant extracts, and disease-focused nutraceuticals have the broadest human literature. By confidence for a healthy adult seeking visible anti-aging results, the answer is narrower: collagen is the most studied but most contested; HA and phytoceramides have promising hydration signals; astaxanthin and carotenoids have plausible adjunctive UV signals; oral vitamin C is biologically necessary but not a validated wrinkle supplement.

## Evidence gaps

- No head-to-head trial establishes that collagen + HA + astaxanthin beats one ingredient.
- Most trials do not test independent lab assay of the purchased supplement, long-term continuation, or withdrawal.
- Many products are proprietary blends, so the active dose and source cannot be compared cleanly.
- Skin-of-color, male, older, acne-prone, and medically complex populations are underrepresented in cosmetic RCTs.
