# Sculptra FDA Label, Safety, and Complication Boundary

*Compiled 2026-07-26. Research orientation, not medical advice. In an emergency after filler injection—especially vision change, stroke symptoms, skin blanching, or unusual severe pain—seek immediate medical evaluation.*

## 0. Bottom line

Sculptra is an FDA-approved **Class III prescription injectable implant**, not an OTC cosmetic. Its U.S. label supports specific facial-wrinkle and HIV-lipoatrophy uses in defined populations, requires trained professional administration, and warns about delayed nodules plus rare catastrophic intravascular events. “FDA approved” does not approve every injection site, body use, combination, dilution, or technique a clinic may advertise. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_approval_letter_2023.pdf)

## 1. U.S. approval timeline

| Date | PMA action | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 2004-08-03 | P030050 original PMA | Sculptra approved for restoration/correction of signs of facial fat loss (lipoatrophy) in people with HIV. [[2]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_original_approval_letter_2004.pdf) |
| 2009-07-28 | P030050/S002 | Sculptra Aesthetic indication for shallow-to-deep nasolabial-fold contour deficiencies and other facial wrinkles in immunocompetent people where the labeled deep-dermal technique is appropriate. [[3]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf) |
| 2021-11-23 | P030050/S034 | Combined Sculptra/Sculptra Aesthetic under one name and added alternative reconstitution/injection procedures plus lidocaine. [[3]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf) |
| 2023-04-25 | P030050/S039 approval order | Expanded to correction of fine lines and wrinkles in the cheek region in immunocompetent subjects; imposed device-specific training/distribution conditions. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_approval_letter_2023.pdf) |

Devices@FDA listed 53 PMA supplements for P030050 in the July 2026 source pass. A supplement can concern manufacturing, labeling, post-approval studies, or other changes; it is not 53 independent efficacy approvals.

## 2. Current U.S. on-label uses

The 2023 FDA-approved labeling states three intended-use lanes: [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

1. Correction of shallow-to-deep nasolabial-fold contour deficiencies and other facial wrinkles in immunocompetent subjects.
2. Correction of fine lines and wrinkles in the cheek region in immunocompetent subjects.
3. Restoration/correction of signs of facial fat loss (lipoatrophy) in people with HIV.

### Commonly marketed uses that need an off-label label

| Use | U.S. research classification |
|---|---|
| Buttocks/hip dips, thighs, knees, arms, abdomen, cellulite | **Off-label body use.** FDA advises against injectable fillers for body contouring/enhancement, including increasing buttock size. [[5]](source_docs/FDA_dermal_fillers_safety_page_2026-07-26.html) |
| Neck/décolletage | **Not a Sculptra U.S. indication in the captured label.** The FDA class page lists neck as an unapproved filler site generally. |
| Tear trough/periorbital region | **Not studied in the Sculptra IFU; increased papule/nodule risk reported.** FDA class guidance lists periorbital filler use as unapproved generally, although certain other branded fillers have separate under-eye approvals. |
| Lips/lip vermilion | **Do not inject into the red lip under the current manufacturer IFU; granuloma hospitalizations have been associated with lip-vermilion use.** [[6]](source_docs/Galderma_Sculptra_IFU_v4.7_2026.pdf) |
| Nose, glabella, forehead | **Not approved filler sites under FDA class guidance and high vascular-consequence areas.** |
| “Preventive Sculptra” in a wrinkle-free young adult | **No direct pivotal population match.** The cheek trial required moderate/severe wrinkles and had a mean age of 60.7. |

Off-label does not automatically mean improper; it means the FDA has not approved that exact product/use based on a reviewed evidence package. It demands clearer consent and a stronger rationale.

## 3. Contraindicated vs not evaluated vs defer

### Label contraindications — do not use

- Hypersensitivity to any Sculptra component.
- Severe allergies manifested by anaphylaxis or a history/presence of multiple severe allergies.
- Known history of or susceptibility to keloid formation or hypertrophic scarring under the U.S. label.
- If lidocaine is added: allergy to lidocaine or another amide local anesthetic. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

### Defer until resolved/controlled

Active inflammation or infection at/near the intended site—including cysts, pimples, rashes, or hives—should be resolved and controlled before injection. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

### Safety/effectiveness not established or not systematically evaluated

The U.S. label says the evidence is not established for pregnancy, lactation/breastfeeding, or patients under 18. It also identifies evidence gaps for connective-tissue disease, bleeding disorders, active hepatitis, significant disease histories such as cancer/stroke/myocardial infarction, immunosuppressive therapy, and same-session use with other drugs/substances/fillers/implants/devices. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

“Not evaluated” is not identical to a formal contraindication, but it is not evidence of safety. These categories require clinician assessment rather than internet self-clearance.

### Precautions that change planning

- Anticoagulants, thrombolytics, or platelet-affecting substances can increase bruising, hematoma, or bleeding.
- Prior herpes eruption can reactivate after injection.
- Prior surgery can alter vascular anatomy/collateral flow in the area.
- Thin skin and superficial/periorbital/perioral placement increase contour-irregularity and nodule risk.
- Unrealistic expectations are a poor-candidate signal in the current manufacturer IFU. [[6]](source_docs/Galderma_Sculptra_IFU_v4.7_2026.pdf)

Do not stop prescribed anticoagulants or other medicines for a cosmetic procedure without the prescribing clinician's direction.

## 4. Expected reactions and delayed complications

### Common short-term procedure effects

Pain/burning, tenderness, swelling, bruising, redness, itching, hematoma, and palpable lumps/bumps were actively captured in diaries. Most trial symptoms were mild-to-moderate and resolved over days to about two weeks, but diary frequency is not the same as investigator-coded adverse-event frequency. [[3]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

### Papules, nodules, and granuloma

- Papules can be small, palpable, asymptomatic, and nonvisible.
- Nodules can appear 1–14 months after the last injection and may persist up to two years in reports.
- Granulomas usually appear months later; some have required corticosteroids, 5-fluorouracil, subcision, antibiotics when infection is suspected, or surgical excision.
- Prevention depends on correct reconstitution, plane, distribution, avoiding overcorrection, and appropriate anatomy/site selection. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf) [[6]](source_docs/Galderma_Sculptra_IFU_v4.7_2026.pdf)

A nodule is not one diagnosis. Non-inflammatory product deposits, inflammatory/immune granulomas, infection/biofilm, and unrelated lesions require different management. Blindly massaging or injecting a persistent lump without diagnosis can worsen harm.

## 5. Vascular and neurologic emergency boundary

All facial fillers can enter or compress blood vessels. The label warns that embolization/occlusion can cause skin ischemia/necrosis, vision impairment or blindness, cerebral ischemia/hemorrhage and stroke, or injury to underlying structures. Sculptra post-market reports include visual disturbance after temple, periorbital, and/or cheek injection. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

### During or shortly after injection: emergency signs

- Any change in vision, eye pain, eyelid droop, or visual field disturbance.
- Skin blanching, dusky discoloration, a net-like mottled pattern, cool skin, or rapidly worsening pain.
- Facial/limb weakness, speech change, severe headache, confusion, or another stroke sign.
- Unusual severe pain out of proportion to the procedure.

The label directs immediate injection cessation and prompt specialist/medical evaluation. A provider should have a written vascular-event plan, not only a statement that aspiration or cannula use makes the procedure “safe.”

## 6. Reversibility is not the same as biodegradability

Sculptra is biodegradable over time, but it is **not instantly dissolvable with hyaluronidase** as HA gel can be. Saline, massage, steroids, 5-fluorouracil, antibiotics, aspiration/drainage, subcision, or surgery may be used for particular complications, but none is a simple universal antidote that removes established PLLA response. This is a major consent difference versus HA filler. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

## 7. Energy procedures, peels, dental work, and combinations

The Sculptra label warns that laser treatment, chemical peeling, or another procedure based on an active dermal response may elicit an inflammatory reaction at the implant site; the same concern applies if Sculptra is injected before skin has healed from such a procedure. It does not provide one universal safe interval. [[4]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

FDA also states that controlled clinical studies have not established the safe combined use of fillers with botulinum toxins or other treatments, or repeated long-term filler use broadly. A provider's staging rule may be reasonable, but it should be identified as clinical judgment rather than a proven universal schedule. [[5]](source_docs/FDA_dermal_fillers_safety_page_2026-07-26.html)

## 8. Provider and product integrity

- Do not self-inject, use needle-free “hyaluron pens,” or buy filler from consumer online marketplaces.
- Ask to see a sealed, labeled vial and record the lot/expiration in the chart.
- Confirm the injector's professional license, Sculptra-specific training, facial-anatomy experience, and complication pathway.
- Ask whether the proposed area and technique are on-label, off-label, or specifically not studied.
- Ask what happens if a delayed nodule appears six months later and whether the practice will evaluate it.

## Evidence gaps

- Real-world denominator-based rates for delayed nodules, granulomas, vascular occlusion, blindness, and stroke.
- Comparative complication management where HA hyaluronidase is unavailable for PLLA.
- Safety of repeated lifetime courses and common energy-device/filler sequences.
- Outcomes and complications in immunosuppressed, autoimmune, pregnant, breastfeeding, and medically complex patients.
- Independent evidence for body indications and very high-volume use.
