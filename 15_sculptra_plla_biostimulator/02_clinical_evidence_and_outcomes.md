# Sculptra Clinical Evidence and Outcome Map

*Compiled 2026-07-26. Research orientation, not medical advice. Percentages are cohort endpoints, not individualized probabilities.*

## 0. Bottom line

Sculptra has credible randomized evidence for cheek wrinkles and nasolabial-fold correction, with results persisting in selected follow-up cohorts to about two years. The most important caveat is **endpoint spread**: at month 12 in the FDA cheek study, 70.7% met the [blinded live one-grade bilateral GCWS response](09_outcome_scales_and_responder_definitions.md), 94.3% were rated globally improved by treating investigators, 92.0% by subjects, but only 37% were distinguishable by independent 2D photo review. Marketing that chooses only the highest number hides the uncertainty. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

## 1. Evidence hierarchy used here

1. FDA SSED/label clinical tables for the exact branded product and indication.
2. Randomized Sculptra trials and registered extensions.
3. Systematic reviews with risk-of-bias appraisal.
4. Prospective or retrospective Sculptra cohorts.
5. Other PLLA/PDLLA/PCL studies as class/comparator context only.
6. Expert technique articles and manufacturer material as implementation context, not independent efficacy proof.

## 2. Pivotal cheek-wrinkle trial — the strongest current anchor

### Design

The FDA reviewed a prospective, randomized 2:1, evaluator-blinded, no-treatment-controlled multicenter study: 97 Sculptra and 52 control participants, age 41–89 (mean 60.7), with moderate or severe bilateral cheek wrinkles. Treatment was 150 mg/vial reconstituted in 8 mL sterile water plus 1 mL 2% lidocaine, up to four sessions four weeks apart. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

### Month-12 outcomes

| Endpoint | Sculptra | Control | What it means |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Primary: [≥1-grade GCWS improvement in both cheeks](09_outcome_scales_and_responder_definitions.md), blinded live evaluator** | **70.7%** (95% CI 61.1–80.4) | **25.9%** (13.4–38.3) | Best regulatory anchor; 0–4 ordinal scale; both cheeks had to improve concurrently; multiple-imputation estimate; p<0.0001. |
| Observed-case version of primary endpoint | 63/88 (71.6%) | 12/46 (26.1%) | Shows the raw analyzed completer counts behind the similar estimate. |
| Treating-investigator GAIS: any bilateral improvement | 83/88 (94.3%) | 2/46 (4.3%) | High but unblinded and broad “any improvement” threshold. |
| Subject GAIS: any bilateral improvement | 81/88 (92.0%) | 3/46 (6.5%) | Patient-perceived change; expectation and lack of masking can inflate separation. |
| Independent reviewer correctly identified month-12 2D photo | 37% | 16% | Stricter visible-photo discrimination; lower sensitivity and different question. |

### Representation limits

- 96.6% were women; only 5 men enrolled.
- 90.6% were White.
- Fitzpatrick types V and VI totaled 14 of 149 participants (9.4%); type VI was 5 participants (3.4%).
- Mean age was 60.7, so the trial is more informative for mature cheek wrinkles than for a healthy 25-year-old seeking “prejuvenation.” [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

### Extension to month 24

Among 65 returning subjects from the original treatment group, investigator GAIS improvement was 93.8% and subject GAIS improvement was 86.2% at month 24. These support persistence but not a universal two-year guarantee: the extension is smaller, has no concurrent untreated comparator, and includes selected returners. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

## 3. Nasolabial-fold evidence

### Sculptra versus human collagen, 233 participants

The pivotal randomized, subject-blinded, multicenter trial assigned 116 to Sculptra and 117 to human-derived collagen, with one to four bilateral sessions three weeks apart. Sculptra had significantly better mean Wrinkle Assessment Scale change at month 13 and maintained benefit through month 25 in its extension. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20159311/)

Secondary patient outcomes reported overall subject global improvement of 99% at week 3, 91% at month 13, and 81% at month 25 for Sculptra. Human collagen declined from 96% at week 3 to 15% at month 13. More than 80% of Sculptra-treated subjects maintained overall satisfaction at month 25. [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21460676/)

The same trial generated multiple publications. They are not independent replications; counting the Narins, Brown, and Brandt papers as three separate RCTs would inflate the evidence base. [[4]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21719865/)

### 5 mL versus immediate-use 8 mL reconstitution

In a randomized active-comparison study, 21 subjects received the older 5 mL preparation and 59 received 8 mL plus 1 mL lidocaine for immediate use. At week 48, the blinded-evaluator WAS responder rate in the 8 mL group was 74.1% (43/58; 95% CI 61.0–84.7). This supports the newer handling protocol but is not a placebo comparison and is too small to establish rare-event differences. [[5]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf)

## 4. Skin-quality evidence

A randomized, double-blind, multicenter trial enrolled 40 healthy women to three PLLA or saline sessions four weeks apart. At 12 months, PLLA participants showed statistically significant increases in elasticity and hydration and blinded ratings of smoother, more radiant skin with decreased pigmentation, erythema, and pore size. No treatment-related adverse events were reported. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30741790/)

This is promising but exploratory: small sample, many endpoints, and outcomes that do not prove the same effect size in routine practice or an approved indication for each claimed “skin-quality” dimension.

## 5. Evidence synthesis and bias

The 2024 systematic review identified 11 RCTs from 1,467 records. Four reported increased dermal thickness or improvements in lipoatrophy/aesthetic scores sustained at least 25 months; two favored PLLA over human collagen. But 5 of the 11 RCTs had high risk of bias, and the authors concluded that evidence quality for facial rejuvenation was low. Two review authors disclosed Galderma speaking relationships. [[7]](source_docs/PMC11435306_systematic_review_PLLA_facial_aesthetics_2024.xml)

### Confidence by question

| Question | Confidence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Does Sculptra improve studied cheek wrinkles versus no treatment at 12 months? | **High for the specific trial endpoint** | FDA-reviewed randomized trial with blinded live evaluator. |
| Will most patients perceive some improvement? | **Moderate** | High GAIS rates but unblinded, broad threshold, and industry-sponsored program. |
| Will the change be obvious in standardized 2D photos? | **Limited/modest** | 37% photo discrimination versus 16% control at month 12. |
| Can benefit persist to 24–25 months? | **Moderate** | Two extension datasets support persistence; attrition and selection remain. |
| Is Sculptra superior to modern HA or CaHA for facial aging overall? | **Unknown** | No definitive same-indication, adequately powered, contemporary Sculptra-specific head-to-head. |
| Does it reliably “lift jowls”? | **Low** | Projection/skin-quality signals do not equal structural lifting or surgical repositioning. |
| Does each vial create a predictable result? | **Unknown** | No validated dose-response rule across anatomy and patients. |

## 6. Adverse-event evidence in context

Expected diary symptoms were common in the cheek program—pain, tenderness, redness, bruising, swelling, itching, and lumps/bumps—but mostly mild or moderate. Investigator-coded related adverse events were less frequent than diary symptoms because they answer different questions. A low trial count of nodules cannot rule out delayed real-world events that appear months later. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

The SSED post-market section reports delayed nodules, granulomas, infection/abscess, ischemia/necrosis, and visual disturbance including blindness. Voluntary reports cannot provide incidence because exposure and underreporting are unknown. “Rarely reported” is not the same as a quantified personal risk. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_SSED_cheek_wrinkles_2023.pdf)

## 7. Practical interpretation

- Use the **70.7% blinded bilateral cheek responder estimate** as the headline efficacy number for on-label cheek wrinkles—not 94% without context—and [name the 0–4 GCWS threshold](09_outcome_scales_and_responder_definitions.md) whenever space permits.
- Use **37% versus 16% independent photo recognition** as a reality check on how subtle results may look in static images.
- Treat **24–25 month data** as durability evidence among follow-up cohorts, not a warranty.
- Do not use class-level PLLA studies to claim that a cheaper or differently formulated PLLA product is Sculptra-equivalent.
- Ask whether a clinic's before/after uses matched lighting, lens, expression, weight, and time point; a result immediately after injection includes water and edema.

## Evidence gaps

- Independent replication of the cheek trial with balanced sex and race/skin-type enrollment.
- Modern head-to-head randomized comparisons with HA and CaHA using the same indication and blinded photography/3D imaging.
- Registry-quality incidence estimates for delayed nodules, granuloma, vascular events, and repeated courses.
- Standardized definitions for “skin quality,” “tightening,” “lift,” and “natural-looking.”
- Controlled data on maintenance timing, lifetime repeat exposure, and procedure combinations.
