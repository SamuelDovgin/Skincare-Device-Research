# Direct Injectable Collagen Stimulators: Sculptra vs Radiesse, Bellafill, PCL, PDLLA, and Other PLLA

*Compiled 2026-07-26. Research orientation, not medical advice. Product approval, indication, formulation, and availability are jurisdiction-specific. “Collagen stimulator” is a commercial/clinical category, not proof that products are interchangeable.*

## 0. Bottom line

For a **U.S. product decision**, the most useful direct comparison is:

- **Sculptra / PLLA** for gradual, distributed correction with an absorbable particle suspension and no immediate HA-style reversal;
- **Radiesse / CaHA** when immediate gel-carrier support plus a later tissue response better matches the intended contour or product-specific indication; and
- **Bellafill / PMMA-collagen** only when a permanent implant is genuinely desired, the labeled treatment area fits, bovine-collagen skin testing is acceptable, and the patient understands that removal may require a procedure.

PCL, PDLLA-plus-HA, and alternative PLLA products deserve their own **international/emerging evidence lane**. Their material class, carrier, particle geometry, local authorization, and clinical dossier differ. A trial showing that one PDLLA or PLLA product was comparable with Sculptra in one nasolabial-fold protocol does not establish U.S. approval, product equivalence, interchangeability, or equal long-term safety. [[1]](source_docs/FDA_P030050_S039_Sculptra_IFU_2023.pdf) [[2]](source_docs/FDA_Radiesse_IFU_P050052S162_2026.pdf) [[3]](source_docs/FDA_Bellafill_IFU_P020012S009_2015.pdf)

> **Most important distinction:** “stimulates collagen” does not describe a single treatment. These products range from a reconstituted absorbable polymer suspension, to mineral particles in gel, to a permanent PMMA scaffold in bovine collagen, to jurisdiction-specific polymer/HA hybrids. Permanence and reversibility matter more clinically than a marketing claim about which product makes “more collagen.”

## 2. What the direct clinical studies actually show

| Comparison | Design and population | Reported result | What it can support | What it cannot support |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sculptra vs Juvelook Volume (PDLLA + HA)** | Four-center, randomized, evaluator-blinded split-face study; 33 Korean adults; three NLF sessions at weeks 0, 4, and 8; follow-up to week 24 | Mean WSRS change at week 24: **−1.37** (95% CI −1.54 to −1.21) PDLLA side vs **−1.20** (−1.37 to −1.04) Sculptra side; authors concluded non-inferiority; GAIS did not differ significantly | Both protocols improved NLF scores over 24 weeks in this small split-face cohort | Long-term safety/durability, U.S. approval, whole-face equivalence, or superiority. Only 20 answered satisfaction survey; all participants were Korean; study was company-funded. [[5]](source_docs/PMC12802405_PDLLA_vs_PLLA_split_face_2026.xml) |
| **Sculptra vs Gana V (alternative PLLA)** | Randomized, double-blind, split-face non-inferiority trial in France; 55 moderate/severe NLF participants; six-month interim report | WSRS mean change: **−0.25** (95% CI −0.49 to −0.01) Gana V vs **−0.20** (−0.42 to 0.03) Sculptra; abstract reported no adverse-reaction difference and claimed non-inferiority | Short-term, product-specific comparison under one NLF protocol | Regulatory interchangeability or strong long-term equivalence. The abstract reports a non-inferiority p value of 0.1787 without enough detail in the abstract alone to independently audit the claim. [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37626137/) |
| **PLLA-SCA vs Radiesse / CaHA-R** | Randomized 21-subject molecular/transcriptomic study | Different gene-expression pathways were reported after treatment | Mechanistic differences between material responses | Better visible result, longer durability, or fewer complications. It was not a powered clinical-outcome superiority trial. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39480040/) |
| **PLLA vs CaHA in opposing arms** | Five women, three sessions, clinical assessment plus biopsies | Authors reported no clinical difference; histology showed material-localized tissue responses | Histologic proof that each material produces a local particulate response | Facial efficacy, safety incidence, or product ranking; n=5 and off-face anatomy are far too limited. [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36098704/) |
| **PCL vs PLLA for NLFs** | Retrospective, nonrandomized cohort; abstract reports 65 PCL and 71 PLLA patients; 12 months | Published abstract reported lower WSRS scores with PCL at months 3, 6, and 12 and similar recorded safety | A hypothesis-generating product/class comparison | Sculptra-specific superiority: the study used Löviselle PLLA, not Sculptra, treatment selection was not randomized, and the article's methods text reverses the abstract's 65/71 group counts—an internal reporting inconsistency. [[9]](source_docs/PMC12392244_PCL_vs_PLLA_nasolabial_retrospective_2025.xml) |

### Evidence-quality reading

- The most clinically direct Sculptra comparison is still **small and short**: 33 participants followed for 24 weeks.
- The Sculptra–Radiesse comparison is biologically interesting but does not answer “which looks better?”
- A 2024 CaHA controlled-trial review found 13 heterogeneous studies and could synthesize them only qualitatively; sponsor/author relationships and subjective outcomes limit certainty. [[10]](source_docs/PMC10971119_CaHA_controlled_trials_systematic_review_2024.xml)
- A 2026 PLLA/CaHA systematic review reported longer duration ranges for PLLA than CaHA across included studies, but that is largely an **indirect comparison across heterogeneous trials**, not a single randomized Sculptra-versus-Radiesse durability trial. [[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41184662/)

## 3. Best-candidate categories

### Category A — Sculptra-first discussion

Strongest fit when the goal is broad, gradual facial or cheek correction; the person accepts temporary loss of injection-day fullness, staged reassessment, and no simple enzyme reversal; and the current Sculptra label and medical history fit. It is a weaker fit when millimeter-level immediate contour, a reversible preview, or major surgical lifting is the real goal.

### Category B — Radiesse-first discussion

Stronger fit when immediate structural support plus later tissue response is desirable, the treatment area matches current product labeling/clinical practice, and a firmer gel-carrier behavior is intentional. It is not automatically “stronger Sculptra,” and neither product has an instant reversal agent. Diluted/hyperdiluted use must be checked against the exact indication and local IFU rather than inferred from social-media technique labels.

### Category C — Bellafill only after a permanence checkpoint

Consider only when the patient wants a permanent implant, fits the exact labeled NLF or qualifying cheek acne-scar indication, completes the required bovine-collagen skin test, and accepts that future aging, surgery, imaging, or dissatisfaction must coexist with permanent PMMA. The FDA acne-scar pivotal trial found a month-six blinded responder rate of **56/87 (64%)** with Bellafill versus **15/46 (33%)** with saline control, using a stringent scar-based definition; this supports that labeled indication, not generalized whole-face rejuvenation. [[12]](source_docs/FDA_Bellafill_SSED_P020012S009_2015.pdf)

### Category D — HA-first rather than a biostimulator

Choose the HA comparison first when the priority is immediate, precise, potentially dissolvable shaping; the person is uncertain about added volume; or the event timeline does not allow months of remodeling. HA remains subject to the same rare vascular-emergency category and is not “fully reversible” in the sense of undoing tissue injury.

### Category E — international PCL, PDLLA, or alternative PLLA

Reasonable only after verifying the **exact brand, variant, local authorization, IFU, composition, injection site, and complication pathway**. The relevant evidence follows the product—not the acronym. A clinic should not use a Sculptra study to market an unstudied PLLA/PDLLA formulation, or use an Ellansé duration claim for another PCL product.

### Category F — defer or avoid elective particulate filler

Defer for active infection/inflammation, unresolved dental or skin disease near the site, pregnancy/lactation evidence gaps, uncontrolled systemic disease, unrealistic expectations, or inability to obtain follow-up. Product-specific contraindications differ: for example, Bellafill adds bovine-collagen/lidocaine allergy and mandatory skin-testing boundaries. Any history of nodules, permanent filler, immune disorder, bleeding risk, keloid tendency, or prior facial implant requires individualized specialist review rather than a chart-based choice.

## 4. Compare-and-contrast by decision variable

| Decision variable | Sculptra | Radiesse | Bellafill | PCL / PDLLA / other PLLA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Desired timing | Gradual | Immediate carrier + later response | Immediate carrier + permanent scaffold | Product-specific; often hybrid immediate/delayed behavior |
| Distribution goal | Broad/diffuse contour | More structural support or diluted product-specific use | Permanent focal correction in labeled areas | Product/variant/protocol-specific |
| Willingness to stage sessions | Often essential | May require fewer initial sessions, but plan varies | Label/protocol-specific | Trials commonly use product-specific series |
| Permanence tolerance | Absorbable implant but not quickly reversible | Bioresorbable but not quickly reversible | **Permanent** | Usually bioresorbable; verify exact variant |
| Allergy/testing burden | Product-specific allergy history; no Bellafill-style bovine test | Product-specific allergy history | Mandatory bovine-collagen skin test; lidocaine/collagen restrictions | Depends on polymer, HA, lidocaine, and excipients |
| Direct comparative certainty | Strongest product-specific FDA dossier, weak modern head-to-head network | Strong FDA dossier, weak direct Sculptra outcome comparison | Strong label for narrow indications, not a general anti-aging peer | Some direct split-face data, but smaller/shorter and jurisdiction-specific |
| If dissatisfied | Time, diagnosis-specific medication/procedure; no enzyme reset | No HA enzyme reset | Permanent material may require physical removal | HA component may be degradable in hybrids; polymer particles are not enzyme-dissolved |

## 5. Questions that expose a weak clinic comparison

1. What is the exact product, manufacturer, lot source, and current local authorization?
2. Is the proposed area on-label for this product in this country? If not, what is the evidence and why is off-label use preferable?
3. Is the immediate result carrier gel/water/HA, or the durable tissue response?
4. Are the particles absorbable, and what remains at one, two, or five years?
5. What part—if any—can hyaluronidase actually dissolve?
6. Which direct study used this **exact product**, area, number of sessions, endpoint, and follow-up?
7. How does the provider diagnose and manage edema, infection, inflammatory nodule, noninflammatory product nodule, granuloma, and vascular compromise?
8. If Bellafill is proposed, where is the documented skin test and permanence consent?
9. If an international product is proposed, can the clinic show the local IFU rather than a Sculptra or Radiesse paper?

## 6. Claims the evidence does not support

- “All collagen stimulators work the same.”
- “One makes more collagen, so it is better.”
- “Biodegradable means immediately reversible.”
- “PDLLA plus HA is fully dissolvable.”
- “Bellafill lasts five years, therefore five-year safety is proven for every facial use.”
- “This PCL/PLLA product uses the same polymer, so Sculptra's FDA data apply.”
- “Radiesse is one-session Sculptra.”
- “A molecular study proves superior cosmetic results.”

## Evidence gaps

- A large, independent, randomized Sculptra-versus-Radiesse trial using the same facial indication, dose strategy, blinded endpoint, and at least two years of follow-up.
- Denominator-based registries for delayed nodules, granulomas, infection, vascular events, and corrective procedures by exact product.
- Long-term randomized Sculptra comparisons with PDLLA and alternative PLLA products beyond 24 weeks.
- Independent replication across men, diverse Fitzpatrick skin types, immune conditions, and older adults.
- Standardized objective 3D-volume, skin-quality, patient-reported, and cost-effectiveness endpoints.
- Clear regulatory/product-equivalence research for international PCL and PDLLA products.
