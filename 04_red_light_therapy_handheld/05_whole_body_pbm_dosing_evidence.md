# Whole-Body PBM Dosing: Human Protocols and What They Actually Show

*Compiled 2026-07-12; PL300 falloff translation updated 2026-07-13. Research orientation, not medical advice. Surface fluence is not absorbed deep-tissue dose, and a light bed is not geometrically equivalent to a single panel used front and back.*

## 0. Bottom line

There is **no validated universal whole-body dose per session or per week**. The five human exercise/recovery studies in the 2025 systematic review used **13.8–33.6 J/cm² per session**, **16.6–46.17 mW/cm²**, and schedules ranging from one session to daily treatment. The review found no convincing benefit for exercise performance or recovery; limited sleep findings were the only recurring positive signal. [[1]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-025-04318-w)

Condition-specific studies add protocols around **20.2–33.6 J/cm²**, generally three times weekly, but they do not establish a healthy-person “wellness optimum.” The defensible use of this evidence is to compare complete protocols—wavelength, irradiance, session fluence, exposure time, frequency, geometry, population, and endpoint—not to extract one magic weekly number.

### Internal reporting caution

The fibromyalgia RCT reports 28 mW/cm², 20 minutes, and 25.2 J/cm². Those three figures are not arithmetically consistent: 28 mW/cm² × 1,200 seconds equals 33.6 J/cm². The paper may reflect active-on time, geometry, or a reporting error, but it does not explain the mismatch clearly enough to “correct” it. Preserve the reported value and the inconsistency.

## 2. Why weekly J/cm² is not the master variable

Weekly surface fluence is useful bookkeeping, but it does not encode:

- **Delivery rate:** 25 J/cm² over 15–20 minutes at 17–28 mW/cm² is not established as equivalent to the same fluence in 3–5 minutes at ~94 mW/cm².
- **Recovery interval:** twice weekly and daily exposure may produce different responses even at equal weekly totals.
- **Geometry:** NovoTHOR surrounds the body and irradiates a large area simultaneously. A 36 × 12-inch panel treats only part of one surface at a time.
- **Spectral composition:** red/NIR ratios, wavelength allocation, and mode-specific irradiance differ.
- **Area and total joules:** J/cm² at one meter point does not show how much body area receives that dose or the total radiant energy delivered.
- **Endpoint and population:** sleep in athletes, fibromyalgia pain, long-COVID cognition, and general wellness are not interchangeable outcomes.

## 2.1 Does human research support everyday dosing?

It supports that daily or near-daily exposure has been **studied**, not that it is generally better:

- **Whole-body daily:** Zhao et al. used **30 J/cm² every day for 14 days** in 20 female basketball players. Sleep and melatonin improved, but running performance was not better than placebo. This is the clearest daily whole-body protocol in the exercise/sleep literature. [[1]](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10103-025-04318-w)
- **Near-daily, dose-ranging but not whole-body:** Slier et al. exposed the **face, neck, and hands** to 850 nm at 1, 4, or 6.5 J/cm² **five days weekly for four weeks**. The 6.5 J/cm² arm was the only dose with consistent positive well-being/health signals. Because treatment covered about 1,600–1,850 cm² rather than the whole body and used a three-hour pulsed exposure window, it should not be translated directly to a high-output body panel. [[7]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9855677/)
- **Condition-specific whole-body:** the positive fibromyalgia and long-COVID protocols used **three sessions weekly**, generally with about 48 hours between treatments. [[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9845459/)[[6]](https://doi.org/10.1002/jbio.202200391)[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10864543/)
- **No frequency superiority trial:** none of these whole-body studies directly randomized participants to daily versus two or three sessions weekly at otherwise matched parameters. Therefore, the evidence cannot say daily is best.

## 3. Translation to the PL300: the 12-inch model is not settled

The earlier archive used one Lambertian estimate: **94.36 mW/cm² at 12 inches**. Newly supplied seller documentation claims **136 mW/cm² at 12 inches** and **145 mW/cm² at 6 inches**. Preserving that seller curve's shape while normalizing it to the owner's measured 161.145 mW/cm² at 6 inches gives **151.14 mW/cm² at 12 inches**. A rectangular lens-array calculation gives **122.68 mW/cm²** if both channels use 60° lenses, or **140.18 mW/cm²** under an unverified 60° red / 30° NIR split. [[9]](pl300_source_docs/seller_listing_screenshots_2026-07-13/)

That is not a rounding error: the plausible model span at 12 inches is presently **94.4–151.1 mW/cm²**. Until a direct 12-inch reading exists, a single time cannot be defended.

## 4. A defensible personal-experiment lane

For a healthy-person, non-medical experiment, the evidence supports caution rather than an “optimal” number:

1. Measure center and four corners at 12 inches in the exact combined mode.
2. Treat front and back as separate exposed surfaces; do not add them and call the result J/cm² to one surface.
3. A **10–15 J/cm² per side, twice-weekly** starting lane is an inference near the low end of studied whole-body protocols—not a validated recommendation.
4. Keep at least 48 hours between initial sessions and track one outcome (sleep, soreness, or another predefined measure).
5. Do not escalate both dose and frequency together. Avoid heat; PBM protocols are generally intended to be non-thermal.
6. For fibromyalgia, long COVID, wounds, active disease, photosensitizing drugs, suspicious lesions, or eye disease, use clinician-guided protocols rather than this general panel translation.

### General schedule menu, calculated from a measured treatment position

These options hold approximate weekly surface exposure in a similar range while varying frequency. Times should be calculated from the irradiance measured at the body, not from one of the competing distance models:

- **Once weekly:** 20–25 J/cm² per side; 20–25 J/cm² weekly per exposed surface.
- **Twice weekly:** 12–15 J/cm² per side; 24–30 J/cm² weekly per surface; leave 48–72 hours between sessions.
- **Three times weekly:** 8–10 J/cm² per side; 24–30 J/cm² weekly per surface.
- **Four times weekly:** 6–8 J/cm² per side; 24–32 J/cm² weekly per surface; avoid four consecutive days initially.
- **Five times weekly:** 5–6.5 J/cm² per side; 25–32.5 J/cm² weekly per surface.
- **Every day:** 4–5 J/cm² per side; 28–35 J/cm² weekly per surface. This is the lowest-confidence option; daily use has not been shown superior.

For a fixed **10-minute** session, the required irradiance is easy to audit: 8–10 J/cm² requires **13.3–16.7 mW/cm²**, and 12–15 J/cm² requires **20–25 mW/cm²**. The current full-output models at 36 inches span approximately 20.8–63.4 mW/cm², so ten minutes per side could be about **12.5–38.0 J/cm²**. The seller curve stops at 24 inches and cannot settle the 36-inch value.

These are harmonized planning schedules, not clinically validated equivalents. A front-and-back session takes twice the listed time, while the J/cm² remains the dose to each separate exposed surface.

## 5. What would change the conclusion

- A measured 12-inch irradiance grid for this exact panel and each mode.
- Confirmation that the HP350IR is the customized high-range version and within calibration.
- Panel wall draw, dimming linearity, duty cycle, and red/NIR channel output at each brightness setting.
- Larger sham-controlled whole-body trials with standardized dosimetry and meaningful healthy-person endpoints.
- Trials directly comparing equal fluence delivered at different irradiances and session frequencies.
