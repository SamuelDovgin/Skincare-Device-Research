# Whole-Body PBM Dosing: Human Protocols and PL300 Translation

*Updated 2026-07-13 after a targeted PubMed, PubMed Central, Europe PMC, and publisher search. Research orientation, not medical advice. Surface fluence is not absorbed deep-tissue dose, and a light bed is not geometrically equivalent to one panel used on the front and back in sequence.*

## 0. Bottom line

There is still **no validated universal whole-body PBM dose**. The newest fully reconstructable human study is a 2025 randomized crossover light-bed experiment using **129 mW/cm² for 12 minutes = 92.9 J/cm²**. It found an acute resting-energy-expenditure increase in women with obesity, but it was a single-session experiment, skin temperature rose substantially, and it does not establish a chronic wellness schedule. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12608151/)

Most earlier whole-body protocols cluster around **13.8–33.6 J/cm² per exposure**. The 2025 systematic review of five exercise/recovery studies found no convincing exercise-performance or recovery benefit; limited sleep findings were the only recurring positive signal. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39883205/)

The targeted search through 2026-07-13 did not identify a 2026 human full-body result with reconstructable uniform surface dose. Newer 2026 PBM studies located during screening used localized muscle or transcranial devices and therefore did not qualify for this library.

The PL300 visualizer now lets the reader select every distinct, surface-dose-translatable protocol, then shows:

- the study's reported irradiance, fluence, exposure time, geometry, and schedule;
- the PL300 time per side required to match reported surface fluence at the selected modeled or measured irradiance;
- the body-position meter reading needed to match both reported fluence and study exposure time;
- the dose delivered by the proposed **10 minutes per side, three times weekly** plan;
- warnings where a study's irradiance, time, and fluence are internally inconsistent.

## 1. What counts as translatable

A study enters the preset library only when it:

1. treated the body broadly with a bed, full-body panel arrangement, or comparable whole-body field;
2. reported surface fluence directly, or reported both irradiance and time so fluence can be reconstructed;
3. reported treatment time and enough schedule information to understand an exposure;
4. studied humans and reported results, rather than only publishing a protocol.

The **translation grade is about dosimetry completeness, not proof of efficacy**:

- **A — complete:** full paper with reconstructable, internally consistent surface dose.
- **B — usable with a dose conflict:** full paper, but irradiance × time does not equal the stated fluence.
- **C — abstract only:** parameters reconstruct, but the full methods are unavailable.

Follow-up, secondary-outcome, and qualitative papers from the same participants share one dose preset. They remain listed at the publication level, but they are not counted as independent replications.

## 3. Dose arithmetic and the two honest matching modes

Surface fluence follows:

`fluence (J/cm²) = irradiance (mW/cm²) × time (seconds) / 1000`

For a selected PL300 body-position irradiance `Ipanel` and study target `Fstudy`:

- **Match study fluence at full output:** `PL300 time per side = 1000 × Fstudy / Ipanel`.
- **Match study fluence and exposure time:** adjust distance or verified output until the body-position meter reads `1000 × Fstudy / study seconds`.
- **Check a fixed 10-minute session:** `10-minute fluence = Ipanel × 0.6`.

The second mode must be meter-guided. A brightness percentage is only an estimate until dimming linearity and channel duty cycle are measured.

### Internally inconsistent reports

- Both Navarro fibromyalgia protocols state **28 mW/cm²**, **1,200 seconds**, and **25.2 J/cm²**. Irradiance × time gives **33.6 J/cm²**, while 25.2 J/cm² over 1,200 seconds implies **21 mW/cm²**. It is impossible to match all three reported numbers simultaneously.
- Ghigiarelli reports **17 mW/cm²**, **900 seconds**, and **25 J/cm²**. Irradiance × time gives **15.3 J/cm²**, while 25 J/cm² over 900 seconds implies **27.8 mW/cm²**.

The visualizer preserves the paper's stated fluence as the default target, calculates the effective irradiance required to deliver that fluence in the stated time, and displays the conflict.

## 4.1 The proposed 10 minutes per side, three times weekly

At 12 inches and full output, the competing PL300 models predict **56.6–90.7 J/cm² in ten minutes per side**. That is higher than every older whole-body target in this audit and approaches—but generally does not reach—the 2025 acute 92.9 J/cm² experiment.

For a lower **8–10 J/cm²** planning exposure in ten minutes, the body-position meter reading must be **13.3–16.7 mW/cm²**. For **12–15 J/cm²**, it must be **20–25 mW/cm²**. Moving farther away can improve coverage and reduce irradiance, but the panel's output at that distance should be measured rather than inferred from the seller curve, which ends at 24 inches.

Three weekly front/back sessions mean six sequential ten-minute exposures and **60 minutes of total panel time per week**. Dose remains separate for each exposed surface; do not add front and back and call the sum a single-surface J/cm² dose.

## 5. Newer articles reviewed but not converted into whole-body presets

New publication does not automatically mean translatable. These human studies are relevant context but fail the uniform whole-body surface-dose rule:

- A 2025 trial used 940-nm blankets and reported **60 or 300 total joules at multiple sites**. Total joules over selected limbs cannot be converted into uniform whole-body J/cm² without the illuminated area and distribution at every site. [[15]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12368566/)
- A 2024 ICU trial applied a red/NIR flexible blanket sequentially to ten body sites and reported per-LED and per-site energy. It was not one uniform full-body surface exposure. [[16]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11065604/)
- A 2023 type-2-diabetes crossover trial applied 830-nm arrays to selected upper- and lower-limb muscles and reported 100 or 240 J per site. It is multisite localized PBM, not whole-body bed dosimetry. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37171054/)
- Recent running, futsal, and CrossFit trials generally use targeted muscle arrays and total-joule dosing. Their results can inform localized sports PBM, but converting those totals to a whole-body panel fluence would be dimensionally misleading.
- Slier et al. treated the face, neck, and hands for a long pulsed exposure window. It is useful dose-ranging context but not a whole-body preset. [[18]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9855677/)

## 6. Practical interpretation

1. **Choose a study because its population and endpoint resemble the question**, not because its J/cm² is attractive.
2. **Use the meter-guided time-match mode when possible.** Matching fluence in two minutes to a study delivered over twenty minutes assumes irradiance reciprocity that has not been established for this panel.
3. **Treat the 92.9 J/cm² study as a high-dose acute experiment.** Its skin-temperature rise makes it a poor default for unsupervised chronic use.
4. **Do not count companion papers as replication.** The 2023 and 2024 Navarro publications are one randomized cohort/regimen; the separate 2022 Navarro trial remains its own preset. The two Fitzmaurice papers are one feasibility cohort/regimen.
5. **Keep geometry visible.** A clamshell bed illuminates front and back simultaneously. One 36 × 12-inch panel covers a smaller, less uniform area and requires sequential positioning.
6. **Use clinician-guided protocols for fibromyalgia, long COVID, metabolic disease, wounds, active disease, photosensitizing drugs, suspicious lesions, or eye disease.**

## 7. What would improve the PL300 match

- Direct combined-mode irradiance readings at 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36 inches.
- A body-plane grid: center, four corners, and at least one lateral point.
- Measurements at several brightness settings to test dimming linearity.
- Separate red-only and NIR-only readings at the actual treatment distance.
- Skin-temperature tracking during longer exposures.
- Larger independent whole-body trials that compare equal fluence at different irradiances and schedules.
