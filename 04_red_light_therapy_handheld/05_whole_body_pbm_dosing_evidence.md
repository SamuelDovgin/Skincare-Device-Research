# Whole-Body RLT / PBM Dosing: Human Evidence, Optimal-Health Synthesis, and PL300 Translation

*Updated 2026-08-11 after a targeted PubMed, PubMed Central, Europe PMC, ClinicalTrials.gov, and publisher search. Research orientation, not medical advice. Surface fluence is not absorbed deep-tissue dose, and a light bed is not geometrically equivalent to one panel used on the front and back in sequence.*

## 0. Bottom line

There is still **no validated universal whole-body PBM dose**, and no study has shown that a particular dose maximizes “optimal health,” longevity, immunity, hormones, or general wellness. The newest fully reconstructable human study is a 2025 randomized crossover light-bed experiment using **129 mW/cm² for 12 minutes = 92.9 J/cm²**. It found an acute resting-energy-expenditure increase in women with obesity, but it was a single-session experiment, skin temperature rose substantially, and it does not establish a chronic wellness schedule. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12608151/)

Most earlier whole-body protocols cluster around **13.8–33.6 J/cm² per exposure**. The 2025 systematic review of five exercise/recovery studies found no convincing exercise-performance or recovery benefit; limited sleep findings were the only recurring positive signal. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39883205/)

The targeted search through 2026-08-11 did not identify a 2026 human full-body result with reconstructable uniform surface dose. Newer 2026 PBM studies located during screening used localized muscle or transcranial devices and therefore did not qualify for this library.

The evidence-weighted conclusion added below is deliberately narrower than a prescription: for a healthy adult who still wants to run a self-observation trial, I would start at **8–10 J/cm² per exposed surface, 2 nonconsecutive sessions/week**, and only test **10–20 J/cm², 2–3 times/week** if the lower dose is well tolerated and a defined outcome is being tracked. This brackets the older whole-body human cluster without treating the 92.9 J/cm² acute experiment as a target. The companion [whole-body dose-response visualizer](whole_body_dose_response_visualizer.html) shows why the central planning trace peaks around the middle of that window and begins to decline after it; the curve is an illustrative model, not a measured clinical response curve.

The PL300 visualizer now lets the reader select every distinct, surface-dose-translatable protocol, then shows:

- the study's reported irradiance, fluence, exposure time, geometry, and schedule;
- the PL300 time per side required to match reported surface fluence at the selected modeled or measured irradiance;
- the body-position meter reading needed to match both reported fluence and study exposure time;
- the dose delivered by the proposed **10 minutes per side, three times weekly** plan;
- warnings where a study's irradiance, time, and fluence are internally inconsistent.

### 0.1 Does a “condition-specific positive signal” matter?

**Yes, but only within its lane.** A randomized positive result in people with fibromyalgia is meaningful evidence for that disease population and those measured outcomes. It raises confidence that whole-body PBM can have effects under some conditions and justifies independent replication. It does **not** prove a benefit in healthy adults, establish an “optimal health” protocol, or tell us that its plotted J/cm² is the top of a universal dose-response curve.

The strongest green markers here are the two separate Navarro-Ledesma fibromyalgia randomized cohorts. They used the same commercial bed and nominal regimen, both have an unresolved 25.2-versus-33.6 J/cm² reporting conflict, and one publication set included device-company coauthors. Their results therefore remain condition-specific positive evidence—not general-wellness replications. [[4]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9845459/)[[5]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10864543/)[[14]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9687325/)

## 1. What counts as translatable

A study enters the preset library only when it:

1. treated the body broadly with a bed, full-body panel arrangement, or comparable whole-body field;
2. reported surface fluence directly, or reported both irradiance and time so fluence can be reconstructed;
3. reported treatment time and enough schedule information to understand an exposure;
4. studied humans and reported results, rather than only publishing a protocol.

The **translation grade is about dosimetry completeness, not proof of efficacy**:

- **A — complete:** full paper with reconstructable, internally consistent surface dose.
- **B — usable with a dose conflict:** full paper, but irradiance × time does not equal the stated fluence.
- **C — abstract only:** parameters reconstruct, but the full methods are unavailable.

Follow-up, secondary-outcome, and qualitative papers from the same participants share one dose preset. They remain listed at the publication level, but they are not counted as independent replications.

## 3. Dose arithmetic and the two honest matching modes

Surface fluence follows:

`fluence (J/cm²) = irradiance (mW/cm²) × time (seconds) / 1000`

For a selected PL300 body-position irradiance `Ipanel` and study target `Fstudy`:

- **Match study fluence at full output:** `PL300 time per side = 1000 × Fstudy / Ipanel`.
- **Match study fluence and exposure time:** adjust distance or verified output until the body-position meter reads `1000 × Fstudy / study seconds`.
- **Check a fixed 10-minute session:** `10-minute fluence = Ipanel × 0.6`.

The second mode must be meter-guided. A brightness percentage is only an estimate until dimming linearity and channel duty cycle are measured.

### Internally inconsistent reports

- Both Navarro fibromyalgia protocols state **28 mW/cm²**, **1,200 seconds**, and **25.2 J/cm²**. Irradiance × time gives **33.6 J/cm²**, while 25.2 J/cm² over 1,200 seconds implies **21 mW/cm²**. It is impossible to match all three reported numbers simultaneously.
- Ghigiarelli reports **17 mW/cm²**, **900 seconds**, and **25 J/cm²**. Irradiance × time gives **15.3 J/cm²**, while 25 J/cm² over 900 seconds implies **27.8 mW/cm²**.

The visualizer preserves the paper's stated fluence as the default target, calculates the effective irradiance required to deliver that fluence in the stated time, and displays the conflict.

## 4.1 The proposed 10 minutes per side, three times weekly

At 12 inches and full output, the revised point-reading band predicts **31.2–79.2 J/cm² in ten minutes per side**, with the audited center estimate producing **54 J/cm²**. That is still higher than every older whole-body target in this audit, although below the 2025 acute 92.9 J/cm² experiment.

For a lower **8–10 J/cm²** planning exposure in ten minutes, the body-position meter reading must be **13.3–16.7 mW/cm²**. For **12–15 J/cm²**, it must be **20–25 mW/cm²**. Moving farther away can improve coverage and reduce irradiance, but the panel's output at that distance should be measured rather than inferred from the seller curve, which ends at 24 inches.

Three weekly front/back sessions mean six sequential ten-minute exposures and **60 minutes of total panel time per week**. Dose remains separate for each exposed surface; do not add front and back and call the sum a single-surface J/cm² dose.

## 5. Newer articles reviewed but not converted into whole-body presets

New publication does not automatically mean translatable. These human studies are relevant context but fail the uniform whole-body surface-dose rule:

- A 2025 trial used 940-nm blankets and reported **60 or 300 total joules at multiple sites**. Total joules over selected limbs cannot be converted into uniform whole-body J/cm² without the illuminated area and distribution at every site. [[15]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12368566/)
- A 2024 ICU trial applied a red/NIR flexible blanket sequentially to ten body sites and reported per-LED and per-site energy. It was not one uniform full-body surface exposure. [[16]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11065604/)
- A 2023 type-2-diabetes crossover trial applied 830-nm arrays to selected upper- and lower-limb muscles and reported 100 or 240 J per site. It is multisite localized PBM, not whole-body bed dosimetry. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37171054/)
- Recent running, futsal, and CrossFit trials generally use targeted muscle arrays and total-joule dosing. Their results can inform localized sports PBM, but converting those totals to a whole-body panel fluence would be dimensionally misleading.
- Slier et al. treated the face, neck, and hands for a long pulsed exposure window. It is useful dose-ranging context but not a whole-body preset. [[18]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9855677/)

## 6. Practical interpretation

1. **Choose a study because its population and endpoint resemble the question**, not because its J/cm² is attractive.
2. **Use the meter-guided time-match mode when possible.** Matching fluence in two minutes to a study delivered over twenty minutes assumes irradiance reciprocity that has not been established for this panel.
3. **Treat the 92.9 J/cm² study as a high-dose acute experiment.** Its skin-temperature rise makes it a poor default for unsupervised chronic use.
4. **Do not count companion papers as replication.** The 2023 and 2024 Navarro publications are one randomized cohort/regimen; the separate 2022 Navarro trial remains its own preset. The two Fitzmaurice papers are one feasibility cohort/regimen.
5. **Keep geometry visible.** A clamshell bed illuminates front and back simultaneously. One 36 × 12-inch panel covers a smaller, less uniform area and requires sequential positioning.
6. **Use clinician-guided protocols for fibromyalgia, long COVID, metabolic disease, wounds, active disease, photosensitizing drugs, suspicious lesions, or eye disease.**

## 7. What would improve the PL300 match

- Direct combined-mode irradiance readings at 12, 18, 24, 30, and 36 inches.
- A body-plane grid: center, four corners, and at least one lateral point.
- Measurements at several brightness settings to test dimming linearity.
- Separate red-only and NIR-only readings at the actual treatment distance.
- Skin-temperature tracking during longer exposures.
- Larger independent whole-body trials that compare equal fluence at different irradiances and schedules.

## 8. Full-body RLT for general health: what is the best-supported dose?

### 8.1 The answer in one paragraph

For **general wellness in an otherwise healthy adult**, the evidence does not support maximizing light, maximizing weekly joules, or copying the most dramatic study. The best defensible trial window is **10–20 J/cm² per exposed surface per session, 2–3 nonconsecutive sessions per week**, with a **starting dose of 8–10 J/cm²** and at least 48 hours between higher-output sessions. This is an inference from the human whole-body dose cluster, the absence of a demonstrated exercise-recovery benefit, the small and condition-specific positive studies, and the well-established but non-specific biphasic PBM literature. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39883205/)[[19]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315174/)

That is a **study design for learning**, not a medically validated optimum. A useful trial needs one primary endpoint—such as sleep quality, pain score, or a standardized recovery measure—recorded before and during 4–6 weeks of use. Temporary warmth, redness, alertness, or a post-session “glow” is not proof of systemic benefit.

### 8.3 Why “maximum benefit” cannot be read directly from the human points

The human protocols are not a dose-ranging experiment. They differ in wavelength mix, bed/panel geometry, irradiance, body coverage, timing around exercise or sleep, population, endpoint, and outcome quality. A positive 20.2 J/cm² long-COVID pilot and a null 20.2–33.6 J/cm² exercise study do not define a universal optimum; they answer different questions in different bodies.

The biphasic or inverted-U idea is scientifically useful, but the strongest direct demonstrations remain cellular, animal, or localized clinical work. The dose-response review explicitly warns that irradiance and exposure time can matter independently, so equal J/cm² is not automatically equal biology. [[19]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315174/)

### 8.4 The graph's central planning model

The [dose-response visualizer](whole_body_dose_response_visualizer.html) uses a transparent, deliberately non-clinical central trace:

- **x-axis:** incident surface fluence per exposed surface, 0–100 J/cm²;
- **central peak:** 16 J/cm², placed near the middle of the 10–20 J/cm² human planning window;
- **decline:** the trace slopes down after the peak and becomes more visibly lower above roughly 25 J/cm²;
- **high-dose anchor:** 92.9 J/cm² is plotted as a study location, not as a benefit claim;
- **not fitted:** the curve is not fitted to the outcomes because no human whole-body dose-ranging dataset exists.

The graph therefore answers the user's practical question—**where would I expect marginal benefit to stop improving in a cautious planning model?**—without pretending that the literature measured a precise human maximum. In this model, maximum modeled benefit is around **15–20 J/cm²**, the modeled decline begins just after about **16 J/cm²**, and the case for routine wellness use becomes especially weak above **25–35 J/cm²**. Those are planning boundaries, not toxicity thresholds or safety clearances.

### 8.5 Practical dose ladder for a healthy-adult self-observation trial

**Primary dose anchor:** use **10 J/cm² per exposed surface per session**, with **2 nonconsecutive treatment days/week** and one session per treatment day for a 4–6 week tracked trial. The resulting **20 J/cm²/week** is a derived exposure total, not the biological quantity that should drive the planner. This is not a discovered physiological optimum. It is the most defensible compromise between the cautious 8–10 J/cm² starting exposure, recovery spacing, and the absence of evidence that daily or twice-daily whole-body use is superior.

The studies support a hierarchy rather than one interchangeable dose number:

1. **Primary comparison metric:** incident surface fluence per exposed surface **per session** (J/cm²), with irradiance and exposure time reported separately.
2. **Separate schedule variables:** sessions per treatment day, treatment days per week, and spacing/recovery.
3. **Derived bookkeeping metric:** weekly cumulative fluence = session fluence × sessions/day × days/week.

Weekly J/cm² is useful for noticing cumulative escalation, but it is not a validated equivalent-dose metric. A schedule of 25 J/cm² × 2 days, 10 J/cm² × 5 days, and 5 J/cm² × 2 sessions/day × 5 days all total 50 J/cm²/week arithmetically; the whole-body literature does not show that they produce the same biological response. PBM reviews likewise warn that equal fluence can behave differently when irradiance and exposure time differ, so session splitting adds another untested assumption. [[19]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315174/)

| Phase | Surface fluence | Frequency | Decision rule |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Start** | **8–10 J/cm²** | 2×/week, nonconsecutive | Hold for 2 weeks; stop or reduce for persistent heat, headache, eye symptoms, sleep disruption, or skin reaction. |
| **Working test** | **10–15 J/cm²** | 2×/week; a third session only if the endpoint and tolerance justify it | Continue 4–6 weeks while tracking one outcome. |
| **Upper planning window** | **15–20 J/cm²** | 2–3×/week, with recovery days | This is where the illustrative curve is highest; it is not proven to outperform the start dose. |
| **Routine-wellness caution zone** | **>25–35 J/cm²** | Do not escalate just to chase more effect | Human evidence becomes sparse, contradictory, or condition-specific; irradiance/heat and non-reciprocity matter more. |
| **High-dose acute study** | **92.9 J/cm²** | Not a routine schedule | Keep as a research anchor only; do not treat it as an optimal-health prescription. |

For a panel, calculate time from a **measured body-position irradiance**, not from LED wattage or a seller headline: `time (s) = 1000 × target fluence / irradiance`. At the PL300's revised **70 mW/cm²** most-likely point estimate at 6 inches, 10, 15, and 20 J/cm² take approximately **143, 214, and 286 seconds** per exposed surface. At the device.report-weighted **90 mW/cm²** conservative timer anchor they take approximately **111, 167, and 222 seconds**. At the one readable meter-screen value of 161.241 mW/cm², the same targets would take approximately 62, 93, and 124 seconds. None establishes a whole-body area average or that 6 inches is the best geometry.

### 8.6 Frequency, wavelength, and “optimal health” caveats

- **Frequency:** the whole-body evidence does not establish that daily use is superior. A 2025 sham-controlled facial trial using 8.05 J/cm² found no meaningful difference between two and three weekly sessions, an adjacent result that supports testing frequency rather than assuming “more days = more benefit.” It is not a whole-body trial and should not be overgeneralized. [[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40167796/)
- **Wavelength:** 660 + 850 nm is the best-represented whole-body combination in this archive, but the studies do not isolate whether the red/NIR ratio or a particular wavelength drives the observed endpoint. The purchased panel's 630/660/810/830/850 = 2:2:2:1:1 claim is therefore shown as a provisional allocation, not as a clinically validated weighting.
- **Coverage:** a NovoTHOR bed exposes front and back simultaneously. A single panel used front then back gives two surface exposures; do not add them into one larger J/cm² number.
- **Alternating body areas:** using the panel daily can be modeled as an A/B cadence—for example, upper-body front + upper-body back on Day A and lower-body front + back of the legs on Day B—so each defined area is exposed about every 48 hours. That may be a reasonable consistency experiment, but it is **not** proven equivalent to daily whole-body treatment or to the 2–3 nonconsecutive-day whole-body studies. If the goal is to keep the default **20 J/cm² per defined area per week** arithmetic target, seven-day A/B alternation averages **3.5 exposures per area/week**, or about **5.7 J/cm² per area each exposure**; applying the full 10 J/cm² each time would instead average about **35 J/cm² per area/week**. Keep the areas bounded, do not “catch up” with doubled sessions, and treat weekly totals as bookkeeping rather than a biological-equivalence metric. [[19]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315174/)[[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40167796/)
- **Health endpoint:** no whole-body RLT study here demonstrates improved lifespan, all-cause disease prevention, immune “boosting,” testosterone, or a durable general-health composite. Those claims remain unverified.
- **Thermal confounding:** longer/high-output sessions can add heat, vasodilation, and relaxation effects. Record skin warmth and room conditions when testing; do not label an acute metabolic change as purely photochemical.

### 8.7 Translating the evidence to the probable SAIDI/PL300 panel

The companion [SAIDI/PL300 planning visualizer](whole_body_dose_response_visualizer.html) now keeps the device arithmetic on the same page as the evidence curve and power audit. Its two dose sliders are linked directly around **dose per side per session**, the closest match to the study protocols. Changing treatment days or sessions/day changes the displayed weekly cumulative exposure without silently changing the session dose. Changing distance changes modeled irradiance and time without changing the session target. The 6–24-inch distance slider also moves the panel in the side-profile diagram. The added **alternating-area cadence** section estimates per-area exposure, per-area weekly bookkeeping, and total panel time when the user alternates two defined body blocks while using the panel more frequently; it is a planning calculation, not a validated schedule recommendation.

The graph's revised most-likely point curve is **70, 60, 50, and 43 mW/cm² at 6, 12, 18, and 24 inches**. The timer defaults to the device.report-weighted **90, 77, 66, and 57 mW/cm²** curve, with a visible workable interval of **60–90 mW/cm² at six inches**. The earlier 150/135/120/105 curve over-weighted one owner screen and seller/family claims; the 161.241 photo now remains a separate hotspot stress case. The revised model gives special weight to seven LightLab reports that used the same two spectral meters, 49-point grid, stabilization standard, and four-distance protocol, then normalizes measured watts per package to the configuration-specific 360 W claim. It is not an independently measured PL300 distance grid. The tool also allows the owner-photo-scaled seller shape, raw seller curve, a drive-scaled accredited-panel analog, or a custom meter input. The panel's probable identity, meter-photo audit, power estimates, wavelength allocation, and source trail are documented in the [rendered PL300 measurement audit](../markdown-viewer.html?file=04_red_light_therapy_handheld/04_pl300_measured_specs_and_dose_model.md).

For a per-session target `Fsession`, `d` treatment days/week, and `n` sessions/treatment day, the planner uses:

`derived weekly fluence = Fsession × d × n`

`time per side per session (seconds) = 1000 × Fsession / measured mW/cm²`

The front and back are **two sequential surface exposures** unless the setup illuminates both at once. At the default 90 mW/cm² timer anchor, the primary schedule—10 J/cm² per side per session on 2 nonconsecutive days/week—is about **111 seconds on the front plus 111 seconds on the back**. At the 70 mW/cm² most-likely estimate it would take about 143 seconds per side. The visualizer can calculate twice-daily and five-day schedules, but it does not treat equal weekly totals as proof of biological equivalence. This is a radiometric conversion, not evidence that 6 inches is the correct distance or that a more frequent schedule is superior.

The frequency control includes a five-day weekday pattern. Under the primary-dose model, changing from two to five days while holding 10 J/cm² per session increases the derived weekly total from **20 to 50 J/cm²**. The separate experimental five-day preset uses **4 J/cm² per session × 5 = 20 J/cm²/week**, but that arithmetic match does not establish biological equivalence to 10 J/cm² twice weekly.

### 8.8 Best-guess goal presets: systemic wellness versus facial skin

There is no single RLT optimum for “health” and skin aging. Whole-body systemic exposure and a facial skin exposure differ in target tissue, geometry, endpoints, and evidence base. The planner therefore treats front and back as two sequential passes for the wellness preset, but treats the face as **one exposed surface with no back pass** for the skin presets. These are cautious decision presets, not discovered biological maxima.

| Goal preset | Planner setting | Why this is the best current guess | Track after a stable baseline | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Whole-body wellness / systemic-health trial** | **10 J/cm² per side per session**, combined 2:2:2:1:1 mode, **2 nonconsecutive days/week**, 6 inches | Starts at the low end of the 10–20 J/cm² whole-body planning cluster without assuming that the illustrative 16 J/cm² curve peak is a clinically proven optimum. | One prespecified outcome for 4–6 weeks: sleep timing/quality, pain, mood, or recovery—not a composite chosen after seeing the result. | **Low**; no healthy-adult whole-body dose-ranging trial. |
| **Skin anti-aging / wrinkles** | **5 J/cm² to the face once per session**, combined red+NIR, **3 days/week**, 24 inches | A practical midpoint among facial trials using 3.8 J/cm² red, 8.05 J/cm² red, and approximately 5.4 J/cm² red+NIR. Red-light trials report modest wrinkle/texture signals, but no study establishes 5 J/cm² as the optimum. [[20]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40167796/)[[25]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36780572/)[[26]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39960921/) | Standardized no-makeup photographs at baseline and weeks 4, 8, 12, and 16; fixed distance, lens, lighting, angle, expression, and time of day. Compare the same crow's-feet/forehead regions for line visibility and texture. | **Moderate for a modest wrinkle signal; low for the exact optimum.** |
| **Pigment-prone / uneven tone tolerance test** | **2 J/cm² to the face once**, red-only 630+660 nm, **2 days/week**, 24 inches | The PL300 lacks the 590 nm amber band used in some pigmentation hypotheses. A small 2025 amber-PBM melasma pilot was underpowered and did not significantly improve MASI or physician global assessment; a review concludes that robust trials are still needed. [[27]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40650752/)[[28]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38018017/) | Standardized photos and the same marked cheek/forehead regions every 2–4 weeks. Stop for persistent darkening, mottling, or heat. Daily tinted broad-spectrum sunscreen remains the primary intervention lane. | **Very low efficacy confidence**; this is a safety-first test, not a melasma treatment. |
| **Diffuse inflammatory redness test** | **3 J/cm² to the face once**, red-only 630+660 nm, **2 days/week**, 24 inches | Red PBM has anti-inflammatory plausibility, but direct facial-erythema dose trials are sparse and visible vessels are not the same endpoint. | Photograph baseline redness before treatment and again the next morning, with fixed lighting and no recent exercise, hot shower, alcohol, or skincare actives. Track persistent baseline redness rather than the temporary post-light flush. | **Low**; not a vascular-rosacea or telangiectasia prescription. |

The 24-inch facial distance is a geometry and heat-control choice, not a literature-derived optimum. The PL300's modeled irradiance remains substantially higher than most studied masks, so matching J/cm² with a shorter exposure assumes reciprocity that may not fully hold. [[19]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3315174/) If red-only or NIR-only mode is selected, the planner estimates irradiance at **50% of combined output** from the purchased 2:2:2:1:1 allocation; that is a workable engineering assumption, not a measurement. A treatment-plane reading in the selected mode should replace it when available.

For all skin presets, make one change at a time, hold the rest of the skincare routine stable, avoid perceptible heating, and stop for persistent irritation, darkening, eye symptoms, or worsening redness. Pigmentation disorders, rosacea, photosensitizing medication use, and active skin disease warrant dermatologist review rather than dose escalation in the simulator.

### 8.9 Shark, TheraFace, and equal-side whole-body comparator presets

The Shark CryoGlow and original TheraFace regulatory tables mix two different quantities: **total radiant energy across an LED array (J)** and **fluence reaching a unit area of skin (J/cm²)**. The large 431.4, 568.8, and 618.16 figures are aggregate array-energy calculations; they should not be loaded into the PL300 planner as uniform facial or body-surface J/cm². Shark's 510(k) table labels some of these aggregate values as J/cm², but the disclosed irradiance, time, battery energy, and independent measurement do not support that interpretation. [[29]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242796.pdf)

| Comparator | Official information | Independent treatment-position result | Weekly arithmetic used by the planner |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shark CryoGlow Better Aging** | 630 + 830 nm, about 6–6.4 minutes daily. The 510(k) gives 64 + 64 = 128 mW/cm² and 431.4 J/cm² “total dose for all LEDs,” but 128 mW/cm² × 384 seconds equals only 49.2 J/cm² even before questioning whether 128 is a facial-plane average. [[29]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf24/K242796.pdf) | A calibrated JETI spectroradiometer test measured about **1.44 J/cm²** at the realistic 16 mm wearing distance for Better Aging; one device, non-accredited commercial reviewer. [[30]](https://www.benrawlings.com/blog-1/2025/2/16/shark-cryoglow-led-mask-review-tested-by-a-professional-led-device-designer) | Preset rounds to **1.5 J/cm² × 7 = 10.5 J/cm²/week** to the face. |
| **Original TheraFace red + red/NIR portion** | The original filing reports 73 mW/cm² red and 55 mW/cm² NIR with three minutes per mode. Therabody now distinguishes aggregate total energy in J from per-area doses around 10–15 J/cm² per light segment. Red and blue arithmetic reconcile; 55 mW/cm² × 180 seconds gives 9.9 J/cm² NIR rather than Therabody's listed 11.52, leaving a small unresolved inconsistency. [[31]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K230293.pdf)[[32]](https://www.therabody.com/blogs/news/theraface-mask-your-most-common-questions-answered) | A spectrometer review measured about **7 J/cm² across the first six minutes** of red and red+NIR, with nonuniformity. The reviewer received a complimentary unit and uses affiliate links. [[33]](https://www.lighttherapyinsiders.com/theraface-red-light-review-therabody-mask-tested/) | Preset uses **7 J/cm² × 7 = 49 J/cm²/week** to the face, matching the daily schedule used in Therabody's 12-week manufacturer-sponsored study. |

The simulator exposes five complete presets at the top:

1. **Whole-body wellness:** 10 J/cm² per side × 2 days = 20 J/cm² to the front and 20 J/cm² to the back each week.
2. **Wrinkle support:** 5 J/cm² to the face × 3 days = 15 J/cm²/week.
3. **Shark measured match:** 1.5 J/cm² to the face × 7 days = 10.5 J/cm²/week.
4. **TheraFace measured match:** 7 J/cm² to the face × 7 days = 49 J/cm²/week.
5. **Higher full body:** 10 J/cm² per side × 5 days = **50 J/cm² to the front and 50 J/cm² to the back each week**.

The higher full-body preset therefore meets or exceeds both independent face-device weekly fluence numbers **on each body side separately**. The front and back timers are locked equal. This does not make the exposures biologically equivalent: treating an entire front and back uses vastly more total radiant energy and illuminated tissue area than treating a face, and no whole-body trial validates five-day use or a TheraFace-derived weekly target. The preset is intentionally labeled an experimental arithmetic comparator.

### 8.10 Translating the seated plan into upper-body and lower-body coverage

The panel planner can be expanded from two broad passes to four defined coverage passes:

| Pass | Position | What the pass is for | Dose rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Seated, upper-body front** | Chest, abdomen, and the front-facing upper-body field | Use the selected local surface target. |
| 2 | **Seated, upper-body back** | Back-facing upper-body field after turning the chair/person 180° | Match the front dose and timer. |
| 3 | **Standing, lower-body front** | Front of thighs, knees, shins, and the forward-facing glute/hip region | Start at 5–8 J/cm² for 2–3 complete sessions, then approach the selected target if tolerated. |
| 4 | **Standing, lower-body back** | Hamstrings, calves, and the rear glute/hip region | Match the lower-body front dose and timer; avoid creating a large overlap with the upper pass. |

The best planning metric remains **local surface fluence per illuminated region per session**. Adding more body regions should increase the number of passes and the total time on the treatment day; it should not multiply the J/cm² target for each region. For example, a 10 J/cm² target used twice weekly gives each of the four defined regions 20 J/cm²/week if all four passes are completed. It does **not** turn into 80 J/cm²/week for each region, and it does not establish that 80 J/cm²/week over the entire body is optimal.

The timer is recalculated from the actual treatment-position irradiance:

> **time (seconds) = 1,000 × target J/cm² ÷ irradiance mW/cm²**

At the current PL300 planning curves, 10 J/cm² is approximately **1.9 minutes per pass at 90 mW/cm²**, **2.4 minutes at 70 mW/cm²**, and roughly **2.8–3.3 minutes** across a 60–50 mW/cm² working estimate. Four passes therefore take about **7.4 minutes**, **9.5 minutes**, or **11.2–13.3 minutes** respectively before any twice-daily split. If the panel is farther away, angled, or partly blocked by body curvature, use a body-position meter reading when available rather than assuming the seated value transfers unchanged.

This is a coverage recommendation, not a new clinical dose claim. The human whole-body studies generally used a broad light bed or clamshell that exposed a large body area in one setup, including the NovoTHOR configurations described in fibromyalgia and recovery research [[6]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10525895/) [[14]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9687325/). They do not directly compare seated versus standing positions, upper versus lower passes, or a half-body consumer panel used four times. A supine light-bed study also reported heat-management considerations during exposure [[11]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7739664/), so lower-body passes should be introduced as a tolerance check rather than assumed to be interchangeable with a short facial session.

For practical coverage, keep the panel face parallel to the region, reproduce the distance, and use a 12–18 inch setup if six inches leaves only a narrow central strip. Mark the chair and foot positions so the upper and lower passes are repeatable. Do not chase a larger weekly number simply because the four-pass layout feels more complete; the evidence supports consistency and defined endpoints more strongly than it supports an “optimal” whole-body total.

## 9. Evidence gaps that would change the recommendation

- A randomized, sham-controlled whole-body dose-ranging trial comparing roughly 5, 10, 20, 35, and 75+ J/cm² with the same wavelength ratio, geometry, and follow-up.
- Separate irradiance and exposure-time arms to test whether reciprocity holds at whole-body distances.
- Direct temperature, spectral, and irradiance-grid measurements for consumer panels at the actual body position.
- Larger trials in healthy adults with preregistered sleep, mood, pain, cardiometabolic, and adverse-event endpoints—not only acute surrogate changes.
- Independent replication of the 2025 metabolism result with sham control, longer follow-up, and thermal monitoring.
