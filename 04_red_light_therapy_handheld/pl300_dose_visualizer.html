The biphasic idea

PBM often behaves like hormesis: a small dose may do little, a middle range may stimulate useful signaling, and a higher dose may lose benefit or become inhibitory. The location of that “sweet spot” is not fixed.

  • Fluence is energy per area, J/cm².
  • Irradiance is delivery rate, mW/cm².
  • Reciprocity can fail: 8 J/cm² delivered very quickly at high irradiance may not equal 8 J/cm² delivered slowly.
  • Frequency matters: cumulative stimulation and recovery time can shift the response.

How certain is “optimal dosing”?

Strong PBM can show non-linear dose responses under controlled laboratory conditions.

Moderate Red LED regimens can improve selected photoaging outcomes in several human studies.

Low There is no proven single dose that maximizes “whole-body health,” longevity, hormones, immunity, or athletic recovery.

The 2025 whole-body systematic review found only five studies and no convincing exercise-performance or recovery benefit; possible sleep findings were preliminary.

Eight-week face plan

Weeks 1–2: 4 J/cm², Monday + Thursday.

Weeks 3–8: 6 J/cm², Monday + Thursday. Increase to 8 only after at least two symptom-free weeks.

Maintenance: 6–8 J/cm² once or twice weekly, based on whether benefits persist.

Take standardized photos every four weeks under the same lighting. Do not judge efficacy by temporary post-treatment redness or “glow.”

What human whole-body studies actually dosed

There is no validated universal weekly target. The 2025 systematic review found five exercise/recovery studies using 13.8–33.6 J/cm² per session and found no convincing performance or recovery benefit; sleep was the only limited recurring signal. A newer 2025 acute metabolism study used 92.9 J/cm², far above that cluster, and should be read as a high-dose single-session experiment rather than a routine target.

Lens-array equation and assumptions

Angular intensity is modeled as I(θ) ∝ cosm(θ), where m = ln(0.5) / ln(cos(β/2)) for a full-width-at-half-maximum beam angle β. The center-axis factor is integrated across the rectangle: J(d,m) = ∬ dm+1 / (d²+x²+y²)(m+3)/2 dxdy. Each channel is then normalized to its 6-inch reading. This produces a central point estimate, not an area average, and omits LED spacing, side lobes, body curvature, spectral-meter response, and manufacturing variation.

Observed falloff from comparable large panels

Each online curve is normalized to its own 6-inch center reading, so this compares shape rather than absolute output. The PL300 seller retains 93.8% at 12 inches and 72.4% at 24 inches; after adding five current HP350IR curves and one LightLab curve, the expanded comparison medians are 85.2% and 63.8%. The seller's shape is possible but optimistic, while the old Lambertian calculation remains a steep-falloff sensitivity bound.

Recent PBM biphasic and dose-response research map

This is a structured evidence map from targeted PubMed/PMC and publisher searches, emphasizing 2023–2026. It is broad but not a formal PRISMA systematic review. “Direct biphasic” means the study actually tested enough doses to observe a non-linear response; many “dose-ranging” studies only compared a few doses and cannot prove the full inverted-U.

Stop or reduce the dose when

  • Redness or warmth lasts into the next day.
  • Skin becomes drier, irritated, itchy, or darker.
  • You develop headache, eye pain, visual afterimages, agitation, or unusual fatigue.
  • A benefit disappears after dose escalation—this can be a clue you moved past your useful range.

Drop the dose by 25–50%, reduce frequency, or extend the distance. Change one variable at a time.

Extra caution

  • Use proper opaque eye protection and never stare at the LEDs.
  • Ask a clinician about photosensitizing medications, active cancer or an undiagnosed lesion, pregnancy, photosensitive epilepsy, significant eye disease, or treatment over a medically unstable area.
  • For melasma or pigment-prone skin, use lower doses and monitor carefully; visible light and heat can worsen pigmentation in susceptible people.
  • Do not use this calculator to treat diabetic ulcers, infections, acute injuries requiring diagnosis, or neurologic/psychiatric disease.

How to make the model trustworthy

  1. Measure center irradiance at 12, 18, 24, 30 and 36 inches in the exact mode you use.
  2. Measure at face center and four corners to estimate uniformity.
  3. Enter the actual irradiance using “custom” rather than relying on distance math.
  4. Keep distance, angle, dose, frequency, skin products and time of day stable for at least two weeks before judging a change.
Prepared as an educational synthesis. Surface fluence is not the same as absorbed dose at a deep target. Device claims, laboratory cell doses, contact-laser doses, LED-mask doses and whole-body panel doses are not automatically interchangeable.
