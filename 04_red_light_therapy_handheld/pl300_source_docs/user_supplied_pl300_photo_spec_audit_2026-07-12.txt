Main research conclusion
A biphasic or inverted-U response is well supported in cellular and animal PBM research: insufficient exposure may have little effect, an intermediate range may stimulate useful responses, and higher exposure can lose efficacy or become inhibitory. But the location of that peak changes with wavelength, irradiance, exposure time, treatment frequency, tissue and biological endpoint. Two treatments delivering the same J/cm² at very different irradiances are not necessarily biologically equivalent. 

The evidence is much less precise in humans. Recent direct dose-response research includes:

A 2024 stem-cell study in which 830 nm at 5 J/cm² performed better than 2.5 or 10 J/cm² for several outcomes.

A 2024 erythrocyte study that found lower NIR exposures antioxidative while higher exposure became detrimental.

A 2025 inflammatory-pain animal study in which 5 J/cm² produced a stronger analgesic response than 2.5 or 10 J/cm².

Recent wound, muscle and transcranial studies comparing several doses, although these cannot be directly converted into a home-panel protocol. 

For facial skin, recent human studies have reported benefits with regimens including:

3.8 J/cm², ten treatments over four weeks

8.05 J/cm², two or three times weekly

15.6 J/cm², twice weekly in a small uncontrolled study

Importantly, the 2025 frequency trial did not find a meaningful advantage from three weekly sessions over two. Taken together, these studies support starting in the lower-middle portion of the published range rather than immediately pursuing the largest cumulative dose. 

For broad “maximal health” or whole-body benefits, the evidence is substantially weaker. A 2025 systematic review found only five whole-body PBM studies involving 105 participants and did not find convincing exercise-performance or recovery benefits. Recent dose-ranging exercise trials have also produced negative or inconsistent findings.
