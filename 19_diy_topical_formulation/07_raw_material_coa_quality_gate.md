# Raw-Material COA and Supplier Quality Gate

*Compiled 2026-08-23. Educational supplier-document audit, not laboratory verification, GMP certification, identity testing, or permission to formulate a facial product. A certificate of analysis (COA) can be genuine and still be insufficient, stale, copied, or unrelated to the lot received.*

## 0. Bottom line

The largest unresolved risk in a DIY powder project often occurs before weighing: **is the bag the named material, from a traceable lot, with actual test results that match meaningful specifications?** The [raw-material COA checker](raw_material_coa_checker.html) separates three outcomes:

- **stop—identity/traceability gap**;
- **hold—document is incomplete for the intended use**;
- **document-ready for further verification—not proven authentic or suitable**.

It deliberately does not award a comforting percentage score.

## 1. Minimum document fields

| Gate | What to look for | Why a pass-only claim is weak |
|---|---|---|
| Material identity | Exact chemical/INCI name, CAS or compendial identity where applicable, grade, manufacturer | “Vitamin C powder” does not distinguish identity, form, grade, or source |
| Lot traceability | Lot/batch number on COA matches the received package; analysis/release date; authorized issuer | A generic PDF may describe another batch |
| Identity test | Named method and actual result, not only “conforms” | Appearance or solubility alone may not discriminate the material |
| Assay/purity | Specification, actual numeric result, units, and method | “99%” without method/result basis is not auditable |
| Relevant contaminants | Microbial limits for water-containing/cold-process risk; heavy metals/residual solvents or other hazards appropriate to source | A COA should be risk-specific; not every material needs the same panel |
| Handling | Storage, packaging, retest/expiry basis, and supplier contact | Stability and mix-up risk continue after testing |

FDA's cosmetic GMP inspection guidance asks whether raw materials are labeled by identity, lot, and control status; sampled/tested for relevant contamination; and examined for identity and physical, chemical, microbial, and hazardous-contaminant specifications. [[1]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-guidance-documents/good-manufacturing-practice-gmp-guidelinesinspection-checklist-cosmetics)

## 2. COA is not identity proof by itself

The tool treats these as critical stops:

- lot on the package does not match the COA;
- exact material identity/grade is absent;
- no identity method/result is reported;
- issuer/manufacturer cannot be traced;
- document appears edited, contradictory, or outside its stated validity/retest period.

FDA's Q7 guidance for active pharmaceutical ingredients is **not a home-cosmetic legal standard**, but it provides a useful high-control benchmark: at least one identity test per batch and periodic checks of supplier-COA reliability rather than blind reliance on a supplier document. [[2]](https://www.fda.gov/regulatory-information/search-fda-guidance-documents/q7a-good-manufacturing-practice-guidance-active-pharmaceutical-ingredients)

## 3. Risk-specific fields

The intended use changes what is material:

- **Botanical/animal-origin/cold-process material:** microbial and species/adulteration questions move up.
- **Mineral or colorant-like material:** elemental impurity/heavy-metal specifications may matter.
- **Solvent-manufactured or synthesized active:** residual solvent and synthesis-impurity limits may matter.
- **Hyaluronic acid or peptide:** molecular-weight/sequence/assay basis and water content may be essential to interpreting the claimed amount.
- **Acid or base:** assay basis, salt/hydrate form, and water content affect concentration math.

The checker asks whether the COA addresses **relevant** hazards; it does not invent a universal numerical specification.

## 4. Practical workflow

1. Request the COA for the exact lot before purchase or use.
2. Match package, invoice, manufacturer, material name, grade, and lot.
3. Require actual results and methods for identity and assay.
4. Identify which contaminant tests are relevant to the source and intended use.
5. Save the original PDF, purchase record, photos of the sealed label, and receipt date.
6. If identity cannot be independently verified, treat the material as an unknown—not as cosmetic/USP/pharmaceutical grade because the listing says so.
7. A strong document still does not validate a water formula's preservation, shelf life, skin delivery, or irritation profile.

## Evidence gaps

- Home users usually cannot qualify the supplier or independently reproduce chromatographic, spectroscopic, microbial, or elemental tests.
- There is no universal COA template or contaminant panel for every cosmetic raw material.
- Marketplace seller identity can differ from the actual manufacturer or repacker.
- Passing this gate does not validate a recipe, preservative system, challenge test, packaging, or clinical use.
