How each variable likely changes the result

Arrows describe the usual direction for the same generator and water—not a guaranteed prediction. PWPAM mode has no independent salt, vinegar, time, or volume adjustment because its manual does not establish those controls.

Planned volume ↑Time should usually rise for the same target ppm

At unchanged current and efficiency, doubling volume doubles the total chlorine mass needed for the same FAC concentration. The planner scales the local time estimate by planned volume ÷ reference-run volume, then requires FAC and pH testing of the new batch.

Salt concentration ↑Conductivity and FAC often rise

The cited report describes higher initial salt producing higher FAC, but excess salt can change current and electrode stress. This planner does not offer an independent salt slider.

Permitted vinegar ↑Starting pH usually falls

More acid can shift the starting mixture lower, but water alkalinity and electrolysis determine final pH. Do not use extra vinegar to chase the final reading, and never add it after electrolysis.

Run time ↑FAC usually rises locally; pH can drift

For one calibrated setup, a modest time increase often raises FAC. The curve can plateau, and pH, FAC, and ORP can be time/current dependent.

Planning target changesThe comparison benchmark changes—not the batch

The target changes the time estimate and dilution math. It does not alter salt, vinegar, current, or measured chemistry.

Final pH risesThe estimated HOCl share falls

At pH 5–6, most measured FAC is estimated as HOCl. Near pH 7.5, the estimate is about half HOCl and half OCl⁻. pH does not tell you total FAC, so both tests are required.

What to test, and when

  1. Prepare one documented batch.
    Record purpose, label, device/cell, water source, manual row, and packaging.
  2. Run the manual-approved cycle.
    Use ventilation; do not lean over the vessel or use odor as a concentration test.
  3. Take clean aliquots.
    Do not contaminate the storage bottle or return tested liquid.
  4. Read the matched strip promptly.
    For PWPAM, choose the closest 20–1,500 ppm color only after following the strip package’s timing.
  5. Log pH only when it is relevant.
    PWPAM does not provide a pH workflow; an optional pH result can document why a batch is not a mildly acidic HOCl comparison.
  6. Repeat or stop.
    For PWPAM, repeat the same manual row once; do not adjust salt, add acid, or interpolate time. Never rescue a finished chlorine-containing batch with acid.

How the bands and uncertainty work

Measured QC band

Preferred: FAC 180–220 ppm and final pH 5.5–6.2. Working: FAC 150–250 ppm and pH 5.0–6.5. Recalibrate: FAC 100–149 ppm or pH 4.0–4.9 / 6.6–7.0. Stop: FAC above 250 for this project, pH below 4 or above 7, or a non-numeric failure signal.

Process-repeatability confidence

A single paired reading is low confidence. Better-range methods plus repeated fresh batches in the same band can raise process-repeatability confidence. This never becomes confidence in skin safety, sterility, shelf life, acne benefit, or regulatory disinfection.

Volume-aware time estimate

No next-run projection exists until a FAC result is entered. When available, the spread is an illustrative sensitivity band—not a statistical confidence interval.

Model boundary

FAC is measured. HOCl share is calculated from measured pH using an approximate pKa of 7.5. The estimate does not assay HOCl, contaminants, sterility, or stability.

HOCl fraction ≈ 1 / (1 + 10^(pH − 7.5)) estimated HOCl ppm ≈ measured FAC × HOCl fraction planned time ≈ reference time × (planned volume / reference volume) × (planning target / measured reference FAC) (FAC correction capped; fresh batch only) dilution water = current volume × (measured FAC / planning target − 1)

Use-specific guardrails

PWPAM face-contact boundary: the supplied sodium-hypochlorite manual is cleaning-oriented, specifies rinsing/ventilation, and does not provide a facial or pillowcase-use path. Keep its output off skin and face-contact bedding regardless of strip color.
Other documented-generator fabric experiment: only after the generator’s manual supports that recipe and you have a paired FAC+pH result. Launder first; use clean, colorfast fabric; apply even coverage; let it dry fully. The rayon study was not a cotton-pillowcase or acne study.
Face boundary: prefer a finished facial/dermal HOCl product. A DIY FAC+pH result does not establish sterility, electrode purity, eye safety, irritation safety, shelf life, or facial suitability. Never use pool tablets.
