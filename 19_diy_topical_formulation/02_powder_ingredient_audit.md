# Powder ingredient audit

*Compiled 2026-08-15. Research orientation, not medical advice. “DIY candidate” means suitable for a controlled learning experiment, not proven safe for long-term home manufacturing.*

## 0. Bottom line

The highest-value powders are not necessarily the most powerful. The best DIY candidates are soluble, dose-tolerant, chemically boring, and supported by human evidence. The matrix below deliberately separates **efficacy** from **home-formulation fit**.

## 1. Decision key

- **Good first candidate:** simple enough for a single-ingredient, fresh or well-controlled prototype; still requires patch testing and mass-based measurement.
- **Conditional candidate:** plausible but needs pH control, a compatible base, or a preservation plan.
- **Buy finished:** the powder may work, but the home vehicle/solubility/stability problem dominates.
- **Do not DIY:** drug-like, hazardous, highly irritating, poorly characterized, or too easy to mis-dose.

## 2. Main audit

| Powder / form | Best-supported role | Solubility / pH burden | Storage and failure modes | DIY verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L-ascorbic acid** | Antioxidant, uneven tone, collagen-support signal | Water soluble; meaningful delivery below pH 3.5; sting/oxidation burden | Oxygen, light, heat, metals, and time drive oxidation; yellow/orange/brown color is a discard warning, not a potency meter | **Good first candidate, fresh only** [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11207686/)[[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37128827/)
| **Glycolic acid** | Texture, discoloration, photoaging maintenance | Soluble, but pH/free-acid fraction and buffering are coupled; low pH irritates | Water formula needs preservation; too-low pH/high free acid can burn and increase PIH risk; UV sensitivity | **Conditional; finished product usually better** [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9598014/)[[4]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/alpha-hydroxy-acids)
| **Niacinamide** | Barrier support, blotchiness, hyperpigmentation, texture | Water soluble; generally comfortable around mildly acidic-to-neutral pH | Relatively stable, but water batch still needs preservation; very acidic/high-heat conditions are undesirable | **Good conditional candidate** [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/)[[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21822427/)
| **N-acetyl glucosamine (NAG)** | Uneven pigmentation; pairs with niacinamide | Water soluble; formulation-friendly in published cosmetic work | More stable than glucosamine; contamination remains the main home risk | **Good conditional candidate** [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348991/)
| **Tranexamic acid** | Melasma/PIH adjunct; evidence is mixed and formulation-specific | Water soluble; needs pH and concentration control; not a sunscreen substitute | Water batch preservation; pigment recurrence/diagnosis not solved by DIY | **Conditional, buy finished if possible** [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22506692/)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37213446/)
| **Alpha-arbutin** | Pigment adjunct | Water soluble; stability and hydroquinone-trace quality matter | Light/heat/pH and raw-material quality; SCCS opinion gives face-cream/body-lotion limits, not a DIY shelf life | **Conditional; COA and finished formula preferred** [[10]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/safety-alpha-arbutin-and-beta-arbutin-cosmetic-products_en)
| **Kojic acid** | Pigment/tyrosinase adjunct | Water solubility and pH/vehicle can be limiting | Oxidizes/discolors; weakened barrier can increase absorption; SCCS safety opinion considers up to 1% in cosmetics | **Buy finished; poor first DIY candidate** [[11]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/kojic-acid_en)[[12]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39555866/)
| **Hyaluronic acid / sodium hyaluronate** | Surface hydration, film, reduced dryness | Technically water dispersible, not “dump-and-stir”; molecular weight and use level matter; clumps | Hygroscopic powder; clumping, stringiness, settling, contamination; molecular weight is rarely verified by a consumer | **Buy finished; conditional lab exercise only** [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829483/)
| **Panthenol (D-panthenol powder)** | Humectant, barrier recovery | Soluble; usually low pH burden | Water batch still needs preservation; evidence often belongs to a complete emollient | **Good conditional candidate, low priority** [[14]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27425824/)
| **Urea** | Hydration, barrier support, keratolytic action at higher levels | Very soluble; pH/heat and concentration matter | Can decompose with heat/alkalinity; high levels sting on compromised skin | **Conditional; simple but not pigment-focused** [[15]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22418868/)
| **Allantoin** | Soothing/protective adjunct | Limited water solubility; can crystallize above its workable level | Crystals/settling; no reason to force a high dose | **Buy finished or use in a designed base**
| **Sodium PCA** | Humectant/NMF-like hydration | Very soluble and hygroscopic | Pulls water from air; water formula preservation; overdosing can feel tacky | **Conditional, low-efficacy priority**
| **Azelaic acid** | Acne/rosacea/pigment adjunct | Poor water solubility; suspension or emulsion required | Settling, gritty feel, inconsistent dose, difficult vehicle | **Buy finished** [[16]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37550898/)
| **Salicylic acid** | Acne/comedones/exfoliation | Poor water solubility; solvent/vehicle and pH matter | Irritation, overexposure, and regulatory/use restrictions; not a casual water recipe | **Buy finished / do not DIY** [[17]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/beta-hydroxy-acids)[[18]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/latest-updates/sccs-final-opinion-salicylic-acid-2023-06-09_en)
| **Ferulic acid** | Antioxidant/stabilizer in CEF-style systems | Poor water solubility; needs co-solvent/vehicle | Precipitation, oxidation, and false CEF equivalence; vitamin E is oil-soluble | **Buy finished; not a water DIY add-on** [[19]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16185284/)
| **Resveratrol** | Antioxidant/brightening hypothesis | Poor water solubility; oxidation/light burden | Crystals, color change, low reproducibility | **Buy finished**
| **Alpha-lipoic acid** | Antioxidant hypothesis | Solubility and odor/irritation burden | Heat/light/oxidation and sting; weak DIY value proposition | **Buy finished / skip**
| **Green tea / EGCG powder** | Antioxidant/anti-inflammatory hypothesis | Extract composition and solubility vary | Oxidation, color, microbial load, batch-to-batch chemistry | **Buy finished**
| **Caffeine** | Limited topical depuffing/vascular or hair-adjacent claims | Water soluble; relatively easy to dissolve | Water formula preservation; clinical facial-aging signal is limited | **Conditional, low priority**
| **Dipotassium glycyrrhizate** | Soothing/anti-inflammatory, possible tone support | Usually water soluble; supplier form matters | Water preservation; evidence often in combinations | **Conditional**
| **Peptide powders / copper peptides** | Ingredient-specific signaling claims | Supplier-specific; some are supplied as solutions, not pure powders | Metal interaction, pH, adsorption, low dose accuracy, contamination; clinical results are formula-specific | **Buy finished** [[20]](../12_peptides_in_skincare/index.html#doc2)
| **Hydroquinone, tretinoin, tazarotene, corticosteroids** | Drug treatments, not cosmetic DIY actives | Drug formulation and medical oversight required | Mis-dosing, irritation, ochronosis or other drug risks, legal/quality issues | **Do not DIY**

## 3. What “good” still requires

Even the greenest row needs:

- a lot-specific certificate of analysis and identity/contaminant limits;
- a 0.01 g or better scale when the batch size requires it;
- distilled/deionized water rather than tap water;
- a measured final pH, not an estimate from ingredient labels;
- a clean, low-touch container and one active at a time;
- an explicit discard rule; and
- a patch test before facial use.

## 4. Why HA ranks below vitamin C

HA is a good cosmetic ingredient but a bad example of “easy powder DIY.” The evidence supports hydration, and a 2024 randomized trial found low-molecular-weight HA moisturizer improved skin capacitance more than high-molecular-weight HA or vehicle after four weeks. But the study tested finished lotions, not a home dispersion. Raw HA's molecular-weight distribution, particle size, hydration protocol, and preservative system determine whether the batch is smooth, effective, and clean. [[13]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829483/)

## Evidence gaps

- Direct head-to-head clinical studies of DIY-style single-ingredient serums are rare.
- Supplier solubility and recommended pH data are not equivalent to finished-product efficacy.
- No matrix score can replace preservative challenge testing or HPLC potency testing.
- Formulation grade, molecular weight, salts, hydrates, and particle size can change the result even when the label name is identical.
