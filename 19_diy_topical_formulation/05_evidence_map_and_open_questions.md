# Evidence map and open questions

*Compiled 2026-08-15. This page separates what the literature actually shows from formulation inference.*

## 0. Bottom line

The topic's main conclusion is robust even where exact recipe data are missing: **a demonstrated active ingredient is not the same as a demonstrated DIY product**. Clinical studies usually test a complete formula, while a home powder batch changes vehicle, pH, packaging, preservation, exposure history, and quality control at the same time.

## 1. Claim-to-evidence map

| Decision claim | Best evidence | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| LAA benefits from low pH for skin delivery | Human/animal percutaneous absorption study | LAA needed pH <3.5 in the tested model; tissue levels saturated near 20% | **Verified, formula-specific** [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11207686/)
| Vitamin C can improve uneven tone/photoaging | Systematic review of 7 prospective RCT publications, 139 volunteers total | Signal for smoothing and lightening, but small heterogeneous literature and long-term uncertainty | **Limited-to-moderate** [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37128827/)
| 5% glycolic acid can improve texture/discoloration | Double-blind randomized human trial | 75 volunteers, 3 months; texture/discoloration favored glycolic; wrinkle result not statistically clear | **Moderate, vehicle-specific** [[3]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9598014/)
| Lower pH always means better glycolic treatment | FDA AHA guidance + acid chemistry | Activity rises with free acid, but irritation/UV sensitivity and injury risk also rise; consumer framework is pH ≥3.5 | **False as a universal rule** [[4]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/alpha-hydroxy-acids)
| HA improves hydration | Randomized human HA moisturizer trial | Low-MW HA moisturizer improved capacitance vs high-MW HA and vehicle after 4 weeks; no clear TEWL difference | **Moderate, finished-lotion-specific** [[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38829483/)
| Niacinamide supports tone/barrier/appearance | Split-face and randomized human studies | 4–5% formulations improved pigmentation/appearance and were generally tolerated | **Moderate, vehicle-specific** [[6]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16029679/)[[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21822427/)
| NAG is a reasonable pigment adjunct | Split-face human study | 2% NAG reduced the appearance of facial hyperpigmentation; combination with niacinamide performed better in another study | **Limited-to-moderate** [[8]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17348991/)[[9]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19845667/)
| TA can help pigment | Small randomized studies | 5% TA improved some pigment measures but was not superior to vehicle in one melasma trial; another PIH trial found similar improvement to 20% azelaic acid | **Limited and mixed** [[10]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22506692/)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37213446/)
| A water-based DIY batch is safe because it is refrigerated | FDA microbiological safety/GMP guidance | Refrigeration does not replace contamination control, preservation, and challenge testing | **Unsupported** [[12]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/potential-contaminants-cosmetics/microbiological-safety-and-cosmetics)[[13]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetics-guidance-documents/good-manufacturing-practice-gmp-guidelinesinspection-checklist-cosmetics)
| Alpha-arbutin/kojic acid limits are a DIY recipe | SCCS opinions | Regulatory safety opinions for cosmetic use are not a home-batch shelf-life or efficacy validation | **Misinterpretation** [[14]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/safety-alpha-arbutin-and-beta-arbutin-cosmetic-products_en)[[15]](https://health.ec.europa.eu/publications/kojic-acid_en)

## 2. What would change the ranking?

The current ranking would change if a candidate had:

1. a lot-specific identity and contaminant certificate;
2. a published human study using the same concentration, pH, vehicle, and application schedule;
3. a validated solubility/dispersion method that stays uniform;
4. a preservative system compatible with the exact formula and a challenge-test result;
5. stability data showing potency and physical integrity across intended storage;
6. packaging that controls repeated contamination and oxygen/light exposure.

Most marketplace powders satisfy only item 1, and sometimes not even that.

## 3. Highest-value future work

- Capture supplier COAs for the exact LAA, glycolic, HA, niacinamide, NAG, TA, and alpha-arbutin lots under consideration.
- Compare the supplier's recommended pH/use level/solubility data with published clinical vehicles.
- Build a formal preservative/challenge-test plan with a cosmetic chemist rather than copying an internet preservative percentage.
- Use HPLC/UPLC to measure LAA and degradation products if long-term potency matters.
- Measure pH drift, color, viscosity, and precipitation over time in the exact package.
- Test one active at a time before attempting a combination.

## 4. Evidence boundary of the explorer

The interactive tool displays a transparent heuristic based on the matrix. It does not estimate clinical improvement, microbial safety, shelf life, or remaining active percentage. Its “fit” output means “how many controllable formulation problems are present,” not “safe” or “effective.”
