Mixing sequence for this worksheet

  1. Confirm the powder is plain L-ascorbic acid, not a derivative or flavored blend; weigh the LAA and baking soda separately by mass.
  2. Combine the weighed powders in the clean, dry bottle.
  3. Add the full distilled-water amount. Keep the container vented while the baking soda reacts; allow fizzing to calm and do not seal an actively fizzing bottle.
  4. Allow the solution to degas and equilibrate, then measure the finished pH.
  5. Use a small morning application only after patch testing. Keep it separate from Differin and other exfoliating acids.

The display is rounded to 0.01 g; it is not a claim that your scale can measure that accurately. Baking soda amounts around 0.20 g call for a suitable fine-resolution scale—never a spoon or pinch. Do not use a dropper bottle that still contains another product or has not been thoroughly cleaned and dried. Single-use aliquots are safer than repeatedly opening an unpreserved water batch. The worksheet is not a sterile or preservative-validated formulation.

Should you order the powder or pH strips?

  • Powder: order only if the listing clearly identifies pure L-ascorbic acid / LAA, gives an assay or COA, and has no added flavor, color, minerals, or “vitamin C blend.” A green/blue/gray powder is not something to normalize as plain LAA.
  • pH strips: a 0–6 strip is more useful than a 0–14 universal strip for an acidic target, but a narrow 2.8–4.4 strip or calibrated pH meter is better if you are trying to distinguish about 3.0 from 3.5. You do not need a green color; green usually represents near-neutral pH, which is not the target here.
  • Dropper bottle: it can hold the batch only if it is clean, fully dry, compatible, and low-touch. Make a small batch rather than filling a large reused bottle.

If you want a yes/no purchasing answer for the exact powder, send the product listing or a photo of its label/COA. The transcript alone does not identify the raw material.

Formula and evidence boundary

LAA_g = target_water_mL × (strength_percent ÷ 100) LAA_moles = LAA_g ÷ 176.12 ascorbate_fraction = 10^(target_pH − 4.17) ÷ (1 + 10^(target_pH − 4.17)) initial_baking_soda_g = LAA_moles × ascorbate_fraction × 84.007 water_total_g = target_water_mL × 1.00

The estimate assumes 100% LAA, an apparent pKa of 4.17, complete bicarbonate-to-CO₂ neutralization, and ideal activity. Those assumptions are not a home-batch validation. Use the result as a starting mass, add the full water amount while vented, then measure pH.
