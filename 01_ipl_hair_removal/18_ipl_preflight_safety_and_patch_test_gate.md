# IPL preflight safety and patch-test gate

*Decision aid updated 2026-08-23. This is a conservative screening aid, not medical advice and not a substitute for the instructions for the exact device model.*

## Bottom line

The archive already explains IPL physics, device selection, cadence, and thermal stacking. The missing step was a single **go / pause / stop gate before the first flash**. Use this page before every new body area, after tanning or meaningful sun exposure, and whenever medication or skin status changes.

An FDA hair-removal clearance supports only the labeled hair-reduction use. Product code OHT is a Class II “light based over-the-counter hair removal” category; it does not validate treatment of melasma, rosacea, vessels, acne, scars, or general rejuvenation. [[1]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfPCD/classification.cfm?ID=OHT)

## 1. The 60-second gate

| Question | If yes | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Is the exact model inappropriate for this skin tone or hair color according to its current manual/skin-tone chart? | Melanin in skin may absorb too much light, while white, gray, red, or very light blond hair may provide too little follicular target. | **Do not treat.** Do not override a skin sensor. |
| Is the area tanned, sunburned, newly darkened, broken, inflamed, infected, tattooed, or covered by a dark mole/lesion that the manual excludes? | Optical absorption and injury risk are changed or the lesion should not be masked by cosmetic treatment. | **Pause.** Wait the model-specific interval or obtain clinical guidance. |
| Is there a photosensitizing medicine/supplement, recent procedure, pregnancy, relevant skin disorder, history of abnormal scarring, or other manual-listed contraindication? | Eligibility cannot be inferred from a generic web checklist. | **Do not improvise.** Check the exact IFU and prescriber/clinician. |
| Has the area, device, intensity, tan status, or meaningful sun exposure changed since the last successful test? | A prior patch test may no longer represent the current exposure. | **Patch-test again.** |
| Is the skin clean, completely dry, hair removed as the IFU directs, and free of deodorant, oils, self-tanner, anesthetic, or other residue? | Residue and surface hair can change heating; numbing can hide an excessive pain signal. | **Prepare first.** |
| Can every flash be placed once, with only edge-to-edge coverage or the exact overlap the IFU permits? | Repeated firing on one spot raises local thermal dose without proving extra benefit. | **Map the area or stop.** |

Philips’ current Lumea manual is a useful conservative reference: it says to test each treatment area, repeat the test after unprotected sun exposure, wait 24 hours before treatment, avoid repeated flashes on one spot, and use only on clean, dry skin without numbing cream. It also gives model-specific sun/tanning restrictions that should not be generalized to every brand. [[2]](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20250509/12b0227077c448c79ec5b2d8008d0655.pdf)

## 2. A reproducible 24-hour patch test

1. **Read the exact-model IFU first.** Record model, body area, current skin/tan state, hair-removal method, and date.
2. **Choose a representative small site** within that body area—not a different area that happens to be easier to reach.
3. **Prepare exactly as for treatment:** remove visible hair using the permitted method, wash, and dry. Do not add numbing cream or a new topical.
4. **Test the permitted intensity sequence.** If the manual instructs multiple adjacent test flashes at increasing settings, follow it. Do not invent a stacked-pulse stress test.
5. **Record the immediate response:** pain/burning, erythema, swelling, unusual odor/smoke, or device fault.
6. **Wait the full IFU interval.** Philips specifies 24 hours before selecting the highest comfortable setting with no reaction; other devices may differ. [[2]](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20250509/12b0227077c448c79ec5b2d8008d0655.pdf)
7. **Inspect in consistent light** and compare with a photo taken before the test. Proceed only if the skin is back to baseline and the selected setting is permitted by the device.

Passing a patch test lowers uncertainty at that site; it does **not** prove that a whole-area session, repeated cadence, or off-label goal is safe.

## 3. First-session guardrails

- Treat one modest area first rather than combining every body area into the first session.
- Use the lowest setting that the exact manual recommends as effective and tolerable; pain is not a performance target.
- Use a washable grid or mental lanes so each location receives one intended exposure. “No visible reaction” is not permission to re-flash.
- Log model, setting, mode, approximate pulses, duration, area, immediate response, and response at 24 and 48 hours.
- Keep sun protection consistent. Philips advises SPF 50 on treated areas during its specified post-treatment window, especially during the first 48 hours; use the exact device’s current instructions. [[2]](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20250509/12b0227077c448c79ec5b2d8008d0655.pdf)

For preparation and suitability, the manufacturer’s current support pages are useful cross-checks, but the manual remains controlling. [[3]](https://www.usa.philips.com/c-f/XC000021834/how-do-i-prepare-for-a-philips-lumea-treatment) [[4]](https://www.usa.philips.com/c-f/XC000003324/is-philips-lumea-suitable-for-all-skin-and-hair-types)

## 4. Stop, pause, or continue

| Observation | Action |
|---|---|
| Mild warmth or brief pinkness that resolves within the exact IFU’s expected window | **Continue cautiously** at the tested setting and cadence; keep logging. |
| Pain that escalates, sharp burning, device repeatedly refusing the skin tone, or unexpected smoke/odor | **Stop the session immediately.** Recheck preparation, window cleanliness, model instructions, and eligibility. |
| Redness lasting into the next day, marked swelling, blistering, crusting, pigment change, or a burn-like pattern | **Stop future treatment.** Follow the manufacturer’s adverse-reaction instructions and seek medical assessment when warranted. Philips identifies redness lasting longer than one day as a possible burn sign. [[2]](https://www.documents.philips.com/assets/20250509/12b0227077c448c79ec5b2d8008d0655.pdf) |
| A lesion changes, bleeds, or was not confidently identified before treatment | **Do not flash it.** Obtain clinical assessment. |
| No hair change after several correctly timed sessions | **Audit eligibility, contact, coverage, cadence, and hair cycle** before raising intensity or stacking flashes. Lack of effect is not proof the skin can tolerate more. |

## 5. What this gate prevents

The gate is deliberately stricter than a marketplace listing. Current product pages may list contraindications and safety claims, but those claims can change and do not establish equivalence across brands or OEM shells. For example, Ulike’s current Air 10 page excludes several skin conditions, photosensitivity scenarios, sunburn, broken skin, and tattooed areas; it is manufacturer guidance for that product family, not a universal IPL protocol. [[5]](https://www.ulike.com/pages/air-10-ads-exclusive)

Use the [cadence guide](index.html#doc10) only after passing this gate, and use the [multi-flash thermal page](index.html#doc15) before interpreting high-frequency or SHR marketing.

## Evidence gaps

- There is no universally validated consumer IPL patch-test protocol spanning every wavelength band, fluence, pulse structure, cooling system, and skin tone.
- A 24-hour observation may not capture every delayed pigment response; exact-model post-treatment monitoring remains necessary.
- Public IFUs often omit measured per-pulse output, sensor thresholds, and the cumulative thermal dose created by overlap.
- Skin-tone charts are coarse categories and do not replace a validated sensor or clinician assessment in borderline cases.
- This page does not establish safety for off-label pigment, redness, vascular, acne, or rejuvenation use.
