🔥 SHR & Multi-Flash Thermal Simulator

Explore how the epidermis, pigmented hair heater, and deeper regenerative target heat over time — then score the full temperature history with an Arrhenius damage integral. There is no single magic temperature: temperature and dwell time act together.

What this shows — and what it can't

The question behind Ulike's "SHR mode" and every "8.33 J/cm² over 3 flashes" claim: do successive flashes add up as heat at the follicle, and does that heat reach a damaging threshold?

A single flash heats the pigmented shaft/bulb fast, then it cools. Fire again before it cools and the peak stacks; fire again after it cools and the pulse largely starts over. The chart keeps three modeled compartments separate: epidermis, pigmented hair/follicle heater, and deeper unpigmented regenerative tissue reached by diffusion. The damage gauges integrate every time step: a very brief 65 °C excursion is not treated as equivalent to holding 65 °C for 100 ms.

Read this as an exposure model, not a patient-specific temperature prediction. The thermal curves are a transparent lumped model calibrated for teaching; home IPL filings do not disclose the optical, geometric, and tissue inputs needed to calculate an individual follicle temperature. The Arrhenius score is a published generic tissue/protein-denaturation model used in a hair-removal simulation, not a follicle-stem-cell-specific validation. Use it to compare timing, splitting, and time–temperature dose—not to clear a device or setting as effective or safe. Not medical advice.

≈65 °C is a modeling convention, not an instant switchFiskerstrand et al.'s clinical/modeling study used 65 °C as a follicular damage threshold and found similar hair reduction from one 30 ms pulse versus two 45 ms pulses separated by 40 ms, despite different peak temperatures.29 Ataie-Fashtami et al. instead integrated Arrhenius damage and treated Ω≥1 as irreversible injury.9

45–50 °C is an SHR protocol claim, not a validated kill lineProfessional low-fluence/high-repetition studies demonstrate hair reduction and lower pain, but the mechanism may include apoptosis and is not reducible to a universal 45 °C destruction threshold.1012 This tool labels time in that band as SHR-zone exposure, not “accumulation kill.”
The heater is not the whole targetMeasured ruby-laser work found only a 5–10 °C rise in many follicles, >30 °C in some, and deeper/broader damage in hotter follicles; it did not establish one universal absolute kill temperature.23 Histology shows acute damage, cystic change, and apoptosis vary by fluence, anatomy, and time.30
Heat-pain threshold ≈42–43 °CHuman thermal-pain testing reports normal heat-pain thresholds around 42 °C, but a person’s pain rating is not a thermometer. Repeated radiant/contact heat can show temporal summation: a longer interval decreases summation. The simulator therefore plots a separate, unitless response cue rather than a fake “sting temperature.” [e] [f]

Why not CEM43? CEM43 was developed for longer hyperthermia exposures. Photothermal kinetics research describes it as most useful for long-duration hyperthermia and instead uses the Arrhenius integral for short laser heating.31 Even Arrhenius parameters vary markedly by tissue and endpoint, so Ω is shown as a model criterion, not a biological certainty.

Timing & pulse count — the part that decides stacking

Edge-to-edge delay. Under the follicle's ~10–100 ms cooling time → follicle peak stacks. By ~500 ms, even coarse follicles have effectively reset; slower heat diffusion toward the deep target can still leave a tail. Separate from that, the nervous system may temporally sum repeated painful inputs. Ulike SHR ≈ 250 ms (4/s); pro SHR ≈ 100 ms (10 Hz); clinical fixed-spot stacking ≈ 10–40 ms. Not disclosed by most home devices — an honest unknown.
One strong shot, or many weak ones (an SHR / in-motion pass hits a spot repeatedly).
Strongly changes the modeled epidermal peak. For the same J/cm², a shorter pulse leaves less time for surface heat diffusion; a longer pulse lowers the peak but spreads heating in time. FDA-disclosed home-IPL widths run ~0.4–12 ms.

3 · The core tradeoff — one strong flash vs. many weak ones

Same Joules, split two ways. This is the actual decision behind "SHR mode" and split-pulse settings.

⚡ One strong flash — high peak, short dwell

All the energy arrives at once, so the pigmented heater reaches a higher peak. Damage is still not instantaneous at 65 °C: the full temperature trace must accumulate enough Ω, and heating the shaft/bulb does not prove the deeper regenerative target received the same dose. A strong pulse can be efficient, but the epidermis is a competing melanin target and must be evaluated separately.

🔁 Many weak flashes — lower peak, longer exposure

Repeated pulses can increase time at elevated temperature and drive heat outward. Professional in-motion SHR trials support comparable efficacy with less pain, but 45–50 °C is not a validated permanent-destruction threshold. The tool therefore reports time in the SHR-associated band and separately reports Arrhenius Ω. A pulse train can enter the SHR band yet remain below the model's coagulative criterion.

4 · The Ulike case — what "SHR mode" and "4 flashes" actually mean

Ulike (Air 10 / Air 3 / X) markets an "SHR mode." Here's the honest read on what it delivers, mapped onto the physics above.

Air 10 burst: up to 4 flashes (2 lamps ×2) Burst energy ≈26 J Effective ≈6.67 J/cm² for the whole burst ≈1.7 J/cm² per sub-flash SHR cadence ≈4 flashes/s (≈250 ms apart) AutoGlide ≈2 flashes/s (500 ms) Sapphire cooling ≈20 °C

Two things fall straight out of the model when you load the Ulike Air 10 SHR preset:

  1. The ~250 ms cadence is far slower than the follicle's ~10–100 ms cooling time, so successive flashes do not stack much into a higher follicle peak. But "mostly reset" is not "all heat is gone": a coarse follicle has a small residual tail and heat can still diffuse toward the deep target.
  2. A stronger last sting does not prove a hotter last follicle. The new lower panel deliberately separates a unitless pain temporal-summation cue from the °C traces. Repeated noxious heat can be perceived as increasingly painful even when the physical follicle peaks repeat; that is neural integration, not evidence that the red curve should climb. Ulike's SHR is still not the fast 10–40 ms fixed-spot stacking that raises follicle peak temperature.
  3. Its efficacy bet is repeated low-peak exposure. A 250 ms gap can leave a slower deep-tissue tail even when the heater peak mostly resets. The simulator now distinguishes that carryover from proof of injury: time in the 45–50 °C SHR band is reported, but only the Arrhenius integral is allowed to cross the generic coagulative-damage criterion.

This mirrors the write-up's conclusion for the Fansizhe "triple-pulse" mode: an "accumulated fluence" headline (26 J, 6.67 J/cm²) is only meaningful alongside timing and per-pulse dose. The strongest professional SHR finding is less pain with generally comparable efficacy in the studied protocols; it does not validate an undisclosed home-device burst. Full multi-flash write-up →

5 · Why timing rules everything — the two clocks

Every threshold in this tool comes from these published timescales. Heat "sticks" only when energy arrives faster than the target sheds it.

StructureTimescaleWhat it means for flash timing
Epidermis (TRT)~3–10 ms23Sheds heat fast → the basis for pulse-splitting & contact cooling. Keep gaps > this so skin recovers.
Hair follicle / shaft (TRT)~10–100 ms (terminal ≈100)456The stacking threshold. Gaps under this → flashes build a higher peak. Gaps over it → no peak gain.
Extended target — Thermal Damage Time~170–1000 ms; one study's best result at 400 ms789A diffusion timescale, not a required dwell time at one temperature. It describes how long heat can take to spread from an absorber to the extended target; it must not be substituted for an injury threshold.
Perceived sting / temporal summationCan build with repeated noxious heat; decreases as inter-stimulus interval lengthens2728Not a temperature compartment and not a follicle-damage metric. A rising last-pulse sensation can be neural temporal summation even when the physical follicle peak mostly resets. Its timing and magnitude cannot be inferred from IPL specs alone.
Tissue injury metricArrhenius Ω integrates the transient temperature trace931Ω=1 is a model endpoint (≈63% transformation), not a universal patient threshold. Parameters depend on tissue and the observed injury endpoint.

Progressive photothermolysis describes repeated low-energy heating into a reported 45–50 °C band.10 Extended selective photothermolysis adds Thermal Damage Time for a target whose absorber and vulnerable structure are spatially separated.7 Neither concept turns one temperature into a universal kill switch; the time history and the biological endpoint still matter.

6 · The clinical evidence on SHR / low-fluence high-rep hair removal

SHR is the one multi-pulse technique with real hair-removal trial data. The pattern: usually comparable to single-pass high fluence, occasionally better, reliably less painful — and it works by a many-pulses-in-motion delivery, not 2–4 shots at a fixed spot.

StudyComparisonResult
Braun 200911SHR (5–10 J/cm², 10 Hz) vs. single-pass (25–40 J/cm², 1 Hz)Comparable 86–91% reduction; SHR much less painful
Omi 201712Dynamic 10 Hz/10 J/cm² vs. static 1 Hz/30 J/cm²No significant difference (41.4%, both); long-term effect attributed to apoptosis, not just bulk heating
Koo 201413Soprano SHR in-motion vs. LightSheer single-pass40.7% vs. 33.5% (n.s.); SHR significantly less pain
Li 201614Same laser, SHR vs. high-fluence modeSHR slightly better (90.2% vs. 87%) and far less painful (VAS 2.75 vs. 6.75)
Wanitphakdeedecha 201215Diode SHR vs. Nd:YAG single-pass high-fluenceNd:YAG won (54.2% vs. 35.7%) — SHR isn't universally superior
Barolet 201216Low-fluence (15 J/cm²) alone at 5 HzSignificant permanent reduction after 4 monthly sessions

Note the fluences: clinical SHR uses 5–15 J/cm² per pulse and dozens of pulses per pass. A home device at ~1.7 J/cm²/sub-flash is well below that per-pulse — which is why the model treats home "SHR" as a comfort-forward, accumulation-dependent mode whose ceiling is set by how many times a spot is actually hit.

7 · The lever that most reliably matters: peak fluence

For thermal injury, total course Joules are not enough. The relevant question is the temperature history at each biological target—and whether that history accumulates a credible injury dose while sparing competing tissue.

Takeaway: a headline Joule total does not prove follicle damage. Use only settings and skin/hair combinations permitted by the device IFU; cooling improves comfort and may lower epidermal heating but is not a license to exceed instructions. Multi-flash claims need per-pulse fluence, pulse width, gap, overlap, and outcome data. Sub-therapeutic exposure has also been associated with paradoxical regrowth on face/neck, so this simulator should not be used to invent a dosing protocol.
