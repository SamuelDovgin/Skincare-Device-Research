## 2. What DPL actually is

### 2.1 The defensible definition

IPL uses a xenon flashlamp to emit noncoherent, polychromatic light. Cutoff or bandpass filters shape that broad output. “DPL” is a commercial name for an IPL configuration that removes more of the unused spectrum and concentrates output into a narrower band—commonly **500–600 nm** for superficial vascular targets. It remains pulsed, noncoherent light; it does not become a laser simply because the spectrum is narrower. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38822948/)[[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649186/)[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711946/)[[10]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4596886/)[[11]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27363767/)

At 500–600 nm, the band includes oxyhemoglobin absorption features near 542 and 577 nm and also substantial melanin absorption. That makes it plausible for superficial redness/telangiectasia and epidermal pigment—but also means epidermal melanin remains a competing absorber and post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation risk does not disappear. [[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649186/)[[10]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4596886/)[[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111558/)

### 2.2 The naming problem

| Source | Expansion or description | What to trust |
|---|---|---|
| Peer-reviewed rosacea paper | **Dye pulsed light**, 500–600 nm | The disclosed band and pulse parameters |
| 2024 randomized comparison | **Delicate Pulse Light**, 500–600 nm IPL arm | The disclosed device configuration |
| JOVS current Blacken page | **Dye Pulsed Light** | Marketing page; useful for claimed specs only |
| Older JOVS copy / reviews | **Dynamic Pulsed Light** | Marketing synonym, not a standard |
| PAKISS/Alibaba AY101 | “DPL,” “PIR,” “LED photon therapy,” or NIR | Internally inconsistent; requires bench documentation |

**Buying rule:** ignore the letters “DPL.” Ask for wavelength transmission, fluence in J/cm², pulse duration in milliseconds, spot size, and pulse repetition behavior.

## 3. DPL compared with ordinary IPL

| Question | Narrow-band DPL | Conventional broadband IPL | What the evidence supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light source | Usually xenon flashlamp | Xenon flashlamp | Same basic class; neither is inherently a laser. |
| Spectrum | Commonly 500–600 or 550–650 nm | Often a cutoff-filtered band extending toward 1,200 nm | DPL discards more off-target wavelengths. |
| Primary chromophores | Hemoglobin + superficial melanin | Depends on filter; hemoglobin, melanin, sometimes deeper targets | Narrower bands can improve energy efficiency for superficial vascular/pigment targets. |
| Penetration | Shorter wavelengths favor superficial targets | Longer wavelengths in the band penetrate more deeply | DPL is not automatically better for deep/coarse vessels. |
| Clinical efficacy | Effective for erythema/telangiectasia in professional systems | Also effective | A 60-person randomized trial found **no significant efficacy difference** among DPL, PDL, and two IPL configurations. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38822948/) |
| Safety | Potentially less wasted spectral heat | More non-target spectral energy, depending on filter | The same trial found all four approaches well tolerated; one broad dual-band vascular arm had more blisters. This does not prove home DPL is safer. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38822948/) |
| Home-device evidence | No independent Blacken trial found | Hair-removal evidence and clearances are much stronger | Professional DPL results cannot validate JOVS or AY101. |

The strongest comparative study located randomized 60 people with erythematotelangiectatic rosacea to nonpurpuric 595 nm PDL, DPL 500–600 nm, M22 IPL 590–1,200 nm, or M22 vascular IPL 530–650 + 900–1,200 nm. All groups improved, with no significant between-group efficacy difference. [[7]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38822948/)

Professional DPL evidence is real but parameter-specific:

- In 65 rosacea patients, three monthly treatments at **500–600 nm, 10–15 ms, 8.4–10.6 J/cm², 3 cm²** improved erythema and telangiectasia; response was poorer for severe telangiectasia. [[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649186/)
- In a retrospective cohort of 181 Fitzpatrick III–IV patients with sensitive skin, two to four monthly sessions at **500–600 nm and 5.0–6.8 J/cm²** reduced sensitivity and erythema scores, with no obvious adverse effects reported. It was not randomized and used a professional Alma workstation with cooling gel and individualized settings. [[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711946/)
- A vascular model showed that 500–600 nm narrow-band IPL can create persistent vascular shutdown, but this was an animal-window model, not a consumer-device clinical trial. [[10]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4596886/)

### 4.2 Which JOVS model is best?

**Most defensible choice: Blacken Pro.** It publishes the clearest complete dose description, has 17% more maximum fluence than the base model (3.5 vs 3.0 J/cm²), and stays within the best-studied superficial 500–650 nm concept. The $80 premium buys a modest output increase, not clinic equivalence.

**Best budget choice: standard Blacken.** It is the least expensive but the 1.8–3 J/cm² range is low. It makes the most sense for a cautious buyer who values the JOVS warranty/cooling and accepts gradual, uncertain results.

**Most interesting but least auditable: Blacken X.** The larger window, 3 Hz movement, and additional long-wavelength targeting could improve treatment speed and broaden pigment targeting. But JOVS does not publish J/cm², pulse duration, or a coherent band diagram, and several claims conflict across its own pages. It should not outrank Pro until JOVS releases exact output data and the underlying clinical report.

## 5. FDA and model-number reality

FDA K231800 clears Shenzhen Qianyu Technology’s **JP1, JR3, JR8, and JR9** only as over-the-counter IPL devices for **removal of unwanted hair**. For JP1, the filing discloses **590–1,200 nm, 1.4–2.5 J/cm², 1.6 cm², and 7.5 ± 2 ms**. [[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/K231800.pdf)

Those numbers do not match JOVS’s current Blacken photofacial claim of **500–650 nm and 1.8–3 J/cm²**. A Japanese retail listing associates “Blacken” with JP1, but the primary sources located do not establish that the current 500–650 nm Blacken configuration is the same FDA-cleared optical configuration. Therefore:

- **Verified:** Qianyu has a hair-removal clearance containing a model named JP1.
- **Not verified:** FDA clearance for Blacken’s pigmentation, redness, pore, rosacea, or anti-aging claims.
- **Not verified:** K231800 coverage of Blacken Pro or Blacken X.
- **Do not infer:** a hair-removal 510(k) validates photofacial efficacy.

JOVS’s general FAQ says all JOVS devices, including DPL devices, are FDA-cleared, but it does not provide a Blacken-specific K-number or reconcile the wavelength mismatch. [[19]](https://jovs.com/pages/faqs)

## 7. Supplier questions that decide the purchase

Send these as one request and require photographs/PDFs tied to **AY101**, not a generic catalog:

1. Provide the lamp type and a calibrated **spectrometer plot from 400–1,200 nm** at every mode.
2. State **fluence (J/cm²)** at each level, with meter model, distance/contact geometry, and spot area.
3. State **pulse duration (ms)**, pulse count per trigger, interpulse delay, and maximum repetition rate.
4. Confirm whether Brighten/Spot/Redness modes change the **filter**, pulse timing, or only the firmware label.
5. Provide sapphire window dimensions, active optical spot size, and stabilized contact temperature after 1, 5, and 10 minutes.
6. Provide exact-model IEC 62471 or IEC 60601-2-57 photobiological safety report, EMC/LVD reports, and Declaration of Conformity. The report must show **AY101** and the same power supply.
7. Provide PCB, lamp, capacitor, filter, and sapphire-window photos for one opened sample.
8. Explain why the official PAKISS page says **900–1,200 nm** while the product is sold as narrow-band DPL for spots/redness.
9. Confirm whether Meisiyu’s **MSY-AY101** is purchased from Xiazhifeng and whether its firmware/filter stack differs.
10. Quote one sample with Trade Assurance, DDP shipping, return terms, replacement lamp policy, and warranty handling.

Any refusal to provide items 1–4 makes optical equivalence untestable.

## 8. Practical safety and expectation setting

- Do not treat an undiagnosed or changing pigmented lesion with any light device. Confirm that a “spot” is a benign lentigo/freckle rather than melasma, PIH, or a lesion needing medical review.
- Narrow-band DPL still heats melanin. Pigment-prone skin can develop burns or post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, especially after recent sun exposure or at excessive overlap. [[17]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25111558/)
- The professional evidence used monthly sessions with clinician-selected parameters, cooling gel/ice, and treatment endpoints. It does not validate JOVS’s several-times-per-week schedule. [[8]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7649186/)[[9]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11711946/)
- Cooling improves comfort but does not make an excessive or off-target optical dose safe.
- Wear the supplied opaque eye protection and keep flashes away from the eyelids/orbit unless the IFU specifically authorizes that area.
- Patch testing for one hour, as JOVS suggests, may miss delayed pigment change. A conservative buyer should observe a test area for several days before expanding treatment.

## Evidence gaps

- No independent clinical trial of Blacken, Blacken Pro, Blacken X, or PAKISS AY101 was found.
- JOVS has not published the SGS protocol/report behind its percentage-improvement claims.
- Blacken X fluence and a coherent transmission spectrum are missing.
- The relationship among JOVS/Qianyu, Xiazhifeng/PAKISS, Shenzhen Meisiyu, and HongKong Meisiyu is not documented by a contract, regulatory filing, teardown, or bill of materials.
- Identical exterior tooling does not prove identical lamp, filter, capacitor, firmware, or safety controls.
- AY101’s own wavelength descriptions conflict across the manufacturer page, Alibaba attributes, and image copy.
- Live supplier metrics and prices can change; recheck before purchase.
