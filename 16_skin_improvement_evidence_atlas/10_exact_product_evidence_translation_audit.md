# Exact-Product Evidence Translation Audit

*Compiled 2026-08-23. Research orientation, not a clinical recommendation, regulatory determination, or validated risk-of-bias instrument. “Exact product” includes the named device model, finished formula, injectable material, or procedure protocol actually being considered.*

## 0. Bottom line

Category evidence answers “can this type of intervention work under studied conditions?” A purchasing or routine decision requires a second bridge: **does the exact product, population, dose, comparator, endpoint, follow-up, and harm reporting match the claim being made?** The [evidence-translation audit](evidence_translation_audit.html) turns that bridge into a visible checklist and returns missing questions—not a pseudo-precise evidence score.

## 1. Why this addition exists

The atlas already ranks categories and maps the current routine. Its largest recurring limitation is product translation: a professional study, ingredient-family paper, or FDA predicate relationship may not establish the performance of the exact consumer product. The audit operationalizes that limitation for one claim at a time.

## 2. The four gates

| Gate | Minimum evidence needed | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Exact model/formula/material and version; current label/IFU; claim matches the named product | A study of a category or predecessor is presented as product proof |
| Exposure | Studied concentration, vehicle, wavelength, fluence, temperature, depth, cadence, or procedure is disclosed and matches use | A shared ingredient or wavelength substitutes for measured delivery |
| Outcome | Relevant population, comparator, prespecified endpoint, assessor, duration, and analyzable denominator | Satisfaction or before/after imagery replaces a controlled endpoint |
| Trust and harms | Funding/conflicts, attrition, adverse-event collection, and durability are reported | Benefits are detailed while harms, dropouts, or post-treatment follow-up are absent |

CONSORT 2025 treats trial registration, protocol access, funding/conflicts, participant flow, outcome denominators, and harms as core reporting information. [[1]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11996237/) ClinicalTrials.gov likewise distinguishes prespecified from post-hoc outcome measures and requires results by arm when results are submitted. [[2]](https://clinicaltrials.gov/policy/results-definitions)

## 3. Regulatory evidence is one input, not the whole answer

FDA explains that the 510(k) standard is comparative substantial equivalence, whereas PMA relies on an independent demonstration for a high-risk device. FDA also notes that most 510(k)s do not require clinical data. [[3]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/510k-clearances/medical-device-safety-and-510k-clearance-process)[[4]](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/premarket-notification-510k/content-510k)

Therefore:

- a correct clearance number and indication strengthen identity/regulatory confidence;
- a clearance does not automatically supply a randomized consumer-outcome trial;
- a predicate's clinical result is not automatically the new product's effect size;
- an unverified marketplace claim of “FDA approved” fails the identity gate.

## 4. Tool logic

The tool asks 12 yes/no/unknown questions. It reports:

- **bridge incomplete** when exact identity, claim match, or exposure match is “no” or “unknown”;
- **outcome interpretation limited** when design, comparator, endpoint, denominator, or duration is missing;
- **trust/harms gap** when funding, attrition, adverse-event collection, or follow-up is unclear;
- **auditable translation candidate** only when every gate is answered “yes.”

“Auditable” means the claim can be checked against disclosed evidence. It does not mean effective, safe, worth buying, or appropriate for an individual.

## Practical takeaways

Run the audit on the exact claim sentence, not on a brand in general. Save the source link beside each “yes.” Treat “not reported” as **unknown**, not as “probably yes.” Then follow the atlas route into the mechanism-specific topic for contraindications, parameters, and alternatives.

## Evidence gaps

- The checklist is an archive decision aid, not a validated RoB 2, GRADE, ROBINS-I, or regulatory-review instrument.
- Different study designs need design-specific appraisal beyond these universal translation gates.
- A complete report can still be biased or clinically unimportant; reporting quality and effect validity are related but distinct.
- Product versions, labels, and regulatory status can change after an audit is saved.
