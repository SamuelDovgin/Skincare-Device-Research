# Skin-improvement evidence atlas

*Compiled 2026-08-18. Research orientation, not medical advice. This is a living scoping map of human evidence, regulatory anchors, and product-translation gaps across skin-improvement devices, ingredients, peptides, procedures, and indoor UV exposure controls.*

## What this topic adds

The archive already contains deep lanes for IPL, diode lasers, fractional and non-fractional lasers, red/NIR light, RF, microneedling, HIFU/MFU, vitamin C, zinc oxide, peptides, combined routines, and injectable PLLA. This topic is the cross-industry layer: it makes those lanes comparable without pretending that a serum, a home mask, a professional laser, and an injectable share the same endpoint or risk profile.

The map is deliberately evidence-weighted. A larger node means more mapped human studies and/or high-level synthesis in this archive, not that the intervention is universally better. Confidence and evidence volume are separate fields.

## TL;DR

1. **Start with the goal, not the device.** Pigment/PIH is primarily a photoprotection-and-irritation-control problem; acne is a treatment-plus-barrier problem; texture/scars are a remodeling problem; laxity is a tissue-heating or volume problem; hair removal is a follicle-selective photothermal problem.
2. **The biggest, most defensible nodes are not always the most dramatic.** Sunscreen/visible-light protection, retinoid-based care, acne topicals, fractional resurfacing, microneedling, RF, and hair-removal light/laser have deeper human evidence than many fashionable peptide blends, microcurrent claims, or unnamed OEM devices.
3. **Home translation is the main uncertainty.** A professional study establishes an intervention class under stated parameters; it does not establish that an unnamed consumer device reaches the same dose, depth, temperature, coverage, or safety boundary.
4. **A good decision is a matched pathway.** Select the lowest-risk category that plausibly reaches the target layer, then add recovery and photoprotection. Do not stack irritation-heavy actives around energy injury just because each component is individually popular.
5. **The evidence map is not a ranking of products.** Product choice still requires an IFU, output measurements, regulatory identifier, skin-type guidance, adverse-event boundary, and a protocol that a person can actually follow.
6. **Your current routine already overlaps most of the highest-ranked relevant lanes.** The new routine crosswalk finds a product-, dose-, endpoint-, and schedule-translation gap—not a general need to add more categories.
7. **Indoor UV control is a separate exposure-reduction option, not a sunscreen equivalence claim.** For a clear home-window intervention, start with architectural film whose data sheet reports high VLT and UV rejection over a stated wavelength range; current clear-film data commonly stop at 380 nm, so ask about 380–400 nm transmission.

## Status / open items

- **Strong:** the map is anchored to the archive's deep topic evidence pages and structured 100-record 2025–2026 paper index. The original compilation reported a 200-identifier citation snapshot, but the repository has since grown and no frozen manifest reproduces that historical total.
- **Moderate:** category counts are manually curated directional estimates, not a reproducible bibliometric census. The current CSV does not preserve the normalized human-volume/design-strength inputs or a frozen PMID-to-category manifest needed to recompute every score.
- **Open:** Embase, Scopus, Web of Science, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA MAUDE, and non-English literature should be added to make this a registered or systematic review rather than a scoping atlas.
- **Open:** most consumer products still need independent output, temperature, dose, coverage, and adverse-event testing; no visual node can solve that missing product-level data.
- **New open item:** modern clear residential films need better independent spectral reporting at 380–400 nm and real-world skin-exposure studies; current manufacturer tables are useful for screening products but do not establish zero indoor UVA.
- **Added decision aid:** the exact-product translation audit turns the atlas's largest recurring caveat into a reusable identity/exposure/outcome/trust checklist; it is not a validated risk-of-bias or regulatory instrument.
