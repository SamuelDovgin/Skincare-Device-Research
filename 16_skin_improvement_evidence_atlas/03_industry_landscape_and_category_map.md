# Industry landscape and category taxonomy

*Compiled 2026-08-02. Categories are grouped by biological target and decision goal, not by marketing department.*

## 0. Bottom line

The industry becomes easier to understand when every intervention is assigned three coordinates: **target layer**, **primary endpoint**, and **delivery context**. A topical mostly works through the stratum corneum/epidermal environment; light and laser depend on wavelength, chromophore, pulse, fluence, and coverage; RF/MFU depend on geometry, heating history, focal depth, and feedback; needles and injectables deliberately change tissue or deliver material below the barrier.

## 1. Target-layer map

| Layer / target | Ingredient lanes | Device / procedure lanes | Best-supported questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barrier / surface | Sunscreen, ceramides, cholesterol, fatty acids, niacinamide, zinc oxide, moisturizers | Cooling, gentle LED, post-procedure dressings | TEWL, irritation, photoprotection, adherence |
| Epidermal pigment / inflammation | Retinoids, azelaic acid, hydroquinone, tranexamic acid, cysteamine, kojic acid, arbutin, vitamin C | IPL, QS/picosecond pigment lasers, selected 1927 nm protocols | Melasma/PIH, dyschromia, relapse, skin-of-color safety |
| Follicle / sebaceous unit | Benzoyl peroxide, salicylic acid, retinoids, clascoterone, antibiotics | Blue/red LED, IPL, 1726 nm acne laser, hair-removal laser/IPL | Lesion count, sebum, hair reduction, adverse events |
| Dermal matrix / scar | Retinoids, peptides, vitamin C as adjuncts, growth-factor formulations | Fractional laser, microneedling, RF microneedling, nonablative 675/1450/1550/1927 nm | Texture, atrophic scars, wrinkle scales, collagen remodeling |
| Dermis / laxity | Topicals have a ceiling; peptides may support skin quality | RF, MFU/HIFU, microcoring, injectables, surgery | Firmness, laxity, contour, patient satisfaction, downtime |
| Hair follicle | No topical replacement for selective photothermal targeting | 755/810/1064 nm lasers, IPL, home diode/IPL | Hair-count reduction, durability, pain, pigment risk |
| Systemic / emerging | Oral collagen peptides, oral photoprotection supplements, microbiome products | Whole-body PBM and combination platforms | Plausibility and early signal; endpoint and translation uncertainty |

## 2. Category families in the visual map

1. **Photoprotection and barrier:** foundational, high-use, lower procedural risk, and central to pigment relapse prevention.
2. **Evidence-based topicals:** retinoids and acne actives have long clinical lineage; pigment actives are more endpoint- and population-specific.
3. **Peptides and delivery science:** diverse and formulation-dependent; separate topical peptide claims from oral collagen-peptide trials.
4. **Non-thermal and low-thermal light:** LED/PBM for selected acne, wrinkle, wound, and hair contexts; dose and wavelength matter.
5. **Selective light and laser:** IPL, diode, Nd:YAG, picosecond, and fractional lasers are not interchangeable; the target chromophore and skin type matter.
6. **Thermal remodeling:** RF and MFU/HIFU can improve selected laxity/texture endpoints, with geometry and thermal history hidden behind many product claims.
7. **Controlled injury and remodeling:** microneedling, RF microneedling, fractional lasers, and microcoring trade downtime and risk for remodeling intensity.
8. **Injectable biostimulation and augmentation:** PLLA, CaHA, HA, PMMA, and related products have different materials, labels, reversibility, and risk boundaries.

## 3. Why the market map needs evidence labels

The same word—“collagen,” “lifting,” “brightening,” “regeneration,” or “phototherapy”—can describe a topical surrogate, a histology finding, a patient-reported improvement, a regulatory indication, or a seller slogan. The atlas uses the most specific endpoint available and preserves the original evidence type. A node is large because the category has more support to inspect; it is not a promise of the magnitude of benefit.
