# Limitations, gaps, and next research

*Compiled 2026-08-02. The gaps below are part of the result, not an appendix to hide uncertainty.*

## 0. Bottom line

The strongest cross-industry conclusion is not that one modality wins. It is that evidence quality improves when studies report exact intervention parameters, validated endpoints, diverse skin types, longer follow-up, patient-centered outcomes, and independent replication. Consumer translation is often the weakest link.

## 1. Highest-value missing evidence

1. **Device identity and dose:** independent measurement of irradiance, fluence, pulse timing, temperature history, focal depth, coverage, and delivered dose on current consumer devices.
2. **Skin-of-color evidence:** prospective studies with Fitzpatrick V–VI, melasma/PIH history, standardized pigment outcomes, and relapse follow-up.
3. **Home-versus-clinic translation:** head-to-head studies that use the same endpoint and report actual device parameters rather than comparing category names.
4. **Durability:** 6–24-month outcomes for LED, home RF, home fractional devices, topical peptides, pigment procedures, and combination regimens.
5. **Independent formulation verification:** finished-product concentration, stability, vehicle, pH, skin delivery, and adverse-event data for peptide and “next-generation” topical products.
6. **Combination evidence:** factorial or sequential trials that test whether barrier care, topical actives, light, RF, laser, or microneedling add benefit without adding unacceptable irritation or PIH.
7. **Reporting standards:** common facial-aging outcome domains, consistent scar scales, standard photos, and transparent conflicts and funding.

## 2. Known sources of bias

Many studies are small, short, single-center, open-label, product-linked, or use subjective global improvement scales. Review-level evidence can look strong while the underlying trials remain heterogeneous. Industry sponsorship is not proof that a result is false, but it increases the value of independent replication. A high satisfaction rate does not substitute for a blinded comparator or a validated objective endpoint.

## 3. What would change the atlas

- A product-specific randomized sham-controlled trial with measured output and ≥6-month follow-up could move a small home-device node upward.
- A well-powered independent negative trial could lower confidence even if the category has many small positive studies.
- Better skin-of-color representation could change the safety weighting for pigment and fractional procedures.
- A standardized peptide or topical endpoint with independent formulation verification could separate active-molecule evidence from product-marketing evidence.

## 4. Future update protocol

Run a quarterly PubMed/FDA/ClinicalTrials.gov/patent delta, deduplicate by PMID/DOI/device model, update the paper ledger, rerun category counts, and record every score change in a dated changelog. Treat current prices, clearances, labels, and product availability as unstable claims that require live re-verification.
