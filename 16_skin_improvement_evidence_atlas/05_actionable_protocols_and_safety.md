# Actionable protocols and safety gates

*Compiled 2026-08-02. These are decision and documentation gates, not individualized treatment instructions.*

## 0. A safe evidence-to-action workflow

1. **Name the endpoint:** pigment, lesion count, scar texture, laxity, hair reduction, or barrier symptoms.
2. **Name the target layer:** surface/barrier, epidermal pigment, follicle, dermal matrix, deeper tissue, or volume.
3. **Choose one primary intervention:** start with the category that has the best evidence–risk fit for that endpoint.
4. **Check the product identity:** exact model, IFU, wavelength/frequency, fluence or irradiance, pulse/timing, temperature feedback, sterile path, regulatory identifier, contraindications, and maintenance schedule.
5. **Set a measurement baseline:** same lighting and camera distance for photos; use a validated scale where possible; record downtime and adverse events.
6. **Run a low-complexity trial:** change one major variable at a time unless a published combination is the question.
7. **Escalate only when the result is measurable:** if no effect is visible after a reasonable study-follow-up window, revisit diagnosis, endpoint, dose, and adherence before adding intensity.

## 1. Safety gates by category

| Category | Gate before use | Stop / escalate signal |
|---|---|---|
| Topicals | Patch test when appropriate; check pregnancy/medication/irritation constraints; introduce one active | Severe burning, swelling, blistering, persistent dermatitis, rapidly worsening pigment |
| LED/PBM | Confirm wavelength, irradiance, eye guidance, heat, photosensitizing medications, and treatment indication | Eye pain/visual symptoms, blistering, persistent erythema, unexpected pigment change |
| IPL/laser | Match skin type and target; verify cooling, eye protection, pulse/fluence, and endpoint; avoid treating an unverified indication | Blistering, gray/white epidermis, severe pain, expanding darkening, paradoxical hair growth |
| RF/MFU | Review implants, pregnancy, sensation, fillers, recent procedures, and device-specific contraindications; verify feedback and depth claims | Focal pain, burns, contour change, prolonged numbness, fat loss concern |
| Needles / RF microneedling | Professional sterile technique, depth/energy, infection control, pigment-risk plan, aftercare | Infection signs, delayed healing, persistent edema, worsening PIH, nerve symptoms |
| Injectables | Licensed qualified injector; product identity, indication, material, reversibility, emergency plan | Vascular-occlusion symptoms, vision change, severe pain, blanching, necrosis, infection |

## 2. Actionable research log

For each intervention, record: date; exact device/formula/material; lot/model; skin type and target; baseline photo/scale; protocol; adherence; adverse events; change in other products; follow-up at 2, 6, 12, and 24 weeks when relevant. This turns a subjective before/after impression into a small N-of-1 observation with known confounding.

## 3. High-value purchasing questions

- What is the exact regulatory identifier and cleared/approved indication?
- Is the output measured on the actual unit, at the treatment surface, with method and uncertainty disclosed?
- Is the study on this exact model/formula, or only on the technology family?
- What skin types, hair colors, acne severity, scar type, or age ranges were studied?
- What happened after the last treatment? Was durability and relapse reported?
- What are the contraindications, eye rules, heat limits, and stop instructions?
- What independent data exist beyond company-sponsored or seller data?

## 4. What not to infer

Do not infer “safe” from a low modeled temperature, “clinic-equivalent” from a shared wavelength, “FDA-cleared” from a marketplace claim, “collagen stimulation” from a patent, or “clinically tested” from a product-linked consumer perception study. The archive’s detailed topic pages preserve those distinctions.
