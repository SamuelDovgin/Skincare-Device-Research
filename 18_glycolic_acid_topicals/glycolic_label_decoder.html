Glycolic label decoder

Compare two disclosed percentage/pH labels with an ideal ionization model. Missing pH stays missing; the tool never invents it.

Safety and uncertainty statement: the output is an ideal dilute-solution chemistry proxy at 25 °C—not measured bottle activity, penetration, peel depth, irritation, collagen change, or a safety clearance. The FDA/CIR consumer screen (≤10% AHA, final pH ≥3.5, sun protection) is a boundary, not a guarantee. Read the model and sources.

What the numbers mean

un-ionized fraction = 1 / (1 + 10(pH − 3.83))
illustrative equivalent = labeled glycolic % × fraction

Buffers, salts, neutralizers, other acids, emulsions, solvents, water binding, activity coefficients, temperature, and skin partitioning are omitted. A lower-pH or larger modeled value is not “better.” If a label does not disclose pH, use the unresolved output as a reason to ask the manufacturer—not as permission to assume 3.5.
