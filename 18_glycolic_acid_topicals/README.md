# Glycolic Acid & AHA Topicals

*Compiled 2026-08-12. Research orientation, not medical advice. This lane covers cosmetic-strength glycolic acid for texture, photoaging support, and uneven tone; professional peels, prescription retinoids, and diagnosis of persistent pigment remain separate questions.*

> **Key distinction:** the strongest “collagen” histology comes from high-strength AHA or controlled formulations that should not be copied into a home routine. For a person already using a retinoid and fractional laser, the useful question is not “what is the strongest acid?” but “what is the lowest-irritation leave-on formula I can use consistently?”

## TL;DR

1. **Glycolic acid is a legitimate but optional topical gap.** A randomized 3-month study of daily 5% unneutralized glycolic acid improved texture and discoloration; wrinkle improvement trended better but was not statistically significant. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9598014/)
2. **The 25% AHA human biopsy study is biologically interesting, not a home-product prescription.** Six months of 25% glycolic/lactic/citric acid on forearm skin produced about a 25% increase in skin thickness and greater collagen density, but this was high-strength, site-specific, and not the same as a 5–10% facial product. [[2]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8642081/)
3. **Formulation matters as much as the headline percentage.** FDA/CIR safety guidance for consumer AHA cosmetics centers on **10% or less and final pH 3.5 or greater**, with sun-protection labeling. [[3]](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/alpha-hydroxy-acids)
4. **My formulation target for this routine:** start with a **5% leave-on glycolic emulsion or cream**, ideally with pH disclosed around **3.5–4.0**, humectants, and barrier-supportive/soothing ingredients. A 7% toner around pH 3.6 is a reasonable body or occasional-face experiment, but it is not automatically better than a buffered 5% cream. [[4]](https://theordinary.com/en-us/glycolic-acid-7-exfoliating-toner-100418.html)[[5]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33864274/)
5. **Keep it off Tria and retinoid nights at first.** Glycolic acid, retinoids, and fractional laser can each irritate; the spacing plan is a conservative tolerance strategy, not a universal published interval. Adapalene labeling specifically warns that alpha-hydroxy/glycolic products may worsen irritation. [[6]](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/020380Orig1s021lbl.pdf)

### Sources

1. [A double-blind randomized clinical trial on daily 5% glycolic acid for photoaging](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9598014/) — 75 volunteers, 3 months; texture/discoloration improved, wrinkles were not statistically significant.
2. [Effects of alpha-hydroxy acids on photoaged skin](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8642081/) — 25% AHA forearm application for about 6 months with clinical/histologic/ultrastructural outcomes.
3. [FDA Alpha Hydroxy Acids guidance page](https://www.fda.gov/cosmetics/cosmetic-ingredients/alpha-hydroxy-acids) — consumer safety context: ≤10%, pH ≥3.5, and sun-protection labeling guidance.
4. [The Ordinary Glycolic Acid 7% official product page](https://theordinary.com/en-us/glycolic-acid-7-exfoliating-toner-100418.html) — current example of a 7% water-based toner with a stated pH range of 3.5–3.9.
5. [Glycolic acid formulation and skin-acidification study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33864274/) — 2% and 10% glycolic-acid emulsions; short- and 7-day in-vivo skin-pH/barrier observations.
6. [Differin FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/020380Orig1s021lbl.pdf) — label warning that alpha-hydroxy/glycolic products may worsen irritation.
