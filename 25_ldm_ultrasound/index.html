LDM ultrasound: local dynamic micromassage

Compiled October 9, 2026. Evidence orientation for skin-quality and inflammatory-skin questions.

Start here

LDM deserves its own section: it is a rapidly alternating, multi-frequency therapeutic ultrasound lane, distinct from the focused-ultrasound lifting procedures in the HIFU archive. Wellcomet uses the LDM name for its technology; a generic “ultrasound facial” or home wand is not automatically the same system. [1]

The reviewed human literature offers preliminary signals for redness/barrier dysfunction, acne and cosmetic skin appearance. It does not establish a best home LDM device, a predictable collagen increase, or superiority to established treatments. The human evidence guide separates actual LDM studies from adjacent ultrasound evidence.

LDM mechanism and the HIFU distinction

Compiled October 9, 2026. Verified platform descriptions, manufacturer hypotheses and research interpretation are separated.

Bottom line

LDM means local dynamic micromassage: therapeutic ultrasound that switches rapidly among frequencies. It should have its own evidence lane rather than inherit the outcomes of HIFU, RF or any other multi-frequency device. [1]

What is actually delivered?

Wellcomet describes dual combinations such as 1/3 and 3/10 MHz, and triple combinations of 1/3/10 and 3/10/19 MHz. Frequency is the acoustic oscillation rate; the alternation schedule is a separate variable. A label listing several frequencies does not reveal switching timing, intensity, duty cycle or calibrated acoustic output. [1]

The manufacturer proposes effects on matrix metalloproteinases, heat shock proteins and hyaluronic acid. These are mechanism claims, not a measured collagen percentage for an individual patient. Hydration, turgor and visible smoothing are different endpoints from durable tissue remodelling. [2]

Section finding — S01/S03: official documentation establishes what the manufacturer describes. It does not independently validate each biological explanation or clinical claim.

Compare the ultrasound lanes

Lane Research target What must be verified
LDM / alternating therapeutic ultrasound Skin quality and inflammatory-skin endpoints Exact generator, handpiece, sequence, acoustic intensity, coupling and study indication
HIFU / microfocused ultrasound Procedure-specific focal heating and tightening Focal geometry, depth, anatomy, imaging/control and operator protocol; see the existing HIFU research
Other dual-frequency ultrasound Outcomes of that particular system Equivalence to LDM must be demonstrated, not assumed from MHz
Ultrasound-assisted delivery Transport of a particular formulation Compatible formulation and measured uptake; see delivery research

This comparison is a research classification, not a claim that every device in a category uses the same physics or produces the same outcome.

Frequency is not a precise treatment depth

The manufacturer gives approximate half-depths of 3 mm at 10 MHz and 1.5 mm at 19 MHz. A half-depth describes attenuation; it is not a hard boundary, an HIFU focal point, or a guarantee of targeting a single skin layer. Tissue properties and the complete exposure matter. [2]

Section finding — S03: these are manufacturer approximations. This section intentionally has no depth-to-collagen or frequency-to-result calculator.

Read the clinical endpoints first

Ask whether a paper measured redness, TEWL (water loss through skin), acne severity, wrinkles, or actual tissue structure. A change in one cannot simply be relabelled as all the others. The evidence guide records those distinctions.

Evidence gaps

Independent acoustic-output comparisons, a common-protocol LDM-versus-HIFU trial, and direct long-term human collagen measurements for current platforms were not established in this initial corpus. The exact IFU remains necessary for clinical use.

LDM and related ultrasound: human evidence

Compiled October 9, 2026. Initial four-study evidence map, not an exhaustive systematic review.

Bottom line

There are human signals worth investigating, especially for redness/barrier dysfunction and acne. Confidence is limited by small samples, observational designs, short or selective follow-up, and differences between devices. A statistically significant change from baseline is not a sham-adjusted treatment effect. [1][2][3][4]

Redness and barrier dysfunction

Study Design and endpoint Finding Translation limit
Park et al., 2018; PMID 29746429 Retrospective, 42 refractory-rosacea patients; erythema index, TEWL and self-assessment Significant decreases in all three measures reported No randomized control; abstract-only appraisal here; exact platform/parameters and conflicts need full-text verification [1]
Kim et al., 2021; PMID 33670631 28 enrolled, 26 completed; four weekly treatments; six-week assessment TEWL decreased by 5.37 ± 13.22 g/m²/h and erythema index by 39.73 ± 44.21 units; no serious adverse effects observed Uncontrolled short follow-up; SONO STYLER (Weyergans), 1/3 MHz, is related ultrasound evidence, not a Wellcomet LDM trial [2]

Section finding — S04/S05: the signal concerns redness and barrier measures. The erythema-index change is an instrument-unit difference, not “39.73% less redness.” The 2021 paper declares no conflict of interest; that does not solve the absence of a control group.

Acne

Chervinskaya et al. (online 2024; journal issue 2025) retrospectively compared two clinics using LDM MED Tri 1/3/10 MHz or LDM Triple 3/10/19 MHz. Twenty-two patients contributed the reported photo-score analysis (11 per group); the methods initially describe 24 individuals. Nine had follow-up controls. Nine dermatologists assessed photos blinded to treatment assignments. [3]

The mean modified Global Evaluation Acne score improvement was 73.69% at treatment completion and 90.14% at follow-up. These are changes in a photo-rating scale, not percentages of patients cured or lesion-count reductions. There was no untreated/sham group, and each clinic used a different platform, confounding device with site. The paper disclosed a Wellcomet CEO coauthor. [3]

Section finding — S06: promising observational acne results; no demonstrated 19-MHz superiority and no basis for replacing established acne care. The small selective follow-up group makes durability especially uncertain.

Wrinkles, pores and collagen claims

The 2012 multicentre LDM-MED study included 39 subjects, with eight to twelve treatments and an assessment about one week after the course. Mean coarse- and fine-wrinkle scores improved by 0.63 and 0.73 grades. It was nonrandomized and unblinded; two preliminary split-face examples were not a controlled trial of the full cohort. A coauthor was a Wellcomet managing partner. Temporary erythema was reported. [4]

Section finding — S07: cosmetic appearance signals do not quantify new collagen, demonstrate sustained lifting, or establish parity with RF, HIFU or resurfacing.

How to use this evidence

Match the treatment's promised outcome to the actual study endpoint and exact model. Before-and-after photos should use consistent lighting, pose and timing. Require a credible comparator before accepting superiority claims. For an adjunct procedure, specify how benefit would be assessed separately from the concurrent skincare or treatment.

Evidence gaps

Independent randomized sham-controlled studies; broader skin-type reporting; comparisons with standard care; standardized adverse-event monitoring; long follow-up; and clinical equivalence data for consumer/OEM devices would materially change confidence. Small reports of few adverse effects do not establish universal safety.

Compiled October 9, 2026. Official specifications are dated snapshots; no current price ranking or purchase recommendation.

Bottom line

Identify the exact device before assessing an LDM offer. A multi-frequency home wand, an OEM “LDM” label, and a studied Wellcomet platform require separate evidence. This initial section establishes the professional reference and the verification questions; it does not certify lookalikes.

Regulatory and safety status

An official product page is a claim/specification source, not a clearance database or IFU. This pass has not verified an exact-model FDA clearance, complete IFU contraindication list, or home-use authorization for LDM. That is an open verification gap, not a statement that no authorization exists anywhere.

Before any procedure, the provider should use the current exact-model IFU to check the indication, treatment site, relevant medical conditions, pregnancy considerations, implants, recent procedures and concurrent treatments. This is a checklist of questions, not a sourced universal contraindication list. Research-paper settings are not a home protocol.

Questions to ask before paying

  1. What is the manufacturer, exact model, serial identity and handpiece? Is “LDM” being used as a brand/technology name or just a service label?
  2. Which study used this model and measured my outcome? Redness, hydration, wrinkles and lifting require different evidence.
  3. What is the local authorized indication, and may I inspect the current IFU and credentialed operator's plan?
  4. What is the full-course cost, reassessment point and maintenance proposal? What result would count as enough benefit to continue?
  5. How are intensity, duty cycle, coupling/contact and servicing controlled? What happens if symptoms worsen?

Section finding: these are evidence-audit questions derived from the gaps in the study map, not a ranking of providers or a clinical prescription.
