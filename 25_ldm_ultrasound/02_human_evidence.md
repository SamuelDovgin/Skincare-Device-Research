# LDM and related ultrasound: human evidence

*Compiled October 9, 2026. Initial four-study evidence map, not an exhaustive systematic review.*

## Bottom line

**There are human signals worth investigating, especially for redness/barrier dysfunction and acne. Confidence is limited by small samples, observational designs, short or selective follow-up, and differences between devices.** A statistically significant change from baseline is not a sham-adjusted treatment effect. [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29746429/)[[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7922048/)[[3]](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.16672)[[4]](https://doi.org/10.4236/jcdsa.2012.22016)

## Redness and barrier dysfunction

| Study | Design and endpoint | Finding | Translation limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Park et al., 2018; PMID 29746429 | Retrospective, 42 refractory-rosacea patients; erythema index, TEWL and self-assessment | Significant decreases in all three measures reported | No randomized control; abstract-only appraisal here; exact platform/parameters and conflicts need full-text verification [[1]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29746429/) |
| Kim et al., 2021; PMID 33670631 | 28 enrolled, 26 completed; four weekly treatments; six-week assessment | TEWL decreased by 5.37 ± 13.22 g/m²/h and erythema index by 39.73 ± 44.21 units; no serious adverse effects observed | Uncontrolled short follow-up; **SONO STYLER (Weyergans), 1/3 MHz, is related ultrasound evidence, not a Wellcomet LDM trial** [[2]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7922048/) |

**Section finding — S04/S05:** the signal concerns redness and barrier measures. The erythema-index change is an instrument-unit difference, not “39.73% less redness.” The 2021 paper declares no conflict of interest; that does not solve the absence of a control group.

## Acne

Chervinskaya et al. (online 2024; journal issue 2025) retrospectively compared two clinics using LDM MED Tri 1/3/10 MHz or LDM Triple 3/10/19 MHz. Twenty-two patients contributed the reported photo-score analysis (11 per group); the methods initially describe 24 individuals. Nine had follow-up controls. Nine dermatologists assessed photos blinded to treatment assignments. [[3]](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.16672)

The mean modified Global Evaluation Acne score improvement was 73.69% at treatment completion and 90.14% at follow-up. These are changes in a photo-rating scale, not percentages of patients cured or lesion-count reductions. There was no untreated/sham group, and each clinic used a different platform, confounding device with site. The paper disclosed a Wellcomet CEO coauthor. [[3]](https://doi.org/10.1111/jocd.16672)

**Section finding — S06:** promising observational acne results; no demonstrated 19-MHz superiority and no basis for replacing established acne care. The small selective follow-up group makes durability especially uncertain.

## Wrinkles, pores and collagen claims

The 2012 multicentre LDM-MED study included 39 subjects, with eight to twelve treatments and an assessment about one week after the course. Mean coarse- and fine-wrinkle scores improved by 0.63 and 0.73 grades. It was nonrandomized and unblinded; two preliminary split-face examples were not a controlled trial of the full cohort. A coauthor was a Wellcomet managing partner. Temporary erythema was reported. [[4]](https://doi.org/10.4236/jcdsa.2012.22016)

**Section finding — S07:** cosmetic appearance signals do not quantify new collagen, demonstrate sustained lifting, or establish parity with RF, HIFU or resurfacing.

## How to use this evidence

Match the treatment's promised outcome to the actual study endpoint and exact model. Before-and-after photos should use consistent lighting, pose and timing. Require a credible comparator before accepting superiority claims. For an adjunct procedure, specify how benefit would be assessed separately from the concurrent skincare or treatment.

## Evidence gaps

Independent randomized sham-controlled studies; broader skin-type reporting; comparisons with standard care; standardized adverse-event monitoring; long follow-up; and clinical equivalence data for consumer/OEM devices would materially change confidence. Small reports of few adverse effects do not establish universal safety.
