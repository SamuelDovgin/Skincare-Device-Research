# Selection and Safety Protocol

*Compiled 2026-07-06. This is research orientation, not medical advice. Confidence legend: verified / limited claim / inference.*

## 0. Bottom line

For this archive, the default recommendation is:

1. **Acne scars or real wrinkle/scar remodeling:** choose a qualified provider using an FDA-authorized prescription device. Do not DIY.
2. **Pigment/PIH/dark marks:** do not lead with microneedling. Lead with sunscreen and pigment topicals; use procedures only with a pigment-risk plan.
3. **Superficial glow/product feel:** microneedling is optional and usually not necessary. If used, keep it superficial, sterile, single-user, and non-bloody.
4. **RF microneedling:** professional only. FDA says RF microneedling devices should not be used at home.

## 1. Why this page exists

The earlier docs establish mechanism, evidence, and regulation. This page turns that into a practical picker: what to ask, what to avoid, and when to stop.

## 2. Goal picker

| Your goal | Best starting path | Microneedling role |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling/boxcar acne scars | Dermatology/aesthetic provider consult | Strong candidate for professional microneedling series |
| Ice-pick scars | Dermatology consult for TCA CROSS/punch/subcision/fractional options | Usually adjunct, not enough alone |
| Fine lines / neck wrinkles | Provider consult with device-specific clearance | Possible, but expect modest series-based improvement |
| Deep laxity/jowls | RF/ultrasound/surgical consult | Mechanical microneedling is not a lifting device |
| PIH/melasma/dark spots | Tinted SPF + topicals + cautious pigment-specific procedures | Not first-line; inflammation can worsen pigment |
| Product absorption | Do not puncture skin to force actives in | FDA does not authorize this use for microneedling medical devices |
| DIY curiosity | Prefer non-needling alternatives | If using a cosmetic tool, keep to superficial non-medical use |

## 3. Provider checklist

Ask these before booking:

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What exact device and K-number/De Novo is being used? | Confirms the device is legally marketed for a relevant indication |
| What indication/body area is the device cleared for? | Acne-scar clearance does not automatically mean neck/periorbital wrinkle clearance |
| What needle depth will be used by face region? | Thin skin, orbital rim, and nerves/vessels make depth region-specific |
| Are cartridges sterile, single-use, and opened in front of me? | Cartridge reuse is unsafe and inconsistent with FDA authorization |
| What is the protocol for my Fitzpatrick type and pigment history? | PIH risk changes with skin tone, melasma, recent sun, and inflammation |
| What do you do for HSV/cold sore history? | FDA tells providers to consider prophylactic antivirals in patients with herpes simplex history |
| What topicals are used during/after? | Avoid unapproved drug/cosmetic/biologic delivery claims |
| What complications should trigger same-day contact? | A clinic should have a plan for infection, pigment shift, tracks, swelling, and pain |
| How many sessions and what endpoint? | "Collagen" without a session plan is not a treatment plan |

## 4. Candidate caution list

FDA's patient page says microneedling may not be suitable for people with:

- known clotting/bleeding disorders;
- immune deficiency/suppression;
- uncontrolled diabetes;
- anticoagulant therapy;
- active bacterial, viral, or fungal skin infection;
- hepatitis or HIV infection;
- eczema, psoriasis, vitiligo, or autoimmune disease;
- active facial rash;
- current herpes/cold sore outbreak;
- isotretinoin use currently or in the past 6 months;
- actinic keratoses, warts, birthmarks/moles, or keloid history in the treatment area;
- darker skin types where darkening/lightening risk is relevant;
- tanning or planned sun exposure in the two weeks after treatment;
- malignancy or chemo/radiation/steroid treatment;
- pregnancy or breastfeeding;
- allergy to stainless steel or topical/local anesthetics. [[1]](source_docs/fda-microneedling-devices-page-2026-07-06.html)

This is not an exhaustive medical screen. It is a reason to stop self-triaging and ask a clinician.

## 5. Pre-treatment logic

### For professional treatment

- Bring medication list, isotretinoin history, cold sore history, keloid history, pigment/melasma history, diabetes/immune status, and recent sun exposure.
- Ask if the provider wants pretreatment with pigment suppressors, antivirals, or barrier support.
- Avoid tanning and aggressive exfoliation before treatment.
- Do not stack other injury procedures unless the clinician designed the combination.

### For superficial cosmetic home use

This archive's conservative boundary is **0.2-0.25 mm cosmetic use only**, single-user, no bleeding, no active disease, no strong actives around use. Anything deeper should be treated as a medical procedure.

## 6. Post-treatment / stop rules

### Expected after professional microneedling

Common short-term responses can include redness, dryness, roughness, tightness, itching, peeling, discomfort, burning, bruising, bleeding, and crusting. [[1]](source_docs/fda-microneedling-devices-page-2026-07-06.html) In FDA-reviewed SkinPen acne-scar data, common responses resolved over days and there were no serious adverse events or nerve/tissue damage. [[2]](source_docs/fda-den160029-skinpen-precision-system-de-novo-summary.pdf)

### Stop and seek care

Contact a clinician promptly for:

- spreading redness, warmth, pus, fever, increasing pain, or pustules;
- blistering, scabbing beyond expected pinpoint crusting, or open sores;
- track marks, lines, or texture that persists;
- darkening/lightening that is worsening or persistent;
- swelling around eyes, severe bruising, numbness, weakness, or nerve-like pain;
- cold sore flare;
- any reaction after RF microneedling that feels like burn, fat loss, contour change, numbness, or deep pain.

## 7. Aftercare logic

| Use | Safer posture |
|---|---|
| Cleanser | Gentle, non-exfoliating |
| Moisturizer | Bland barrier support; follow provider instructions |
| Sunscreen | Mandatory once skin can tolerate it; pigment risk depends heavily on UV/visible light exposure |
| Actives | Pause retinoids, acids, benzoyl peroxide, strong vitamin C, fragrance, and exfoliants until healed or cleared by provider |
| Makeup | Avoid until the provider's window has passed and skin is closed/calmed |
| Occlusion | Do not trap heat or irritation after fresh procedure unless clinician-directed |
| Swimming/sauna/sweating | Avoid while channels are fresh and irritation is active |

Do not interpret "product absorption" as a reason to apply stronger actives. Freshly disrupted barrier makes a normal product behave like a much more aggressive exposure.

## 8. Decision matrix

| Scenario | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| You have acne scars and can afford a provider | Book consult; prioritize FDA-authorized Rx device, scar-subtype plan, PIH prevention, and 3-6 session expectations |
| You have mild texture, no scars, and sensitive/pigment-prone skin | Skip needling; use retinoid/azelaic/niacinamide/tinted SPF and consider LED/barrier work |
| You own a 0.2 mm roller | Treat as cosmetic exfoliation only; do not chase bleeding or scars |
| You own a 0.5-1.0 mm stamp | Do not use for facial scar treatment at home; the risk/benefit is poor |
| A product says "FDA facility" | Ask for device K-number and indication; facility language is not clearance |
| A med spa offers RF microneedling cheap | Ask provider license/training, exact device, indication, depth/energy plan, complication plan; avoid discount-driven deep heat |
| You are considering RF microneedling at home | Do not buy/use. FDA says these devices should not be used at home. |

## 9. What would change the conclusion

The archive would soften its stance on home tools only if a consumer device had:

- FDA OTC authorization for a specific scar/wrinkle indication;
- sterile single-use cartridges or a validated replacement system;
- reliable depth limitation and depth accuracy data;
- clear exclusion criteria and patient labeling;
- prospective clinical outcomes with adverse-event tracking;
- no topical/drug/PRP delivery claim drift.

No such home medical microneedling device was found in the 2026-07-06 source pass.

## Evidence gaps

- Need direct provider IFUs and patient labels for all major QAI devices, not just FDA summaries.
- Need real-world adverse-event pattern extraction from MAUDE.
- Need better evidence for pigment-prone skin protocols and PIH prevention.
- Need independent trials comparing microneedling, fractional laser, RF microneedling, subcision, and peels by acne-scar subtype.
