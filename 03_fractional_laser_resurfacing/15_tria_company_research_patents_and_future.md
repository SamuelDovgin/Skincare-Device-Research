# Tria Beauty — Company Research, Patents, Clinical Evidence & What May Come Next

**Research page compiled:** 2026-07-15  
**Scope:** Tria Beauty, its predecessor/legacy patent holders, The Beauty Tech Group (the company that acquired Tria), and clearly separated parent-company signals.  
**Confidence legend:** ✅ official or primary record · ◇ company-hosted clinical/marketing evidence · 🔍 reasoned inference · ⚠️ unresolved or legally sensitive point.

> **Important distinction:** “Tria” has three different identities in the public record: the consumer brand, the operating company that was acquired by The Beauty Tech Group, and the older legal entities that still appear as patent assignees. A patent-assignment record is not, by itself, proof of who currently sells or operates the Tria brand.

## 0. Bottom line

1. **The Beauty Tech Group acquired Tria in 2024.** Its current portfolio includes CurrentBody Skin, ZIIP and Tria; the group says it operates R&D in California and Manchester and sells in more than 80 countries. ✅
2. **The major 2026 Tria release has already happened:** second-generation **Tria 4X Hair Removal Laser** and **Tria FRX / SmoothBeauty Laser** were unveiled in the March 30, 2026 relaunch. The refresh is primarily hardware, battery, ergonomics, branding, and channel strategy—not a publicly documented new wavelength. ✅
3. **The strongest Tria research is company-hosted clinical work, not a large modern peer-reviewed RCT program.** The FRX study reports 90 adults across Fitzpatrick I–VI and the 4X study reports 21 adults with eight monthly treatments. These are useful device-specific signals, but the public summaries do not provide the same level of independently peer-reviewed detail as a dermatology-journal trial. ◇
4. **The core Tria IP is old but technically coherent.** It covers diode hair removal, skin-contact/optical sensing, thermal control, antimicrobial windows, and a 2011–2012 fractional dermatology family that is highly relevant to the 1440 nm FRX/SmoothBeauty concept. ✅
5. **Patent ownership is now split from brand ownership.** Google Patents shows important legacy families assigned from Tria/SpectraGenics through Athyrium and Channel Investments to **Aesthete Holding Corporation** in 2025. That is not the same thing as a public statement that Aesthete owns the commercial Tria brand. ⚠️
6. **I found no public announcement of a Tria 5X, new Tria wavelength, Tria LED mask, or new Tria acne device as of this review date.** The most defensible forecast is a continued second-generation rollout, accessories/bundles, and eventual adjacent-device innovation under the wider Beauty Tech Group—not a confidently identifiable next Tria laser.

## 1. What question this page answers

This page is designed to answer four related questions:

- Who owns or controls the Tria brand now?
- What did Tria actually research, patent, and commercialize?
- Which claims are peer-reviewed, company-hosted, regulatory, or merely inferred from patents?
- What could the current owner release next, and what evidence would confirm that a product is genuinely in development?

It is not a legal opinion on patent validity, ownership, freedom to operate, or the medical safety of any device. Patent status below is a research snapshot; Google Patents itself warns that legal-status labels can be assumptions rather than determinations by a court or patent office.

## 3. What Tria has actually researched

### 3.1 Tria FRX / SmoothBeauty clinical evidence

The best device-specific public evidence is Tria’s [FRX Clinical Study 1](https://www.triabeauty.sg/pages/tria-frx-clinical-study-1), a company-hosted summary of a multi-center study.

| Feature | Publicly reported detail | Evidence grade |
|---|---|---|
| Device | 1440 nm non-ablative fractional diode laser | ✅ device description |
| Participants | 90 adults, ages 32–70, Fitzpatrick I–VI | ◇ company-hosted summary |
| Sites | KGL Skin Study Center in Pennsylvania, Baumann in Miami, and Nashville Center | ◇ company-hosted summary |
| Principal investigator | James J. Leyden, associated with dermatology at the University of Pennsylvania | ◇ company-hosted summary |
| Protocol | Five treatments per week for 12 weeks; low, medium, and high treatment levels | ◇ company-hosted summary |
| Outcomes | Self-assessment plus blind expert/investigator ratings on a nine-point scale | ◇ company-hosted summary |
| Headline self-assessment | 89% smoother texture, 82% improved pigmentation, 78% reduced wrinkles in the study summary | ◇ marketing-facing summary |
| New-product marketing figures | The relaunched FRX page reports 84% improved pigmentation, 80% wrinkle reduction, and 89% smoother skin in a 12-week, five-times-weekly course; it identifies n=87 for the displayed study claims | ◇ company study/marketing |

The important interpretation is not that the results are worthless; it is that the public evidence is **not equivalent to a current, independently peer-reviewed, preregistered randomized clinical trial with a fully available methods and statistical report**. The result language should therefore be read as device-specific clinical support, not as a general claim that every 1440 nm home laser produces the same effect.

The prior Tria owner guide in this topic remains useful for the regulatory and practical limits of the older unit: [13 — Tria SmoothBeauty owner’s guide](13_tria_smoothbeauty_owners_guide.md). It records the historical IFU’s narrow periorbital-wrinkle indication and its warnings that pigment, freckles, and veins may not respond as a user might expect.

### 3.2 Tria 4X hair-removal clinical evidence

Tria’s [4X Clinical Study 1](https://www.triabeauty.sg/pages/tria-4x-clinical-study-1) reports a smaller but more clearly quantified hair-count study:

| Feature | Publicly reported detail | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Participants | 21 adults, ages 18–45, brown or black hair, Fitzpatrick I–IV | Small, selected sample; not evidence for Fitzpatrick V–VI. |
| Protocol | Eight monthly treatments at three fluence levels with an untreated control area | Gives a useful dose-response structure, but monthly treatment is not the same as every commercial usage pattern. |
| Site and investigator | University of Arizona; Ronald Wheeland | Institutional setting is a positive signal, but the public page is still a company-hosted summary. |
| Measurement | Quantitative hair counts at each visit, plus self-assessment 12 months after the final treatment | Stronger than a purely photographic testimonial. |
| Reported result | High-setting clinician hair count was approximately 73% lower one month after the eighth treatment, with substantial variation | The “up to 100%” marketing statement should not be read as the typical result. |

The current 4X product page reports “up to” hair reduction after two to six treatments and carries the relevant skin-tone and facial-use restrictions. Those are product-page claims, not a substitute for a regulator’s indication or a peer-reviewed comparative trial.

### 3.3 Historical blue-light acne research

Tria previously publicized a small [Skin Perfecting Blue Light study](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/journal-of-clinical-and-aesthetic-dermatology-publishes-clinical-study-demonstrating--safety-and-effectiveness-of-tria-skin-perfecting-blue-light-158086085.html). The 2012 company press release describes an eight-week, single-center, open-label study of 32 people ages 13–45 with mild-to-moderate acne.

This is historically relevant because it shows Tria once explored a non-laser acne modality. It is **not** a current release signal: the device is not prominent in the 2026 Tria relaunch, and the source available here is a press release rather than the full journal article. I would not forecast a new Tria blue-light device from this historical study alone.

### 3.4 Peer-reviewed papers cited by Tria

Tria’s [clinical-studies hub](https://www.trialaser.com/pages/clinical-studies-hub) cites papers about the broader diode-laser category, including:

- [Courtney & Goldberg, 2016, Journal of Drugs in Dermatology](https://jddonline.com/articles/dermatology/S1545961616P1330X) — clinical evaluation of hair removal using an 810 nm diode laser with a scanning device.
- [Alster & Handrick, 2001, Dermatologic Surgery / PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11442611/) — early clinical experience with an 810 nm diode laser for hair removal.
- [Jo et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.5021/ad.2015.27.5.517) — diode-laser hair removal evidence in a dermatology journal.

These papers support the **technology class**. They should not automatically be treated as independent trials of the exact current Tria 4X hardware, firmware, treatment schedule, or manufacturing revision.

## 4. Evidence quality: what is published versus what is marketed

| Evidence type | Examples in the Tria record | What it can establish | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regulatory record / IFU | FDA clearance history and Tria instructions for use | A defined device, indication, warnings, and operating envelope | Broad cosmetic outcomes beyond the cleared indication |
| Company clinical summary | FRX n≈90 and 4X n=21 study pages | Device-specific feasibility and outcome signals | Independent replication, comparative effectiveness, or absence of bias |
| Peer-reviewed dermatology paper | JDD and PubMed papers on 810 nm diode lasers | Evidence for a broader modality or clinical context | That the exact Tria product has identical performance |
| Patent | Fractional, diode, sensor, thermal, antimicrobial, and connected-device claims | What the applicant sought to protect and how it conceptualized a system | That a product was built, launched, safe, or covered by every claim |
| Product page | 2026 4X/FRX claims, battery and USB-C changes | Current commercial positioning and announced specifications | A peer-reviewed efficacy result or future-launch commitment |
| Press release / RNS | Relaunch, acquisition, pipeline, group strategy | Corporate intent and disclosed plans | A detailed product roadmap or guarantee of launch timing |

For this archive, the most useful conclusion is: **Tria has real device-specific research, but the public evidence base is much thinner than its patent history and much less independent than the leading clinical-laser literature.**

The old hair-removal portfolio is better understood as a mature platform than as evidence of an imminent new wavelength. The 2026 4X relaunch appears to be a second-generation product and brand refresh built on that established category.

### 5.3 Safety, skin sensing, thermal control, and device architecture

Other Tria/SpectraGenics families address the practical failure modes of a handheld home laser:

- [US 7,413,567](https://patents.google.com/patent/US7413567B2/en) — optical skin-presence sensing; the device should know when a treatment surface is actually in contact with skin.
- [US 8,709,003 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8709003B2/en) — capacitive skin sensing; a complementary approach to contact/safety detection.
- [US 8,518,027](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8518027B2/en) and [US 10,342,617 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10342617B2/en) — phototherapy thermal-control concepts and a continuation.
- [US 8,512,322](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8512322B1/en) — antimicrobial layer for an optical output window.
- [US 8,965,214 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8965214B2/en) — manufacturing/IR communications infrastructure; useful as an engineering signal, not proof of a current consumer feature.
- [US 8,777,935 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8777935B2/en) — optical sensor system for identifying skin conditions/presence.

These families suggest that Tria’s defensible engineering advantage was not simply “a diode at a particular wavelength.” It was the combination of a laser source, contact/safety sensing, thermal management, handheld ergonomics, and a dose envelope that could be used without a clinician.

### 5.4 Trademarks and product naming

The historical brand record includes TRIA BEAUTY, TRIA HAIR REMOVAL LASER PRECISION, and SMOOTHBEAUTY LASER. Product-name searches did not provide a reliable independent registration trail for every current marketing name, including FRX and 4X. That does not prove no filing exists; it means the public search used for this page did not establish one.

## 6. What the 2025 Aesthete assignments mean

The assignment history visible on [US 9,308,390 B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US9308390B2/en) shows a chain from Tria Beauty through Athyrium Opportunities Fund and Channel Investments, LLC to Aesthete Holding Corporation on 2025-08-26. Similar current-assignee results appear across other legacy families.

Three conclusions are reasonable:

1. **The older patent assets were actively consolidated or transferred.** They were not simply left under the original Tria Beauty name in the public patent record.
2. **Aesthete may be an IP-holding vehicle or successor entity.** The accessible records do not, by themselves, establish Aesthete as the consumer-facing owner of the relaunched Tria site.
3. **The Beauty Tech Group’s acquisition and Aesthete’s patent assignment may be related economically, but the relationship is not proven by the records used here.** Keep “brand owner” and “patent assignee” as separate columns in any future diligence.

For freedom-to-operate work, the next step would be a professional chain-of-title review using USPTO assignment documents, corporate filings, and the exact claims that matter to a proposed product. This page intentionally does not make that legal determination.

## 7. The Beauty Tech Group: parent strategy and current innovation signals

### 7.1 What the parent has disclosed

The group’s [FY25 annual-report preview](https://www.thebeautytechgroup.com/assets/documents/annual-report-fy25-preview.pdf) describes four core technology areas—LED, radiofrequency, microcurrent, and laser—and says the portfolio is expanded through internal development and selective acquisitions. It reports FY25 brand revenue of approximately £125.8m for CurrentBody Skin, £13.2m for ZIIP, and £2.0m for Tria.

The group’s [final-results announcement](https://www.thebeautytechgroup.com/regulatory-news/6585A) says it has a pipeline of more than 40 products and range extensions across its three brands and that development cycles commonly run two to three years. That is a meaningful innovation signal, but it is **not a Tria-specific list of 40 devices**.

### 7.2 Parent-family patent signals

The clearest recent patent signal associated with the CurrentBody side of the group is:

- [US 2025/0128086 A1 — Facial irradiation device](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20250128086A1/en), published April 24, 2025, assigned to Currentbody.com Limited, and pending in the public record at the time of review. The application describes a wearable facial irradiation device with red/near-infrared LED elements, monitoring/control, and wavelength-related configurations.
- [USD 1,082,026 S1 — wearable LED hair-regrowth design](https://patents.google.com/patent/USD1082026S1/en), published July 1, 2025, assigned to Currentbody.com Limited. This is a design patent: it is a form-factor signal, not evidence of performance or a Tria product.

These filings show that the parent/group is still protecting wearable energy-based beauty hardware. They do **not** show that Tria will become an LED brand or that the pending facial-irradiation application will launch as a product.

### 7.3 A useful false-positive to avoid

[US 2026/0014387 A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20260014387A1/en) describes a modular LED mask with detachable sections, magnetic connectors, and multiple wavelength bands. The accessible record identifies Tech Activated Beauty LLC, not Tria, CurrentBody, or The Beauty Tech Group. It is a good example of the kind of filing that can look strategically relevant while lacking a verified corporate link. It should not be attributed to Tria.

## 8. What already released in 2026

### Tria FRX / SmoothBeauty 2nd Gen

The [official FRX product page](https://www.trialaser.com/products/smoothbeauty-laser-mask-starter-kit) describes the new device as a 1440 nm non-ablative fractional laser. The relaunch material highlights:

- more than 50% more battery life;
- USB-C charging;
- a larger battery and exhaust vent;
- a streamlined silhouette and tighter seams;
- a 45-minute full-charge figure on the product page;
- a 12-week course at five uses per week followed by a rest period;
- updated company-study outcome claims.

The meaningful product story is a **hardware and usability refresh of a known 1440 nm category**, not a publicly disclosed 1927 nm pigment laser or a new optical architecture.

### Tria 4X 2nd Gen

The [official 4X page](https://www.trialaser.com/products/hair-removal-laser-4x?variant=39759036186701) continues the home 810 nm diode hair-removal positioning. The page describes a rechargeable handheld product, study-based “up to” reduction claims, and restrictions that include Fitzpatrick I–IV and facial-use limitations. The 2026 relaunch announcement calls it the second-generation 4X.

### Phytograph aftercare gel

The [Phytograph gel](https://www.trialaser.com/products/phytograph-hydrating-calm-soothing-gel) is an adjunct product positioned for cooling and soothing. Its botanical ingredient list may be relevant to user experience, but the product page does not establish that it adds laser efficacy. It is better categorized as an aftercare/retention product than as a new treatment platform.

### The most likely strategic path

🔍 **Inference:** The Beauty Tech Group is more likely to extract more value from the Tria name through the already-launched 4X/FRX platforms, replenishment/aftercare, channel expansion, and incremental hardware revisions before it introduces a completely new Tria laser. The parent’s public strategy favors a portfolio of consumer energy devices and long development cycles; that makes a future adjacent product plausible, but not necessarily one branded Tria or based on a new wavelength.

## 10. What would count as real evidence of a next Tria device

For future updates, rank signals in this order:

1. **A new official Tria product page or user manual** with a model name, wavelength, treatment protocol, and contraindications.
2. **An FDA 510(k), De Novo, or other regulator-facing record** naming the applicant, manufacturer, device, and intended use.
3. **A patent application with a verified assignee chain** connecting Tria, The Beauty Tech Group, CurrentBody, or a clearly documented successor entity.
4. **A peer-reviewed clinical paper or registered clinical study** naming the exact device and protocol.
5. **A Beauty Tech Group RNS/annual-report disclosure** that names Tria rather than referring only to the group-wide pipeline.
6. **A launch-page teaser, retailer listing, or job posting**—useful early signals, but weaker until corroborated by one of the records above.

Do not count a generic LED-mask patent, an unrelated “Tria” trademark, an OEM listing, or a patent whose assignee has no documented relationship to the group as proof of a coming Tria product.

## 11. Research gaps and next update targets

- Obtain the complete FRX and 4X clinical-study reports, not only the public summaries, and extract sample flow, missing data, statistical tests, and adverse-event definitions.
- Verify the current FDA database entries and labeling for the 2nd-gen products rather than assuming that the 2026 hardware refresh has the same regulatory indication as the older SRL/SmoothBeauty unit.
- Pull USPTO assignment documents for the key Aesthete-held patent families and map the exact legal chain to Tria’s 2024 acquisition.
- Search new patent publications quarterly under Tria Beauty, Aesthete Holding Corporation, The Beauty Tech Group, Currentbody.com Limited, CurrentBody, and relevant subsidiaries.
- Track whether the pending CurrentBody facial-irradiation application becomes granted, abandoned, or tied to a named commercial product.
- Look for a new Tria manual before relying on any retailer specification. Manuals usually reveal the real wavelength, treatment limits, skin-tone restrictions, charging system, and safety changes earlier than promotional copy.
