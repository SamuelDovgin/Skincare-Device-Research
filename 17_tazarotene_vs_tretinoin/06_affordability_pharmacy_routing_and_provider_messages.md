## 4. Every savings route worth checking

### A. Insurance: check this before cash coupons

Ask your insurer or pharmacy benefits manager for the exact copay for **generic tazarotene cream 0.1%, 60 g**, and whether the drug requires prior authorization or step therapy. A plan may prefer a particular retail chain or mail-order pharmacy. Some plans price a 90-day supply differently, while others impose a quantity limit.

Insurance may be the best option if the copay is low, but a discount card may be cheaper when the drug is excluded or the copay is high. Discount cards generally cannot be combined with insurance; the pharmacist must process one route or the other. Use the same route for the whole prescription and ask which is cheaper before paying.

Insurer question to copy:

> Please quote my out-of-pocket cost for generic tazarotene cream 0.1% in a 60 g tube at an in-network pharmacy near ZIP 60487. Is 60 g covered, what quantity limit or day-supply limit applies, is prior authorization required, and would a 90-day or mail-order fill reduce my cost? ### I. Compounding pharmacy

Compounding is a fallback, not the first affordability move here. FDA says compounded drugs are not FDA-approved and are not reviewed by FDA for safety, effectiveness, or quality before marketing; the agency recommends compounding when a patient has a medical need that cannot be met by an FDA-approved product. [[13]](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/human-drug-compounding/compounding-and-fda-questions-and-answers)

Because a commercial 60 g tazarotene 0.1% cream exists, “I want a larger tube” alone is not a strong reason to prefer compounding. A compound could make sense if you need a specific excipient-free vehicle, have an allergy, cannot obtain the commercial product, or your prescriber has another documented reason. If you ever use one, verify the pharmacy’s state license, ask who actually compounds it, request the beyond-use date and storage instructions, and ask what quality testing is performed.

Avoid unlicensed overseas or marketplace sellers. A low price does not establish that the product contains the labeled strength, was manufactured under U.S. quality controls, or was legally dispensed for you.
