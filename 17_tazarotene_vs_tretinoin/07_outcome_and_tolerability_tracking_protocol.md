# Retinoid Outcome and Tolerability Tracking Protocol

*Compiled 2026-08-23. Research orientation, not a dosing, switching, pregnancy, or treatment protocol. Follow the exact prescription and current label; contact the prescriber for persistent irritation or any decision to stop, restart, or change a prescription retinoid.*

## 0. Bottom line

The practical winner between tazarotene and tretinoin is not the molecule with the strongest internet reputation. It is the prescribed product that produces meaningful change **with enough adherence and tolerability to continue**. The [retinoid outcome tracker](retinoid_outcome_tracker.html) creates a one-product-at-a-time record so acne response, slow photoaging change, adherence, irritation, and confounders are not collapsed into a vague impression.

## 1. Why this addition exists

The evidence pages establish that the most relevant acne trials commonly assess about 12 weeks, while photoaging trials commonly run 24 weeks or longer. The existing decision guide also identifies long-term comparative adherence as a major gap. A simple prospective record cannot replace a trial, but it can prevent three common errors:

1. changing products before the chosen endpoint has had a reasonable observation window;
2. calling a biologically active product a success when persistent dermatitis makes it unusable;
3. attributing a result to the retinoid after changing several actives or procedures at once.

## 2. Pre-register one question

Before starting the record, write down:

- exact drug, strength, vehicle, manufacturer/NDC if known, and prescribed schedule;
- one primary endpoint: acne lesions, acne maintenance, fine-wrinkle/texture change, or another prescriber-defined measure;
- a baseline measured the same way each time;
- planned assessment horizon (typically 12 weeks for the acne evidence lane or 24 weeks for the photoaging evidence lane—not a promise that either product will work by then);
- other products/procedures that will stay constant;
- label/prescriber stop and contact rules.

AAD's current acne guideline recommends topical retinoids as an established acne class, but that class recommendation does not choose the exact molecule or vehicle for an individual. [[1]](https://www.aad.org/member/clinical-quality/guidelines/acne)

## 3. What to record weekly

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Days used exactly as prescribed | Separates exposure from calendar time |
| Primary endpoint | Uses the same count/scale/photograph protocol each time |
| Redness, burning/stinging, peeling, swelling/crusting | Makes tolerability visible instead of treating it as background noise |
| Missed applications because of irritation | Connects irritation with adherence cost |
| New actives, procedures, sunburn, illness, or climate change | Identifies confounding |
| Standardized photo date | Supports slower photoaging comparisons without daily mirror noise |

The tool's 0–4 irritation scale is an archive documentation scale, not a validated severity instrument. A “4” is defined as blistering, crusting, marked swelling, or severe/persistent discomfort and triggers label/prescriber guidance rather than an algorithmic dose adjustment.

## 4. Safety boundaries

Current tazarotene labeling contraindicates use in pregnancy and instructs interruption/reassessment for excessive irritation. [[2]](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=478ff059-9d7b-438e-a64e-c853dfac2872) Current tretinoin labels warn about excessive redness, edema, blistering/crusting, eczematous skin, sunburn, and photosensitivity. [[3]](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=b5e385a2-f6f9-41cb-8a33-eedba397043f)

The tracker therefore never recommends increasing frequency, “pushing through,” switching molecules, or combining the drugs. Pregnancy/trying-to-conceive, severe symptoms, or persistent dermatitis move the decision to the prescriber.

## 5. Interpretation rules

- Compare the same endpoint, site, lighting, and scale.
- Do not compare a 12-week acne result with a 24-week photoaging claim as if they were one outcome.
- If adherence is low, record **insufficient exposure to interpret**, not “drug failed.”
- If the endpoint improves but irritation remains persistent or disrupts use, record both; do not erase the trade-off.
- If another active/procedure changes, mark the interval confounded.
- Use the record to structure a prescriber conversation, not to self-prescribe a new regimen.

## Evidence gaps

- No validated N-of-1 method establishes that a topical retinoid caused an individual's observed change.
- Acne lesion counts and home photographs are vulnerable to measurement error and regression to the mean.
- The archive irritation scale is deliberately simple and not a substitute for clinician grading or quality-of-life instruments.
- There is still no long, independent 0.1%-versus-0.1% study that jointly measures efficacy, adherence, irritation, and collagen endpoints.
