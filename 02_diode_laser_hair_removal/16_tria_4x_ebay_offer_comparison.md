## 2. What the 20 versus 22 J/cm² labels tell us

### Short answer

The user-supplied analysis was **directionally right**: among these two Channel-branded legacy 4Xs, Listing 1's **20 J/cm²** label is credible evidence of a later specification than Listing 3's **22 J/cm²** label. It is not enough to assign either device an exact year.

“J/cm²” is fluence—laser energy delivered per unit area in a pulse—not electrical input power or battery capacity. The printed maxima differ by **2 J/cm², or 10% relative to 20 J/cm²**. No located Tria document explains whether the change reflects different hardware, firmware, measurement method, tolerances, regulatory labeling, or some combination. Accordingly:

- **Supported:** 22 J/cm² appears in earlier manuals; 20 J/cm² appears in later manuals and the current specification.
- **Reasonable inference:** with otherwise matching Channel-era labels, Listing 1 is probably later than Listing 3.
- **Not supported:** “20 J/cm² means it was made in year X,” or “22 J/cm² guarantees a stronger pulse today.”
- **Buying implication:** if both devices demonstrate equal battery/runtime and window condition, prefer Listing 1 for likely lower age. If only Listing 3 supplies convincing proof, proven function is worth more than the age inference.

### Corrections and cautions from the pasted research

1. **Do not decode the serial endings as dates.** No official Tria serial-date decoder was located. For example, treating Listing 1's ending “1432” as “2014, week 32” would contradict stronger document and corporate-label evidence and should be discarded. The official AccessGUDID record flags serial number as a production identifier but manufacturing date as absent; that does not prove Tria cannot decode a serial internally, only that the public UDI record supplies no such mapping. [[23]](https://accessgudid.nlm.nih.gov/devices/B5863271)
2. **Do not compare unlike electrical fields.** The older IFUs state the **charger's output** as 5 V / 3.0 A or 2.3 A; the eBay base labels state the **device input** as 5 V / 2 A. Those are not automatically the same specification field. Listing 1's photographed charger itself appears to be 5 V / 2 A, consistent with the 2021-era manual, but chargers can be replaced.
3. **A guide dates the booklet, not necessarily the laser—and its front cover is not enough.** The same pale model-image artwork is visible in the 2013 10471D, 2017 15017B, and 2019 15017C guides. Ask Listings 1 and 2 for the back/copyright panel. A 10471D/©2013/Dublin, 15017B/©2017/Newark, or 15017C/©2019/San Leandro mark would identify the accessory edition, but a booklet or charger can migrate between used devices. [[14]](source_docs/tria_4x_manual_timeline/2017_Tria_4X_QSG_15017B_Channel.pdf)[[15]](https://manualzz.com/doc/90394203/tria-4x-hair-removal-laser-quick-start-guide)[[32]](https://manuals.plus/wp-content/sideloads/tria-hair-removal-laser-4x-quick-start-guide-original.pdf)
4. **20 versus 22 is not an output test.** The base label states a rated range; it does not reveal current calibration, optical-window loss, battery voltage sag, or whether the unit can pulse repeatedly.
5. **“Three years minimum” is a design-life statement, not an expiry date.** It is still important: a likely 2017–2020 Listing 3 is well beyond that minimum, so its battery should be priced as an unverified aging component unless the seller demonstrates runtime. [[4]](Tria_4X_IFU_older.pdf)[[13]](source_docs/tria_4x_manual_timeline/2015_2016_Tria_Beurer_LAS100_IFU_16545A_7-22Jcm2.pdf)
6. **Shell color is not a generation code.** This is now positively disproven rather than merely unconfirmed: contemporaneous Tria releases document green, hot/fuchsia pink, gray, and lavender in 2013, with more limited colors in Japan. Green remained offered alongside fuchsia in 2018. [[21]](https://www.atpress.ne.jp/news/37647)[[22]](https://www.newswire.co.kr/newsRead.php?no=699017)[[31]](https://prtimes.jp/main/html/rd/p/000000001.000035618.html)
7. **GUDID publication dates are not launch or build dates.** Fuchsia is documented in retail by July 2013 even though its U.S. UDI records were published in 2017 and 2018. Use those records to identify official variant/catalog families, not to date an individual laser. [[24]](https://accessgudid.nlm.nih.gov/devices/B5863174)[[26]](https://www.amazon.sg/Tria-Beauty-Removal-Laser-Fuchsia/dp/B00DLJ7N2U)
8. **The “6/24/2007” line is not a hidden manufacture date.** It refers to FDA Laser Notice 50 printed on both visible base labels. It does not mean either device was built in 2007.
9. **The charger's trace string is not safely decoded.** Listing 1's adapter appears to be a Tria-branded PSA10A-050, 5 V / 2 A supply with a partly visible code beginning around “J2142…”. No reliable Phihong date-code key was located, and the same charger type is shown with all three listings. Treat any tempting “2021/week 42” interpretation as unverified unless Tria or Phihong confirms it.

## 6. Arrival-day acceptance test

Record one continuous unboxing video before cleaning or charging the device.

1. Show the sealed package and shipping label, then every side of the device, treatment window, base label, charger, and accessories.
2. Photograph any cracks, clouding, burnt residue, damaged vents, loose seams, or charger-cord damage before using it.
3. Confirm the charger label says **5 V DC / 2.0 A** and is the correct Tria-style supply. [[4]](Tria_4X_IFU_older.pdf)
4. Charge fully; then disconnect the charger. The 4X cannot perform a treatment while connected. [[4]](Tria_4X_IFU_older.pdf)
5. Power on, unlock with the base skin sensor, and cycle all five levels.
6. After a conservative patch test, verify repeated pulses at a tolerated setting and confirm the display remains on.
7. Check practical runtime. The current official FAQ says around **30 minutes per full charge**; a legacy used unit that cannot provide at least roughly 15–20 useful minutes has a material battery defect for this purchase decision. [[5]](https://www.trialaser.com/products/hair-removal-laser-4x)
8. Preserve the listing, messages, proof video, unboxing video, and test notes. If it arrives faulty or materially unlike the listing, eBay says most covered purchases can be returned for that reason even when the seller says “no returns.” This is buyer protection, not a warranty against later battery aging. [[8]](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210)

### Ordered Listing 1: battery benchmark and fair return line

The seller answered **“Yup it is”** when asked whether the Tria was in working condition. That representation helps if the device is materially faulty, but it does **not** promise new-battery capacity or a particular runtime. [[40]](source_docs/tria_4x_seller_history/2026-07-24_hunlar-39_working-condition-message.txt)

Tria publishes two approximate anchors: its FAQ says a full charge provides around **30 minutes of treatment**, while the legacy quick-start guide maps **600 pulses** to roughly **25–30 minutes** for one upper or lower leg. The same workflow tells the user to choose the highest level they can tolerate, but it does not expressly guarantee 600 **level-5** pulses per charge. The full legacy manual also warns that high-energy or large-area treatment may require recharging. Therefore **600 pulses / 30 minutes is a comparison proxy, not a warranty specification**. [[4]](Tria_4X_IFU_older.pdf)[[5]](https://www.trialaser.com/products/hair-removal-laser-4x)[[14]](source_docs/tria_4x_manual_timeline/2017_Tria_4X_QSG_15017B_Channel.pdf)

The user's proposed **60% screen** converts to **360 pulses** and **18 minutes**. The archive's practical decision bands are:

| Result after a genuine full charge | Practical interpretation | Return position |
|---|---|---|
| **450–600+ pulses / 23–30 min** | Good result for an older unit | Keep, absent another defect |
| **360–449 / 18–23 min** | Degraded but reasonably usable | Usually keep; not a strong not-as-described case by itself |
| **300–359 / 15–18 min** | Weak / borderline | Retest after another complete charge; return is arguable, not assured |
| **Under 300 or under 15 min** | Materially poor practical runtime | Fair return threshold if repeated and documented |
| **Under 200 or under 10 min, only a few dozen pulses, or repeated load shutdowns** | Functionally severe battery failure | Strongest factual basis for “faulty / does not work as represented” |

These bands are **purchase-decision heuristics**, not Tria specifications or eBay adjudication rules. Pulse count and elapsed time should be considered together: slow repositioning can inflate minutes, while fast repositioning can lower them. Run the test twice after complete charges, at room temperature, with the charger disconnected. Record the full battery indicator, pulse counter starting at zero, level, elapsed time, final counter, and shutdown/low-battery behavior in one continuous video.

Use level 5 only after a 24-hour patch test and only if it is tolerable on suitable, shaved, clean, dry skin. Move to a new overlapping spot after every pulse. Do not fire toward a camera, into open air, on unsuitable skin, or repeatedly on one spot merely to accumulate a test count. If level 5 is not tolerable, test at the highest safe level and do not present that result as a level-5 measurement. [[4]](Tria_4X_IFU_older.pdf)[[14]](source_docs/tria_4x_manual_timeline/2017_Tria_4X_QSG_15017B_Channel.pdf)

### What eBay protection does—and does not—cover

eBay's Money Back Guarantee covers an eligible purchase when the item arrives **faulty, damaged, or materially unlike the listing**, even if the seller says “no returns.” For a standard not-as-described return, eBay currently states that the request must normally be opened within **30 calendar days after the estimated or actual delivery date**, whichever applies; the seller is responsible for return shipping when the item does not match the listing. eBay does not publish a “60% battery” rule, and a truthful claim should describe the measured failure—not assert that an invented threshold is official. [[8]](https://www.ebay.com/help/policies/ebay-money-back-guarantee/ebay-money-back-guarantee-policy?id=4210)

If the repeated test establishes a material failure, preserve the seller's working-condition message and report the facts in this form:

> The seller confirmed in messages that the device was in working condition. After two complete charges, it repeatedly shuts down after approximately ___ pulses and ___ minutes while used unplugged. Tria states that a full charge should provide around 30 minutes of treatment. The device cannot sustain normal cordless treatment as represented.

The most defensible archive position is: **under 300 pulses or under 15 minutes after two complete charges is fair grounds to seek a return; under 200 pulses / 10 minutes or erratic shutdown is substantially stronger.** A result above 360 pulses / 18 minutes is unlikely to be a strong battery-only not-as-described claim when the listing merely said “used” and the seller only confirmed “working.” eBay makes the final coverage decision.

## Evidence gaps

- None of the three listings supplies an unplugged unlock-and-pulse video.
- None supplies a timed runtime test or purchase/manufacture date.
- No located Tria source publishes a consumer-facing serial-number date decoder; serial-derived years should be treated as unsupported.
- The official GUDID records identify color/catalog families and flag serial numbers as production identifiers, but they do not expose a manufacturing-date identifier or serial-to-date key.
- The exact 22→20 J/cm² transition remains unresolved because the dated 2017 and 2019 quick-start guides omit technical specifications.
- The boxed Marketplace specimen narrows plausibility through a Channel-era 20 J/cm² label, but its outer sleeve has no visible barcode/revision panel and its serial has no public date decoder; the approximately 2019–2022 estimate therefore remains inferential.
- No located manufacturer source explains why the printed maximum changed or demonstrates that a used 22 J/cm² unit delivers more energy today than a used 20 J/cm² unit.
- A charger or booklet can be swapped between devices, so its date/specification can corroborate but not prove the laser's manufacture date.
- Listing 1's and Listing 2's guide back/copyright panels are not photographed; the shared front artwork was used in at least 2013, 2017, and 2019.
- No public Phihong key was located for the partly visible charger trace string, so it cannot safely be converted into a year/week.
- Dated company releases establish that green and fuchsia/pink variants both existed in 2013, but no complete global discontinuation/reintroduction ledger was located for every shade and regional SKU.
- Listing 3's window photo cannot rule out fine scratches, clouding, residue, or edge damage.
- Listing 2 has no base-label or serial photo, so exact identity is inferred from the shell.
- The seller-entered country-of-origin, body-area, battery-life, and power-source fields are not reliable technical evidence.
- Coupon stacking with an accepted Best Offer was not verified; only checkout can establish the final total.
- Active eBay asking prices do not establish completed-sale value.
- The seven-item hunlar-39 sample exposes three paid-price discounts but no rejected offers, counteroffer sequence, time-on-market, acquisition cost, or Tria-specific transaction; it supports a negotiation range rather than a reliable acceptance probability.
- Listing 1's **$60 accepted offer is now verified**, but the sold page does not establish whether the earlier $50 or $53 offer would ever have been accepted; both expired without a seller response.
- No located Tria source guarantees a minimum number of level-5 pulses per charge. The 600-pulse/25–30-minute guide and 30-minute battery FAQ support a practical benchmark, not a warranty threshold.
- eBay does not publish a battery-capacity percentage that automatically qualifies for Money Back Guarantee coverage; the relevant question is whether the received unit is materially faulty or unlike its description.
